# Preface

The Law on Juvenile Justice has been prepared and drafted in accordance with the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia, the Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure, the political program, and the Rectangular Strategy of the Royal Government of Cambodia, which focuses on four priority reform programs, including legal and justice system reform, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, minimum standards for the management of juvenile justice, United Nations rules for the protection of juveniles deprived of their liberty, protocols, covenants, and various international conventions related to women and children, and human rights conventions ratified by Cambodia, as well as other national laws related to children.

The Law on Juvenile Justice is a significant achievement of near 14 years of cooperation between the Ministry of Social Affairs, Veterans and Youth Rehabilitation, the Ministry of Justice, the Ministry of Interior, the Cambodia National Council for Children, the Council of Jurists, the Council for Social, Economic and Cultural Affairs of the Office of the Council of Ministers, and relevant ministries and partner organizations. This law was drafted to address and supplement the gaps in the implementation of criminal justice procedures for juveniles who commit offenses, in accordance with national and international standards to be more clear and comprehensive, aimed at preventing and reducing all forms of committing offenses in society effectively.

The Law on Juvenile Justice is a very important instrument that not only provides understanding and clearer implementation for judicial officers and law enforcement officers in the protection and management of the criminal justice sector for juveniles, but also makes law enforcement officers understand the rights and preventive procedures which are primary principles and the reintegration of juveniles who commit offenses to live in the community, with the aim of serving the best interests of the child, making all procedures related to juveniles who commit offenses child-friendly, with incriminating evidence in taking measures against juveniles, arrest, detention, and imprisonment of juveniles as a last resort, and striving to avoid all negative impacts on children, especially in the criminal record.

Furthermore, the Law on Juvenile Justice also ensures consistency, smoothness, and harmony with existing laws for implementation towards juveniles and has contributed to promoting awareness among Cambodian citizens, including juveniles who commit offenses, to understand more clearly about their rights and the limits of exercising their rights, especially understanding the procedures that actors in the criminal justice system perform towards them regarding what needs to be done and what is prohibited.

# CHAPTER 1

## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1.- Purpose

This law has the following purposes:

- To ensure the protection of the rights and best interests of juveniles who commit offenses

- To support the rehabilitation and reintegration of juveniles into society and the community

- To ensure the protection of social and community interests.

#### Article 2.- Goal

This law aims to define the measures and procedures to be strictly observed and followed in order to clearly determine the existence of any criminal offense committed by a juvenile.

The provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure, the Penal Code, and other separate laws shall be applied, except in cases provided for in this law.

#### Article 3.- Scope

This law has the scope of application to juveniles who commit offenses.

#### Article 4.- Definitions

Key terms used in this law have the following definitions:

1- Juvenile Justice refers to the protection of juvenile rights through mechanisms, rules, and procedures to be strictly observed and implemented, as well as rules for sentencing in cases where a juvenile commits an offense.

2- Competent Authority refers to a person or institution that legally performs its functions related to juvenile cases.

3- Juvenile refers to a person who is under 18 (eighteen) years of age at the time of committing an offense.

#### Article 4.- (Continued)

4- Juvenile in Need of Protection and Care refers to a juvenile who requires protection and care because they have no legally determined representative, or lack proper care and management from a legally determined representative, and are facing danger, abuse, or threats to safety and public order, or threats to their personal safety.

5- Legally Determined Representative refers to the person having parental authority, a guardian for the juvenile, and a general guardian.

6- Support Person refers to a natural or legal person who is selected by the juvenile or by the competent authority with the juvenile's consent to be responsible for supporting the juvenile during the implementation of the procedures established in this law.

7- Child-Friendly Procedure refers to processes and procedures related to encouraging juveniles to participate in cases through the use of language and behavior at a level that juveniles can understand and an environment that provides juveniles with safety, security, rights, and basic needs.

8- Report on Welfare and Social Situation Assessment of Juvenile refers to a report on the general situation of the juvenile and various evaluative recommendations made by a social agent.

9- Social Agent refers to an officer of the ministry, department, or office in charge of social affairs in the capital, province, municipality, district, or khan, or other persons who have received skills training in child and juvenile-related law and have been appointed or recognized by the Minister of the ministry in charge of social affairs.

10- Youth Rehabilitation Center refers to a center under the management of the ministry in charge of social affairs to provide protection, care, education, rehabilitation, and reintegration services for juveniles.

# CHAPTER 2

## PRINCIPLES AND FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS OF JUVENILES

#### Article 5.- Fundamental Principles

Every person who performs any function related to juveniles shall ensure respect for the following principles:

- Must pay attention to and consider all activities related to the best interests of the juvenile, especially ensuring the right to life and maximum growth and development.

- Must act with humanity and dignity towards the juvenile.

- Must provide the juvenile with the opportunity to express their views freely, and those views shall be taken into consideration, balanced according to the age, physical growth, mental intelligence, and the level of understanding of the juvenile regarding their own activities.

- Must implement according to child-friendly procedures.

- Must implement arrest, restraint, detention, and sentencing of the juvenile as a measure of last resort and for the shortest possible duration.

- Must not discriminate against juveniles based on, among others, race, color, gender, language, religious belief, social status, wealth, national origin, political tendency, or other status.

- Must not use handcuffs and other instruments that affect the physical integrity of the juvenile, except in cases of absolute necessity where other measures cannot be implemented or are ineffective.

- Must not use torture, punishment, or other cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment in any form on the physical and mental well-being of the juvenile.

- Must not reveal or disseminate images and all information related to the activities of the juvenile and which leads to showing the identity of the juvenile, and which may have an effect on the child's equality in receiving education, work, residence, or safety. Dissemination of cases can be done only with permission from the prosecutor for the public interest, but must not show the identity of the juvenile.

- Presumption of innocence until the court has final judgment.

#### Article 6.- Fundamental Procedural Rights

Every juvenile who is suspected of, or charged with, having committed an offense shall have the following fundamental procedural rights:

- The right to remain silent in the absence or presence of a lawyer.

- The right not to be coerced into confessing against oneself.

- The right to privacy.

- The right to medical examination and treatment.

- The right to be informed of the charges.

- The right to have an appointment representative notified of the arrest.

- The right to receive assistance from a lawyer of their choice or a court-appointed lawyer for free, in accordance with the conditions set by the Law on the Statute of Lawyers, from the very first stage of the proceedings.

- The right to have a legally determined representative participate in all proceedings, unless such participation is contrary to the best interests of the juvenile.

- The right to receive free assistance from an interpreter, when necessary.

- The right to present evidence.

- The right to request the summons and questioning of witnesses.

- The right to request release on bail.

- The right to request adjustment of judicial supervision measures.

- The right to communicate with an embassy or consulate in cases involving a foreign juvenile.

- Other rights as provided for in existing legal norms.

# CHAPTER 3

## CRIMINAL AND CIVIL RESPONSIBILITY OF JUVENILES

#### Article 7.- Criminal Responsibility

The age of criminal responsibility is set at 18 (eighteen) years and above. However, the court may impose criminal sanctions on juveniles from 14 (fourteen) years of age and above if the circumstances of the offense or the personality of the juvenile so require, unless otherwise specifically provided for in other laws.

#### Article 8.- Civil Responsibility

The provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 745 (Incapacity for Responsibility) and Article 746 (Responsibility of Supervisor) of Chapter 16 (Unlawful Acts) in Book 5 (Main Contracts and Unlawful Acts) of the Civil Code shall apply to the civil responsibility of juveniles.

#### Article 9.- Age Determination

The age for criminal responsibility of a juvenile shall be determined at the time the juvenile commits the offense.

Evidence of the juvenile's age shall be confirmed by a birth certificate or other documents that can confirm the birth. In the absence of documents, or where there is doubt or lack of clarity regarding the authenticity of the document, evidence of the juvenile's age can be provided by all other means that the judicial authority can trust.

Competent authorities shall strive to find evidence to determine the age of the juvenile as quickly as possible.

In case of doubt regarding the juvenile's age, the benefit of the doubt shall be given to the juvenile.

# CHAPTER 4

## SOCIAL SERVICE ASSISTANCE

#### Article 10.- Appointment and Recognition of Social Agents

The appointment, recognition, and management of social agents are under the jurisdiction of the ministry in charge of social affairs.

The procedures for appointment and recognition of social agents shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister in charge of social affairs.

#### Article 11.- Social Agents

Social agents have the following duties, among others:

- To meet with juveniles at all stages of the proceedings, especially at the first stage of the judicial police officer's proceedings and in prisons, to collect assessment information and provide urgent services to the juvenile.

#### Article 11.- (Continued)

- To collect information on family situation, behavior, education history, schooling, living conditions, growth and development, and other information related to the level of understanding, emotional problems, mental and social aspects of the juvenile in order to prepare and provide reports and recommendations on welfare and social situation assessment of the juvenile to judges, prosecutors, and the court.

- To provide emotional and social support to juveniles at all stages of the proceedings.

- To prepare and provide diversion plans to the authorities in charge of diversion for review and decision.

- To provide rehabilitation services and reintegration for juveniles.

- Other necessary duties as determined by a Prakas of the Minister in charge of social affairs.

# CHAPTER 5

## JUDICIAL POLICE AND INVESTIGATION

#### Article 12.- Jurisdiction of Judicial Police in Investigating Felonies and Misdemeanors

Specialized judicial police in charge of juvenile affairs have the duty to investigate felonies and misdemeanors, identify and arrest juveniles, and collect evidence.

Judicial police who arrest or judicial police officers who detain a juvenile must submit a report and detailed record of the arrest or detention and transfer the juvenile to the department or unit of specialized judicial police in charge of juvenile affairs.

#### Article 13.- Power of Judicial Police Officers in Investigating and Resolving Petty Offenses

In case a juvenile between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age commits a petty offense, judicial police officers shall provide education, guidance, and verbal or written warning before immediately handing over the juvenile to their legally determined representative.

In case the juvenile confesses to the offense and the offense is a petty offense, judicial police may facilitate a conciliation between the parties involved to end the dispute.

In the cases provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 above, judicial police shall make a report and send the case file to the prosecutor for legal action.

#### Article 13.- (Continued)

In case the juvenile is in need of protection and care, judicial police officers shall transfer the juvenile to the department or office in charge of social affairs in the capital, province, municipality, district, or khan to take appropriate measures according to the welfare and social situation of the juvenile. The department or office in charge of social affairs shall report the receipt of the juvenile from the judicial police to the prosecutor.

In case they find that the juvenile is in need of protection and care, the department or office shall take measures to request a suspension or deprivation of parental authority or dismissal of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in cases where the juvenile has a legally determined representative, so that the court can decide on the suspension, deprivation, or dismissal of the legally determined representative and the guardianship rights over the juvenile. In cases where the juvenile has no legally determined representative, the department or office shall request the court to decide on the guardianship rights over the juvenile.

#### Article 14.- Procedures Related to Juveniles under 14 (fourteen) Years of Age

Juveniles under 14 (fourteen) years of age who are suspected of committing an offense shall not be detained, and judicial police officers shall hand over the juvenile to their legally determined representative.

In case the juvenile is in need of protection and care, judicial police officers shall transfer the juvenile to the department or office in charge of social affairs in the capital, province, municipality, district, or khan to take appropriate measures according to the welfare and social situation of the juvenile. The department or office in charge of social affairs shall report the receipt of the juvenile from the judicial police officer to the prosecutor.

In case they find that the juvenile is in need of protection and care, the department or office shall take measures to request a suspension or deprivation of parental authority or dismissal of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in cases where the juvenile has a legally determined representative, so that the court can decide on the suspension, deprivation, or dismissal of the legally determined representative and the guardianship rights over the juvenile. In cases where the juvenile has no legally determined representative, the department or office shall request the court to decide on the guardianship rights over the juvenile.

All acts that constitute an offense committed by a juvenile under 14 (fourteen) years of age, judicial police officers shall send the case file to the prosecutor.

In case of dispute regarding the age of the juvenile at the time of committing the offense, judicial police officers shall immediately send the case file to the prosecutor.

#### Article 15.- Arrest

Judicial police may arrest a juvenile between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age only in cases where the juvenile commits a flagrant misdemeanor or felony. However, for juveniles between 14 (fourteen) and under 16 (sixteen) years of age, arrest can only be made if the juvenile commits an intentional misdemeanor. Every arrest of a juvenile must be reported to the prosecutor immediately.

In carrying out the arrest, judicial police shall avoid humiliating the juvenile or causing a loss of their dignity, and shall transfer the juvenile to the nearest department or unit of specialized judicial police in charge of juvenile affairs.

#### Article 16.- Body Search

Judicial police officers may perform a body search on a juvenile. The body search shall be performed by a person of the same gender and shall not involve a person who is implicated in the offense, has a conflict of interest, is a relative, or has a close relationship with the juvenile, except in cases where it is unavoidable.

Judicial police officers shall make a record and note regarding this matter.

#### Article 17.- Detention of Juveniles who Commit Offenses

Judicial police officers may detain a juvenile between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age if they believe the juvenile may flee, destroy evidence, endanger the safety of witnesses, victims, or public security, or for the purpose of protecting the juvenile's own safety. However, for juveniles between 14 (fourteen) and under 16 (sixteen) years of age, detention can only be made if the juvenile commits an intentional misdemeanor. Every detention of a juvenile must be reported to the prosecutor immediately. This detention shall only be used as a last resort.

Judicial police officers shall complete the investigation as quickly as possible

#### Article 18.- Notification after Arrest or Detention of Juvenile

Judicial police officers shall immediately notify the juvenile's legally determined representative after the arrest or detention of the juvenile. In case it is not possible to provide this notification immediately, the legally determined representative shall be notified as soon as possible about:

- The reason for the arrest or detention and the type of offense

- The location where the juvenile is being held

- The right of the legally determined representative to participate in the police stage.

In case it is not possible to contact the juvenile's legally determined representative, judicial police officers shall strive to contact a support person selected by the juvenile or by the judicial police officer with the juvenile's consent.

Judicial police officers shall also immediately notify a social agent after the juvenile is brought to the judicial police unit to provide emotional and social support services to the juvenile.

Judicial police officers shall immediately notify the juvenile's lawyer, if any. In case no lawyer has been selected, judicial police officers shall find a lawyer for the juvenile for free as quickly as possible after the juvenile is brought to the judicial police unit.

Judicial police officers shall maintain a list of lawyers for the juvenile to choose from.

Judicial police officers shall find a free interpreter for the juvenile in case the juvenile does not have their own interpreter.

Judicial police officers shall make a separate record of the notification or attempt to notify the legally determined representative, support person, the juvenile's lawyer, and the social agent, and the search for a lawyer and support person, regardless of whether the notification or attempt was successful.

#### Article 19.- Physical or Mental Health Examination

In case a juvenile is injured or has health problems and requires treatment, judicial police shall transfer the juvenile to the nearest health center or hospital for examination and treatment.

All costs for examination and treatment shall be borne by the state in case the parents or guardian are unable to pay.

Health-related records or reports shall be included in the case file.

#### Article 20.- Records of Arrest or Detention of Juvenile

Copies of the arrest record and the summary record of the juvenile's detention shall be provided to the lawyer upon request.

Each judicial police unit shall maintain a separate list of juvenile detentions.

#### Article 21.- Procedures Before Interrogation

A juvenile shall be given the opportunity to converse or communicate with their legally determined representative, support person, social agent, or lawyer privately and confidentially if requested, unless these persons are involved in the case. This meeting or communication shall last for 30 (thirty) minutes and shall be conducted before the interrogation, if possible.

#### Article 22.- Interrogation

Before starting the interrogation, judicial police officers shall inform the juvenile of their fundamental rights, especially the right to remain silent in the absence or presence of a lawyer.

The interrogation shall be conducted in the presence of a lawyer using child-friendly procedures, privately and confidentially.

The legally determined representative or support person, and the social agent who has rights and can provide assistance to the juvenile, have the right to participate in the juvenile's interrogation.

Judicial police officers may refuse to allow the legally determined representative to participate in the interrogation if their presence is contrary to the best interests of the juvenile. This refusal shall be made after consultation with the lawyer or social agent and the juvenile. If the legally determined representative cannot participate, a support person for the juvenile shall be allowed to participate instead, if any.

In case of absence of the legally determined representative, support person, the juvenile's lawyer, or the social agent during the interrogation, judicial police officers shall note the reason for that absence in the record and include it in the juvenile's case file.

Immediately after the interrogation is concluded, the juvenile has the right to read the record of the interrogation. In case the juvenile cannot read, the judicial police officer or the interpreter must read the record of the interrogation to the juvenile.

The judicial police officer must allow the lawyer or the legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile, or the social worker to read the record of the interrogation.

The juvenile may request to correct or add to the statement in the record of the interrogation before signing or thumbprinting the record, with a notation made.

The legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile, or the social worker present must sign or thumbprint the record. In case the juvenile or any participant refuses to sign or thumbprint the record, the judicial police officer must make a notation in that record.

The rules stipulated in this article are mandatory to follow; otherwise, it will lead to the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 23. Treatment of detained juveniles

Judicial police officers must absolutely not display the juvenile's face in case there is no prior consent from the juvenile, with consultation from the lawyer, the legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile. In case the juvenile agrees, there must be a written record and a thumbprint.

Detained juveniles must be kept separate from adults and according to gender.

Juveniles must not be subjected to torture, coercion, threats, harassment, or any other punishment that affects the dignity of the juvenile or any inhuman treatment.

The rules stipulated in this article are mandatory to follow; otherwise, it will lead to the nullity of the proceedings.

### Chapter 6: Prosecution

#### Article 24. Competence of the Prosecution Office

Public prosecution shall be carried out on behalf of the general interest by the prosecution. The prosecution shall prosecute criminal offenses involving juveniles.

Prosecutions in cases involving juveniles must be handled by a prosecutor who has received a special assignment to handle juvenile cases, whether that assignment is exclusively for juvenile cases or not. Prosecutors who are assigned to handle juvenile cases must receive specialized training in children's rights, law, and other regulations related to juvenile justice.

#### Article 25. Verification of the juvenile's age

The prosecutor must examine the case file and the complaint received in order to verify the age of the juvenile.

In case of doubt regarding the age of the juvenile, the prosecutor must open a preliminary investigation as soon as possible to find the true age of that juvenile.

In case that preliminary investigation cannot lead to a conclusion regarding the true age of the juvenile, the prosecutor must issue a closing order to send the case file to the investigating judge.

#### Article 26. Interrogation

The prosecutor must inform the juvenile and the participants in the interrogation about the rights provided for in Article 6 (Fundamental Rights in Proceedings) of this law, using language and demeanor appropriate to the level of understanding of the juvenile before conducting the interrogation. In case no lawyer is selected, the prosecutor must appoint a lawyer for the juvenile free of charge.

The interrogation of a juvenile must be conducted in private and using child-friendly procedures. This interrogation must be attended by a lawyer, a social worker, a legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile. The prosecutor may not allow the legal representative to participate in the interrogation if their presence is contrary to the best interests of the juvenile. This non-allowance must involve consultation with the lawyer or social worker and the juvenile.

In case of the absence of any of the above-mentioned persons, the prosecutor must make a notation in the interrogation record, specifying the reason.

Immediately after the interrogation is concluded, the juvenile has the right to read the interrogation record. In case the juvenile cannot read the interrogation record, the lawyer or the interpreter must read the interrogation record to the juvenile. The juvenile may request to correct or add to the statement in the interrogation record before signing or thumbprinting.

The prosecutor must allow the lawyer, the legal representative, or the person in charge, or the social worker to read the interrogation record.

The lawyer, the legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile, or the social worker present must sign or thumbprint the record. In case the juvenile or any participant refuses to sign or thumbprint the record, the prosecutor must make a notation in that interrogation record.

The rules stipulated in this article are mandatory to follow; otherwise, it will lead to the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 27. Case file closure for juveniles under 14 (fourteen) years of age

In case a juvenile is under 14 (fourteen) years of age at the time of the alleged offense, the prosecutor must decide to close the case file and must immediately return the juvenile to their legal representative.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the prosecutor must send the juvenile to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

After receiving the juvenile from the prosecutor, if the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs considers that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department of Social Affairs shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile. In case the juvenile has no legal representative

The Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

The victim or their legal representative may file a claim for compensation in civil court.

#### Article 28. Prosecutor's power to divert

In cases where a juvenile is between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age and commits a petty offense or a misdemeanor, the prosecutor must first consider diversion as provided for in Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law.

In cases where the prosecutor decides on diversion, the provisions of Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law shall apply. In this case, the prosecutor must decide to suspend the prosecution proceedings by a formal decision.

If the preparation of a diversion plan requires time, the prosecutor must suspend the prosecution proceedings and order the juvenile to be returned to their legal representative or to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs for management and care pending the implementation of the diversion plan.

The prosecutor shall notify the victim or the victim's legal representative, in case the victim is a juvenile, and the juvenile's lawyer of the decision to suspend the prosecution proceedings. The victim or the victim's legal representative may file a claim for compensation in civil court.

The prosecutor must issue a closing order in case the implementation of the diversion plan is successful, based on the results of a joint report from the social worker and the person in charge of implementing the diversion plan.

In case the juvenile fails to successfully implement the diversion plan, the prosecutor may issue a new diversion decision, considering the reasons for the failure to implement the diversion plan, the views of the juvenile, the legal representative, and consultation with the social worker or taking procedural measures.

#### Article 29. Notification of prosecution

In any case, the prosecutor's decision to prosecute must be immediately notified in person and in writing to the juvenile and, if appropriate, through the legal representative or the person in charge and the juvenile's lawyer in order to prepare for the juvenile's defense.

#### Article 30. Separation of case files

If the prosecutor examines the case file and finds that the offense involves co-perpetrators, instigators, or accomplices who are both juveniles and adults, the prosecutor may decide to issue an order to separate the case files between juveniles and adults separately.

### Chapter 7: Investigation

#### Article 31. Competence of investigations

Investigations in all cases involving juveniles are the competence of an investigating judge who has expertise related to juveniles.

#### Article 32. Opening of investigations

After receiving the closing order to send the case for investigation from the prosecution representative, the investigating judge must immediately open the investigation.

A juvenile who is accused must always have a lawyer present during the interrogation before the investigating judge. In case no lawyer is selected, the investigating judge must appoint a lawyer under the conditions provided for by the law on the statute of lawyers.

#### Article 33. Notification of being placed under examination

When the juvenile first appears, the investigating judge must verify the juvenile's identity and inform the juvenile of the evidence for the prosecution and the names of the offenses as defined by law, as well as inform the juvenile of their rights related to the investigation stage as provided for in Article 6 (Fundamental Rights in Proceedings) of this law.

The investigating judge must notify in writing the legal representative, or the person in charge, and the social worker of the decision to place the juvenile under examination.

The rules stipulated in this article are mandatory to follow; otherwise, it will lead to the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 34. Prioritizing juvenile cases

The investigating judge must prioritize handling of juvenile cases, especially cases where the juvenile is in pre-trial detention.

#### Article 35. Interrogation of juveniles

The interrogation must be conducted in private and using child-friendly procedures. This interrogation must be attended by a defense lawyer, a legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile, and a social worker.

The investigating judge may not allow the legal representative to participate in the interrogation if their presence is contrary to the best interests of the juvenile. This non-allowance must involve consultation with the lawyer or social worker and the juvenile.

The investigating judge must make a notation in the interrogation record, specifying the reasons in case of the absence of the legal representative, or the person in charge, and the social worker.

Immediately after the interrogation is concluded, the juvenile has the right to read the interrogation record. In case the juvenile cannot read the interrogation record, the court clerk must read the interrogation record to the juvenile. The juvenile may request to correct or add to the statement in the interrogation record before signing or thumbprinting.

The investigating judge must allow the lawyer, the legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile, or the social worker to read the interrogation record if requested. In case the juvenile or any participant refuses to sign or thumbprint the record, the investigating judge must make a notation in that interrogation record.

The rules stipulated in this article are mandatory to follow; otherwise, it will lead to the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 36. Postponement of interrogation

In case of the absence of a lawyer, the investigating judge must postpone the interrogation and summon the lawyer to participate in the interrogation, except in urgent cases caused by danger to life or loss of evidence. The type of urgency must be noted in the record.

The presence or absence of a lawyer does not prevent the investigating judge from deciding on whether to release or place the juvenile in pre-trial detention.

#### Article 37. Continuation of education and work for juveniles

The investigating judge may order the educational institution or employer not to suspend the juvenile's education or work if the juvenile is not in pre-trial detention.

In case the juvenile is in pre-trial detention or is implementing a diversion plan, the institution or employer may suspend the education or work until there is a final court decision. In case the court decides that the child is not guilty, the educational institution or employer must allow the juvenile to resume their education or work as normal.

#### Article 38. Investigating judge's power to divert

The investigating judge must first consider diversion as provided for in Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law for juveniles accused of committing a misdemeanor. Misdemeanors with aggravating circumstances may also be considered for diversion. In case the investigating judge decides on diversion, the provisions in Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law shall apply. In this case, the investigating judge must suspend the investigation proceedings by a formal decision and order the release of the detained juvenile if the juvenile was in pre-trial detention. The investigating judge may order the juvenile to be returned to their legal representative or to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs for management and care pending the implementation of the diversion plan, even if the diversion plan requires time for preparation.

The investigating judge shall notify the victim or the victim's legal representative, in case the victim is a juvenile, and the juvenile's lawyer of the decision to suspend the investigation proceedings. The victim or the victim's legal representative may file a claim for compensation in civil court.

The investigating judge shall issue a dismissal order in case the implementation of the diversion plan is successful, based on the results of a joint report from the social worker and the person in charge of implementing the diversion plan.

In case the juvenile fails to successfully implement the diversion plan, the investigating judge may issue a new diversion decision, considering the reasons for the failure to implement the diversion plan, the views of the juvenile, the legal representative, and consultation with the social worker or taking procedural measures.

#### Article 39. Pre-trial detention

The pre-trial detention of a juvenile is a measure of last resort.

In addition to the provisions on the conditions and reasons for pre-trial detention stipulated in the Code of Criminal Procedure, before deciding on pre-trial detention, the investigating judge must also consider the assessment report on the well-being and social situation of the juvenile, if any.

Juveniles under 14 (fourteen) years of age shall not be placed in pre-trial detention. In case a juvenile who is in detention is found to be under 14 (fourteen) years of age, the investigating judge must issue a dismissal order and order the immediate release of that juvenile, even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor, and return that juvenile to the legal representative.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the investigating judge must send the juvenile to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

In case it is found that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department or Office shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile has no legal representative, the Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

The victim or the legal representative may file a claim for compensation in civil court.

#### Article 40. Placing under judicial supervision

The investigating judge may place an accused juvenile under judicial supervision at any time instead of pre-trial detention, if the juvenile is accused of an offense punishable by imprisonment. In addition to the provisions on judicial supervision in the Code of Criminal Procedure, the investigating judge may order a juvenile who is not in detention to comply with one or more of the following obligations:

- To be under the direct supervision of parents, a guardian for the juvenile, or a general guardian, or the person in charge of the juvenile, or any appropriate family.

- To be under the control and care of a state center or a non-governmental organization center authorized to receive and care for juveniles.
- Ordering the juvenile not to go out past certain hours or to consume alcohol.
- Other measures that the court deems appropriate.

#### Article 41. Release on bail/provisional release

The investigating judge may order the release of a juvenile on bail at any time on their own initiative. An investigating judge who intends to release a juvenile on bail must inform the prosecutor for an opinion; the prosecutor must provide their opinion within the shortest possible time. In urgent cases, the investigating judge may order the immediate release of the juvenile without waiting for the prosecutor's opinion or even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor. The investigating judge must specify the reasons for such urgency in their decision.

The investigating judge must order the juvenile to be returned to the legal representative.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the investigating judge must send the juvenile to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

In case it is found that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department or Office shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile has no legal representative, the Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

The prosecutor, the juvenile, the legal representative, or the person in charge of the juvenile may request the release of the juvenile on bail at any time. The lawyer may request the release of the juvenile on bail with the consent of the juvenile. The investigating judge must decide within 5 (five) days from the date of receipt of the request.

#### Article 42. Closing orders of the investigating judge

Before issuing a closing order, the investigating judge must examine and consider the report on the assessment of the well-being and social situation of the juvenile. In case the report...

...on the assessment of the well-being and social situation of the juvenile is not sufficient, the investigating judge must order the social worker to provide an additional report as soon as possible, not exceeding 10 (ten) working days.

A dismissal order has the effect of releasing the juvenile in detention even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor.

The investigating judge must order the juvenile to be returned to the legal representative.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the investigating judge must send the juvenile to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

In case it is found that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department or Office shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile has no legal representative, the Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

#### Article 43. Appeals to the Investigation Chamber of the Court of Appeal

Before deciding to appeal to the Investigation Chamber of the Court of Appeal against a dismissal order, an order to detain, an order not to detain, an order for release on bail, or a diversion order of the investigating judge, the prosecutor must primarily consider the best interests of the juvenile.

### Chapter 8: Competence of the Court of First Instance

#### Article 44. Competence of trials

The Court of First Instance with specialized competence related to juveniles shall try felonies, misdemeanors, and petty offenses where the accused is a juvenile.

The Court of First Instance shall decide as a collegial bench of 3 (three) judges for felonies, as well as for misdemeanors and petty offenses related to felonies and misdemeanors.

The Court of First Instance shall decide by a single judge for petty offenses.

#### Article 45. Priority for juvenile cases

The court must prioritize handling of juvenile cases, especially cases where the juvenile is in pre-trial detention.

In the best interests of the juvenile while awaiting trial, the court must consider releasing the juvenile in pre-trial detention. In case the trial is postponed, the court must also consider releasing the juvenile in pre-trial detention.

#### Article 46. Pre-trial procedures

Before the trial, the court must examine the report and opinion on the assessment of the well-being and social situation of the juvenile provided by the social worker.

In case the report and opinion on the assessment of the well-being and social situation of the juvenile have not been prepared and the court needs more information, the court must order the social worker to prepare and supplement that report and opinion as required.

#### Article 47. Non-public trials

The debate and the announcement of the judgment in juvenile cases must be conducted in a non-public trial.

Only persons who have rights, obligations, or interests related to the case are entitled to enter the trial.

The legal representative or the person in charge of the juvenile must participate in the trial, unless there is a request from the juvenile to the contrary or the court considers that their participation does not serve the best interests of the juvenile.

#### Article 48. Trials

During the trial, the court must use child-friendly procedures.

In special circumstances, the juvenile's lawyer, the social worker, or the prosecutor may request the court to use a screen during the trial or use any appropriate method for the testimony.

#### Article 49. Acquittal/Exemption from prosecution

In case the court finds that the accused juvenile was under 14 (fourteen) years of age at the time of the offense, the court must decide to acquit the juvenile and order the immediate release of the detained juvenile, even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor, and return the juvenile to the legal representative.

In case the court finds that the juvenile mentioned above indeed committed the offense, the court must decide on civil compensation if there is a civil claim.

If the court finds that the act accused against the juvenile between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age is not an offense or the accused juvenile is not guilty, the court must decide to acquit the juvenile and order the immediate release of the juvenile, even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor, and return the juvenile to the legal representative, provided that the juvenile is not detained for another offense.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the court must order the juvenile to be sent to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

In case it is found that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department or Office shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile has no legal representative, the Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

#### Article 50. Assistance of a lawyer

The assistance of a lawyer is absolutely mandatory during the trial. If none is selected by the juvenile, a lawyer shall be appointed free of charge on the initiative of the trial president, under the conditions provided for in the Law on the Statute of Lawyers. The fees for the work [and] copying of the case file shall be the responsibility of the state.

The court must provide appropriate time and the right to copy the case file to the lawyer for preparing the defense. The fees for copying the case file shall be the responsibility of the juvenile in case they selected the lawyer.

#### Article 51. Providing information on rights to juveniles

At the start of the trial, the court must inform the juvenile, using language the juvenile understands, about the following rights:

- Rights related to the trial stage as provided for in Article 6 (Fundamental Rights in Proceedings) of this law.
- The right to be present at the trial.
- The right to be protected by the court from improper behavior or inappropriate questioning.
- The right to challenge judges.
- The right to make a final request; and
- The right to appeal.

#### Article 52. Court's power to divert

At the trial stage for juveniles between 14 (fourteen) and under 18 (eighteen) years of age accused of a petty offense or a misdemeanor, the court must first consider diversion as provided for in Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law. Misdemeanors with aggravating circumstances may also be considered for diversion.

In case the minimum conditions are met, the court must issue a judgment ordering a stay of proceedings and then:

- Consult with the juvenile, the legal representative, the person in charge, the lawyer, the social worker, the victim, the civil plaintiff, and other relevant persons.
- Prepare a diversion plan.
- Have the juvenile sign or thumbprint the implementation of the diversion plan.

In case the court decides on diversion, the provisions in Chapter 10 (Diversion) of this law shall apply. The court may issue a judgment deciding on civil compensation in case there is a claim.

If the juvenile is in detention, the court must order the immediate release of that juvenile, even if there is an appeal from the prosecutor, and return that juvenile to the legal representative.

In case the juvenile needs protection and care, the court must send the juvenile to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs to take appropriate measures according to the well-being and social situation of the juvenile.

...it is found that the juvenile needs protection and care, the Department or Office shall take measures to request the suspension or removal of parental authority or the termination of the guardian for the juvenile or the general guardian, in case the juvenile has a legal representative, for the court to decide on the suspension, removal, or termination of the legal representative and the guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile has no legal representative, the Department or Office must apply to the court for a decision on guardianship rights over that juvenile.

In case the juvenile successfully implements the diversion plan, with a joint report from the social worker and the person in charge of implementing the diversion plan, the court must issue a judgment deciding that the juvenile is not criminally responsible.

In case the implementation of the diversion plan fails, the court may issue a new diversion decision, considering the reasons for the failure to implement the diversion plan, the views of the juvenile, the legal representative, and consultation with the social worker or taking procedural measures.

#### Article 53. Confession at trial

The court must examine, weigh, and confirm that the confession obtained from the juvenile's own account during the trial was made voluntarily, without threats or coercion, and with an understanding of the consequences of this confession. A confession obtained through threats, coercion, or a lack of understanding of the consequences of the confession shall not be considered evidence of guilt.

A confession obtained from a diversion plan shall not be considered evidence of guilt for the same offense for any subsequent criminal proceedings.

During the trial, the court must verify the validity of the confession and other evidence of the offense.

#### Article 54. Announcement of judgment

The judgment must be announced during the trial after the debate or at a later trial session.

As a principle, the judgment must be announced in a non-public session in the presence of the juvenile, the legal representative, the person in charge, the lawyer, the social worker, and the parties with an interest in the case.

The judgment may be announced in the absence of the juvenile in case announcing the judgment in their presence would harm the best interests of that juvenile.

### Chapter 9: Competence of Higher Courts

#### Article 55. Composition of the trial council

The trial council of the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court must include at least 1 (one) judge who is specialized in juvenile matters.

#### Article 56. Sending case files to the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court

In case of an appeal, the court clerk of the Court of First Instance must prepare the case file to be sent to the Court of Appeal within a maximum of 10 (ten) days from the date of the appeal, unless there are unavoidable circumstances which must be specified in the transmittal letter.

In case of an appeal to the Supreme Court, the court clerk of the Court of Appeal must prepare the case file to be sent to the Supreme Court within a maximum of 10 (ten) days from the date of the appeal, unless there are unavoidable circumstances which must be specified in the transmittal letter.

#### Article 57. Priority of juvenile cases

The Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court must prioritize handling and trying juvenile cases, especially cases where the juvenile is in detention. For cases where the juvenile is in detention, the trial must be conducted within a maximum of 3 (three) months from the date of receipt of the case file, unless there are objective reasons that cannot be avoided.

In the best interests of the juvenile while awaiting trial, the Court of Appeal or the Supreme Court must consider releasing the juvenile in pre-trial detention. In case the trial is postponed, the Court of Appeal or the Supreme Court must also consider releasing the juvenile in pre-trial detention.

#### Article 58. Power of the Court of Appeal to divert

The provisions of Article 52 (Court's power to divert) of this law shall apply to the power of the Court of Appeal to divert.

#### Article 59. Extension of rules to Higher Courts

The rules to be applied to the Court of First Instance shall apply to the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court, unless those rules are contrary to the nature of the rules applied in Higher Courts.

### Chapter 10: Diversion

#### Article 60. Diversion

Diversion is a measure that can be used in case the juvenile has confessed to committing an offense and it can be believed that the offense was indeed committed, at each stage of the court proceedings.

#### Article 61. Authorities in charge of diversion

Authorities in charge of diversion include the prosecutor, the investigating judge, the trial judge of the Court of First Instance, and the trial council of the Court of Appeal.

#### Article 62. Objectives of diversion

Diversion of juveniles has the following objectives:

- Encouraging the juvenile to take responsibility for the mistakes and damage they caused.
- Promoting reconciliation between the victim and the juvenile.
- Encouraging the repair of damage or compensation for emotional distress to the victim.
- Educating and reforming the behavior of the juvenile.
- Responding to the real needs of the juvenile in order to reintegrate them into the family, community, or society.
- Avoiding bad emotional scars and preventing the acquisition of bad criminal experience from others during the execution of punishment, and avoiding recidivism.
- Preventing discrimination against juveniles and avoiding having a criminal record in the criminal record registry.

#### Article 63. Minimum conditions for diversion

Diversion must meet the following minimum conditions:

a- There is sufficient evidence to prosecute or demonstrate the guilt of the juvenile.
b- The juvenile voluntarily confesses to the mistake, without coercion, and apologizes to the victim.
c- The offense committed is a petty offense or a misdemeanor.
d- The juvenile agrees to the diversion after receiving consultation from a social worker or a lawyer regarding their rights and considering the effects and impacts of implementing the diversion measure.

#### Article 64. Criteria for diversion

In case a case meets the minimum conditions for diversion as provided for in Article 63 (Minimum conditions for diversion) of this law, the authority in charge of diversion, before deciding whether to divert or not, must consider the following factors:

a- Circumstances of the offense.
b- Severity of the offense.
c- Personal characteristics of the juvenile, such as age, level of understanding, issues, and character.
d- Influence from family and the surrounding environment where the juvenile lives.
e- Harm caused to the victim.
f- Safety of the victim, community, and society.

#### Article 65. Diversion measures

In case of diversion, the authority in charge of diversion may choose one or more of the following measures for the juvenile to fulfill as an obligation:

- One or more points provided for in the Code of Criminal Procedure regarding judicial supervision obligations.
- Attending school regularly according to the plan for a period not exceeding 6 (six) months.
- Spending specific time with family for a period not exceeding 6 (six) months.
- Requiring participation in education or vocational training for a period not exceeding 6 (six) months.

- Performing community service for a period not exceeding 100 (one hundred) hours. This community service is only for juveniles aged 16 (sixteen) years and older, and must be in the nature of training and facilitating their reintegration into society.
- Participating in community programs or centers that are beneficial for helping juveniles avoid recidivism.
- Staying in a specific location for a specific period of time.
- Repairing the damage to the victim.
- Other appropriate measures that fulfill the objectives of the diversion.

All diversion measures related to work must comply with the labor law and international labor conventions recognized by the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 66. Preparation and creation of a diversion plan

After receiving the diversion order from the authority in charge of diversion, the social worker must prepare and create a diversion plan. The diversion plan must be decided within a maximum of 1 (one) month from the date of receiving notification of the diversion order, unless there are special circumstances that cannot be avoided.

In creating an individualized diversion plan for any juvenile, the social worker must consider the following factors:

- Views of the juvenile, the legal representative, and other persons affected by the offense regarding the appropriateness of the proposed diversion plan.
- Background, culture, religion, and language of the juvenile.
- Educational level, intellectual capacity, circumstances, and family situation of the juvenile.
- Proportion between the proposed or selected plan and the situation of the juvenile, the nature of the offense, and the best interests of society; and
- Age and development needs of the juvenile.

The diversion plan for the juvenile must:

- Provide benefits for the juvenile.
- Make the juvenile understand the impact of their behavior on the victim of the offense and other persons.
- Correct the mistakes caused by the offense and may include compensation for damage.

- Implemented at a location where the juvenile can perform them.
- Avoid exploitation or causing injury or endangering the mental or physical health of the juvenile.
- Must not negatively affect the juvenile's education, if the juvenile is attending school; and
- Must not involve deprivation of liberty in any form of punishment.

#### Article 67. Notations to be included in the diversion plan

The diversion plan must include the following notations:

- Any diversion measure as provided for in Article 65 (Diversion measures) of this law and the duration for implementing the diversion measure.
- Detailed specifications of services and assistance to be provided to the juvenile.
- Clearly specifying the individuals or organizations providing those services and assistance.
- Specifying the responsibilities of the juvenile and the legal representative.
- Specifying the personal objectives of the juvenile and the legal representative.
- Incorporating other programs related to education, employment, recreation, and well-being of the juvenile according to the relevant situation.
- Specifying the authorities or appropriate persons in charge of implementing the diversion plan. These authorities or appropriate persons can be police officers, staff of non-governmental organizations, members of the commune/Sangkat council, teachers, or any appropriate members of the community.
- Specifying the social worker in charge of monitoring and implementing the diversion plan.
- Specifying the consent of the juvenile to the diversion plan.

The diversion plan must include:

- The identity and signature of the social worker.
- The identity and thumbprint of the juvenile.
- The identity, signature, or thumbprint of the relevant persons.

A copy of the diversion plan must be kept confidential at the office of the social worker.

#### Article 68. Decision on the diversion plan by the authority in charge of diversion

The social worker must send the diversion plan to the authority in charge of diversion for examination and decision.

In case the authority in charge of diversion agrees with the diversion plan, the authority in charge of diversion must issue a decision to implement the diversion plan.

In case the authority in charge of diversion does not agree with the diversion plan prepared by the social worker, the authority in charge of diversion must order the social worker to re-prepare a new diversion plan. The diversion plan must be decided within a maximum of 1 (one) month from the date of receiving notification of the order, unless there are special circumstances that cannot be avoided.

#### Article 69. Implementation of the diversion plan

The social worker and the person in charge of implementing the diversion plan must ensure the implementation of the decision and the diversion plan of the authority in charge of diversion in managing and monitoring the juvenile who is implementing the diversion plan.

The person in charge of implementing the diversion plan must report on the successful or unsuccessful results or obstacles encountered during the implementation of the above-mentioned plan to the social worker. The social worker must send that report along with their opinion to the authority in charge of diversion and provide a copy to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs.

#### Article 70. Modification of diversion measures

During the implementation of the diversion plan, the authority in charge of diversion may modify the diversion measures on their own initiative or at the request of the social worker, according to the actual situation, for the best interests of the juvenile.

The authority in charge of diversion must decide within 5 (five) working days after receiving the request from the social worker. In case the authority in charge of diversion fails to decide within this timeframe, the request shall be deemed approved.

The modification of a diversion plan shall not be considered a failure to implement the diversion plan.

#### Article 71. Failure to implement diversion measures

Failure to implement diversion measures refers to cases where the juvenile fails to comply with the obligations in the diversion plan or in the modified diversion measures.

The social worker must provide a report on the results of non-compliance with the obligations set in the diversion plan or in the modified diversion plan, along with an opinion, to the authority in charge of diversion for decision-making, providing a copy to the Provincial/Municipal Department of Social Affairs.

After examining the above report, the authority in charge of diversion must summon the juvenile, the legal representative or the person in charge, the social worker, the person in charge of implementing the diversion plan, and the juvenile's lawyer, if any, to appear within a timeframe not exceeding 15 (fifteen) days.

The authority in charge of diversion must question the reasons why the juvenile failed to comply with the diversion measures.

After considering the views of the juvenile, the legal representative, or the person in charge, and after consulting with the social worker, the authority in charge of diversion may issue a new diversion order or continue the proceedings according to the criminal procedure in force.

The provisions of Article 66 (Preparation and creation of a diversion plan) to Article 70 (Modification of diversion measures) above shall apply in case the authority in charge of diversion issues a new diversion order.

In case the juvenile fails to appear as summoned without a proper reason, the authority in charge of diversion must continue the proceedings according to the criminal procedure in force.

### Chapter 11: Conditional Release

#### Article 72. Conditional release

Conditional release may be granted to a juvenile convict serving a sentence in a juvenile rehabilitation center:

- After one-third of the sentence is served, if the sentence is less than or equal to 1 (one) year.
- After half of the sentence is served in other cases.

#### Article 73. Persons entitled to request conditional release

Persons entitled to request the conditional release of a juvenile include:

- The juvenile convict or their lawyer.
- The legal representative.
- The head of the juvenile rehabilitation center.

- The social worker relevant to the juvenile's case.
- Foreign diplomatic missions or consulates, in case the juvenile is a foreigner.

### Chapter 12: Granting and Reinstatement of Rehabilitation

#### Article 74. Conditions on duration for applications for judicial reinstatement of rehabilitation

An application for reinstatement of rehabilitation may be submitted to the Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal for examination only after the following periods have elapsed:

- 2 (two) years for felonies.
- 6 (six) months for misdemeanors.
- 1 (one) month for petty offenses.

This period shall start from the completion of the sentence or when the sentence is barred by the statute of limitations.

Applications may be made by:

- The juvenile convict who committed the offense while being a juvenile.
- The legal representative, in case the convict is a juvenile.

#### Article 75. Reinstatement of rehabilitation by operation of law

Rehabilitation shall be reinstated by operation of law after the following periods have elapsed:

- 3 (three) years for felonies.
- 1 (one) year for misdemeanors.
- 3 (three) months for petty offenses.

This period shall start from the completion of the sentence or when the sentence is barred by the statute of limitations.

### Chapter 13: Creation and Competence for Managing Juvenile Rehabilitation Centers

#### Article 76. Creation of juvenile rehabilitation centers

Juvenile rehabilitation centers shall be created in provinces and municipalities for the management of juveniles. These centers shall be under the management of the Ministry in charge of Social Affairs.

The organization and functioning of juvenile rehabilitation centers shall be determined by sub-decree.

#### Article 77. Categorization of juvenile management in juvenile rehabilitation centers

Management of juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers shall be categorized into 3 (three) separate sections:

- Section for juveniles in pre-trial detention.
- Section for juvenile convicts for rehabilitation programs.
- Section for juveniles preparing for release for reintegration programs.

The procedures for managing juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers who are in detention shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister in charge of Social Affairs, the Minister of Interior, and the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 78. List of juvenile names

Every juvenile rehabilitation center must have a separate register and procedures for registering and managing juveniles, as well as completing all relevant information and ensuring safety.

This separate register and procedures shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister in charge of Social Affairs and the Minister of Justice.

### Chapter 14: Measures Related to the Detention of Juveniles

#### Article 79. Objectives of detention

Detention of juveniles is conducted for the purpose of rehabilitation, specifically training, treatment, care, protection, education, and providing vocational skills with a view to helping the juvenile become a righteous and useful citizen for the nation.

Officers of the juvenile rehabilitation center in charge of managing juveniles must receive training in psychology, well-being, national and international standards related to children, and measures for protecting juveniles deprived of their liberty.

#### Article 80. Fundamental rights of detained juveniles

All detained juveniles shall have the following fundamental rights:

- The right to education and training.
- The right to be free from torture, cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment, or the use of various forms of punishment, including isolation, restriction or denial of contact with family, food reduction, and forced labor.
- The right to disciplinary measures that are consistent with the dignity of the juvenile.
- The right to be informed of internal regulations, rights, and obligations.
- The right to provide notification of detention, transfer, or release to the legal representative, the person in charge, and the lawyer.
- The right to protection, health examinations, and treatment for illness or injury, and to notify the legal representative, the person in charge, and the lawyer.
- The right to be permitted private visits from family members or friends.
- The right to an effective and confidential complaint mechanism regarding the conditions and treatment while in detention.
- Other rights recognized by the legal norms in force.

#### Article 81. Services for juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers

Competent authorities in charge of all juvenile rehabilitation centers must provide services to juveniles in detention as follows:

- Protection, care, and provision of all necessary personal needs according to the personality, beliefs, religion, gender, and age of the juvenile.
- Accommodation in groups or individually.
- Education, vocational training, arts, sports, recreation, and rest from work.

- Appropriate physical and mental health.
- Health assessment by medical officers. Information on injuries, mental illness, physical disabilities, or other relevant information must be included in the detained juvenile's case file.
- Other services that promote health and self-esteem, encourage a sense of responsibility, and encourage behavior and skills that will help the juvenile develop their potential to become a good citizen in society and help them reintegrate into society after release.

#### Article 82. Keeping of juveniles

Juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers shall be kept as follows:

- Juveniles must be kept separate from adults.
- In separate detention buildings according to gender, type and severity of offense, age, character, and physical and mental health of the juvenile. Female juveniles must be under the care of female staff.
- In separate detention buildings and transported by separate means for juveniles with contagious diseases.

If a juvenile convict reaches the age of 18 (eighteen) years while serving their sentence in a juvenile rehabilitation center, that convict shall be kept in the juvenile rehabilitation center until under 24 (twenty-four) years of age. When the convict reaches the age of 24 (twenty-four), they shall be transferred to continue serving their sentence in an adult detention center.

The procedures for transferring, handing over, and receiving convicts shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister in charge of Social Affairs, the Minister of Interior, and the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 83. Security classification in juvenile rehabilitation centers

The security classification for juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers must be in the lowest classification and shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister in charge of Social Affairs, the Minister of Interior, and the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 84. Staff in juvenile rehabilitation centers

All staff working with juveniles in juvenile rehabilitation centers, including center officers, social workers, education and vocational training specialists, counselors and life skills experts, and psychologists or psychiatrists, must receive proper training in child psychology, child well-being, and international standards on child rights and the treatment of juveniles deprived of their liberty.

Officers or staff of juvenile rehabilitation centers must not use detainees to discipline juveniles or to perform duties on their behalf.

#### Article 85. Inspection of juvenile rehabilitation centers

The prosecutor general of the Court of Appeal, the prosecutor of the Court of First Instance, the president of the investigation chamber, and the investigating judge must conduct regular inspections of juvenile rehabilitation centers within their jurisdiction and ensure that the conditions of treatment and detention are implemented in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure and the laws in force.

National preventive mechanisms and other competent officers authorized by law have the right to visit juvenile rehabilitation centers at any time.

### Chapter 15: Transitional Provisions

#### Article 86. Provisions related to judges

Prosecutor judges and judges who have been assigned to handle juvenile cases according to the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure shall continue their duties until there are judges specialized in juvenile matters.

#### Article 87. Specialized judicial police in charge of juvenile matters

Judicial police who are handling juvenile cases according to the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure shall continue their duties until there are specialized judicial police in charge of juvenile matters.

#### Article 88. Juvenile rehabilitation centers

In case there are no juvenile rehabilitation centers yet, all measures currently being implemented for juveniles shall continue to be implemented until there are juvenile rehabilitation centers.

The procedures for transferring, handing over, and receiving juveniles shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister in charge of Social Affairs, the Minister of Interior, and the Minister of Justice.

### Chapter 16: Final Provisions

#### Article 89. Implementation of this law

After entering into force throughout the country, this law shall be disseminated for a period of 6 (six) months before implementation.
