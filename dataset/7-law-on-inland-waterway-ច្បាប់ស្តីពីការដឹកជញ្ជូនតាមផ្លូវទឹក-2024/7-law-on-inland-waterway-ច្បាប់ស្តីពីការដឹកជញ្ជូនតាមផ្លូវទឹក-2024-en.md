# Law

## on

# Waterway Transport

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

### Article 1 .-
This law defines the management and development of the waterway transport and port sector in the Kingdom of Cambodia, aiming to maintain order, ensure safety and security of waterway transport, build and maintain related infrastructure, contribute to environmental protection, and provide fair, equitable, and transparent investment and competitive opportunities to serve national and international trade and support national economic development.

### Article 2 .-
This law applies to vessels, crew, ports, vessel owners or vessel lessees, shipyards, waterway infrastructure, and other related activities in the territory and jurisdiction of the Kingdom of Cambodia, except for vessels, ports, and technical equipment of the competent forces of the Ministry of National Defense and the Ministry of Interior, which are determined in separate legal instruments.

### Article 3 .-
Key terms used in this law are defined in the Glossary, which is an annex to this law.

# Chapter 2
# Competent Authority

### Article 4 .-
The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the competence to manage the waterway transport sector of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport may delegate authority or delegate management competence of certain waterway transport sectors to sub-national administrations in accordance with the laws and legal instruments in force.

# Chapter 3
# Principles

### Article 5 .-
Principles of waterway transport management are as follows:

1- Ensure free, fair, equitable, and non-discriminatory navigation and port entry/departure within the framework of national and international regulations.

* Ensure to prevent impacts on the public interest in respect of navigation and port development.

* Ensure safety, order, security, environmental protection, and harmonization with national development plans for navigation and port development.

* Comply with national and international laws regarding safety, security, and environment for vessels, crew, and ports.

* Ensure balance, harmonization, and compliance between vessels and ports, the interests of users and service providers, private and public interests, and national and international interests.

# Chapter 4
# Vessel Management

## Section 1
## Management of Maritime Vessels and Domestic Waterway Vessels

### Article 6 .-
Maritime vessels and domestic waterway vessels navigating waterways in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be registered, except for types of vessels used for family transport as determined by Sub-decree.

Vessel owners, even if foreign, who possess maritime vessels or domestic waterway vessels, shall apply for registration of the vessel.

Vessel lessees, even if foreign, may apply for registration of maritime vessels and domestic waterway vessels in accordance with the contract with the vessel owner.

### Article 7 .-
Registration of maritime vessels and domestic waterway vessels shall obtain a vessel registration certificate with permanent validity or temporary validity not exceeding 1 (one) year.

A duplicate of the registration certificate of a maritime vessel or domestic waterway vessel has equal value to the registration certificate of the maritime vessel or domestic waterway vessel issued for the first time.

### Article 8 .-
Vessels imported from abroad to apply for registration of maritime vessels or domestic waterway vessels shall have an import tax receipt. Vessels imported temporarily shall be under the temporary import customs regime.

### Article 9 .-
Upon registration of a vessel, the vessel owner or vessel lessee shall provide a contact address located in the Kingdom of Cambodia. In case of change of address, the vessel owner or vessel lessee shall notify of this change and shall provide the new address to the competent entity for vessel registration within a maximum period of 15 (fifteen) days from the date of the change.

Article 10 .-

Every sale, transfer, donation, or other legal cause leading to the transfer of vessel ownership must transfer the vessel ownership and change the registration contract of the tourist vessel or domestic inland waterway vessel.

In case of failure to change the vessel registration contract, the vessel owner remains liable under the laws in force.

Article 11 .-

The change of vessel identity must change the registration contract of the tourist vessel or domestic inland waterway vessel.

Article 12 .-

In case a tourist vessel or domestic inland waterway vessel is completely lost, or its ownership is transferred abroad, or it is unusable, the vessel owner or the charterer must request to deregister the vessel.

The vessel owner or the charterer shall be obligated to pay various duties in accordance with the laws and legal instruments in force of the Kingdom of Cambodia, if they fail to fulfill the obligations as stipulated in the first paragraph above.

Article 13 .-

The vessel owner or the charterer must comply with the registration certificate of the tourist vessel or domestic inland waterway vessel. The registration certificate of the vessel shall be dissolved or revoked in case the vessel is used contrary to the purpose for which it was granted in accordance with the vessel registration certificate.

Article 14 .-

Tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels navigating along the waterways in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia are required to undergo vessel technical inspection.

Vessel technical inspection of tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels must obtain a vessel technical inspection certificate.

Article 15 .-

Vessel technical inspection of tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels must be carried out by vessel technical inspection officers who have the professional competence to inspect vessel technical characteristics.

Article 16 .-

Vessel technical inspection of tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels may also be carried out by private agents upon the delegation of vessel technical inspection tasks for tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels, if such agent has the professional competence to inspect vessel technical characteristics.

Article 17 .-

Tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels operating transport businesses of goods or passengers must have a vessel operation permit.

Tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels operating passenger transport businesses must have insurance against damages in accordance with the laws and legal instruments in force.

Article 18 .-

The conditions and procedures for the management of tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 19 .-

Vessel owners or charterers, in accordance with the contract with the vessel owner, who own or possess merchant vessels of any type, may apply for registration of a Cambodian vessel in order to obtain the legal right to navigate internationally.

Article 20 .-

In registering Cambodian vessels, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority shall issue the Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate, the Minimum Safe Manning Certificate, the Technical Certificate, and coordinate the issuance of radio licenses, in accordance with the laws and legal instruments in force.

Article 21 .-

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate has two types:
- Permanent Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate, with a maximum validity of 5 (five) years;
- Temporary Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate, with a maximum validity of 6 (six) months.

Article 22 .-

Merchant vessels to be registered as Cambodian vessels must have a name written in Latin letters and/or Arabic numerals approved by the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

Any change of the name of a Cambodian vessel must be permitted by the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

A Cambodian vessel must not have any name other than the name specified in the Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate.

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority has the right to suspend the use of a vessel name granted temporarily or permanently, after giving 30 (thirty) days written notice to the vessel owner or the charterer.

In case the vessel owner or the charterer fails to provide a new name for the vessel as stipulated in the fourth paragraph above, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority has the right to suspend the registration of the Cambodian vessel.

Article 23 .-

In registering a Cambodian vessel, the vessel owner or the charterer must provide an address for contact and a mandatory legal representative. In case of change of address, the vessel owner or the charterer must notify this change and provide the new address to the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority at the latest within 7 (seven) days after the change.

Article 24 .-

Merchant vessels registered by other countries may be registered as Cambodian vessels in the following cases:
- Having a certificate certifying that the vessel has been deregistered from the registration of that country;
- Having a certificate certifying that the vessel must be deregistered from the registration of that country within a specific determined period.

Article 25 .-

Registration of Cambodian vessels does not require import tax on the vessel.

Article 26 .-

Merchant vessels to be registered as Cambodian vessels or Cambodian vessels must have insurance.

The vessel owner or the charterer has the right to decide and choose the insurance for their merchant vessel to be registered as a Cambodian vessel or Cambodian vessel, in accordance with international principles.

Article 27 .-

The vessel owner who is to register a Cambodian vessel or has registered a Cambodian vessel has the right to register a vessel mortgage. The vessel mortgage determined in this law does not require the transfer of possession of the vessel to the creditor. The vessel mortgage remains effective only when there is registration of the vessel mortgage. The registration of the vessel mortgage must be done at the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

Apart from the provisions related to pledge and right of possession of the creditor under pledge, the provisions of the Civil Code related to pledge rights shall also apply to the vessel mortgage specified in this law.

After fulfilling the conditions for vessel mortgage registration, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority shall issue a vessel mortgage registration certificate.

The formalities and procedures for the registration of vessel mortgages shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 28 .-

The registration of a vessel mortgage must have a clear date.

In case there are two or more registrations of vessel mortgages for one vessel, the payment to the creditors of the amount derived from the auction sale of the vessel must be made in the chronological order of the recorded dates.

Article 29 .-

Every sale, transfer, donation, or other legal cause leading to the transfer of vessel ownership must register the transfer of ownership and change the registration certificate of the Cambodian vessel.

The transfer of vessel ownership may be made if there is consent from the vessel creditor, in accordance with the data recorded in the registration of the Cambodian vessel or the registration of the vessel mortgage, unless otherwise determined by the court in accordance with the laws and procedures in force.

Article 30 .-

The transfer, amendment, or extinguishment of the right of ownership of a vessel must be recorded in the Cambodian Vessel Register, which shall be kept at the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

In case the vessel is owned by two or more persons, the joint ownership right of the vessel must be recorded in the Cambodian Vessel Register.

Article 31 .-

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority shall issue a duplicate Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate in case the Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate is lost, torn, or damaged and no longer usable.

The duplicate Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate has the same value as the Cambodian Vessel Registration Certificate initially issued.

Article 32 .-

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority has the right to refuse to register a Cambodian vessel in any of the following cases:

១- Not having sufficient documents, such as vessel owner documents, vessel documents, etc.

២- Failing to fulfill sufficient technical conditions related to safety, security, and environment, as well as health and welfare conditions of the crew.

៣- Vessels which are prohibited from registration, or where there is evidence certifying that the vessel may be used for any illegal purpose, or may cause damage to the interests or prestige of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Article 33 .-

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority shall terminate the registration of a vessel in any of the following cases:

១- The vessel owner or the charterer refuses to pay fines for offenses committed by their vessel.

២- The vessel owner or the charterer refuses to pay the vessel registration fee or other annual fees for a period exceeding 3 (three) months.

៣- The vessel owner or the charterer requests to terminate the registration of the Cambodian vessel.

៤- Having evidence certifying that the vessel has committed a serious offense.

៥- Having evidence certifying that the registered vessel is completely lost.

The termination of the registration of a Cambodian vessel as stipulated in the first paragraph above requires a deregistration certificate from the Cambodian Vessel Register.

In case of termination of the registration of a Cambodian vessel as stipulated in point 1, point 2, and point 4 of the first paragraph above, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority must notify in writing the vessel owner or the charterer and the creditor, and the vessel owner or the charterer and/or the creditor have the right to appeal to the court in accordance with the laws and procedures in force.

debt, must respond in writing to the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority within a period not exceeding 30 (thirty) working days from the date of receipt of the notification. After this period of 30 (thirty) days, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority may decide to terminate the registration of the Cambodian vessel permanently.

Before the termination of the registration of the Cambodian vessel as stipulated in point 3 and point 5 of the first paragraph above, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority must notify in writing the shareholders and the creditor, and the shareholders and the creditor must respond in writing to the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority within a period not exceeding 30 (thirty) working days from the date of receipt of the notification. After this period of 30 (thirty) days, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority may decide to terminate the registration of the Cambodian vessel permanently.

The deregistration of a Cambodian vessel as determined in point 3 of the first paragraph above must have the written consent of the shareholders and the creditor.

After completing all the conditions for deregistration of a Cambodian vessel, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority shall issue a certificate of deregistration of the Cambodian vessel.

Article 34 .-

When a Cambodian vessel is completely lost, the vessel mortgage is extinguished. In this case, the payment to the creditor must be made in accordance with the conditions stipulated in the debt repayment contract or the vessel mortgage contract or legal instruments in force.

Article 35 .-

In case of forced execution against a Cambodian vessel by a competent court, it shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of the Civil Procedure Code. The suspension or other registrations in this procedure must be sent to the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

The Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority must notify about this forced sale to the vessel shareholders, the privileged creditor, the secured creditor, or the plaintiff claiming compensation arising from the operation of the vessel.

After completing the forced execution procedure against the Cambodian vessel, the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority must register the Cambodian vessel for the new owner in accordance with the conditions stipulated in this law or legal instruments in force.

The priority rights to receive compensation from the forced sale of a Cambodian vessel are determined in the following order:
១- Privileges.
២- Vessel mortgages.
៣- Other claims related to the vessel.

Article 36 .-

Merchant vessels to be registered as Cambodian vessels must undergo technical inspection and receive a safety certificate with a validity of 5 (five) years, which must be verified through annual inspections according to the inspection schedule.

Vessel technical inspection includes inspection of the engine, equipment, and necessary accessories to ensure safety, security, and environment in navigation.

Article 37 .-

Technical inspection of Cambodian vessels must be carried out by technical inspection officers of the Cambodian Vessel Registration Authority.

Article 38 .-

Technical inspection of Cambodian vessels and the issuance of technical certificates may also be carried out by vessel classification societies, upon delegation of the technical inspection of Cambodian vessels by the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

Article 39 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to request any member state of the International Maritime Organization to inspect the technical characteristics of a vessel and issue a technical certificate.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to inspect the technical characteristics of a vessel and issue a technical certificate upon request by any member state of the International Maritime Organization.

Article 40 .-

The formalities and procedures for the management of Cambodian vessels shall be determined by sub-decree.

The procedures for issuing radio licenses shall be determined by inter-ministerial proclamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport and the Minister of Post and Telecommunications.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall organize and promote the establishment of seafarer training institutions.

The establishment of seafarer training institutions must have, among others, specialization, curriculum and internship, degree level, tools, materials, equipment, lecturers or trainers.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall cooperate with the Ministry of Education, Youth and Sports and the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training and relevant ministries and institutions to obtain recognition of seafarer training in accordance with the provisions of this law and legal instruments in force.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to permit the private sector to establish and operate driving schools for vessels, for tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels.

The conditions and procedures for the establishment and operation of driving schools for vessels shall be determined by proclamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

Article 46 .-

Seafarers working as deck officers, marine engineers, vessel operators, assistant deck officers, and assistant marine engineers must have a seafarer competence certificate.

The seafarer competence certificate shall be issued to candidates in accordance with the type, size, engine power, and operational area of the vessel, under the following conditions:
- Being at least 18 (eighteen) years old;
- Having a health certificate certifying physical fitness issued by the Ministry in charge of the health sector;
- Having a certificate certifying study in the country or abroad and a certificate certifying that the seafarer has served on board a vessel;
- Having passed an examination or test certifying that the seafarer has the professional competence to perform duties on board a vessel.

Article 47 .-

Seafarer competence certificates for deck officers are divided into 3 (three) levels as follows:
- Level 1: Deck officers who are to work on board vessels with a length from 35 (thirty-five) meters and above;
- Level 2: Deck officers who are to work on board vessels with a length from 24 (twenty-four) meters to less than 35 (thirty-five) meters;
- Level 3: Deck officers who are to work on board vessels with a length of less than 24 (twenty-four) meters.

Article 48 .-

Seafarer competence certificates for marine engineers are divided into 3 (three) levels as follows:
- Level 1: Marine engineers who are to work on board vessels with engine power from 750 (seven hundred and fifty) horsepower and above;
- Level 2: Marine engineers who are to work on board vessels with engine power from 250 (two hundred and fifty) horsepower to less than 750 (seven hundred and fifty) horsepower;
- Level 3: Marine engineers who are to work on board vessels with engine power of less than 250 (two hundred and fifty) horsepower.

Article 49 .-

Seafarer competence certificates for vessel operators are divided into 3 (three) levels as follows:
- Level 1: Vessel operators on board vessels with a length from 12 (twelve) meters and above;
- Level 2: Vessel operators on board vessels with a length from 7 (seven) meters to less than 12 (twelve) meters;
- Level 3: Vessel operators on board vessels with a length of less than 7 (seven) meters which are of the type of support vessels or other vessels navigating to cross navigation canals, navigation channels, or navigation routes with busy traffic.

The requirement to have a Level 3 seafarer competence certificate for vessel operators is exempted for vessels used for family transport.

Article 50 .-

Seafarer competence certificates for assistant deck officers shall be issued to seafarers performing duties as skilled seafarers working in the deck department and as assistants to deck officers.

Seafarer competence certificates for assistant marine engineers shall be issued to seafarers performing duties as skilled seafarers working in the engine department and as assistants to marine engineers.

Article 51 .-

If there is a bilateral or multilateral international agreement or convention recognizing seafarer competence certificates with the Kingdom of Cambodia, foreigners legally residing in the Kingdom of Cambodia who have a seafarer competence certificate in accordance with the laws of the issuing country may operate vessels.

Article 52 .-

Skilled seafarers must perform duties on board vessels in accordance with the duties and competence levels specified in the seafarer competence certificate.

Seafarers holding a lower-level seafarer competence certificate are not allowed to perform duties that require seafarers holding a higher-level seafarer competence certificate.

Seafarers holding a higher-level seafarer competence certificate may perform duties on board vessels that require seafarers holding a lower-level seafarer competence certificate.

In case of an emergency on board a vessel where the deck officer and marine engineer lose the capacity to command and operate the vessel, the next subordinate seafarer has the right to command and operate the vessel until the end of the vessel's voyage.

Article 53 .-

The procedures for issuing seafarer competence certificates for seafarers working on board tourist vessels and domestic inland waterway vessels shall be determined by proclamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

Article 54 .-

Each Cambodian vessel must have at least 20% (twenty percent) Cambodian seafarers to work on board the vessel, except when there are no Cambodian seafarers who can perform the duties on that vessel.

Cambodian seafarers working on board merchant vessels must have the following positions:
- Management level: Master, Chief Mate, Chief Engineer, Second Engineer
- Operational level: Second Mate, Third Mate, Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch, Radio Officer, Assistant Radio Officer, Third Engineer, Officer in Charge of an Engineering Watch, Electro-Technical Officer
- Support level: Skilled seafarers in navigational department, seafarers forming part of a navigational watch, skilled seafarers in engineering department, seafarers forming part of an engineering watch, skilled seafarers in electro-technical department.

Cambodian seafarers performing the duties stipulated in the second paragraph above must hold a seafarer competence certificate.

Article 55 .-

Cambodian seafarers working on board merchant vessels according to the positions stipulated in Article 54 of this law must have special training to perform duties on board the vessel.

Article 56 .-

The Master has the right to lead, manage, and command on board the vessel.

Seafarers working on board Cambodian vessels must comply with the orders of the Master and must perform the duties assigned by the Master with diligence.

The rights and responsibilities of the Master and seafarers working on board Cambodian vessels shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 57 .-

Cambodian seafarers and foreign seafarers working on board Cambodian vessels must wear seafarer uniforms.

Seafarer uniforms shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 58 .-

Cambodian seafarers to work on board merchant vessels must be at least 18 (eighteen) years old, in good health with a seafarer health certificate, appropriate degree level, documents certifying seafarer service on board the vessel, and professional competence tests.

Cambodian seafarers who are trainees to work on board vessels must be at least 15 (fifteen) years old, provided that the work does not affect the physical growth of the seafarer.

Article 59 .-

Foreigners have the right to work on board Cambodian vessels if they have the prescribed seafarer competence certificate.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall arrange agreements concerning the recognition of the professional competence of seafarers and the issuance of endorsement certificates of professional competence to foreign seafarers who are to serve on Khmer-national vessels.

#### Article 20 .-

The procedures and conditions for issuing certificates of competency, endorsement certificates of competency, and seafarer medical certificates for seafarers working on sea-going vessels shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 21 .-

Cambodian seafarers working on sea-going vessels must hold a seafarer's book. The seafarer's book shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

Cambodian seafarers working on sea-going vessels must also hold a passport.

Foreign seafarers working on Khmer-national vessels must hold a passport and/or equivalent document issued by the competent authority of the seafarer's country.

#### Article 22 .-

Khmer-national vessels must have a minimum safe manning level in accordance with the type, size, engine power, operating area of the vessel, and functions to be performed by seafarers to ensure maritime safety.

The minimum safe manning level shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 23 .-

Cambodian or foreign seafarers working on Khmer-national vessels must have a seafarer's employment agreement.

The seafarer's employment agreement shall specify, among other things, the identity of the parties, the capacity in which the seafarer is to serve, wages and other benefits and the method of payment, health insurance, paid annual leave, and conditions for termination of employment.

The seafarer's employment agreement shall be made in writing and signed by the seafarer and the shipowner or ship operator, in 3 (three) copies, of which one copy shall be kept by the shipowner or ship operator, one copy by the seafarer, and the other copy by the Khmer-national vessel registration unit.

Cambodian seafarers working on foreign vessels must have a seafarer's employment agreement in accordance with the laws of the flag State of the vessel and international instruments.

#### Article 24 .-

In the event of the termination of a seafarer's employment agreement of a seafarer on a Khmer-national vessel due to the expiration of the seafarer's employment agreement:

The shipowner or ship operator shall:

- fully settle all wages to the seafarer;

- repatriate the seafarer to their place of origin free of charge.

The master of the vessel shall:

1- provide a seafarer's work certificate specifying the duration and experience of service on board.

2- The master of the vessel shall provide a character reference for the seafarer's work, certifying the rank, duration, and experience of service on board.

#### Article 25 .-

A seafarer has the right to terminate the seafarer's employment agreement prior to its expiration for any of the following reasons:

1- The vessel is in an unsafe condition, the accommodation on board is not comfortable/hygienic, and the master still neglects to resolve it.

2- The master neglects to check and resolve it, even though up to 50% of the seafarers have protested.

3- The master fails to care for a sick seafarer.

The shipowner or ship operator shall provide the seafarer with wages for a maximum of 3 (three) months, counting from the date of termination of the seafarer's employment agreement, in the event of early termination of the seafarer's employment agreement for any of the reasons specified in paragraph 1 above.

#### Article 26 .-

The shipowner or ship operator has the right to terminate the seafarer's employment agreement prior to its expiration for any of the following reasons committed by the seafarer:

1- Incompetence in performing duties on board.

2- Failure to comply with the internal regulations on board.

3- Other misconduct related to the duties to be performed.

4- Strike or negligence in performing duties on board.

5- Committing any offense or causing an accident.

6- Committing other offenses contrary to the laws in force.

The master has the right to dismiss the seafarer from service on board a Khmer-national vessel before the expiration of the seafarer's employment agreement in the event that the seafarer has committed a serious misconduct for any of the reasons specified in paragraph 1 above.

The shipowner or ship operator shall pay the seafarer's wages up to the date of the early termination of the seafarer's employment agreement.

#### Article 27 .-

When a Khmer-national vessel is deregistered from the Khmer-national vessel registry, the seafarers serving on that vessel shall also have their seafarer's employment agreements terminated.

The shipowner or ship operator shall provide a character reference for the seafarer's work and shall pay the seafarer's wages for at least 2 (two) months, starting from the date of deregistration from the Khmer-national vessel registry.

In the event that a Khmer-national vessel is damaged and is undergoing repairs, the seafarer shall be assigned duties in accordance with their professional skills. If the seafarer does not accept those duties, the shipowner or ship operator shall terminate the seafarer's employment agreement.

The shipowner or ship operator shall provide a character reference for the seafarer's work and shall pay the seafarer's wages up to the date of early termination of the seafarer's employment agreement.

#### Article 28 .-

The master shall repatriate the seafarer whose seafarer's employment agreement has been terminated to their country, in accordance with the seafarer's employment agreement.

All expenses for accommodation and food, or other necessary expenses during the period of waiting for repatriation to their country, and the seafarer's travel to their country as specified in paragraph 1 above, shall be borne by the shipowner or ship operator.

#### Article 29 .-

In the event of the total loss of a Khmer-national vessel, the seafarer serving on that vessel shall have their employment agreement terminated prior to its expiration, as specified in the seafarer's employment agreement.

The shipowner or ship operator shall provide a character reference for the seafarer's work and shall pay the seafarer's wages for a period of 2 (two) months from the date of the loss of the vessel.

#### Article 30 .-

Seafarers have the right to receive payment for various expenses for vessel operations, even if the Khmer-national vessel is totally lost, and other benefits during salvage operations of the vessel.

#### Article 31 .-

The shipowner or ship operator shall be responsible for seafarers serving on Khmer-national vessels while abroad in the following cases:

1- A sick seafarer must receive treatment or surgery that cannot be delayed.

2- In the event of a seafarer's death, the body may be repatriated to their country of origin, or buried, or cremated abroad.

#### Article 32 .-

Seafarers serving on sea-going vessels shall receive seafarer's wages in accordance with the agreement as specified in the seafarer's employment agreement.

Seafarer's wages shall be paid to seafarers regularly on a monthly basis.

Seafarer's wages shall be paid to seafarers even during annual leave and overtime work.

#### Article 33 .-

Overtime work of seafarers when the vessel is navigating shall not exceed 2 (two) hours per day in addition to the daily working hours of the seafarer.

In case of necessity, the overtime work of seafarers shall not exceed 6 (six) hours per day in addition to the daily working hours of the seafarer.

The total working hours of seafarers on board shall not exceed 72 (seventy-two) hours per week.

Overtime work of seafarers on board shall not be applied in cases of force majeure.

#### Article 35 .-

An arbitration commission for seafarers' labor disputes shall be established to coordinate labor disputes of seafarers.

The organization and functioning of the seafarers' labor dispute coordination commission shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

#### Article 36 .-

Labor disputes of seafarers serving on Khmer-national vessels may be resolved by any other dispute resolution mechanism or by a competent court.

#### Article 37 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall promote the establishment of seafarer recruitment agencies in order to seek markets for seafarers, improve vocational training and quality of seafarers' capacity, and protect the rights and interests of seafarers serving on vessels.

The conditions and procedures for establishing seafarer recruitment agencies shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

### Chapter 6
### Navigation
#### Section 1
#### General Provisions of Navigation
##### Sub-section 1
##### General Provisions

#### Article 38 .-

Khmer-national vessels shall comply with the navigation regulations as specified in this Law and legal instruments in force, and shall comply with navigation regulations of the country where the vessel is located.

Coaster vessels, inland waterway vessels, and foreign vessels navigating in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall comply with navigation regulations as specified in this Law and legal instruments in force.

The provisions in this Chapter shall not prevent operations under special rules applied in waters, ports, canals, and closed waterways connected to the sea.

#### Article 39 .-

Navigation regulations include steering rules, steering gear, steering signals, light signals, and sound signals.

#### Article 40 .-

The master or pilot or operator of the vessel shall pay attention to and be cautious of all types of navigation hazards, collision hazards, and all special circumstances, including limitations of the vessel, which may allow the master or pilot or operator of the vessel not to comply with rules in order to avoid immediate danger.

The master or pilot or operator of the vessel shall not be relieved of responsibility for consequences arising from negligence to comply with steering rules or negligence required to be implemented by seafarers' custom or by special circumstances of any case.

##### Sub-section 2
##### Steering Rules

#### Article 41 .-

Seafarers shall maintain a proper look-out at all times by sight and hearing as well as by all available means appropriate to the prevailing circumstances and conditions so as to make a full appraisal of the situation and of the risk of collision.

#### Article 42 .-

Every vessel shall at all times proceed at a safe speed so that she can take proper and effective action to avoid collision and be stopped within a distance appropriate to the prevailing circumstances and conditions.

#### Article 43 .-

Every vessel shall use all available means appropriate to the prevailing circumstances and conditions to determine if risk of collision exists. If there is any doubt, such risk shall be deemed to exist.

Proper use shall be made of radar equipment if fitted and operational, including long-range scanning to obtain early warning of risk of collision and radar plotting or equivalent systematic observation of detected objects.

Assumptions shall not be made on the basis of scanty information, especially scanty radar information.

In determining if risk of collision exists, the following considerations shall be taken into account:

- Such risk shall be deemed to exist if the compass bearing of an approaching vessel does not appreciably change;

- Such risk may sometimes exist even when an appreciable bearing change is evident, particularly when approaching a very large vessel or a tow or when approaching a vessel at close range.

#### Article 44 .-

Any action taken to avoid collision shall be taken in accordance with the Steering Rules and shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, be positive, made in ample time and with due regard to the observance of good seamanship.

Any alteration of course and/or speed to avoid collision shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, be large enough to be readily apparent to another vessel observing visually or by radar; a succession of small alterations of course and/or speed should be avoided.

If there is sufficient sea-room, alteration of course alone may be the most effective action to avoid a close-quarters situation provided that it is made in good time, is substantial and does not result in another close-quarters situation.

Action taken to avoid collision with another vessel shall be such as to result in passing at a safe distance and its effectiveness shall be carefully checked until the other vessel is finally past and clear.

If necessary to avoid collision or allow more time to assess the situation, a vessel shall slacken her speed or take all way off by stopping or reversing her means of propulsion.

In passing situations, a vessel shall comply with the following measures:

- A vessel which, by any of these Rules, is required not to impede the passage or safe passage of another vessel shall, when required by the circumstances of the case, take early action to allow sufficient sea-room for the safe passage of the other vessel.

- A vessel required not to impede the passage or safe passage of another vessel is not relieved of this obligation if she approaches the other vessel so as to involve risk of collision and shall, when taking action, have full regard to the action which may be required by the Steering Rules.

- When two vessels approach each other so as to involve risk of collision, the vessel whose passage is not to be impeded shall remain fully obliged to comply with the Steering Rules.

#### Article 45 .-

Navigation in narrow channels:

1- A vessel proceeding along the course of a narrow channel or fairway shall keep as near to the outer limit of the channel or fairway which lies on her starboard side as is safe and practicable.

2- A vessel of less than 20 (twenty) meters in length or a sailing vessel shall not impede the safe passage of a vessel which can safely navigate only within a narrow channel or fairway.

3- A vessel engaged in fishing shall not impede the passage of any other vessel navigating within a narrow channel or fairway.

4- A vessel shall not cross a narrow channel or fairway if such crossing impedes the safe passage of another vessel which can safely navigate only within such channel or fairway. The latter vessel may use the sound signal prescribed in paragraph 4 of Article 106 of this Law if in doubt as to the intention of the crossing vessel.

5- Overtaking in a narrow channel or fairway:

- In a narrow channel or fairway when overtaking can take place only if the vessel to be overtaken has to take action to permit safe passing, the vessel intending to overtake shall indicate her intention by sounding the appropriate signal prescribed in point (a) of paragraph 3 of Article 106 of this Law. The vessel to be overtaken shall, if in agreement, sound the appropriate signal

as prescribed in point (b) of paragraph 3 of Article 106 of this Law, and take steps to permit the overtaking vessel to pass safely. If in doubt, the vessel to be overtaken may sound the signal prescribed in paragraph 4 of Article 106 of this Law.

(b) This Rule does not relieve the overtaking vessel of her obligation under Article 48 of this Law.

6- A vessel nearing a bend or an area of a narrow channel or fairway where other vessels may be obscured by an intervening obstruction shall navigate with particular alertness and caution and shall sound the appropriate signal prescribed in paragraph 5 of Article 106 of this Law.

7- Any vessel shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, avoid anchoring in a narrow channel. Except in cases permitted by circumstances.

#### Article 46 .-

The provisions of this Article apply to traffic separation schemes adopted by the International Maritime Organization and do not relieve any vessel of her obligation under any other Rules.

A vessel using a traffic separation scheme shall:

- proceed in the appropriate traffic lane in the general direction of traffic flow for that lane;

- so far as practicable keep clear of a traffic separating line or separation zone;

- normally join or leave a traffic lane at the termination of the lane, but when joining or leaving from either side shall do so at as small an angle to the general direction of traffic flow as practicable.

A vessel shall, so far as practicable, avoid crossing traffic lanes but if obliged to do so shall cross on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles to the general direction of traffic flow.

In inshore traffic zones, a vessel shall comply with the following:

- A vessel shall not use an inshore traffic zone when she can safely use the appropriate traffic lane within the adjacent traffic separation scheme. However, vessels of less than 20 (twenty) meters in length, sailing vessels and vessels engaged in fishing may use the inshore traffic zone;

- Notwithstanding the above provision, a vessel may use an inshore traffic zone when en route to or from a port, offshore terminal or structure, pilot station or any other place situated within the inshore traffic zone, or to avoid immediate danger.

A vessel, other than a crossing vessel or a vessel joining or leaving a lane, shall not enter a separation zone or cross a separating line except:

- in cases of emergency to avoid immediate danger;

- to engage in fishing within a separation zone.

A vessel navigating in areas near the terminations of traffic separation schemes shall do so with particular caution.

A vessel shall so far as practicable avoid anchoring in a traffic separation scheme or in areas near its terminations.

A vessel not using a traffic separation scheme shall avoid it by as wide a margin as is practicable.

A vessel engaged in fishing shall not impede the passage of any vessel following a traffic lane.

A vessel of less than 20 (twenty) meters in length or a sailing vessel shall not impede the safe passage of a power-driven vessel following a traffic lane.

A vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver when engaged in an operation for the maintenance of safety of navigation in a traffic separation scheme, laying, servicing or picking up a submarine cable within a traffic separation scheme, is exempted from complying with the provisions of this Rule.

#### Article 47 .-

Except where Articles 45, 86 and 89 of this Law otherwise require, vessels shall comply with the following principles:

1- A power-driven vessel shall keep out of the way of a vessel not under command, a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver, a vessel engaged in fishing, and a sailing vessel.

2- A sailing vessel shall keep out of the way of a vessel not under command, a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver, and a vessel engaged in fishing.

3- A vessel engaged in fishing shall keep out of the way of a vessel not under command and a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver.

4- Except where circumstances otherwise require, any vessel other than a vessel not under command and a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, avoid impeding the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draft, exhibiting the signals of Article 101 of this Law. A vessel constrained by her draft shall navigate with particular caution, having full regard to her special condition.

5- A seaplane on the water shall, in general, keep well clear of all vessels and avoid impeding their navigation. In circumstances, however, where risk of collision exists, she shall comply with the Rules of this Chapter.

6- Wing-in-ground (WIG) craft:

- shall, when taking off, landing and in flight near the surface, keep well clear of all other vessels and avoid impeding their navigation;

- shall, when operating on the water surface, comply with the provisions of this Chapter as a power-driven vessel.

#### Section 2

When two sailing vessels are approaching one another, so as to involve risk of collision, one of them shall keep out of the way of the other as follows:

1- When each has the wind on a different side, the vessel which has the wind on the port side shall keep out of the way of the other;

2- When both have the wind on the same side, the vessel which is to windward shall keep out of the way of the vessel which is to leeward;

3- If a vessel with the wind on the port side sees a vessel to windward and cannot determine with certainty whether the other vessel has the wind on the port or on the starboard side, she shall keep out of the way of the other.

For the purposes of this Article, the windward side shall be deemed to be the side opposite to that on which the mainsail is carried or, in the case of a square-rigged vessel, the side opposite to that on which the largest fore-and-aft sail is carried.

#### Article 48 .-

Any vessel overtaking any other shall keep out of the way of the vessel being overtaken.

A vessel shall be deemed to be overtaking when coming up with another vessel from a direction more than 22.5 degrees abaft her beam. At night, the overtaking vessel would be able to see only the sternlight of the vessel being overtaken but none of her sidelights.

When a vessel is in any doubt as to whether she is overtaking another, she shall assume that this is the case and act accordingly.

Any alteration of the bearing between the two vessels shall not make the overtaking vessel a crossing vessel within the meaning of these Rules or relieve her of the duty of keeping clear of the overtaken vessel until she is finally past and clear.

#### Article 49 .-

When two power-driven vessels are meeting on reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses so as to involve risk of collision, each shall alter her course to starboard so that each shall pass on the port side of the other.

Such a situation shall be deemed to exist when a vessel sees the other ahead or nearly ahead and by night she would see the masthead lights of the other in a line or nearly in a line and/or both sidelights.

When a vessel is in any doubt as to whether such a situation exists she shall assume that it does exist and act accordingly.

#### Article 50 .-

When two power-driven vessels are crossing so as to involve risk of collision, the vessel which has the other on her own starboard side shall keep out of the way and shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, avoid crossing ahead of the other.

#### Article 51 .-

Every vessel which is directed to keep out of the way of another vessel shall, so far as possible, take early and substantial action to keep well clear.

#### Article 93 .-

If one of two vessels is keeping out of the way, the other vessel shall maintain her course and speed, and shall take necessary measures to avoid collision if the giving-way vessel does not take measures in compliance with the provisions of this Law.

When two vessels are avoiding each other, the vessel that is being avoided shall maintain her course and speed. In the event that the vessel that is being avoided finds that the vessel required to keep out of the way is not taking appropriate action in compliance with the provisions of this Law, the vessel that is being avoided shall take action to avoid collision by maneuvering as early as possible.

When, from any cause, the vessel required to maintain her course and speed finds herself so close that collision cannot be avoided by the action of the giving-way vessel alone, she shall take action to avoid collision.

A power-driven vessel which takes action in a situation as in paragraph 2 of this Article to avoid collision with another power-driven vessel shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, not alter course to port for a vessel on her port side.

This provision does not relieve the giving-way vessel of her obligation to keep out of the way of the vessel that is being avoided.

#### Article 94 .-

The provisions of this Article apply to vessels not in sight of one another when navigating in or near an area of restricted visibility.

Every vessel shall proceed at a safe speed adapted to the prevailing circumstances and conditions of restricted visibility, and a power-driven vessel shall have her engines ready for immediate maneuver.

When complying with the provisions of Sub-section 2, Section 1 of this Chapter, every vessel shall have due regard to the prevailing circumstances and conditions of restricted visibility.

A vessel which detects the presence of another vessel by radar alone shall determine if a close-quarters situation is developing and/or risk of collision exists. If so, she shall take avoiding action in ample time, provided that when such action consists of an alteration of course, so far as possible the following shall be avoided:

- An alteration of course to port, if the other vessel is forward of the beam and is not a vessel being avoided;

- An alteration of course towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam.

Except where it has been determined that a risk of collision does not exist, every vessel which hears the fog signal of another vessel apparently forward of her beam, or which cannot avoid a close-quarters situation with another vessel forward of her beam, shall reduce her speed to the minimum at which she can be kept on her course. She shall if necessary take all her way off and in any event navigate with extreme caution until danger of collision is over.

### Sub-section 3
### Navigation Lights and Shapes

#### Article 95 .-

The provisions concerning navigation lights and shapes shall be complied with in all weathers.

The navigation lights prescribed in these Rules shall be exhibited from sunset to sunrise, and during such times no other lights shall be exhibited, except such lights as cannot be mistaken for the lights specified in these Rules or do not impair their visibility or distinctive character, or interfere with the keeping of a proper look-out.

The navigation lights prescribed in these Rules shall also, if carried, be exhibited from sunrise to sunset in restricted visibility and may be exhibited in all other circumstances when it is deemed necessary.

The navigation shapes prescribed in these Rules shall be complied with by day.

#### Article 96 .-

A power-driven vessel underway shall exhibit:

- a masthead light forward;

- a second masthead light abaft of and higher than the forward one; except that a vessel of less than 50 (fifty) meters in length shall not be obliged to exhibit such light;

- sidelights;

- a sternlight.

An air-cushion vessel when operating in the non-displacement mode shall, in addition to the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 above, exhibit an all-round flashing yellow light.

A WIG craft only when taking off, landing and in flight near the surface shall, in addition to the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 of this Article, exhibit a high intensity all-round flashing red light.

A power-driven vessel of less than 12 (twelve) meters in length may in lieu of the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 exhibit an all-round white light and sidelights.

A power-driven vessel of less than 7 (seven) meters in length whose maximum speed does not exceed 7 (seven) knots may in lieu of the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 exhibit an all-round white light, and shall, if practicable, also exhibit sidelights.

A power-driven vessel of less than 12 (twelve) meters in length, whose beam is insufficient for the sidelights to be exhibited separately, may exhibit them in one combined lantern carried on the fore and aft centerline or stern of the vessel.

#### Article 97 .-

A power-driven vessel when towing shall exhibit:

- instead of the forward masthead light or the second masthead light, two masthead lights in a vertical line. When the length of the tow, measuring from the stern of the towing vessel to the after end of the tow exceeds 200 (two hundred) meters, three such lights in a vertical line;

- sidelights;

- a sternlight;

- a towing light in a vertical line above the sternlight;

- when the length of the tow exceeds 200 (two hundred) meters, a diamond shape where it can best be seen.

When a pushing vessel and a vessel being pushed ahead are rigidly connected in a composite unit they shall be regarded as a power-driven vessel and shall exhibit the lights prescribed in Article 96 above.

A power-driven vessel when pushing ahead or towing alongside, other than in a composite unit, shall exhibit:

- instead of the forward masthead light or the second masthead light, two masthead lights in a vertical line;

- sidelights;

- a sternlight.

The second bullet of paragraph 1 of Article 96 of this Law shall also apply to power-driven vessels as prescribed in paragraph 1 and paragraph 3 of this Article.

A vessel or object being towed, other than those mentioned in paragraph 7 of this Article, shall exhibit:

- sidelights;

- a sternlight;

- a diamond shape where it can best be seen, if the length of the tow exceeds 200 (two hundred) meters.

In the case where vessels are being towed alongside or pushed in a group, they shall exhibit lights as a single vessel:

- a vessel being pushed ahead, not being part of a composite unit, shall exhibit sidelights at the forward end;

- a vessel being towed alongside shall exhibit a sternlight and sidelights at the forward end.

An inconspicuous, partly submerged vessel or object, or combination of such vessels or objects being towed, shall exhibit:

- if it is less than 25 (twenty-five) meters in width, one all-round white light at or near the forward end and one at or near the after end, except that dracones need not exhibit a light at or near the forward end;

- if it is 25 (twenty-five) meters or more in width, two additional all-round white lights at or near the lateral extremities;

- if it exceeds 100 (one hundred) meters in length, additional all-round white lights between the lights prescribed in the first and second bullets above, in such a manner that the distance between the lights shall not exceed 100 (one hundred) meters;

- a diamond shape at or near the aftermost part of the vessel or object being towed last, and if the length of the tow exceeds 200 (two hundred) meters, an additional diamond shape where it can best be seen.

Where from any sufficient cause it is impracticable for a vessel or object being towed to exhibit the lights or shapes prescribed in paragraph 5 and paragraph 7 of this Article, all possible measures shall be taken to light the vessel or object towed or at least to indicate the presence of such vessel or object.

Where from any sufficient cause it is impracticable for a vessel not normally engaged in towing operations to display the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 and paragraph 3 of this Article, such vessel shall not be required to exhibit those lights when engaged in towing another vessel in distress or otherwise in need of assistance. All possible measures shall be taken to indicate the character of the connection between the towing vessel and the vessel being towed as authorized by Article 108 of this Law, in particular by illuminating the towline.

#### Article 98 .-

A sailing vessel underway shall exhibit:

- sidelights;

- a sternlight.

In a sailing vessel of less than 20 (twenty) meters in length the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 of this Article may be combined in one lantern carried at or near the top of the mast.

A sailing vessel underway may, in addition to the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 of this Article, exhibit at or near the top of the mast, where they can best be seen, two all-round lights in a vertical line, the upper being red and the lower green, but these lights shall not be exhibited in conjunction with the combined lantern permitted by paragraph 2 of this Article.

A sailing vessel of less than 7 (seven) meters in length shall, if practicable, exhibit the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of this Article, but if she does not, she shall have ready at hand an electric torch or lighted lantern showing a white light which shall be exhibited in sufficient time to prevent collision. A rowing boat or paddle boat may, if practicable, comply with the provisions prescribed in paragraph 1 of this Article, but if she does not, she shall comply with the provisions prescribed in paragraph 4 of this Article.

A vessel proceeding under sail when also being propelled by machinery shall exhibit forward where it can best be seen a conical shape, apex downwards.

#### Article 99 .-

A vessel engaged in fishing, whether underway or at anchor, shall exhibit only the lights and shapes prescribed in this Article.

A vessel when engaged in trawling shall exhibit:

- two all-round lights in a vertical line, the upper being green and the lower white, or a shape consisting of two cones with their apexes together in a vertical line one above the other;

- a masthead light abaft of and higher than the all-round green light; a vessel of less than 50 (fifty) meters in length shall not be obliged to exhibit such a light;

- when making way through the water, in addition to the lights prescribed in this paragraph, sidelights and a sternlight.

A vessel engaged in fishing, other than trawling, shall exhibit:

- two all-round lights in a vertical line, the upper being red and the lower white, or a shape consisting of two cones with apexes together in a vertical line one above the other;

- when there is outlying gear extending more than 150 (one hundred and fifty) meters horizontally from the vessel, an all-round white light or a cone apex upwards in the direction of the gear;

- when making way through the water, in addition to the lights prescribed in this paragraph, sidelights and a sternlight.

A vessel engaged in fishing in close proximity to other vessels engaged in fishing may exhibit the additional signals prescribed below:

- a vessel of 20 (twenty) meters or more in length when shooting her nets, two white lights in a vertical line; when hauling her nets, a white light over a red light in a vertical line; when the net has come fast upon an obstruction, two red lights in a vertical line;

- a vessel of 20 (twenty) meters or more in length engaged in pair trawling, by night, shall direct her searchlight forward and in the direction of the other vessel of the pair; when shooting or hauling their nets or when their nets have come fast upon an obstruction, they may exhibit the lights prescribed in the first bullet of this paragraph;

- a vessel of less than 20 (twenty) meters in length engaged in pair trawling may exhibit the lights prescribed in the first and second bullets above;

- a vessel engaged in fishing with purse seine gear may exhibit two yellow lights in a vertical line. These lights shall flash alternately every second and with equal periods of light and darkness, and these lights may be exhibited only when the vessel is hampered by its purse seine gear.

A vessel engaged in fishing which is not engaged in fishing shall not exhibit the lights or shapes prescribed in this Article, but only those prescribed for a vessel of her length.

#### Article 100 .-

A vessel not under command shall exhibit:

- two all-round red lights in a vertical line where they can best be seen;

- two balls or similar shapes in a vertical line where they can best be seen;

- when making way through the water, in addition to the lights prescribed in this paragraph, sidelights and a sternlight.

Except for a vessel engaged in mineclearance operations, a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver shall exhibit:

- three all-round lights in a vertical line where they can best be seen. The highest and lowest of these lights shall be red and the middle light shall be white;

- a shape consisting of three shapes in a vertical line where they can best be seen, the highest and lowest being balls and the middle a diamond;

- when making way through the water, masthead light(s), sidelights and a sternlight;

- when at anchor, in addition to the lights or shapes prescribed in the first and second bullets, the light, lights or shape prescribed in Article 103 of this Law.

A power-driven vessel engaged in a towing operation such as severely restricts the towing vessel and her tow in their ability to deviate from their course shall, in addition to the lights or shapes prescribed in paragraph 1 of Article 97 of this Law, exhibit the lights or shapes prescribed in the first and second bullets of paragraph 2 of this Article.

A vessel engaged in dredging or underwater operations, when restricted in her ability to maneuver, shall exhibit the lights and shapes prescribed in the first, second and third bullets of paragraph 2 above, and shall in addition, when an obstruction exists, exhibit:

- two all-round red lights or two balls in a vertical line to indicate the side on which the obstruction exists;

- two all-round green lights or two diamonds in a vertical line to indicate the side on which another vessel may pass;

- when at anchor, the lights or shapes prescribed in this paragraph shall replace the lights or shape prescribed in Article 103 of this Law.

Whenever the size of a vessel engaged in diving operations makes it impracticable to exhibit all lights and shapes prescribed in paragraph 4 above, the following shall be exhibited:

- three all-round lights in a vertical line where they can best be seen, the highest and lowest of these lights shall be red and the middle light shall be white;

- a rigid replica of the International Code flag "A" not less than 1 (one) meter in height from the deck of the vessel, and measures shall be taken to ensure its all-round visibility.

A vessel engaged in mineclearance operations shall, in addition to the lights prescribed for a power-driven vessel in Article 96 of this Law or to the lights or shape prescribed for a vessel at anchor in Article 103 of this Law, exhibit three all-round green lights or three balls. One of these lights or shapes shall be exhibited near the foremast head and one at each end of the fore yard. These lights or shapes indicate that it is dangerous for other vessels to approach within 1000 (one thousand) meters of the mineclearance vessel.

Except when engaged in diving operations, vessels of less than 12 (twelve) meters in length shall not be required to exhibit the lights and shapes prescribed in this Article.

#### Article 101 .-

A vessel constrained by her draft may, in addition to the lights prescribed for power-driven vessels in Article 96 of this Law, exhibit where they can best be seen three all-round red lights in a vertical line, or a cylinder.

#### Article 102 .-

A vessel engaged on pilotage duty shall exhibit:

- at or near the masthead, two all-round lights in a vertical line, the upper being white and the lower red;

- when underway, in addition, sidelights and a sternlight;

- when at anchor, in addition to the light prescribed in the first bullet above, the light, lights or shape prescribed in Article 103 of this Law.

A pilot vessel when not engaged on pilotage duty shall exhibit the lights or shapes prescribed for a similar vessel of her length.

#### Article 103 .-

A vessel at anchor shall exhibit where they can best be seen:

- in the fore part, an all-round white light;

- at or near the stern and at a lower level than the light prescribed in the first bullet, an all-round white light.

A vessel of less than 50 (fifty) meters in length may exhibit an all-round white light where it can best be seen, in lieu of the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 above.

A vessel at anchor of 100 (one hundred) meters and more in length shall also use the available working or equivalent lights to illuminate her decks.

A vessel aground shall exhibit the lights prescribed in paragraph 1 or paragraph 2 of this Article and in addition, where they can best be seen:

- two all-round red lights in a vertical line;

- three balls in a vertical line.

A vessel of less than 7 (seven) meters in length, when at anchor, not in or near a narrow channel, fairway or anchorage, or where other vessels normally navigate, shall not be required to exhibit the lights or shape prescribed in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of this Article.

A vessel of less than 12 (twelve) meters in length, when aground, shall not be required to exhibit the lights or shapes prescribed in the first and second bullets of paragraph 4 of this Article.

#### Article 104 .-

Where it is impracticable for a seaplane or a WIG craft to exhibit lights and shapes of the technical characteristics or positions prescribed in this Section, she shall exhibit lights and shapes as closely similar in characteristics and position as is possible.

### Sub-section 4
### Sound and Light Signals

#### Article 105 .-

A vessel of 12 (twelve) meters or more in length shall be provided with a whistle, a vessel of 20 (twenty) meters or more in length shall be provided with a bell in addition to a whistle, and a vessel of 100 (one hundred) meters or more in length shall, in addition, be provided with a gong, the tone and sound of which cannot be confused with that of the bell. The bell or gong or both may be replaced by other equipment having the same respective sound characteristics, and capable of giving the required signals.

A vessel of less than 12 (twelve) meters in length shall not be obliged to carry the sound signaling appliances prescribed in paragraph 1 above. However, if she does not, she shall be provided with some other means of producing an efficient sound signal.

#### Article 106 .-

When vessels are in sight of one another, a power-driven vessel underway, when maneuvering as authorized or required by these Rules, shall indicate that maneuver by the following signals on her whistle:

- one short blast to mean "I am altering my course to starboard";

- two short blasts to mean "I am altering my course to port";

- three short blasts to mean "I am operating astern propulsion".

Any vessel may supplement the whistle signals prescribed in paragraph 1 above by light signals:

A. These light signals shall have the following significance:

- one flash to mean "I am altering my course to starboard";

- two flashes to mean "I am altering my course to port";

- three flashes to mean "I am operating astern propulsion".

B. The duration of each flash shall be about 1 (one) second, the interval between flashes shall be about 1 (one) second, and the interval between successive signals shall be not less than 10 (ten) seconds.

C. The light used for this signal shall, if fitted, be an all-round white light, visible at a minimum range of 5 (five) miles.

When vessels are in sight of one another in a narrow channel or fairway:

A. A vessel intending to overtake another shall, in compliance with Point A of paragraph 5 of Article 88 of this Law, indicate her intention by the following signals on her whistle:

- two prolonged blasts followed by one short blast to mean "I intend to overtake you on your starboard side";

- two prolonged blasts followed by two short blasts to mean "I intend to overtake you on your port side".

The vessel about to be overtaken when acting in agreement shall, in compliance with Point A of paragraph 5 of Article 88 of this Law, indicate her agreement by the following signal on her whistle:

- one prolonged, one short, one prolonged and one short blast, in that order.

When vessels in sight of one another are approaching, and from any cause either vessel fails to understand the intentions or actions of the other, or is in doubt whether sufficient action is being taken by the other

the vessel in doubt shall immediately indicate such doubt by giving at least five short and rapid blasts on the whistle. Such whistle signal may be supplemented by a light signal of at least five short and rapid flashes.

A vessel nearing a bend or an area of a channel or fairway where other vessels may be obscured by an intervening obstruction shall sound one prolonged blast. Any approaching vessel that may be within hearing around the bend or behind the intervening obstruction shall answer such signal with a similar prolonged blast.

If whistles are fitted on a vessel at a distance apart of more than 100 (one hundred) meters, only one whistle shall be used for giving maneuvering and warning signals.

#### Article 107 .-

In or near an area of restricted visibility, whether by day or night, the signals prescribed in this Article shall be used as follows:

1- A power-driven vessel making way through the water shall sound at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes one prolonged blast.

2- A power-driven vessel underway but stopped and making no way through the water shall sound at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes two prolonged blasts in succession with an interval of about 2 seconds between them.

3- A vessel not under command, a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver, a vessel constrained by her draft, a sailing vessel, a vessel engaged in fishing and a vessel engaged in towing or pushing another vessel shall, instead of the signals prescribed in point 1 and point 2 above, sound at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes three blasts in succession, namely one prolonged followed by two short blasts.

4- A vessel engaged in fishing, when at anchor, and a vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver when carrying out her work at anchor, shall sound the signal prescribed in point 3 above instead of the signal prescribed in point 7 of this Article.

5- A vessel being towed or if more than one vessel is towed, the last vessel of the tow, if manned, shall at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes sound four blasts in succession, namely one prolonged followed by three short blasts. When practicable, this signal shall be made immediately after the signal made by the towing vessel.

6- A pushing vessel and a vessel being pushed ahead rigidly connected in a composite unit shall be regarded as a power-driven vessel and shall sound the signals prescribed in point 1 and point 2 of this Article.

7- A vessel at anchor shall ring the bell rapidly for about 5 (five) seconds at intervals of not more than 1 (one) minute. In a vessel of 100 (one hundred) meters or more in length the bell shall be rung in the forepart of the vessel and immediately after the ringing of the bell the gong shall be sounded rapidly for about 5 (five) seconds in the afterpart of the vessel. A vessel at anchor may in addition sound three blasts in succession, namely one prolonged, one short and one prolonged blast, to give warning of her position and of the possibility of collision to an approaching vessel.

8- A raft vessel shall ring a bell and, if necessary, beat a gong as prescribed in point 7 above, and shall immediately ring the bell with a loud and distinct sound 3 (three) times before and after ringing the bell. A raft vessel may sound additional signals using a whistle.

9- A vessel with a length of 12 (twelve) meters to under 20 (twenty) meters is not required to ring a bell as prescribed in point 7 and point 8 above. In case a vessel does not have a bell, the vessel must use other effective methods of sound signaling, which can be made consecutively at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes.

10- A vessel with a length of less than 12 (twelve) meters is not required to emit the sound signals as prescribed above. In case it is not possible to emit such sound signals, the vessel must emit sound signals by using other effective methods, which can be made consecutively at intervals of not more than 2 (two) minutes.

11- A pilot vessel engaged on pilotage duty may sound 4 additional sound signals to identify its identity, in addition to the sound signals prescribed in point 1, point 2, or point 7 of this Article.

#### Article 108 .-

If it is necessary to use warning signals to alert other vessels, a vessel may use light signals or sound signals that are not confused with the signals specified in navigation regulations, or may shine a searchlight in the direction of danger without interfering with the navigation of any vessel.

The warning light to alert other vessels must not be confused with navigation aid signals. For the purposes of this Article, the use of high-intensity searchlights or lights that dazzle the eyes must be avoided.

#### Article 109 .-

When in distress and in need of assistance, a vessel must use distress signals.

#### Article 110 .-

The technical specifications of signal lights, waterway signals, sound signals, distress signals, and the types and use of international signals shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

### Part 5
### Special Rules

#### Article 111 .-

Vessels navigating on waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall not be operated under the following circumstances:

- A passenger vessel or a vessel carrying dangerous goods shall not tow other vessels, and other vessels shall also not tow a passenger vessel or a vessel carrying dangerous goods, except in cases of rescue or force majeure.

- A vessel shall not overtake at places where no-overtaking signs are posted, when passing under bridges, and at unsafe locations, such as narrow channels while other vessels are traveling in the opposite direction under limited visibility conditions, blind curves, or conditions of strong drifting.

- A vessel shall not navigate in the middle of a vessel crossing path, at blind spots, curves, under bridges, and at locations where signs prohibiting vessel navigation are posted.

#### Article 112 .-

Before passing under a bridge, the ship master, ship manager, or vessel operator must comply with the following rules:

- Clearly inspect the conditions under the bridge to be passed, including the clearance height under the bridge, the width between bridge piers, water currents, as well as crossing conditions.

- Check the steering system, gear system, and collision protection system.

- Navigate through according to the under-bridge crossing signs and within the limits designated by aids to navigation.

- Ensure safety when navigating under the bridge under swirling water current conditions; if navigating through is unsafe, the vessel must be stopped.

#### Article 113 .-

Except for vessels restricted in their ability to maneuver and vessels constrained by their draft, all types of vessels shall avoid causing interference to the safe passage of vessels on special missions and vessels on public safety activities.

A vessel on a special mission, while performing its duties, must turn on a blue flashing light with a flash rate between 50 (fifty) and 70 (seventy) times per minute.

A vessel on public safety activities, while performing its duties, must turn on a red flashing light with a flash rate between 50 (fifty) and 70 (seventy) times per minute.

Flashing lights installed on vessels on special missions and vessels on public safety activities must be placed at the highest position to be visible 360 (three hundred and sixty) degrees.

In addition to the flashing lights, vessels on special missions and vessels on public safety activities may use special signals, signal flags, and sirens with technical characteristics different from the sound and light signals prescribed in navigation regulations.

#### Article 114 .-

In addition to the rules prescribed in Section 2, Section 3, and Section 4 of this Part 1, vessels navigating on waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia may use other means, including international signal flags and VHF radio communication between vessels, and between vessels and coast radio stations or port agents, to avoid collision hazards.

## Section 2
## Safety, Security and Environment in Navigation

### Part 1
### Safety, Security and Environment

#### Article 115 .-

For safety, security, and public order, commercial vessels must ensure safety, including the minimum safe manning level of the vessel, and exercise caution to ensure that the vessel navigates safely without causing environmental impacts.

This regulation shall apply to tourism vessels, sea-going vessels, domestic waterway vessels, and Cambodian flag vessels, wherever they may be.

#### Article 116 .-

The ship owner, bareboat charterer (vessel operator by contract), shipmaster, or ship manager must maintain the engines, equipment, and apparatus of the vessel recorded in the vessel registration certificate, the vessel technical inspection certificate, and the vessel safety certificate after the vessel has undergone technical inspection in accordance with the specified schedule.

#### Article 117 .-

Cambodian flag vessels must have a vessel security assessment report, a vessel security plan, a vessel security certificate, a vessel security officer, and crew members in charge of vessel security.

Vessel security shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

### Part 2
### Coordination Mechanism for Safety, Security and Environment

#### Article 118 .-

Mechanisms and centers shall be established to ensure cooperation and coordination in preparedness and response to oil spills and hazardous substances, search and rescue in emergencies as follows:

1- National Committee for Oil Spills and Hazardous Substances Preparedness and Response.

2- National Committee for Search and Rescue in Emergencies.

3- Search and Rescue Center in Emergencies.

The organization and functioning of each National Committee and center as prescribed in paragraph 1 above shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

## Section 3
## Navigation Accidents

### Part 1
### Navigation Accidents

#### Article 119 .-

The shipmaster or ship manager must make every effort to provide assistance and rescue to any person who is in danger due to a navigation accident, provided that their own vessel, crew, and passengers remain in a safe condition.

#### Article 120 .-

The shipmaster or ship manager must immediately report navigation accident occurrences as soon as possible to the nearest competent authority in the event that passengers or any person are injured or lose their lives.

#### Article 121 .-

The shipmaster or ship manager must make every effort to avoid danger to their own vessel, crew, and passengers, and must provide assistance and rescue to other vessels, crew, and passengers after a collision.

A shipmaster or ship manager who is involved in a vessel collision that causes a navigation accident, or causes injury or loss of human life on board, must immediately report this accident, including the identity of their own vessel, other vessels involved in the collision, and the port of destination where their vessel is to dock, to the nearest competent authority.

#### Article 122 .-

The shipmaster or ship manager must immediately report to other vessels in the vicinity, or to the nearest competent authority as soon as possible, in the event of security threats on board, such as the commission of an offense or an attempt to commit any offense.

#### Article 123 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to conduct investigations or participate in investigations of navigation accidents that occur within and outside the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The investigation of navigation accidents must gather information, question the crew and other persons regarding the occurrence that caused the navigation accident.

Reports or information regarding the results of navigation accident investigations shall be kept as documentation for technical improvement.

### Part 2
### Compensation for Vessel Collisions

#### Article 124 .-

Compensation for damages caused by a vessel collision includes compensation for damages to the vessel, persons, cargo on board, and third parties.

If a vessel collision leads to a dangerous situation caused by force majeure, or if the cause of the accident is in doubt so that it is impossible to determine the liable party, each vessel shall bear its own damages.

#### Article 125 .-

In the event that the collision is caused by one vessel, that vessel shall be liable to compensate for the damages resulting from the collision.

In the event that the collision is caused by multiple vessels, each vessel shall be liable to compensate for the damages resulting from the collision in proportion to the fault committed by that vessel.

In the event that the collision is caused by multiple vessels with equal fault causing the collision, or if it is not possible to determine the degree of fault caused by the collision, each vessel shall be deemed to have committed equal fault and shall be jointly and severally liable to compensate for the damages of the collision equally.

In the event of a vessel collision that causes injury or loss of life on board a vessel involved in the collision, all vessels at fault in the collision shall be jointly and severally liable to compensate for those damages.

#### Article 126 .-

Lawsuits seeking compensation for damages caused by a vessel collision must be filed in a competent court in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure.

#### Article 127 .-

The provisions of Article 126 of this Law do not affect the rights of the parties concerned to file claims for compensation for damages caused by a vessel collision in an arbitration court or any other dispute resolution mechanism, both inside or outside the country, which those parties have agreed to choose. This agreement does not restrict the rights of third parties to file lawsuits in competent courts of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 128 .-

Recourse actions arising from a vessel collision must be brought before the court that has jurisdiction over the initial lawsuit, in accordance with the provisions of Article 126 of this Law.

In cases involving multiple plaintiffs, each plaintiff must file claims for compensation for damages caused by a vessel collision before the court that has assumed jurisdiction over the compensation claims filed by the party causing the collision. In the event that another court has received such a claim, that court must transfer the case file to the court currently handling the compensation claims from the party causing the collision.

#### Article 129 .-

Lawsuits seeking compensation for damages caused by a vessel collision are subject to a statute of limitations of 3 (three) years, calculated from the date the collision accident occurred.

#### Article 130 .-

In the event of a vessel collision, the vessel causing the damages may be released from liability for compensation for damages caused by the collision after obtaining a letter of release in accordance with international principles.

### Part 3
### Provision of Salvage Assistance to Vessels

#### Article 131 .-

Any salvage operation of a vessel in danger and in need of assistance that achieves a useful result shall entitle the salvor to receive a reward.

In all cases of salvage, the total reward must not exceed the value of the saved property.

#### Article 132 .-

Saving human lives in danger and salvaging other vessels does not entitle the salvor to receive a reward for such salvage, except where the service provided is an additional service other than the vessel salvage contract.

Salvage rewards for saving a vessel shall also be paid to the salvor even if the vessel saved belongs to the same shipowner.

#### Article 133 .-

The amount of salvage reward shall be determined by agreement between the salvor and the recipient of the salvage. In the event that no agreement can be reached, a lawsuit must be filed in a competent court.

The apportionment of the salvage reward among salvors shall be agreed upon among all salvors, and in the event of failure to reach an agreement, a lawsuit must also be filed in a competent court.

#### Article 134 .-

The amount of salvage reward shall be determined based on the following criteria:

- The degree of success achieved

- The efforts of the salvor

- The state of danger of the vessel to be saved, passengers, crew, cargo on board, salvor, and the salvage vessel

- Time, expenses incurred, and losses sustained

- The risk run by the salvors and the value of property exposed to such risks during the salvage operation

- The value of the property saved.

#### Article 135 .-

Salvage rewards for saving human lives cannot be priced or determined and must be carried out in the name of humanity.

Salvors of human life are entitled to a fair share of the salvage reward awarded to salvors of the vessel, cargo, and equipment on board that vessel.

#### Article 136 .-

Lawsuits seeking salvage rewards for saving a vessel are subject to a statute of limitations of 3 (three) years, calculated from the time the salvage operations are completed.

# Chapter 7
# Stowaways

#### Article 137 .-

Whenever a stowaway is found at sea or in any port, the shipmaster must hand over the stowaway to the competent authority at the first port of call where the stowaway is discovered.

Upon handing over the stowaway to the competent authority, the shipmaster must provide a signed certificate stating the details concerning the stowaway, including nationality, port of embarkation, time and geographical position of the vessel where the stowaway was able to board, and the ports of call of the vessel in sequence along with dates.

Except where a stowaway is subject to deportation or is prohibited from entering the country, the competent authority must receive the stowaway handed over in accordance with this Article and the provisions of this Law.

#### Article 138 .-

The competent authority that has received the stowaway at the port of arrival of the vessel shall act as follows:

1- Return the stowaway to the state of nationality of the stowaway or to a state which agrees to accept the stowaway.

2- Whenever the competent authority finds that the state of nationality of the stowaway or the state which agrees to accept the stowaway refuses to receive them, or if the competent authority finds that the stowaway has no nationality, and in accordance with the provisions of paragraph 2 of Article 140 of this Law, the stowaway cannot be sent back to their own country of origin, the competent authority must hand over the stowaway to the state where the port at which the stowaway boarded the vessel is located.

3- If the stowaway cannot be returned in accordance with points 1 and 2 above, the competent authority, in accordance with paragraph 2 of Article 140 of this Law, must send the stowaway to the state where the last port of call of the vessel before the stowaway was discovered is located.

4- If, in the end, the stowaway still cannot be returned in accordance with points 1, 2, and 3 above, the competent authority must send the stowaway to the flag state of the vessel on board of which the stowaway was found. In this case, the flag state of the vessel has the obligation to accept the stowaway.

#### Article 139 .-

The costs of maintenance of the stowaway at the port of arrival and the costs of returning the stowaway to their state of nationality shall be borne by the shipowner, and the shipowner has the right to claim compensation from the state of nationality of the stowaway.

The shipowner's liability to pay for the maintenance of the stowaway shall be limited to a period of 30 (thirty) days from the date the stowaway was handed over to the competent authority.

#### Article 140 .-

The powers vested in the shipmaster or the competent authority under this Law regarding the handling of stowaways are additional powers and shall not affect any other powers or obligations of the shipmaster or the competent authority with respect to handing over the stowaway.

In implementing the provisions of this Law, the shipmaster or the competent authority of the port receiving the stowaway must take into account the reasons why the stowaway cannot leave the vessel or cannot be sent back to the port of the state from which the stowaway was sent.

# Chapter 8
# Arrest of Sea-Going Vessels

#### Article 141 .-

Sea-going vessels, whether flying the Cambodian flag or a foreign flag, may be arrested in ports, in internal waters, or in the territorial sea after leaving internal waters, within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia, as the subject of a maritime claim.

#### Article 142 .-

Maritime claims include, inter alia:

- Damage caused by the vessel

- Loss of life or personal injury caused by the vessel or occurring in direct connection with the operation of the vessel

- Salvage operations of the vessel, crew, passengers, and cargo on board

- Agreements relating to the use of the waterway

- Agreements relating to the carriage of goods

- Loss of or damage to goods, including passenger baggage

- General average contributions

- Bottomry or loans contracted by the shipowner

- Vessel insurance/guarantee

- Towage or push operations

- Pilotage, port dues, and canal tolls

- Goods or materials supplied to the vessel for its operation or maintenance

- Construction, repair, equipment, or dock/shipyard charges of the vessel

- Wages of crew members, including the shipmaster or crew

- Disbursements made on behalf of the vessel by the shipmaster, shippers, charterers, or agents on behalf of the shipowner

- Crew wages and vessel earnings.

#### Article 143 .-

Maritime claims secured by a maritime lien on the vessel shall be ranked in the following order:

1- Wages and other sums due to the shipmaster and crew members in respect of their employment on the vessel, including repatriation costs and social security contributions

2- Port dues, waterway canal tolls, and pilotage dues

3- Claims against the shipowner or bareboat charterer in respect of loss of life or personal injury occurring, whether on land or on water, in direct connection with the operation of the vessel.

4- Claims against the shipowner or bareboat charterer based on tort/contractual liability in respect of loss of or damage to property occurring, whether on land or on water, in direct connection with the operation of the vessel.

5- Claims in respect of salvage, wreck removal, and general average.

No maritime claim secured by a maritime lien shall arise against the shipowner or bareboat charterer for loss of life, personal injury, or loss of or damage to property caused by radioactive materials or toxic, explosive, or hazardous radioactive substances originating from nuclear fuel, nuclear products, or waste of the vessel, if the operator of a nuclear installation on board the vessel is liable under an international convention or laws in force in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 144 .-

The payment of claims secured by a maritime lien on the vessel shall be executed within 1 (one) year, calculated from the time the maritime lien was recognized, unless the vessel has been arrested and sold by forced sale before the expiry of the 1 (one) year period.

#### Article 145 .-

The arrest of a sea-going vessel to secure one or more maritime claims as prescribed in Article 142 of this Law, and the release of the vessel after payment of claims, or provision of security or bank guarantee for the release of the vessel, shall be carried out by a competent court.

#### Article 146 .-

A claimant may arrest any sea-going vessel which is the subject of the maritime claim, and if that vessel cannot be arrested, the claimant may arrest any other sea-going vessel owned by the same shipowner for the same claim. This arrest does not apply to claims relating to the ownership of the vessel.

A sea-going vessel shall not be arrested for the security of the same claim and the same deposit or bank guarantee more than once in the same case and by the same claimant by a competent court. In this case, the sea-going vessel shall also not be arrested if the same case and claimant are currently under the jurisdiction of a foreign court.

The arrest of a sea-going vessel to secure a maritime claim against the shipowner cannot be executed in the event that the vessel is under the control and operated by a bareboat charterer.

In the case of a bareboat charterer, the claimant may arrest that bareboat chartered vessel as security for the maritime claim, and if that vessel cannot be arrested, the claimant may arrest any other sea-going vessel owned by that bareboat charterer for the same claim.

#### Article 147 .-

A vessel that has been arrested as the subject of a maritime claim shall be released upon the deposit of sufficient security or a bank guarantee, or under conditions where satisfactory financial security or other reasonable grounds are provided, and with the consent of the claimant.

A request for the release of a vessel as set forth in paragraph 1 above shall not be construed as an acknowledgment of liability or benefit of the security of the vessel owner.

Article 148 .-

To claim maritime claims, all damages must be compensated to the vessel owner for the arrest of a seagoing vessel, deposit costs, or security or guarantee and other expenses arising from the arrest of the vessel in case of losing the lawsuit.

Article 149 .-

Maritime claims shall be filed before the competent court of the Kingdom of Cambodia, and shall be implemented in accordance with this Law and other legal instruments in force in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Chapter 9
### Carriage and Liability

#### Section 1
#### Carriage of Goods

#### Part 1
#### Contract of Carriage

Article 150 .-

The carriage of goods by coastal vessels, inland waterway vessels, or seagoing vessels shall be carried out under a contract of carriage of goods.

The carrier shall be liable for damage to or loss of the goods carried, whether or not freight is charged.

Article 151 .-

A bill of lading shall be deemed as evidence of a contract of carriage of goods by water and a contract of carriage of goods by sea.

A bill of lading shall include the following data:

1- General nature of the goods, identification marks of the goods, and if possible, the dangerous character of the goods, the number of packages or pieces, the weight of the goods or quantity of the goods as provided by the shipper.

2- The apparent condition of the goods.

3- The name and principal place of business of the carrier.

4- The name of the shipper.

5- The name of the consignee if provided by the shipper.

6- The port of loading under the contract of carriage of goods, the date on which the carrier took over the goods at the port of loading.

7- The port of discharge under the contract of carriage of goods.

8- The number of originals of the bill of lading, if more than one.

9- The place of delivery of the bill of lading.

10- The signature of the carrier or a person acting on behalf of the carrier.

11- The freight to the extent payable by the consignee or other indication that freight is payable by the shipper.

12- The statement referred to in paragraph 3 of Article 170 of this Law.

13- The statement, if applicable, that the goods shall be carried on deck.

14- The date or the period of delivery of the goods at the port of discharge, if agreed by the parties.

15- Any increased limit or limits of liability as agreed in accordance with Article 184 of this Law.

The carrier shall, after the goods are loaded on board the vessel, issue a bill of lading to the shipper if the shipper so demands, which, in addition to the particulars referred to in paragraph 1 above, shall state that the goods are on board a named vessel or vessels, and the date or dates of loading. The carrier may request the return of any previously issued bill of lading or other document relating to the goods loaded on board from the shipper before issuing a new bill of lading or other document relating to the new goods stating that they are on board its vessel.

The absence of one or more of the particulars in the bill of lading shall not affect the validity of the bill of lading.

#### Part 2
#### Liability of the Carrier

Article 152 .-

The responsibility of the carrier for the goods covers the period during which the carrier is in charge of the goods at the port of loading, during the carriage, and at the port of discharge.

The carrier shall be deemed to be in charge of the goods from the time he has taken over the goods from the shipper or a person acting on his behalf, or from an authority or other third party at the port of loading, until the time he has delivered the goods to the consignee, or by placing them at the disposal of the consignee in accordance with the contract, or by handing them over to an authority or other third party at the port of discharge.

Article 153 .-

The carrier shall be liable for compensation for loss of or damage to the goods, as well as delay in delivery, if the occurrence which caused the loss, damage or delay took place while the goods were in his charge. This case shall be exempted if the carrier proves that he or a person acting on his behalf took all measures that could reasonably be required to avoid the occurrence and its consequences.

Delay in delivery occurs when the goods have not been delivered at the port of discharge within the time expressly agreed in the contract of carriage of goods or, in the absence of such agreement, within the time which it would be reasonable to require of a diligent carrier, having regard to the circumstances of the case.

A person entitled to make a claim for the loss of goods may treat the goods as lost if they have not been delivered within 60 (sixty) consecutive days following the expiry of the time limit for delivery.

The carrier shall be liable for the loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods caused by fire, if the claimant proves that the fire arose from fault or neglect or omission on the part of the carrier or a person acting on his behalf to take all measures to suppress the fire.

The carrier shall not be liable for the loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods in the case of live animals, if the carrier or a person acting on his behalf has followed any special instructions given by the shipper, unless there is proof that the carrier or a person acting on his behalf was at fault or negligent.

The carrier shall not be liable for the loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods resulting from measures to save life or to save property in danger during navigation, except in general average.

Where fault or neglect on the part of the carrier or a person acting on his behalf combines with another cause to produce loss, damage or delay in delivery, the liability of the carrier shall be limited only to the amount of loss, damage or delay attributable to his fault or neglect, provided that the carrier proves the amount of loss, damage or delay not attributable thereto.

Article 154 .-

Unless otherwise agreed by the parties, the compensation for loss, damage or delay in delivery of goods shall be applied as follows:

A- The liability of the carrier for loss of or damage to goods as provided in Article 153 of this Law is limited to an amount equivalent to 835 SDR (eight hundred thirty-five SDR) per package or other shipping unit, or 2.5 SDR (two point five SDR) per kilogram of gross weight of the goods lost or damaged, whichever is the higher.

B- The liability of the carrier for delay in delivery as provided in Article 153 of this Law is limited to an amount equivalent to 2.5 times the freight payable for the goods delayed, but not exceeding the total freight payable under the contract of carriage of goods.

C- In any case, the aggregate liability of the carrier as provided in points A and B above shall not exceed the limit of liability for total loss of the goods as determined under point A above.

For the purpose of calculating which amount is the higher as provided in point A of paragraph 1 above, the following rules shall apply:

- Where a container, pallet or similar article of transport is used to consolidate goods, the packages or shipping units recitals in such article of transport shall be deemed to be packages or shipping units.

- In case the article of transport itself has been lost or damaged, and is not owned or provided by the carrier, such article of transport shall be deemed to be a separate shipping unit.

Article 155 .-

The defenses and limits of liability provided for in this Law apply in any action against the carrier in respect of loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods covered by the contract of carriage of goods, whether the action be founded in contract or in tort.

If such an action is brought against a person acting on behalf of the carrier, such person is entitled to avail himself of the defenses and limits of liability which the carrier is entitled to invoke under this Law, if he proves that he acted within the scope of his employment.

Except as provided in Article 156 of this Law, the aggregate of the amounts recoverable from the carrier and any person acting on his behalf shall not exceed the limits of liability provided for in this Law.

Article 156 .-

The carrier is not entitled to the benefit of the limitation of liability provided for in Article 154 of this Law if it is proved that the loss, damage or delay in delivery resulted from an act or omission of the carrier done with the intent to cause such loss, damage or delay, or recklessly and with knowledge that such loss, damage or delay would probably result.

The provisions of paragraph 1 above also apply to any person acting on behalf of the carrier.

Article 157 .-

The carrier is entitled to carry the goods on deck only if such carriage is in accordance with an agreement with the shipper or with the usage of the particular trade or is required by statutory rules or regulations.

If the carrier and the shipper have agreed that the goods shall or may be carried on deck, the carrier shall insert in the bill of lading or other document evidencing the contract of carriage a statement to that effect. In the absence of such a statement the carrier has the burden of proving that an agreement for carriage on deck has been entered into, but the carrier is not entitled to invoke such agreement against a third party, including a consignee, who has acquired the bill of lading in good faith.

Where the goods have been carried on deck contrary to the provisions of paragraph 1 above or where the carrier cannot invoke an agreement for carriage on deck, the carrier is liable for loss of or damage to the goods, as well as for delay in delivery, which result solely from the carriage on deck.

Carriage of goods on deck contrary to an express agreement for carriage under deck shall be deemed to be an act or omission of the carrier within the meaning of Article 156 of this Law.

Article 158 .-

The carrier remains responsible for the entire carriage under the contract of carriage of goods, notwithstanding that the carriage or part thereof has been entrusted to an actual carrier. The carrier

shall be liable as actual carrier for the acts or omissions of the actual carrier and of his servants and agents acting within the scope of their employment.

The provisions of this Law governing the liability of the carrier also apply to the actual carrier.

Any special agreement under which the carrier assumes obligations not imposed by this Law shall affect the actual carrier only if agreed to by him expressly and in writing. Whether or not the actual carrier has so agreed, the carrier nevertheless remains bound by the obligations resulting from such special agreement.

The carrier and the actual carrier shall be jointly and severally liable according to their respective scopes.

The aggregate of the amounts recoverable from the carrier, the actual carrier and their servants and agents shall not exceed the limits of liability provided for in this Law.

Article 159 .-

Notwithstanding the provisions of Article 158 of this Law, where a contract of carriage of goods provides explicitly that a specified part of the carriage is to be performed by a named person other than the carrier, the contract may also provide that the carrier shall not be liable for loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods caused by an occurrence which takes place while the goods are in the charge of the actual carrier during such part of the carriage. Such restriction or exemption of liability shall be without effect if no judicial proceedings can be brought against the actual carrier before a court of competent jurisdiction as provided in paragraph 1 of Article 168 of this Law. The burden of proving that the loss, damage or delay in delivery was caused by such an occurrence shall rest upon the shipper.

The actual carrier is liable for loss of or damage to the goods, as well as for delay in delivery, caused by an occurrence which takes place while the goods are in his charge, in accordance with the provisions of paragraph 2 of Article 158 of this Law.

#### Part 3
#### Liability of the Shipper

Article 160 .-

The shipper or a person acting on his behalf shall not be entitled to compensation for loss of or damage to the goods covered by the carrier or actual carrier if the loss or damage resulted from the fault or neglect of the shipper or a person acting on his behalf.

Article 161 .-

The shipper shall mark or label in a suitable manner dangerous goods as dangerous.

Where the shipper hands over dangerous goods to the carrier or an actual carrier, the shipper shall inform him of the dangerous character of the goods and, if necessary, of the precautions to be taken. If the shipper fails to do so and such carrier or actual carrier does not otherwise have knowledge of their dangerous character, the following shall apply:

A- The shipper shall be liable to compensate the carrier or actual carrier for any loss or damage resulting from the loading of such goods on board the vessel.

B- Such goods may be unloaded, destroyed or rendered innocuous, as the circumstances may require, without payment of compensation.

The provisions of paragraph 2 above may not be invoked by any person if, during the period he was in charge of the goods, he had knowledge of their dangerous character.

Where the provisions of point B of paragraph 2 above cannot be invoked, and such dangerous goods become an actual danger to life or property, they may be unloaded, destroyed or rendered innocuous, as the circumstances may require, without payment of compensation, except in respect of an obligation to contribute to general average or where the carrier is liable to pay compensation in accordance with the provisions of Article 153 of this Law.

Article 162 .-

When the carrier or the actual carrier takes charge of the goods, the carrier shall, on demand of the shipper, issue to the shipper a bill of lading.

The bill of lading may be signed by a person having authority from the carrier. A bill of lading signed by the master of the vessel carrying the goods shall be deemed to have been signed on behalf of the carrier.

The signature on the bill of lading may be in writing, printed in facsimile, perforated, stamped, in symbols, or made by any other mechanical or electronic means.

Article 163 .-

If the bill of lading contains particulars concerning the general nature, leading marks, number of packages or pieces, weight or quantity of the goods which the carrier or a person issuing the bill of lading on his behalf knows or has reasonable grounds to suspect do not accurately represent the goods actually taken over or, if he had no reasonable means of checking such particulars, the carrier or such person shall insert in the bill of lading a reservation specifying these inaccuracies, grounds of suspicion or the absence of reasonable means of checking.

If the carrier or a person issuing the bill of lading on his behalf fails to note on the bill of lading the apparent condition of the goods, he is deemed to have noted that the goods were in apparent good condition, unless a reservation permitted under paragraph 1 above has been inserted.

A- The bill of lading is prima facie evidence of the taking over or, if a "shipped" bill of lading is issued, loading by the carrier of the goods as described in the bill of lading.

B- Proof to the contrary by the carrier shall not be admissible if the bill of lading has been transferred to a third party, including a consignee, who has acted in good faith in reliance on the description of the goods therein.

A bill of lading which does not indicate the freight or otherwise indicate that freight is payable by the consignee as referred to in point 11 of paragraph 2 of Article 151 of this Law, or does not indicate demurrage or other charges incurred at the port of loading payable by the consignee, is prima facie evidence that no such freight or charges are payable by the consignee. However, proof to the contrary by the carrier shall not be admissible when the bill of lading has been transferred to a third party, including a consignee, who has acted in good faith in reliance on the absence of any such indication in the bill of lading.

Article 164 .-

The shipper shall be deemed to have guaranteed to the carrier the accuracy of the particulars relating to the general nature of the goods, their marks, number, weight and quantity, as furnished by him for insertion in the bill of lading. The shipper shall indemnify the carrier against all loss resulting from inaccuracies in such particulars. The shipper remains liable even if the bill of lading has been transferred by him. The right of the carrier to such indemnity shall in no way limit his liability under the contract of carriage of goods to any person other than the shipper.

Any letter of guarantee or agreement by which the shipper undertakes to indemnify the carrier against loss resulting from the issuance of the bill of lading without entering a reservation relating to the particulars furnished by the shipper or the apparent condition of the goods, shall be null and void and of no effect against any third party, including a consignee, to whom the bill of lading has been transferred.

Such letter of guarantee or agreement is valid against the shipper unless the carrier or a person acting on his behalf, by omitting the reservation referred to in paragraph 2 above, intends to defraud a third party, including a consignee, who acts in reliance on the description of the goods in the bill of lading. In the latter case, if the omitted reservation relates to particulars furnished by the shipper for insertion in the bill of lading, the carrier has no right of indemnity from the shipper pursuant to paragraph 1 above.

In the case of intended fraud, as referred to in paragraph 3 above, the carrier is liable for the loss incurred by the third party, including a consignee, who acted in reliance on the description of the goods in the bill of lading without the benefit of the limitation of liability provided for in this Law.

Article 165 .-

Where a carrier issues a document other than a bill of lading to evidence the receipt of the goods to be carried, such document is prima facie evidence of the conclusion of the contract of carriage and the taking over by the carrier of the goods as therein described.

Article 166 .-

Unless notice of loss or damage, specifying the general nature of such loss or damage, is given in writing by the consignee to the carrier not later than the day after the day when the goods were delivered to the consignee, such delivery is prima facie evidence of the delivery by the carrier of the goods as described in the document of carriage. In case of failure to give notice, the goods shall be deemed to have been delivered in good condition.

Where the loss or damage is not apparent, the provisions of paragraph 1 above apply if notice is not given in writing within 15 (fifteen) consecutive days after the day when the goods were delivered to the consignee, the goods shall be deemed to have been delivered in good condition.

If the state of the goods has at the time of their delivery to the consignee been the subject of joint survey or inspection by the parties, notice in writing need not be given of loss or damage ascertained during such survey or inspection.

In the case of any actual or apprehended loss or damage, the carrier and the consignee must give all reasonable facilities to each other for inspecting and tallying the goods.

No compensation shall be payable for loss resulting from delay in delivery unless a notice has been given in writing to the carrier within 60 (sixty) consecutive days after the day when the goods were delivered to the consignee.

If the goods have been delivered by an actual carrier, any notice given to the actual carrier in writing as provided in the paragraphs above shall have the same effect as if it had been given to the carrier, and any notice given to the carrier shall have the same effect as if it had been given to the actual carrier.

Unless notice of loss or damage, specifying the general nature of the loss or damage, is given in writing by the carrier or actual carrier to the shipper within 90 (ninety) consecutive days after the occurrence of such loss or damage or after the delivery of the goods in accordance with paragraph 2 of Article 152 of this Law, whichever is later, the failure to give such notice is prima facie evidence that the carrier or actual carrier has sustained no loss or damage due to the fault or neglect of the shipper or a person acting on his behalf.

For the purpose of this Article, notice given to a person acting on behalf of the carrier or the actual carrier, including the master or the crew, or the person in charge of the vessel, or to a person acting on behalf of the shipper, is deemed to have been given to the carrier, to the actual carrier or to the shipper, respectively.

Article 167 .-

Any action relating to carriage of goods under this Law is time-barred if judicial or arbitral proceedings have not been instituted within a period of 2 (two) years, commencing from the day on which the carrier delivered the goods.

Article 168 .-

Actions for compensation for loss of or damage to goods shall be brought before the competent court in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure.

In cases where there are multiple claimants, each claimant shall bring his action for compensation for loss of or damage to goods before the court which first seized of an action brought by any of the claimants. Where actions are brought before different courts, such courts shall transfer the cases to the court first seized of an action by any of the claimants.

An appeal shall be brought before the court having jurisdiction over the first instance decision, in accordance with the provisions of paragraph 1 above.

Article 169 .-

The parties may agree to choose a court or arbitration or any other dispute resolution mechanism, either in the country or abroad, to resolve their disputes. Such agreement shall not affect the right of a third party to bring an action before the competent court of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Where a contract of carriage of goods contains a clause providing that disputes arising between the parties shall be resolved by a court or arbitration or any other dispute resolution mechanism, and the bill of lading issued pursuant to such contract does not refer to such clause, the carrier may not invoke such clause unless it is in accordance with the bill of lading.

Article 170 .-

Any clause in a contract of carriage of goods or in a bill of lading or any other document evidencing the contract of carriage of goods shall be null and void to the extent that it deviates directly or indirectly from the provisions of this Law. The nullity of such clause shall not affect the validity of the contract of carriage.

Notwithstanding the provisions of paragraph 1, the carrier may increase his liability and obligations under this Law.

Where a bill of lading or any other document evidencing the contract of carriage of goods is issued, such document shall contain a statement that the carriage is subject to the provisions of this Law which make null and void any clause deviating therefrom to the detriment of the shipper or the consignee.

Where a claimant has brought an action in respect of loss of goods resulting from a clause which is null and void, or from a mistake in the statement, the carrier shall compensate for the loss of, damage to or delay in delivery of the goods.

Article 171 .-

The provisions of this Law shall not affect the application of clauses in a contract of carriage of goods concerning general average or the determination of salvage reward for assisting a vessel in distress or raising a vessel.

#### Article 172 ._

The provisions of this Law do not alter the rights and obligations of the carrier, performing carrier, and persons acting on behalf of the carrier and performing carrier, as provided for in international law or national law relating to the limitation of liability of shipowners.

The provisions of Article 169 and Article 170 of this Law shall not affect the application of provisions as provided for in other international laws in force, and those other international laws shall also not affect the application of paragraph 4 of Article 170 of this Law.

The carrier shall not be liable for compensation for damage caused by a nuclear incident if the operator of a nuclear installation is liable for compensation in accordance with international law or national law in force in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Section 2
### Carriage of Passengers and Luggage

#### Article 173 ._

The carriage of passengers, including their luggage, by cruise vessels, domestic waterway vessels, or Cambodian nationality vessels, shall be carried out in accordance with the passenger carriage contract.

The carrier shall be liable for damages resulting from personal injury or death of passengers, or damage to or loss of passengers' luggage under the carriage contract they have carried, whether for a carriage fee or free of charge.

#### Article 174 ._

The passenger ticket shall include the following key information:

- Vessel name, vessel identification number

- Passenger's name, passenger's current address

- Passenger's luggage

- Seat number or seat allocation

- Port of departure, port of destination, date of departure, and date of arrival.

The passenger ticket shall be signed by the carrier or a person acting on behalf of the carrier, and shall have a clear date.

#### Article 175 ._

The carrier shall pay compensation for damages resulting from personal injury or death of passengers, or loss of or damage to passengers' luggage, if the incident causing such damage occurred due to the fault or neglect of the carrier or a person acting on behalf of the carrier, during the period of carriage.

The burden of proving that the incident causing the loss or damage occurred during the period of carriage, and the extent of the loss or damage, shall be borne by the claimant.

The fault or neglect of the carrier or a person acting on behalf of the carrier shall be presumed to have occurred, unless the contrary is proved, if the personal injury or death of the passenger, or the loss of or damage to the passenger's luggage, was caused by an incident related to shipwreck, collision, stranding, grounding, explosion, or maritime casualty.

#### Article 176 ._

The carrier shall remain liable for the carriage, even if the performance of such carriage is entrusted to a performing carrier.

In the case of entrustment of the carriage performance as provided for in paragraph 1 above, the carrier shall be liable for the faults committed by the performing carrier.

#### Article 177 ._

The carrier shall not be liable for the loss of or damage to currency, negotiable securities, gold, silver, diamonds, jewelry, or other valuables, unless there is a declaration of the quantity and value, and the carrier has agreed to accept them for safekeeping on the vessel during the carriage.

#### Article 178 ._

If the carrier proves that the personal injury or death of a passenger, or the loss of or damage to the passenger's luggage, was caused or contributed to by the fault or neglect of the passenger, the carrier may be exonerated from liability in whole or in part.

#### Article 179 ._

Unless otherwise agreed by the parties, the carrier's liability for personal injury or death of a passenger shall not exceed 46,666 SDR (forty-six thousand six hundred sixty-six Special Drawing Rights) per passenger per voyage.

#### Article 180 ._

Unless otherwise agreed by the parties, the carrier's liability for the loss of or damage to personal luggage shall not exceed 833 SDR (eight hundred thirty-three Special Drawing Rights) per passenger per voyage.

Unless otherwise agreed by the parties, the carrier's liability for the loss of or damage to vehicles, including the luggage inside the vehicle, shall not exceed 3,333 SDR (three thousand three hundred thirty-three Special Drawing Rights) per vehicle per voyage.

Unless otherwise agreed by the parties, the carrier's liability for the loss of or damage to other luggage of the passenger, other than the luggage as provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 above, shall not exceed 1,200 SDR (one thousand two hundred Special Drawing Rights) per passenger per voyage.

#### Article 181 ._

The values calculated in SDR as provided for in this Law shall be converted into Riels to be applied in the competent court, based on the value determined by the International Monetary Fund.

#### Article 182 ._

The carrier and the passenger may agree in writing on the carrier's compensation for personal injury or death, or loss of or damage to the passenger's luggage, to be higher or lower than the limits of liability as provided for in Article 179 and Article 180 of this Law.

#### Article 183 ._

If a claim is brought against a person acting on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier to claim compensation as provided for in this Law, such person shall have the right to defend himself within the limits of liability of the carrier or performing carrier, if he proves that he performed his work on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier.

#### Article 184 ._

The limits of liability as provided for in Article 179 and Article 180 of this Law shall be effective; these limits of liability shall apply to the aggregate of all claims for compensation arising from personal injury or death, or loss of or damage to luggage, for a single passenger at the place where such limits of liability are applied.

In the case where the carriage is performed by a performing carrier, the aggregate of the compensation from the carrier and the performing carrier, as well as from the persons acting on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier, shall not exceed the amount that should be awarded to either the carrier or the performing carrier.

In all cases where the person acting on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier has received the rights as provided for in Article 183 of this Law to receive the right to defend himself within the limits of liability as provided for in Article 179 and Article 180 of this Law, the aggregate of the compensation from the carrier, the performing carrier, and the person acting on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier shall not exceed the limits of liability.

#### Article 185 ._

The carrier shall not be entitled to benefit from any limits of liability as provided for in Article 179, Article 180, and Article 182 of this Law, if it is proved that the damage resulted from an act of fault of the carrier done with intent to cause damage, or recklessly and with knowledge that the result of such damage would probably occur.

The provisions of paragraph 1 above shall also apply to the employees or agents of the carrier or performing carrier who performed the carriage on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier.

#### Article 186 ._

The passenger shall provide written notice to the carrier or the person acting on behalf of the carrier as follows:

a- In the case of visible damage to luggage:

- For personal luggage, before or at the time of disembarkation of the passenger

- For other luggage, before or at the time of redelivery of the luggage.

b- In the case of damage to luggage which is not visible or loss of luggage, within 15 (fifteen) days from the date of disembarkation of the passenger or the date of redelivery of the luggage, or the time when the luggage should have been redelivered.

Failure to comply with the provisions as provided for in paragraph 1 above shall be deemed that the passenger has received the luggage without damage or loss.

The notification as provided for in paragraph 1 above is not required to be given if the condition of the luggage received was subject to a joint inspection at the time of receipt.

#### Article 187 ._

Claims for compensation arising from personal injury or death of a passenger, or for loss of or damage to a passenger's luggage, shall have a limitation period of 3 (three) years.

The limitation period shall be calculated as follows:

a- In the case of personal injury, it shall be calculated from the date on which the passenger disembarked.

b- In the case of death occurring during the period of carriage, it shall be calculated from the date of death.

c- In the case of personal injury occurring during the period of carriage and resulting in death after disembarkation, it shall be calculated from the date of death.

d- In the case of loss of or damage to luggage, it shall be calculated from the date on which the passenger disembarked or the date on which the passenger should have disembarked, whichever is later.

The limitation period specified in paragraph 1 above shall be 5 (five) years calculated from the date of the occurrence of the incident in the case where the date of death cannot be determined.

#### Article 188 ._

Claims for compensation for personal injury or death, or loss of or damage to passenger's luggage, shall be brought before a competent court in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure.

In cases where there are multiple plaintiffs, each plaintiff shall file a claim for compensation for personal injury or death, or loss of or damage to passenger's luggage, before the court that has accepted and processed the claim for compensation from any party. In cases where another court has received this claim, that court must transfer the case file to the court that is currently processing the claim for compensation of any such party.

An appeal shall be filed before the court having jurisdiction over the initial claim, in accordance with the provisions as provided for in paragraph 1 above.

After the occurrence of the incident causing damage, the parties may agree to select a court, arbitration, or any other dispute resolution mechanism, both inside or outside the country, to resolve their dispute. This agreement shall not obstruct the rights of third parties to file a claim before the competent court of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 189 ._

Any clause in a passenger and luggage carriage contract made before the occurrence of the incident causing personal injury or death, or loss of or damage to luggage, by giving

exonerating the carrier from liability to the passenger, or prescribing a lower limit of liability than that fixed in this Law, or shifting the burden of proof which rests on the carrier, or restricting the options of the parties as provided for in Article 188 of this Law, shall be null and void.

The nullity of such clauses as provided for in paragraph 1 above shall not affect the validity of the contract between the parties.

#### Article 190 ._

The provisions of this Law do not alter the rights and obligations of the carrier, performing carrier, or persons acting on behalf of the carrier or performing carrier, as provided for in international law, and in particular national laws relating to the limitation of liability of shipowners.

#### Article 191 ._

No compensation shall be paid for damage caused by a nuclear incident if the operator of a nuclear installation on the vessel is liable for such damage in accordance with international conventions or national laws in force in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

## Chapter 10
## Port Management

### Section 1
### Port Classification and Port Management Authorities

#### Article 192 ._

Ports are classified into 4 classes as follows:

- Class 1: Ports serving vessels performing international navigation and having the highest socio-economic potential

- Class 2: Ports serving vessels performing navigation with neighboring countries that have separate agreements

- Class 3: Ports serving vessels performing navigation within the Kingdom of Cambodia

- Special Class: Ports other than Class 1, Class 2, and Class 3 ports.

#### Article 193 ._

Each port shall be established based on the criteria as provided for in Article 192 of this Law and other key criteria as follows:

- Port name

- Geographical location which is naturally favorable, having potential in the transportation system, activities, and functions aligned with the purpose of the port establishment

- Port capacity or size capable of loading and unloading goods or embarking and disembarking passengers

- Land boundary and water boundary of the port.

The establishment of Class 1, Class 2, and Special Class ports serving vessels performing international navigation or navigation with neighboring countries shall be determined by sub-decree.

The establishment of Class 3 and Special Class ports serving vessels performing navigation within the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 194 ._

Ports of all classes shall have a port area, which includes the water boundary and the land boundary.

#### Article 195 ._

Ports of all classes shall have a port management authority.

### Section 2
### Port Master

#### Article 196 ._

Class 1 and Class 2 ports shall have a port master.

The port master shall be appointed by a decision of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

The Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to issue a decision to appoint a port master for other ports.

The port master must be a civil servant of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

The criteria for the port master shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 197 ._

The port master has the following duties:

- Manage the entry and exit procedures of vessels in the port

- Direct the shifting of vessels' position within the port water boundary or shifting position to outside the port water boundary

- Prohibit, restrict, suspend, or obstruct any activities within the port in order to ensure the safety of operations of port facilities and all vessels

- Have the right to refuse to grant permission for vessels to depart from the port

- Instruct and take measures regarding cases of vessels carrying dangerous goods.

The regulations of the port master shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

### Section 3
### Permission for Vessels to Enter and Leave the Port

#### Article 198 ._

To enter the port, vessels performing international navigation must have a port entry permit.

In case of force majeure, vessels performing international navigation in transit through the water territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia have the right to seek shelter in any port or any place temporarily, by merely notifying the nearest competent authority, and shall not be required to have a port entry permit.

The procedures for granting a port entry permit shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 199 ._

Upon departing from the port, vessels performing international navigation must have a port departure permit.

The procedures for granting a port departure permit shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 200 ._

Vessels shall not be granted a port departure permit in the case where the vessel is undergoing inspection by foreign vessel inspection officers or the vessel is performing obligations with involved parties, including port operators, shippers, consignees, etc.

#### Article 201 ._

Port entry and departure shall be prioritized for vessels serving rescue operations in case of emergency.

#### Article 202 ._

Each port must accept vessels for berthing in accordance with its class, as provided for in Article 192 of this Law.

In case of necessity, the Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to permit vessels to enter or leave ports of other classes.

#### Article 203 ._

Foreign vessels and Cambodian nationality vessels shall not perform the carriage of goods or passengers between ports located within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia, unless permitted by the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 204 ._

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall promote the establishment of shipping agencies to provide services to vessels entering and leaving ports in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Legal entities or physical persons acting as shipping agencies must have a shipping agency permit.

The procedures for granting a shipping agency permit shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 205 ._

All foreign vessels entering and leaving ports in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia must use shipping agencies as provided for in Article 204 of this Law.

The provisions of paragraph 1 above shall be exempted in cases where the foreign vessel is under the management and operation of a shipping company having its headquarters, branch, or representative office in the Kingdom of Cambodia. In this case, the shipping company does not have the right to coordinate and provide services for vessels of other shipping companies.

### Section 4
### Port Development, Construction, and Operation

#### Article 206 ._

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to decide on the phased port development plan of the port development plan of each port, in accordance with the criteria as provided for in the port master plan.

The port master plan shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 207 ._

The construction and/or repair of port areas shall require a port area construction and/or repair permit.

The procedures for granting a port area construction and/or repair permit shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 208 ._

The construction and repair of port areas shall be carried out in accordance with technical standards for the construction and repair of port areas.

The technical standards for the construction and repair of port areas shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 209 ._

The operation of port areas shall require a port area operation permit.

Completed port areas or any completed parts of port areas must be guaranteed for safety, security, and environment before being put into operation.

The port area operation permit shall be valid for 2 (two) years.

The procedures for granting a port area operation permit shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 210 ._

The port operator must have professional capacity, which shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 211 ._

The port operator must report on port area operations to the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

The reporting form of the port area operations shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

### Section 5
### Security and Environment

#### Article 212 ._

Ports shall have port area waste reception facilities to receive waste from vessels.

Technical standards for port area waste reception facilities shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 213 ._

Ports shall have an emergency prevention plan to respond to oil spills or dangerous substance spills at the port.

#### Article 214 ._

Ports serving vessels performing international navigation must have a port area security assessment report, a port area security plan, a port area security certificate, a port area security officer, and port area security personnel.

Port area security shall be determined by sub-decree.

### Section 6
### Regulations on Navigation

#### Article 215 ._

Ports shall have regulations on navigation.

Regulations on navigation shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

### Section 7
### Port Charges

#### Article 216 ._

Ports shall have a tariff of port charges determined by the port management authority.

The port management authority shall collect port charges as provided for in paragraph 1 above.

The Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport have the right to determine the level of port charges to ensure fair competition.

#### Article 217 ._

All vessels have the right to receive port services at any port, in any form, on an equal basis.

#### Section 8
#### Port Electronic Data Interchange System

#### Article 218 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall organize, lead, and manage the Port Electronic Data Interchange System.

The Port Electronic Data Interchange System shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Chapter 11
#### Waterway Infrastructure

#### Article 219 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall organize, develop, and protect the waterway infrastructure, which includes navigation channels, waterway art works, aids to navigation, and systems to support navigation, transport, and ports.

The management of waterway infrastructure shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Article 220 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall study and prepare waterway charts, navigation charts, and publish them for navigation.

Waterway charts shall be prepared for the purpose of developing the waterway network, showing the general geography of waterway areas.

Navigation charts shall be prepared for navigation purposes, showing waterway geography and accurate information for navigation service to ensure reliability, steering efficiency, and avoidance of navigation accidents.

#### Article 221 .-

Art works and water level raising structures that affect or cause impact on the safety of vessels in normal navigation along navigation channels shall be prohibited.

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall prevent all activities that obstruct navigation or activities that cause difficulties to navigation, and shall take urgent measures to avoid accidents. In case of necessity, the Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall cooperate with relevant ministries, institutions, and competent authorities.

#### Article 222 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport has the right to temporarily suspend navigation along any waterway for the purposes of security, safety, and public order.

#### Chapter 12
#### Pilotage

#### Article 223 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall organize pilotage for vessels navigating through sovereign waters or departing from ports in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 224 .-

Vessels navigating within pilotage areas or entering/leaving ports in the Kingdom of Cambodia shall use pilotage services, which include compulsory pilotage and non-compulsory pilotage.

#### Article 225 .-

In pilotage operations, the pilot shall perform as follows:

- Provide information and advice to the vessel's master regarding the conditions of the pilotage area, the position and method for the vessel to berth, and inspect the navigation conditions of the vessel that can safely proceed.

- Promptly report to the port captain or competent authorities in case of detecting any irregularity related to safety, security, and the environment of the vessel and persons on board, cargo, or any practice that could affect the interests of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

In pilotage operations, the pilot has the right to use one assistant pilot. An apprentice pilot may also be used under the responsibility of that pilot.

In pilotage operations, the pilot shall wear the pilot uniform.

In all cases of pilotage, the master shall remain fully responsible for their vessel.

#### Article 226 .-

In pilotage operations, the master shall provide the pilot with information regarding the maneuverability characteristics and key specifications of their vessel relevant to pilotage.

#### Article 227 .-

The pilot has the right to refuse to continue pilotage if they deem that, in starting or continuing pilotage, the vessel is in a condition that poses a danger to the safety of navigation, persons on board, other vessel traffic, or the environment. In this case, the pilot shall immediately report to the port captain.

#### Article 228 .-

The pilot shall possess a pilot certificate with a validity of not more than 5 (five) years.

The pilot certificate shall be granted to candidates who possess the following qualifications:

- Have training in pilotage specialization or possess a seafarer competency certificate as master or a seafarer competency certificate as master navigating the vessel

- Have a health certificate certifying fitness to perform duties on a similar deck of vessel

- Have received training on the pilotage area and served as an assistant pilot in the pilotage area where they are to perform duties

- Have knowledge of the English language

In accordance with the successful completion of examinations organized by the General Department in charge of Waterway, Maritime Transport and Ports.

The pilot certificate granted to a candidate to perform duties as a pilot as stipulated in the first paragraph above shall be revoked or declared null and void in the following cases:

- The pilot loses work capacity

- The pilot fails to perform duties as a pilot for a period of 30 (thirty) months after receiving the pilot certificate

- The pilot performs pilotage operations and provides incorrect instructions that cause danger to the safety of navigation

- The pilot commits offenses or demands any illegal benefits during the performance of duties.

#### Article 229 .-

Compulsory pilotage shall be exempted if the master obtains a pilotage exemption certificate after fulfilling any of the following conditions:

1- The master has safely conducted transits through the pilotage area multiple times, without affecting the traffic of other vessels, and without violating the interests of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2- The master has fulfilled the conditions as stipulated in the second paragraph of Article 228 of this Law.

The pilotage exemption certificate shall have a validity of not more than 5 (five) years.

The pilotage exemption certificate shall be revoked or declared null and void in the event that the master causes impact on other vessel traffic or violates the interests of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Compulsory pilotage is also exempted in cases of force majeure.

#### Article 230 .-

Pilotage operations shall be performed by pilots who are permanent staff of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

In case of necessity, the Minister of Public Works and Transport has the right to delegate to the private sector the performance of pilotage operations in any pilotage area.

#### Article 231 .-

The conditions and procedures for pilotage shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

The pilot uniform shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Chapter 13
#### Vessel Shipyards

#### Article 232 .-

The establishment of a vessel shipyard requires a vessel shipyard certificate with permanent validity.

The vessel shipyard must possess a vessel shipyard business license for construction and repair of vessels.

The procedures for granting the vessel shipyard certificate and the vessel shipyard business license for construction and repair of vessels shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 233 .-

The construction and/or repair of a vessel requires a construction and/or repair permit for the vessel.

The procedures for granting construction and/or repair permits for vessels shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 234 .-

The construction and repair of vessels shall comply with the technical standards for construction and repair of vessels.

The technical standards for construction and repair of vessels shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Chapter 14
#### Inspection and Control

#### Section 1
#### Inspection of Maritime Vessels, Inland Waterway Vessels, Vessel Shipyards, Port Facilities, Art Works, Water Level Raising Structures, and Other Related Activities

#### Article 235 .-

The Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall appoint vessel and port inspectors to inspect maritime vessels, inland waterway vessels, vessel shipyards, port facilities, art works, water level raising structures, and other activities that affect the safety of navigation in accordance with the provisions of this Law.

Vessel and port inspectors shall be qualified as judicial police officers to investigate offenses as stipulated in this Law, in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

When performing duties to investigate offenses, vessel and port inspectors who are qualified as judicial police officers shall act in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The format and procedures for granting qualification as judicial police officers to vessel and port inspectors shall be determined by Joint Proklamation between the Minister of Justice and the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 236 .-

Vessel and port inspectors who are qualified as judicial police officers shall have the following rights and duties:

- Enter any site or board any vessel at ports to monitor, inspect, and perform duties within their competence in accordance with the provisions of this Law

- Temporarily detain vessels with deficiencies that could pose a danger to the safety of navigation

- Invite persons related to vessels, vessel shipyards, port facilities, art works, water level raising structures, and other activities to provide information, documents, or various items for research and preservation of evidence in cases of flagrante delicto offenses, in accordance with the provisions of this Law

- Cooperate with other competent officers of ministries, institutions, and competent authorities to suppress navigation offenses within the waters of the Kingdom of Cambodia

- Take other measures within the scope of implementing the provisions of this Law.

The format and procedures for vessel and port inspections shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 237 .-

Vessel and port inspectors may inspect vessels without prior notice.

Vessel and port inspectors may enter vessel shipyards, port facilities, art works, and water level raising structures after providing a written notice on a scheduled date, within working hours, to perform their duties.

#### Section 2
#### Inspection of Foreign Vessels at Ports

#### Article 238 .-

Foreign vessels entering ports in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be subject to inspection by foreign vessel control officers appointed by the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 239 .-

The inspection of foreign vessels shall be conducted for the purpose of checking technical and compliance aspects to ensure that foreign vessels entering ports in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia comply with relevant international conventions.

The inspection of foreign vessels shall be conducted upon the initiative of the foreign vessel control officers, or upon a request from the port of last departure, or upon a request from seafarers on board the vessel, or upon a request from the port.

#### Article 240 .-

The inspection of foreign vessels includes:

1- Initial inspection: Inspection of documents and general external condition of the vessel.

2- Detailed inspection: Conducted after the initial inspection if there are clear grounds to believe that the vessel has deficiencies in both documentation and safety.

In detailed inspections, the following shall be applied:

- Foreign vessel control officers shall allow the vessel to depart from the port normally if they find that the vessel has no deficiencies

- Foreign vessel control officers shall temporarily detain the vessel to rectify deficiencies if they find that the vessel has deficiencies that warrant detention

- Foreign vessel control officers shall suspend the detailed inspection and continue the inspection on a part-by-part basis if they find that the vessel is below technical standards.

After completing the inspection, the foreign vessel control officers shall prepare an inspection report in the presence of the master.

The report shall specify the deficiencies, the person to be notified and the deficiencies, dates and places where the vessel must make rectifications.

The persons to be notified as stipulated in the second paragraph above include the foreign vessel control officer at the next port of call, the vessel's flag State and seafarers, representatives of the embassy or consulate of the vessel's flag State and seafarers, and the vessel's classification society.

#### Article 242 .-

Foreign vessel control officers shall possess qualifications to be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Article 243 .-

The control of foreign vessels at ports shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Section 3
#### Control of Vessels navigating Waterways within the Territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia

#### Article 244 .-

Vessels navigating waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be subject to control by competent officers of ministries and institutions charged with the duty of controlling vessels on waterways in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia in accordance with this Law and other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 245 .-

Competent officers who are qualified as judicial police officers have the right to order maritime vessels and domestic waterway vessels navigating waterways to stop and to board the vessels to investigate offenses in case there are clues or reasons to believe that the vessel has violated the provisions of this Law, other laws and regulations in force.

In controlling vessels as stipulated in the first paragraph above, competent officers qualified as judicial police officers have the right to take measures to detain the vessel and/or detain any person who commits an offense, and to build a case file to be forwarded to the competent court in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure and relevant legal regulations.

The control and taking of measures on the vessel shall be conducted without delay and without causing risks to the safety of navigation, and notification of the case shall be made to the Ministry of Public Works and Transport or the sub-national administration where the vessel is registered.

#### Article 246 .-

Foreign vessels conducting transit navigation along waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall conduct innocent passage by maintaining continuous and expeditious navigation and shall not commit any of the following acts:

a- Threatening or using force against the sovereignty, territorial integrity, independence, or in any manner that violates the principles of national and international law.

2- Collecting information or military intelligence with the purpose of affecting security or national defense.

3- Transferring or loading goods, equipment, or persons.

4- Willfully causing pollution of the environment.

5- Fishing activities.

6- Study and research activities.

7- Disturbing communication systems or other radio stations.

8- Anchoring without notification.

9- Other activities which may cause indirect impact.

Foreign vessels conducting transit navigation along the territorial sea within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall hoist the flag of their State of nationality and the flag of the Kingdom of Cambodia, and possess valid documentation for the vessel, seafarers, cargo, or passengers.

#### Article 247 .-

Competent officers qualified as judicial police officers have the right to order and board foreign vessels conducting transit navigation along waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia to investigate offenses committed by or on board the vessel.

The investigation of offenses as stipulated in the first paragraph above includes questioning the master, checking vessel and seafarer documents, and establishing evidence on board the vessel; and after discovering a flagrante delicto offense, the vessel control officers have the right to arrest the vessel, arrest offenders, and build a case file to be sent to the competent court in accordance with the Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure, and relevant legal regulations.

In all cases of stopping and boarding a foreign vessel conducting transit navigation along waterways within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia, competent officers qualified as judicial police officers must possess evidence or reasons to believe that the foreign vessel has committed an offense against this Law, other laws and regulations in force, and shall investigate the offense and take potential measures without delay.

#### Article 248 .-

In case of an offense occurring on board a foreign vessel conducting transit navigation along internal waters or the territorial sea of the Kingdom of Cambodia, the competent officers have the right to arrest the vessel or arrest any person in any of the following cases:

- The consequences of the offense affect the sovereignty, territorial integrity, independence, and interests of the Kingdom of Cambodia

- Upon request of the master, or the embassy, or representative mission of the vessel's flag State

- For the suppression of transnational crimes, such as drug offenses, smuggling, human trafficking.

#### Article 249 .-

In all cases of stopping, boarding a vessel, and taking any measures as stipulated in Article 247 and Article 248 of this Law, the competent officers shall notify the vessel owner or their representative, the vessel's flag State, or the embassy or representative mission. This notification shall be made concurrently with taking the measures or immediately after taking the measures.

In all cases of stopping a vessel in navigation to investigate offenses, or in cases of hot pursuit when the vessel refuses to stop, the competent officers shall not cause danger to the safety of navigation.

#### Section 4
#### Uniform

#### Article 250 .-

During the performance of duties, vessel and port inspectors and foreign vessel control officers shall wear uniforms, badges, and ranks, and possess mission orders.

The uniforms, badges, and ranks of vessel and port inspectors and foreign vessel control officers shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Chapter 15
#### Joint Provisions

#### Article 251 .-

Public service fees required under this Law shall be determined by Joint Proklamation between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

#### Chapter 16
#### Penal Provisions

#### Article 252 .-

Sanctions under this Law include administrative sanctions and penal sanctions.

#### Article 253 .-

Administrative sanctions include:

- Written warning

- Temporary suspension

- Requirement to rectify

- Requirement to make restitution

- Suspension, revocation, or cancellation of certificates, permits, or documents granted in accordance with this Law.

The procedures for imposing administrative sanctions shall be determined by Proklamation of the Minister of Public Works and Transport.

The decision to impose administrative sanctions shall be under the competence of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

Any person who is dissatisfied with an administrative decision on administrative sanctions, except for a written warning as stated in the first paragraph above, has the right to file a written complaint to the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the decision.

The Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport shall decide on this complaint within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the complaint.

Any person who is dissatisfied with the decision of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport on the complaint has the right to appeal to a competent court in accordance with the procedures within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the decision.

The appeal made by the above person does not have the effect of suspending the execution of the decision on administrative sanctions.

#### Article 54.-

Criminal sanctions include:

- Transaction fine

- Fine

- Imprisonment.

Offenses subject to transaction fines in accordance with this law shall be determined by sub-decree.

The decision to impose a transaction fine is the competence of ship and port inspectors who have the qualification of judicial police officers.

The payment of a transaction fine shall extinguish the public prosecution. The payment of the transaction fine is not an obstacle to the implementation of administrative sanctions and criminal sanctions in accordance with the provisions of this law and other applicable criminal laws.

In case the offender refuses to pay the transaction fine, the ship and port inspectors who have the qualification of judicial police officers shall compile the offense case file and send it to court in accordance with the procedures.

The forms and procedures of transaction fines, payment of transaction fines, management of transaction fine receipts, and management of revenues received from transaction fines for offenses as stated in the provisions of this law shall be determined by inter-ministerial declaration of the Minister of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport, the Minister of the Ministry of Justice, and the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

The Royal Government may provide incentives to officials who have participated in cracking down on offenses as stated in this law.

#### Article 55.-

Ship and port inspectors, foreign ship inspectors, or competent officials who enforce the provisions of this law and other applicable relevant provisions in the implementation of their duties shall bear administrative responsibility in accordance with this law and other applicable laws and regulations.

Administrative responsibility is not an obstacle to prosecution, criminal punishment, as well as civil liability in accordance with this law and other applicable laws and regulations.

#### Article 56.-

A master or crew member of a vessel who refuses without clear reason to provide assistance to a vessel, crew member, passenger, or any person who is in danger after a collision shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 9,000,000 (nine million) Riels.

#### Article 57.-

Any person who clearly knows that the vessel they are navigating has caused an accident or damage and flees from criminal or civil liability shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels.

#### Article 58.-

Any person who navigates with negligence, carelessness, imprudence, or non-compliance with the regulations on mandatory navigation, causing injury to others and resulting in the victim's disability to work for a period equal to or exceeding 8 (eight) days from the date of the incident, shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels.

The above offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Riels to 12,000,000 (twelve million) Riels in any of the following cases:

1- Not having the certificate of competency of the crew or upgraded certificate of competency of the crew as required in accordance with the provisions of this law.

2- Causing injury to several people.

3- Fleeing from the scene of the incident for the purpose of evading liability.

4- Being in a state of drunkenness with an alcohol concentration of 0.40 (zero point forty) milligrams per liter of air or 0.80 (zero point eighty) grams per liter of blood or more.

#### Article 59.-

Any person who navigates with negligence, carelessness, imprudence, or non-compliance with the regulations on mandatory navigation, causing the death of another person, shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Riels to 12,000,000 (twelve million) Riels.

The above offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and a fine from 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels in any of the following cases:

1- Not having the certificate of competency of the crew or upgraded certificate of competency of the crew as required in accordance with the provisions of this law.

2- Causing the death of several people.

3- Fleeing from the scene of the incident for the purpose of evading liability.

4- Being in a state of drunkenness with an alcohol concentration of 0.40 (zero point forty) milligrams per liter of air or 0.80 (zero point eighty) grams per liter of blood or more.

#### Article 60.-

Any crew member who navigates without a certificate of competency of the crew or upgraded certificate of competency of the crew during the period when those certificates of competency are suspended, revoked, or invalidated shall be subject to a transaction fine as determined by sub-decree.

The above offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 6 (six) months and a fine from 400,000 (four hundred thousand) Riels to 2,000,000 (two million) Riels in case the offender has already been subject to a transaction fine and still continues to commit the same offense within 1 (one) year from the date of the previous transaction fine.

#### Article 61.-

Any person who navigates without a registration certificate of a sea-going vessel or inland waterway vessel in the country shall be subject to a transaction fine as determined by sub-decree.

The above offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) days to 1 (one) month and a fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Riels to 400,000 (four hundred thousand) Riels in case the offender has already been subject to a transaction fine and still continues to commit the same offense within 1 (one) year from the date of the previous transaction fine.

#### Article 62.-

Any person who navigates by fraudulently flying the Cambodian national flag without registering the vessel under Cambodian nationality in accordance with the provisions of this law shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and a fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels.

#### Article 63.-

Any ship and port inspector, foreign ship inspector, or competent official who, in the scope of their duties or on the occasion of performing their duties, revokes the registration certificate of a sea-going vessel or inland waterway vessel in the country, the certificate of technical inspection of the vessel, the permit for the vessel to operate transport business, the certificate of competency of the crew, or ship document without clear reason shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) days to 1 (one) month and a fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Riels to 400,000 (four hundred thousand) Riels.

In case the above offense causes damage or loss to any part of the vessel, the relevant unit shall be responsible for compensating for such damage or loss in accordance with the provisions of the Civil Code and other applicable laws.

In case a ship and port inspector, foreign ship inspector, or competent official commits corruption by receiving benefits in performing their duties or on the occasion of performing their duties, the offense as stated in the first paragraph above shall be punished in accordance with the provisions of the Law on Anti-Corruption which is a corruption offense and shall be under the sole competence of the Anti-Corruption Unit in investigating and compiling the case file to be sent to court.

Other units and individuals who are aware of the corruption offenses as stated in this law and in other laws and regulations in force in the Kingdom of Cambodia shall report the facts of these offenses to the Anti-Corruption Unit or Capital/Provincial Anti-Corruption Offices.

#### Article 64.-

Any person who blocks or uses any means to obstruct navigation along the waterway shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels.

#### Article 65.-

Any person who builds engineering structures or installs water barriers or performs other activities that affect the safety of vessels in normal navigation along the channel, and stubbornly refuses to dismantle and restore, shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels.

#### Article 66.-

Any person who builds or repairs a port concession/facility without a construction or repair permit for the port concession/facility, and stubbornly refuses to temporarily suspend and refuses to complete the application process for the permit, shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels.

#### Article 67.-

Any person who operates a port concession/facility without an operating permit for the port concession/facility as stated in Article 20 of this law shall be subject to a transaction fine as determined by sub-decree.

The above offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 400,000 (four hundred thousand) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels in case the offender has already been subject to a transaction fine and still continues to commit the same offense within 1 (one) year from the date of the previous transaction fine.

#### Article 68.-

Any person who refuses to give priority to a vessel entering or exiting the port as stated in Article 21 of this law shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 1 (one) year and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels.

#### Article 69.-

For physical persons who commit offenses as stated from Article 56 to Article 59, paragraph 1 of Article 60, paragraph 2 of Article 61, Article 62, paragraph 1 of Article 63, Article 64 to Article 66, paragraph 2 of Article 67, and Article 68 of this law, the court may declare one or more additional penalties as stated in Article 53 (Types of additional penalties) of the Penal Code.

The types, forms, and procedures for implementing these additional penalties shall follow the provisions of the Penal Code.

#### Article 70.-

Legal entities may be declared criminally responsible under the conditions stated in Article 42 (Criminal liability of legal entities) of the Penal Code for offenses stated from Article 58 to Article 62 and Article 64 to Article 68 of this law.

Legal entities shall be punished by a fine from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels to 200,000,000 (two hundred million) Riels, as well as one or more additional penalties as stated in Article 168 (Additional penalties applicable to legal entities) of the Penal Code.

#### Article 71.-

The application of the provisions of Chapter 16 of this law does not prevent the application of other criminal laws in case the act defined as an offense under this law is an offense under other criminal laws.

### Annex of the Law on Inland Waterway Transport
### Glossary

**Navigation or to navigate** refers to the voyage of a vessel or the movement of a vessel.

**International navigation** refers to navigation in which a vessel departs from a port located in one country to a port located in another country by crossing the sea.

**Vessel detention** refers to (1)- preventive measures taken by foreign ship inspectors at ports when the condition of the vessel and its crew do not comply with key provisions of international conventions to ensure that the vessel will not depart until it can cross the sea safely for the vessel, crew, and environment, (2)- detention of a vessel to inspect offenses in accordance with the provisions of this law, and (3)- detention of a sea-going vessel to secure the payment of maritime claims.

**Registration of ship hypothec or ship mortgage** refers to the registration of ship hypothec or ship mortgage made by the shipowner to obtain loans for construction or operation of the vessel, according to the rate determined in the ship hypothec contract or shipbuilding contract made between the shipowner and the creditor, such as a bank or any insurance company.

**Shipping company** refers to a physical or legal person that manages, arranges, and uses a vessel or operates a vessel.

**Port** refers to an area with specific water and land boundaries and has port infrastructure to serve vessels entering, exiting, anchoring, and berthing to lift, transfer, load, and store cargo and transfer passengers.

**Pilot** refers to a navigator whose duty is to assist the master in navigating the vessel for safe navigation in pilotage areas or port waters.

**Pilotage** refers to the service that a pilot must perform on a vessel to enable the vessel to navigate through pilotage areas or enter and exit the port safely in the waters of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Propulsive force** refers to the force generated by the use of mechanical systems, including engines, propellers or jet systems, to propel a vessel to move on the water surface, generally referring to forward movement.

**Reverse propulsive force** refers to the propulsive force of a vessel by using mechanical systems to make the vessel move backward.

**Total loss of vessel** refers to a vessel lost by theft, unable to be found, or having a maritime accident by sinking in the sea which cannot be salvaged, broken into pieces, completely burned, or exploded and cannot be repaired for reuse.

**Main sail** refers to the type of sail fitted on the main mast of a sailing vessel.

**Square sail** refers to the type of sail arranged and fitted on the mast, having a vertical, horizontal, or gaff shape, perpendicular to the mast and also perpendicular to the vessel's keel.

**Vessel collision** refers to a maritime accident occurring due to touching, scraping, or colliding between two or more vessels, which causes loss or damage to the vessels or cargo on board, or causes injury or loss of human life on board, as well as damage to third parties, such as environmental impact and interruption of other lawful economic activities in the waters.

**Force majeure** refers to natural phenomena that are unforeseeable and irresistible by humans.

**Clause** refers to a provision or term contained in a contract.

**Maritime accident** refers to an accident of a vessel occurring during navigation due to various incidents, including collision, fire, sinking, grounding, touching, caused by natural phenomena or human activities.

**Traffic separation lane** refers to a navigation lane separating traffic directions and determined by the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

**Fairway** refers to a navigation channel in a river, lake, sea area, port waters, etc., between ice areas, submerged dykes, and/or islands, etc.

**Deficiencies to be detained** refers to deficiencies showing a threat to the safety of the vessel, crew, and environment when the vessel sails across the sea.

**Draft** refers to the length from the bottom of the vessel to the waterline of the vessel on the water surface.

**Port waters** refers to the area including the entrance, lightening area, anchorage, turning basin, berth, and other facilities, etc. Lightening area is an area located inside or outside the port waters for vessels with deep draft or oil/gas tankers with risk of oil/gas spill during transfer to berth using anchors to transfer cargo. Anchorage is an area located inside or outside the port waters for vessels to berth by using anchors connected to the water bed. Berth includes dry berth and wet berth. Dry berth is a solid structure for vessel berthing located directly adjacent to the land of the port. Wet berth is an area located inside the port waters for vessel berthing by mooring the vessel to dolphins/posts firmly secured to the water bed.

**Port land** refers to the land area with facilities serving the loading, unloading, transfer, storage of cargo, embarkation and disembarkation of passengers, and other auxiliary facilities.

**Internal waters** refers to sea areas located landward of the baseline.

**Pilotage area** refers to a part of the navigation channel determined for vessels to use pilotage services.

**Hot pursuit** refers to the action of competent officials pursuing a foreign vessel in the territorial sea after that vessel has left the internal waters, to board and inspect for offenses if there is evidence or suspicion of an offense, and the vessel refuses to stop upon the request of the competent officials.

**Port facility** refers to a location where operations between vessels and ports take place. Port facility includes berths, anchorages, waiting areas, navigation channels, and buoys, as well as structures and equipment for vessel operations.

**Radiation source on board** refers to X-ray machines, radioactive material processing machines, machines emitting or generating radiation energy, radioactive material storage machines, or any type of apparatus containing radiation energy, radioactive materials, or radioactive waste.

**Knot** refers to the unit of speed calculated in nautical miles per hour. 1 nautical mile for measuring speed is assumed to be the international nautical mile equal to 1852 (one thousand eight hundred fifty-two) meters.

**Anchorage area** refers to a location in a waterway designated for vessels to anchor.

**Territorial sea** refers to the sea area located within a distance of 12 nautical miles from the baseline seaward.

**Port dues/charges** refers to charges for gross tonnage, fairway dues, anchorage dues, mooring and unmooring charges, berthing charges, cargo handling charges, cargo storage charges, pilotage fees, tug/towage services charges, and other service charges.

**Dangerous goods** refers to goods classified in the International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code and easily dangerous to transport if there are no preventive measures or precautions in advance.

**Goods** refers to products in the form of solids, liquids, and gases, in packaging or container form, bulk cargo, and live animals or live plants.

**Heading** refers to the direction of the vessel, expressed in degrees measured clockwise from the earth's north longitude to the longitudinal line of the vessel.

**Bearing** refers to the direction towards the observed object, measured in degrees clockwise from the earth's north longitude to the line intersecting the observer and the observed object.

**Vessel** refers to a watercraft built for traveling on waterways.

**Coastal vessel** refers to a vessel registered as a coastal vessel to obtain legality for navigating along gulfs, open seas, and canals adjacent to the sea in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Inland waterway vessel** refers to a vessel registered as an inland waterway vessel to obtain legality for navigating along rivers, canals, streams, lakes in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Sea-going vessel** refers to a vessel navigating across the sea.

**Cambodian vessel** refers to a sea-going vessel registered as a Cambodian vessel by the Cambodian Ship Registration Unit of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport to obtain legality for navigating across the sea.

**Foreign vessel** refers to a sea-going vessel registered as a foreign vessel by a foreign competent authority to obtain legality for navigating across the sea.

**Cargo vessel** refers to a vessel built for the purpose of transporting cargo, which includes general cargo vessels, container vessels, bulk carriers, oil tankers, gas carriers, chemical tankers, and barge carriers, etc.

**Passenger vessel** refers to a vessel built for the purpose of transporting passengers, which includes passenger vessels, cruise ships, high-speed vessels, etc.

**Public service or duty vessel** refers to a vessel built for the purpose of public service or duty.

Rescue vessel: refers to a vessel constructed for the purpose of rescuing vessels and persons in distress and recognizable as a rescue vessel.

Competent force vessel: refers to a vessel of the competent forces of the Ministry of National Defense and the Ministry of Interior for the purpose of maintaining national security and national defense.

Vessel on special mission: refers to a vessel operated by officers assigned to perform traffic order duties.

Vessel performing public safety activities: refers to a vessel for patrol, convoy, special ceremonies, traffic inspection, rescue, firefighting, medical assistance, vessel salvage, search and rescue, etc.

Power-driven vessel: refers to any vessel propelled by machinery.

Fishing vessel: refers to any vessel constructed and equipped with fishing gear for fishing.

Vessel engaged in fishing: refers to any vessel fishing with nets, lines, trawls, or other fishing apparatus which restrict maneuverability.

Sailing vessel: refers to any vessel under sail provided that propelling machinery, if fitted, is not being used.

Towing vessel: refers to any vessel constructed for towing or pushing other vessels.

Pushing vessel: refers to any vessel constructed for pushing other vessels.

Vessel restricted in her ability to maneuver: refers to a vessel which from the nature of her work is restricted in her ability to maneuver, including a vessel engaged in laying or picking up a navigation aid, laying cables or pipelines, dredging or surveying operations, underwater operations, transferring cargo or persons, mining operations, salvaging shipwreck or debris in water, and towing or pushing operations.

Vessel not under command: refers to a vessel which through some exceptional circumstance is unable to maneuver as required by these rules and is therefore unable to keep out of the way of another vessel.

Vessel constrained by her draft: refers to a power-driven vessel which, because of her draft in relation to the available depth and width of navigable water, is severely restricted in her ability to deviate from the course she is following.

Support vessel: refers to small vessels providing services as required by large vessels, including logistics services, emergency rescue services, supply services, etc.

Substandard vessel: refers to a vessel whose machinery, equipment, or operational safety conditions are under the standards required by relevant international conventions.

Honorary vessel: refers to a vessel for transporting VIP guests and constantly displaying an honorary flag on the vessel.

Crew member: refers to a person serving on a vessel and registered as a crew member.

Shipmaster: refers to the crew member who commands and is in charge of the entire vessel.

Chief mate: refers to an officer in the deck department who is next in rank to the shipmaster and who will take command of the vessel if the shipmaster becomes incapacitated.

Second mate: refers to an officer in the deck department who is next in rank to the chief mate.

Third mate: refers to an officer in the deck department who is next in rank to the second mate.

Officer in charge of a navigational watch: refers to an officer in the deck department who is lowest in rank.

Able seafarer deck: refers to a skilled crew member who is qualified for the navigational watch and serves as an assistant on the vessel in daily work.

Rating forming part of a navigational watch: refers to a crew member who is qualified for the navigational watch.

Chief engineer officer: refers to an officer in the engine department who is highest in rank and is responsible for mechanical propulsion, operation, and maintenance of mechanical and electrical installations of the vessel.

Second engineer officer: refers to an officer in the engine department who is next in rank to the chief engineer officer and who will be responsible for mechanical propulsion, operation, and maintenance of mechanical and electrical installations of the vessel if the chief engineer officer becomes incapacitated.

Third engineer officer: refers to an officer in the engine department who is next in rank to the second engineer officer.

Officer in charge of an engineering watch: refers to an officer in the engine department who is lowest in rank.

Electro-technical officer: refers to an officer in the engine department who is responsible for the operation and maintenance of electrical equipment of the vessel.

Able seafarer engine: refers to a skilled crew member who is qualified for the engineering watch and serves as an assistant in the engine room in daily work.

Rating forming part of an engineering watch: refers to a crew member who is qualified for the engineering watch.

Electro-technical rating: refers to a skilled crew member who is qualified as an assistant for the operation and maintenance of electrical equipment of the vessel.

Radio operator: refers to an officer in the deck department who is qualified to operate and maintain radio equipment and systems on the vessel for communication, sending and receiving information, and sending and receiving distress signals.

Marine equipment or apparatus: refers to technical materials used on a vessel.

Master of coastal vessel: refers to a crew member who is qualified to command a coastal vessel or domestic waterway vessel and holds a certificate of competency as a coastal vessel master.

Technical characteristics: refers to the special characteristics of a person, object, or location used to identify that person, object, or location.

Navigational channel: refers to a navigation area that is narrow, has sufficient depth, and is equipped with navigation aids to ensure navigation safety.

Port operator: refers to a legal entity or physical person that provides port services to port service users, including vessel berthing, loading and unloading cargo from vessels and passengers, storing cargo, and other services related to cargo and passengers.

Ship security officer: refers to a person designated or recognized by the competent authority to act as a ship security officer.

Waterway: refers to domestic waterways, including rivers, lakes, streams, canals, and sea routes including gulfs, straits, and coastal canals, which can be used for navigation.

Navigational route: refers to a section of a waterway for navigation for various purposes in all seasons.

Port development plan: refers to a plan for constructing new ports that is linked to national economic development and public interest, or strengthening port infrastructure currently operating based on the growth of cargo and passenger traffic.

Canal: refers to an artificial navigational canal developed outside a waterway to connect navigational routes.

Damage: refers to the loss or damage to property, injury, or loss of life, which requires compensation.

Vessel characteristics: refers to specific features used to identify a vessel, including vessel identification number and/or name, length, beam, draft, vessel type, engine type, color of the vessel, etc.

Navigation light: refers to a signal light fitted on a vessel to display information on the status and direction of navigation of the vessel, including masthead light, sidelights, sternlight, towing light, all-round light, and flashing light.

Masthead light: refers to a white light placed over the fore and aft centerline of the vessel showing an unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of 225 (two hundred and twenty-five) degrees.

Sidelight: refers to a red light on the port side and a green light on the starboard side, each showing an unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of 112.5 (one hundred and twelve point five) degrees from right ahead to 22.5 (twenty-two point five) degrees abaft the beam on its respective side.

Sternlight: refers to a white light placed as nearly as practicable at the stern showing an unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of 135 (one hundred and thirty-five) degrees.

Towing light: refers to a yellow light placed as nearly as practicable at the stern showing an unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of 135 degrees.

All-round light: refers to a light showing an unbroken light over an arc of the horizon of 360 (three hundred and sixty) degrees in a horizontal plane.

Risk of collision: refers to the certainty that a collision may occur.

Booking: refers to the reservation of space for a passenger to be carried by a carrier pursuant to a contract of carriage of passengers.

Cabin luggage: refers to items that the passenger carries with them or has deposited in the luggage compartment on the vessel, or has placed in their vehicle carried on the vessel.

Shipowner: refers to a person who has ownership rights over a vessel. Shipowner includes a person who is an individual owner of a vessel or a shipping company.

Harbor master: refers to a public official appointed by the Ministry of Public Works and Transport to be in charge of ensuring and protecting the safety of port waters, managing vessel traffic, monitoring and inspecting water pollution prevention, issuing orders, and approving various activities in the port within the scope of their jurisdiction. Generally, the harbor master has the role of managing waterways in the port.

Demise charterer: refers to a physical person or legal entity that has received the rights to control and operate a vessel from the shipowner in accordance with a lease contract.

Coastal and domestic waterway vessel technical inspector: refers to a civil servant within the framework of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport and sub-national administrations.

Vessel and port inspector: refers to a civil servant within the framework of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport who is qualified to inspect coastal vessels, domestic waterway vessels, shipyards, port areas, civil engineering structures, floating structures, and other activities.

Foreign ship inspector: refers to a civil servant within the framework of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport who is qualified to inspect foreign vessels.

Competent official: refers to an official of a ministry or institution whose duty is to inspect vessels along waterways in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia in compliance with this Law and other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Clear grounds: refers to evidence showing that a vessel, marine equipment, or crew does not comply with the requirements of relevant international conventions, or that the shipmaster or crew is not familiar with essential shipboard procedures relating to the safety of the vessel and pollution prevention.

Seaplane: refers to an aircraft constructed for the purpose of taking off and landing on water.

Wing-in-ground (WIG) craft: refers to a seaplane constructed for flying at low altitude by utilizing technology that provides lift from the water surface, combined with aerodynamic reaction and other parts of the hull shaped aerodynamically.

Marine engineer: refers to a crew member responsible for managing and operating the vessel's machinery.

Letter of protest: refers to a written and official letter made for the purpose of declaring a denial of liability, showing authority concerning the relevant responsibility for any loss or damage.

Safe speed: refers to a speed that allows appropriate action to be taken to avoid collision and can stop the vessel.

Waterway transport and port services or services related to waterway transport: refers to activities or services related to vessel operation, navigation, transport, cargo and passenger loading and unloading, ports, berths, shipyards, construction and maintenance of related infrastructure, etc.

Technical certificate: refers to a certificate required for a vessel navigating internationally in compliance with international conventions related to safety, security, and the environment after the technical inspection of the vessel.

Endorsement of recognition of certificate of competency: refers to a crew's certificate of competency of one country recognized by another country under an agreement between the two countries so that the crew member can serve on a vessel of another flag state.

Radio officer: refers to an officer in the deck department who is next in rank to the radio operator and is qualified to operate and maintain radio communication equipment on the vessel.

Waterway civil engineering structure: refers to bank protection dikes, ports, breakwaters, navigation locks, navigation water depth measuring stations, central control stations, vessel traffic coordination centers, ferries, shipyards, and navigation aids.

Civil engineering structure: refers to general civil engineering structures other than waterway civil engineering structures, including bridges, electricity transmission poles, various pipelines under the seabed or riverbed, water gates, hydrological stations, etc.

Navigation aid: refers to signaling devices, systems, or services installed on a waterway and operated to enhance safety and efficiency of navigation.

General average: refers to a loss caused by the intentional sacrifice of any part of the vessel or cargo on the vessel to avoid total loss of the vessel, which shall be shared among all parties concerned in the interest of that voyage.

Day shape: refers to visual shapes displayed on a vessel to indicate information on the vessel type and activities, including balls, cones, diamond, bicone, and cylinders.

Sound signal: refers to a sound emitted by a vessel's sound signaling appliance to another vessel to indicate the intention of navigation of that vessel.

Light signal: refers to a light signal provided by one vessel to another vessel to indicate the intention of that vessel.

Distress signal: refers to signals indicating that a vessel is in distress, providing various signals specified in international laws to seek rescue. These signals include light signals, sound signals, various visual signals, and radio distress signals.

Waterway infrastructure: refers to waterways, waterway civil engineering structures, and systems related to navigation.

Nearest competent authority: refers to the unit of a ministry, institution, or communication center that receives information, or the port, or the communication station between the vessel and land-based center, which is located nearest.

General Department of Waterway Transport, Maritime Transport, and Ports: refers to the General Department of Waterway Transport, Maritime Transport, and Ports of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport.

Competent authority for ship registration: refers to the General Department of Waterway Transport, Maritime Transport, and Ports and relevant sub-national administrations.

Classification society: refers to an organization established by a group of shipowners or shipowners' association to provide technical inspection services for vessels and issue relevant technical certificates in compliance with international conventions.

Port authority: refers to a public or private entity responsible for the administration, management, and development of ports, and in some cases, operating ports and developing dry yards as well as port water areas.

Floating structure: refers to a floating installation used for various purposes, including vessel berths, storage and handling of cargo, construction or repair of vessels, cages, pens or bridges, housing, restaurants, entertainment, aquaculture, business, etc.

Passenger: refers to any person carried on a vessel other than the crew members and whose name is included in the passenger list.

Carrier: refers to a person or representative of a carrier who has concluded a contract of carriage of goods or contract of carriage of passengers, whether performed by themselves or performing that contract through a performing carrier.

Performing carrier: refers to a person other than the carrier or representative of the carrier who performs the contract of carriage of goods or contract of carriage of passengers, whether the performance of that contract has been entrusted entirely or partially by the carrier or representative of the carrier.

Stowaway: refers to a person who, at any port or water near that port, secretes themselves on a vessel without the consent of the shipowner or shipmaster or person in charge of the vessel, or any person who goes on board the vessel after the vessel has left the port or water near that port.

Good seamanship: refers to a crew member who has skills, knowledge, and experience in managing time and carrying out navigational duties meticulously in vessel operation.

Vessel operator: refers to a person who operates a coastal vessel or domestic waterway vessel by controlling the steering and machinery alone.

Privilege: refers to the right of a vessel to obtain payment/compensation from the shipowner or other operators for services or activities that the vessel has performed in the interest of the shipowner or other operators.

SDR unit of value: refers to the unit of currency value defined by the International Monetary Fund.
