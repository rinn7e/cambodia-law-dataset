### MINISTER OF JUSTICE

The Law on the Organization of the Judiciary, the Law on the Statute of Judges and Prosecutors, and the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy are types of fundamental organizational laws that are enshrined in the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia, aiming to strengthen the independence of the judiciary and ensure that judicial organizations function well in promoting the provision of justice services to citizens in accordance with the principles of the rule of law. At the same time, these three fundamental laws are also an important priority to establish a clear and solid approach in the judicial sector to promote effective implementation of legal and judicial reform work, which is part of the core of the Rectangular Strategy Phase III of the Royal Government, which was officially launched at the first plenary meeting of the Council of Ministers under the high presidency of Samdech Akka Moha Sena Padei Techo Hun Sen, Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Cambodia, on September 25, 2013.

Based on the importance described above, the Ministry of Justice has given great importance and priority to the preparation of these three fundamental draft laws quickly, by considering all aspects of the core principles to ensure the independence of the judiciary in accordance with international standards, as well as improving the judicial process in promoting the provision of justice services for the public. After many meetings, especially consultations with the Supreme Council of the Magistracy and advice from various ministries and institutions, these three draft laws were approved by the plenary meeting of the Council of Ministers on April 18, 2014, and were approved by the National Assembly on May 22-23, 2014, during the 2nd session of the National Assembly's 5th legislature, and were completely reviewed for form and legal substance by the Senate on June 12, 2014, during the 5th session of the 3rd legislature. In addition, the three fundamental laws were declared by the Constitutional Council to be in accordance with the Constitution on July 02, 2014, and were promulgated by the King by Royal Decree on July 16, 2014. On this occasion, the Ministry of Justice would like to express its deep gratitude to all relevant institutions that participated in reviewing and providing comments to further refine these three laws.

The approval and promulgation of the above three fundamental laws at the beginning of this 5th legislature of the National Assembly clearly demonstrated the high attention and firm commitment of the Royal Government in continuing its deep reform work aimed at improving judicial service delivery and increasing public trust in these public services.

Indeed, the preparation of laws, as well as the approval of laws, is an important stage in building a democratic rule of law and promoting the rule of law for the Kingdom of Cambodia. In addition, the education and dissemination of the substance of these laws are also important to ensure the effectiveness of law enforcement, as widespread dissemination of the law will help judicial officers as well as relevant officials to implement these laws correctly, in accordance with the substance and legal principles, while understanding of the law and respect for the law by citizens will also be at a higher level.

Recognizing the necessity and importance of disseminating this law, the Ministry of Justice, which serves as the staff for the Royal Government in the field of justice, has prepared the publication of these three fundamental laws for distribution to legal professionals, judicial officers, law enforcement officers, and relevant institutions.

The Ministry of Justice firmly believes that the publication and dissemination of these three laws will become an important tool for training and education, as well as contributing to the promotion of the right to access information about the law and the right to judicial services of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### PROMULGATE

The Law on the Organization of the Judiciary, which was approved by the National Assembly on May 22, 2014, during the 2nd session of the 5th legislature, and which was completely reviewed for form and legal substance by the Senate on June 12, 2014, during the 5th session of the 3rd legislature, and which was declared by the Constitutional Council to be in accordance with the Constitution in its Decision No. 149/003/2014 CC.D dated July 02, 2014, with the following full text:


# LAW

## ON

## THE ORGANIZATION OF THE JUDICIARY


# CHAPTER 1
## GENERAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 1.-
This law aims to:

- Ensure the independence of the judiciary

- Ensure the maintenance of the law and the protection of the rights and freedoms of citizens

- Ensure the smooth operation of judicial and prosecution organizations

- Enhance public services to be efficient and timely

- Ensure the provision of justice in all types of cases to increase public confidence and contribute to strengthening social safety.

### ARTICLE 2.-
This law aims to:

- Determine the organization and functioning of judicial organizations in all sectors and at all levels that are the courts of the Kingdom of Cambodia

- Determine the organization and functioning of prosecution organizations attached to the courts at all levels of the Kingdom of Cambodia

- Determine the jurisdiction of the courts in all sectors and at all levels according to expertise

- Organize management work, administrative work and the functioning process of the courts.

### ARTICLE 3.-
The courts of the Kingdom of Cambodia consist of:

- First Instance Courts

- Appeal Courts

- Supreme Court.

The First Instance Courts are lower courts. Appeal Courts and the Supreme Court are higher courts.

Based on actual necessity, the establishment of any court or specialized court shall be made by law.

### ARTICLE 4.-
The courts in all sectors and at all levels shall exercise judicial power, which covers all cases, including administrative cases.

### ARTICLE 5.-
Lower courts shall be divided into specialized courts. The creation, abolition, and modification of specialized courts shall be determined by Royal Decree.

The President of a Court of First Instance has direct administrative hierarchy over all Presidents of the specialized courts of that Court of First Instance. Each President of a specialized court has direct administrative hierarchy over the judges who are members. The President of a Court of First Instance cannot serve as a President of any specialized court. Each President of a specialized court can serve as the President of only one specialized court of that Court of First Instance.

Higher courts shall be divided into specialized chambers. The creation, abolition, and modification of specialized chambers shall be determined by Royal Decree.

The President of a higher court has direct administrative hierarchy over all Presidents of the specialized chambers of that higher court. Each President of a specialized chamber has direct administrative hierarchy over the judges who are members. The President of a higher court cannot serve as a President of any specialized chamber. Each President of a specialized chamber can serve as the President of only one specialized chamber of that higher court.

### ARTICLE 6.-
Only judges have the right to adjudicate cases. Judges shall fulfill this duty independently, strictly respecting the law and with all their heart and conscience.

Adjudication of justice shall be done in the name of the Khmer people according to the procedures and laws in force.

Only courts have the power to judge and issue court decisions.

### ARTICLE 7.-
Hearings shall be held in public, unless such publicity could pose a danger to public order or good customs or if there are special provisions in the law stating otherwise.

In all cases, judgments and court decisions shall be pronounced in open court.

Deliberations of the trial panel for decision-making shall be kept secret.

Unless otherwise provided, the pronouncement of a judgment or a court decision shall be made by the President of the trial panel or by one of the judges who is a member of the trial panel.

All judgments and court decisions must be reasoned.

Unless otherwise provided, judgments and court decisions shall be signed by the President of the trial panel and countersigned by the court clerk of the hearing.

### ARTICLE 8.-
At each Court of First Instance, there shall be a prosecution office called the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance.

At each Appeal Court, there shall be a prosecution office called the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court.

At the Supreme Court, there shall be a prosecution office called the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court.

Representatives of the prosecution office must be present at all hearings of criminal cases and other cases as determined by law.

### ARTICLE 9.-
At all judicial and prosecution organizations at all levels, there shall be court clerks and administrative officials, and there may be legal experts as assistants as necessary.

Court clerks assist judges and prosecutors at all stages of judicial and prosecution procedures.

The roles and functions of court clerks shall be determined in the Statute of Court Clerks.

The procedures and conditions for the recruitment of legal experts shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### ARTICLE 10.-
At each court of all levels, there shall be an administrative unit under the central administration of the Ministry of Justice to support the functioning of judicial and prosecution organizations.

At the Court of First Instance level, this administrative unit is called the Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance and has a rank equivalent to a Department.

At the Appeal Court level, this administrative unit is called the General Secretariat of Administration of the Appeal Court and has a rank equivalent to a General Department.

At the Supreme Court level, this administrative unit is called the General Secretariat of Administration of the Supreme Court and has a rank equivalent to a General Department.

### ARTICLE 11.-
The Ministry of Justice has the right to monitor the administrative affairs of all courts, with a General Department of Judicial Administrative Affairs functioning as staff. The Ministry of Justice may issue legal instruments and necessary instructions to ensure the proper functioning of judicial administration at all levels.

In case of necessity, the Minister of Justice may order an inspection of any specific issue and then report to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and decision.

The higher courts have the jurisdiction to monitor the judicial work of the lower courts. This monitoring is carried out within the framework of the appeal procedure, in accordance with the provisions of the procedures in force.

# CHAPTER 2
## COURTS OF FIRST INSTANCE AND PROSECUTION OFFICES ATTACHED TO COURTS OF FIRST INSTANCE
### SECTION 1: COURTS OF FIRST INSTANCE
#### SUB-SECTION 1: ORGANIZATION AND FUNCTIONING OF THE COURTS OF FIRST INSTANCE

### ARTICLE 12.-
Courts of First Instance are the first level courts, which shall be established in Phnom Penh and in all provinces of the Kingdom of Cambodia. The putting into operation of the Courts of First Instance shall be made by Royal Decree.

Courts of First Instance shall be divided into specialized courts. The Court of First Instance is a court of general jurisdiction that has the competence to adjudicate all cases, except for cases that fall under the jurisdiction of specialized courts or extraordinary courts established by separate laws.

### ARTICLE 13.-
The composition of a Court of First Instance includes:

- President

- Vice-President

- Judges

- Court Clerks

- Administrative officials and may include legal experts who serve at the Court of First Instance.

### ARTICLE 14.-
Each Court of First Instance is divided into specialized courts as follows:

- Civil Court

- Criminal Court

- Commercial Court

- Labor Court.

Other specialized courts of the Court of First Instance may be established according to necessity. This establishment shall be made by Royal Decree.

Each specialized court of the Court of First Instance shall issue its decisions with autonomy within its jurisdiction, in the name of the Court of First Instance where that specialized court is located.

### ARTICLE 15.-
The composition of each specialized court includes:

- President of the specialized court

- Judges

- Court Clerks.

The President of each specialized court, as well as the judges and court clerks, shall be assigned by the President of the Court of First Instance. The operation of these specialized courts shall be determined by the President of the Court of First Instance in accordance with legal instruments in force.

### ARTICLE 16.-
Each specialized court of the Court of First Instance shall decide cases with a single judge or a trial panel, in accordance with the provisions of civil procedure, criminal procedure, or other procedural provisions in force. In case any judge is busy or in a situation in which they are unable to fulfill their duties, that judge may be replaced by a judge in the next order as determined in the annual decision of the President of the Court of First Instance.

In case any specialized court cannot hold a hearing due to a shortage of judges, the President of the Court of First Instance shall assign judges from other specialized courts to fill in. If it is necessary to request one or more judges from other Courts of First Instance to complete the composition of this specialized court, the President of the Court of First Instance shall request the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 17.-
The President of the Court of First Instance shall take necessary management measures for the operation of the Court of First Instance. In case the President of the Court of First Instance is unable to fulfill their duties or in case the position is vacant, the Minister of Justice shall appoint a Vice-President of the Court of First Instance or, if there is no Vice-President, may appoint any judge to serve as Acting President to ensure the operation of the Court of First Instance until an official President of the Court of First Instance is appointed. The appointment of the President of the Court of First Instance shall be made as soon as possible and at the latest no more than 30 (thirty) days.

The President of the Court of First Instance shall issue decisions to:

- Appoint any Vice-President of the Court of First Instance to represent them in case of necessity;

- Appoint a Vice-President of the Court of First Instance to be in charge as President of each specialized court based on expertise or work experience. In case there is a lack of Vice-Presidents, the President of the Court of First Instance may appoint any judge with a high rank or considerable experience to serve as President of a specialized court;

- Determine the order for replacing judges in each specialized court and in the trial panels of these specialized courts in case any judge is absent or busy;

- Appoint and change court clerks in each specialized court according to actual necessity.

### ARTICLE 18.-
In case of necessity, the Minister of Justice may decide to allow the Court of First Instance to hold hearings outside its headquarters upon the request of the President of the Court of First Instance.

#### SUB-SECTION 2: JURISDICTION

### ARTICLE 19.-
Courts of First Instance have jurisdiction to adjudicate cases that fall within their territorial jurisdiction, except for cases assigned by law to other courts.

In case of necessity, in order to ensure public interest, safety, social security and the smooth operation of court justice, the Minister of Justice may decide to transfer jurisdiction for handling a case from one Court of First Instance to another.

In case of jurisdictional disputes between different specialized courts within a Court of First Instance, the President of the Court of First Instance shall convene a meeting of the relevant presidents of specialized courts, either by exercising their own right or upon the request of any president of a specialized court, to decide on the jurisdiction. The President of the Court of First Instance shall decide on this jurisdictional dispute by court order. This order is a final decision without appeal.

### ARTICLE 20.-
The Criminal Court of the Court of First Instance adjudicates all criminal cases within its jurisdiction, by applying the provisions of criminal procedure, unless otherwise provided by law.

The Criminal Court of the Court of First Instance adjudicates criminal cases by a single judge or a trial panel, in accordance with the provisions of criminal procedure.

The Investigation Chamber of the Court of First Instance is a part of the Criminal Court and has jurisdiction to investigate criminal cases by applying the provisions of criminal procedure. The President of the Court of First Instance shall appoint at least 02 (two) judges by a court order to fulfill the role of investigating judges at the Investigation Chamber of the Criminal Court of the Court of First Instance. This order shall also determine the order of work assignments. In case of busy investigating judges or a shortage of investigating judges, the President of the Court of First Instance may appoint judges from other specialized courts to fulfill investigation duties.

In case of necessity, an investigating judge may perform their duties as a judge in other specialized courts according to the assignment of the President of the Court of First Instance.

### ARTICLE 21.-
The Civil Court of the Court of First Instance has jurisdiction to adjudicate civil cases and all...

...which are not assigned by any other provision of law to other specialized courts, by applying the provisions of civil procedure.

The Civil Court of the Court of First Instance adjudicates civil cases with a single judge or a trial panel, by applying the provisions of civil procedure.

### ARTICLE 22.-
The Commercial Court of the Court of First Instance has jurisdiction to adjudicate commercial cases, including commercial bankruptcy cases, by applying the provisions of commercial procedure.

### ARTICLE 23.-
The Commercial Court adjudicates with 03 (three) judges and accompanied by 02 (two) advisors who are merchants or persons knowledgeable in commercial law, in commercial cases where the value of the subject of the claim is 1,000,000,000 (one billion) Riels or more.

The Commercial Court adjudicates with 01 (one) judge and accompanied by 02 (two) advisors who are merchants or persons knowledgeable in commercial law, in commercial cases where the value of the subject of the claim is from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels to less than 1,000,000,000 (one billion) Riels.

The Commercial Court adjudicates with 01 (one) judge without the participation of commercial advisors in commercial cases where the value of the subject of the claim is less than 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels.

The judgment of the Commercial Court shall be decided by 01 (one) judge or 03 (three) judges in accordance with the first and second paragraphs above, after consultation with the commercial advisors.

The commercial advisors do not fulfill their roles on a permanent basis in the Commercial Court of the Court of First Instance. Commercial advisors shall come to perform their duties at the Court of First Instance upon the invitation of the President of the Commercial Court of the Court of First Instance.

### ARTICLE 24.-
The procedures and conditions for the appointment and performance of the duties of commercial advisors shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with the Minister of Commerce.

### ARTICLE 25.-
The Labor Court of the Court of First Instance has jurisdiction to adjudicate labor cases, by applying the provisions of labor procedure.

### ARTICLE 26.-
The Labor Court of the Court of First Instance adjudicates cases with 01 (one) judge and accompanied by 02 (two) labor advisors, one of whom is an employee and one of whom is an employer.

The judgment of the Labor Court of the Court of First Instance shall be decided by 01 (one) judge after consultation with the labor advisors.

Labor advisors do not fulfill their roles on a permanent basis in the Labor Court of the Court of First Instance. Labor advisors shall participate in performing their duties at the Court of First Instance upon the invitation of the President of the Labor Court.

### ARTICLE 27.-
The Labor Court with jurisdiction is the Labor Court of the Court of First Instance at the place where the labor dispute occurs. Notwithstanding the determination of jurisdiction as mentioned above, an employee may file a lawsuit with the Court of First Instance as determined below, unless otherwise provided by law:

- At their place of residence; or

- At the location of the headquarters or principal place of business of the company; or

- At the place of residence of the representative or person in charge of the company, if there is no headquarters or principal place of business.

### ARTICLE 28.-
The procedures for the election and performance of duties of labor advisors shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with the Minister in charge of the labor sector.

## SECTION 2: PROSECUTION OFFICES ATTACHED TO COURTS OF FIRST INSTANCE

### ARTICLE 29.-
The composition of a Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance includes:

- Prosecutor

- Deputy Prosecutors

- Court Clerks

- Administrative officials and may include legal experts who serve at the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance.

### ARTICLE 30.-
The prosecution organization is an indivisible body. The prosecution has the duty to conduct public prosecution and request the application of the law before the investigation jurisdiction and trial jurisdiction, as well as perform other duties as determined by law and provisions in force.

The Prosecutor and deputy prosecutors are representatives of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance where they serve. Deputy prosecutors participate in performing the duties of the prosecution under the leadership and responsibility of the Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance.

In case of being busy, ill or absent, the Prosecutor shall assign a deputy prosecutor to substitute. In case the Prosecutor is unable to fulfill their duties or the position is vacant, the Minister of Justice shall appoint a deputy prosecutor to serve as Acting Prosecutor to ensure the operation of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance until an official Prosecutor is appointed.

### ARTICLE 31.-
The Prosecutor has authority over all deputy prosecutors within their jurisdiction. The Prosecutor assigns and distributes work to all deputy prosecutors.

The Prosecutor has the right to issue orders to deputy prosecutors to prosecute or to have prosecution conducted or to make whatever conclusions they see fit. The conclusions of the deputy prosecutor must be submitted to the Prosecutor for review before the hearing. If the Prosecutor does not agree with the conclusions and the representative of the prosecution who made the conclusions still refuses to follow the Prosecutor's instructions, the Prosecutor may assign another deputy prosecutor or assign themselves to represent the prosecution at the hearing of the Court of First Instance.

However, during the hearing, the deputy prosecutor may freely make oral observations as they see fit according to their conscience. No disciplinary action may be taken against a representative of the prosecution due to their oral observations during the hearing being different from written conclusions.

# SECTION 3
## SECRETARIAT OF ADMINISTRATION OF THE COURT OF FIRST INSTANCE

### ARTICLE 32.-
The Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance functions as staff for the Court of First Instance and the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance in:

- Personnel, administrative and financial work;

- Judicial administrative management work to provide technical support to the work programs of the Court of First Instance and the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance;

- Preparing budget proposals for the President of the Court and the Prosecutor for review and decision;

- Reporting on personnel, administrative and financial work regularly to the President of the Court and the Prosecutor;

- Implementing budget work according to the approved proposal;

- Public relations work;

- Sending first instance judgments of the Court of First Instance to the Ministry of Justice regularly;

- Preparing and sending regular monthly, quarterly, semi-annual and annual reports on personnel, administrative and financial work to the Ministry of Justice;

- Other tasks assigned by the President of the Court of First Instance and the Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance;

- Implementing other tasks as determined by legal instruments.

The Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance shall be divided into offices according to actual necessity. Each office shall be led by a chief and a number of deputy chiefs as necessary.

The organization and functioning of the Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 33.-
The Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance is led by a chief and a number of deputy chiefs as necessary. The Chief of the Secretariat shall be appointed by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with the President of the Court of First Instance and the Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance. Officials serving at the Secretariat level below the Chief shall be appointed by Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

The Chief and Deputy Chiefs of the Secretariat shall be selected from civil servants who have at least 05 (five) years of experience in legal, administrative or financial fields. The chiefs and deputy chiefs of offices shall be selected from civil servants who have at least 03 (three) years of experience in legal, administrative or financial fields.

All officials serving at the Secretariat, including the Chief of the Secretariat, shall belong to the framework of the Ministry of Justice.

### ARTICLE 34.-
The assembly of the Court of First Instance shall be held under the chairmanship of the President of the Court of First Instance and the Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance to summarize past work results and determine the direction of administrative work of the Court of First Instance for the following year. The assembly report of the Court of First Instance shall be submitted to the Ministry of Justice.

# CHAPTER 3
## APPEAL COURTS AND GENERAL PROSECUTION OFFICES ATTACHED TO APPEAL COURTS
### SECTION 1: APPEAL COURTS
#### SUB-SECTION 1: ORGANIZATION AND FUNCTIONING OF APPEAL COURTS

### ARTICLE 35.-
Appeal Courts are the second level courts.

Appeal Courts include the Phnom Penh Appeal Court and Regional Appeal Courts. The putting into operation of each Regional Appeal Court and the determination of the territorial jurisdiction of these Regional Appeal Courts shall be made by Royal Decree.

### ARTICLE 36.-
The composition of an Appeal Court includes:

- President

- Administrative officials and may include legal experts who serve at the Appeal Court.

### ARTICLE 37.-
An Appeal Court has specialized chambers as follows:

- Criminal Chamber;

- Civil Chamber;

- Investigation Chamber;

- Commercial Chamber;

- Labor Chamber.

When necessary, other specialized chambers of the Appeal Court may be established by Royal Decree.

Each specialized chamber shall issue its decisions with autonomy within its jurisdiction, in the name of the Appeal Court where the specialized chamber is located.

### ARTICLE 38.-
The composition of each specialized chamber includes:

- President of the chamber;

- Judges; and

- Court Clerk.

The President of each specialized chamber shall be assigned by the President of the Appeal Court.

The number of judges, court clerks and the operation of these chambers shall be determined by the President of the Appeal Court.

### ARTICLE 39.-
Each specialized chamber of the Appeal Court shall decide cases with 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President, in accordance with the procedural provisions in force.

Any case remanded by the Supreme Court shall be adjudicated by the Appeal Court in a Plenary Chamber consisting of 05 (five) judges, all of whom must be judges who did not participate in the previous adjudication, by applying the procedural provisions in force.

### ARTICLE 40.-
The President of the Appeal Court shall take necessary management measures for the operation of the Appeal Court. The President of the Appeal Court shall issue decisions to:

- Appoint any Vice-President of the Appeal Court to represent them in case of necessity;

- Appoint a Vice-President of the Appeal Court to be in charge as President of each specialized chamber based on expertise or work experience. In case there is a lack of Vice-Presidents, the President of the Appeal Court may appoint any judge with a high rank or considerable experience to serve as President of a chamber;

- Determine the order for replacing judges in the trial panels of each specialized chamber in case any judge is absent or busy;

- Appoint and change court clerks in each specialized chamber according to actual necessity.

#### SUB-SECTION 2: JURISDICTION

### ARTICLE 41.-
The Appeal Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate cases that fall within its territorial jurisdiction, except for cases assigned by law to other courts.

### ARTICLE 42.-
The Appeal Court shall decide on both facts and the law for cases within its jurisdiction, in accordance with the laws and provisions in force.

### ARTICLE 43.-
The Criminal Chamber has jurisdiction to adjudicate appeals against criminal judgments of the Court of First Instance and other cases within its jurisdiction, by applying the provisions of criminal procedure in force.

### ARTICLE 44.-
The Civil Chamber has jurisdiction to adjudicate appeals against judgments and appeals against court orders of the Court of First Instance in civil cases and other cases within its jurisdiction...

...their jurisdiction, by applying the procedural provisions in force. Court decisions shall be made by 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President.

### ARTICLE 45.-
The Investigation Chamber has jurisdiction to resolve appeals against acts of investigation or various court orders of the investigating judge and other cases within its jurisdiction, by applying the provisions of criminal procedure in force. Court decisions of the Investigation Chamber shall be made by 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President.

### ARTICLE 46.-
The Commercial Chamber has jurisdiction to adjudicate appeals against judgments and appeals against court orders of the Court of First Instance in commercial cases and other cases within its jurisdiction, by applying the provisions of commercial procedure. The Commercial Chamber of the Appeal Court shall adjudicate cases with 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President, and accompanied by 02 (two) advisors who are merchants or persons knowledgeable in commercial law, and who were not commercial advisors in the same case adjudicated by the Court of First Instance and the Appeal Court. Court decisions of the Commercial Chamber of the Appeal Court shall be made by 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President, after consultation with the commercial advisors.

The commercial advisors do not fulfill their roles on a permanent basis in the Commercial Chamber of the Appeal Court. Commercial advisors shall come to perform their duties only upon the invitation of the President of the Commercial Chamber of the Appeal Court.

The procedures for the appointment and performance of the duties of commercial advisors shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with the Minister of Commerce.

### ARTICLE 47.-
The Labor Chamber of the Appeal Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate appeals against judgments and appeals against court orders of the Court of First Instance in labor cases and other cases within its jurisdiction, by applying the provisions of labor procedure. The Labor Chamber of the Appeal Court shall adjudicate cases with 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President, and accompanied by 02 (two) labor advisors, one of whom is an employee and one of whom is an employer, and who were not labor advisors in the same case adjudicated by the Court of First Instance and...

...Appeal Court. Court decisions of the Labor Chamber of the Appeal Court shall be made by 03 (three) judges, one of whom is the President, after consultation with the labor advisors.

Labor advisors do not fulfill their duties on a permanent basis in the Labor Chamber of the Appeal Court. Labor advisors shall participate in performing their duties only upon the invitation of the President of the Labor Chamber of the Appeal Court.

The procedures for the appointment and performance of the duties of labor advisors shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with the Minister in charge of the labor sector.

### ARTICLE 48.-
Mixed Chambers may be put into operation by a decision of the President of the Appeal Court on their own initiative or upon the request of any specialized chamber President, when a case involves issues falling within the jurisdiction of multiple specialized chambers or if such issues have received conflicting resolutions before different specialized chambers of the same Appeal Court. The Mixed Chambers shall be convened by the President of the Appeal Court.

The decision of the Mixed Chambers on jurisdiction between different specialized chambers and on legal disputes shall be binding on all specialized chambers of the Appeal Court.

When the Mixed Chambers are established, this chamber shall be composed of at least 05 (five) judges, divided equally between the judges representing the relevant specialized chambers. The Mixed Chambers shall be under the chairmanship of the President of the Appeal Court.

The President of the Appeal Court shall issue a decision in accordance with the decision of the Mixed Chambers. The decision on jurisdictional disputes and legal disputes is final and not subject to appeal.

## SECTION 2: GENERAL PROSECUTION OFFICES ATTACHED TO APPEAL COURTS

### ARTICLE 49.-
Attached to each Appeal Court, there shall be a General Prosecution Office consisting of:

- Prosecutor General

- Deputy Prosecutor General

- Prosecutors

- Court Clerks; and

- Administrative officials and may include legal experts who serve at the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court.

### ARTICLE 50.-
The duties of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court are assigned to the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court. Deputy Prosecutors General and prosecutors attached to the Appeal Court participate in performing these duties under the leadership and responsibility of the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court.

In case of being busy, ill or absent, the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court shall assign a Deputy Prosecutor General or, if there is no Deputy Prosecutor General, may assign any prosecutor to represent them.

In case the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court is unable to fulfill their duties or the position is vacant, the Minister of Justice shall appoint a Deputy Prosecutor General to serve as Acting Prosecutor General to ensure the operation of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court until an official Prosecutor General is appointed.

### ARTICLE 51.-
The Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court has administrative authority over all Deputy Prosecutors General and prosecutors attached to the Appeal Court.

The Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court assigns and distributes activities to Deputy Prosecutors General and prosecutors attached to the Appeal Court within the various tasks of the General Prosecution Office and trial panels.

The conclusions of a Deputy Prosecutor General or a prosecutor attached to the Appeal Court must be submitted to the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court for review before the hearing. If the Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court does not agree with the conclusions and the representative of the general prosecution who made the conclusions still refuses to follow the instructions, the Prosecutor General may assign another Deputy Prosecutor General or prosecutor or assign themselves as representative of the general prosecution at the hearing.

However, during the hearing, the Deputy Prosecutor General or prosecutor attached to the Appeal Court may freely make oral observations as they see fit according to their conscience. No disciplinary action may be taken against a representative of the general prosecution due to their oral observations during the hearing being different from written conclusions.

The Prosecutor General Attached to the Appeal Court has the right to issue orders to the Prosecutor Attached to the Court of First Instance to conduct public prosecution, in accordance with the provisions of criminal procedure in force.

# CHAPTER 4
## THE SUPREME COURT AND THE GENERAL PROSECUTION OFFICE ATTACHED TO THE SUPREME COURT
### SECTION 1: THE SUPREME COURT
#### SUB-SECTION 1: ORGANIZATION AND FUNCTIONING OF THE SUPREME COURT

### ARTICLE 55.-
There is only one Supreme Court, which is located in Phnom Penh.

### ARTICLE 56.-
The composition of the Supreme Court includes:

- President;

- Vice-Presidents;

- Judges;

- Court Clerks; and

- Administrative officials and may include legal experts who serve at the Supreme Court.

### ARTICLE 57.-
The Supreme Court has specialized chambers as follows:

- Criminal Chamber;

- Civil Chamber;

- Commercial Chamber;

- Labor Chamber.

When necessary, other specialized chambers of the Supreme Court may be established by Royal Decree.

Each specialized chamber issues its decisions with autonomy in the name of the Supreme Court.

### ARTICLE 58.-
The composition of each specialized chamber includes:

- President of the chamber;

- Judges; and

- Court Clerk.

The President of each specialized chamber shall be assigned by the President of the Supreme Court. The number of judges, court clerks and the operation of these chambers shall be determined by the President of the Supreme Court.

### ARTICLE 59.-
Each specialized chamber of the Supreme Court shall decide cases with 05 (five) judges, one of whom is the President, in accordance with the procedural provisions in force.

### ARTICLE 60.-
The President of the Supreme Court shall take necessary management measures for the operation of the Supreme Court and guide all chambers of the Supreme Court to implement the law.

The President of the Supreme Court shall issue decisions to:

- Appoint any Vice-President of the Supreme Court to represent them in case of necessity;

- Appoint a Vice-President to be in charge as President of each specialized chamber based on expertise and work experience. In case of lack of Vice-Presidents, the President of the Supreme Court may appoint any judge to serve as President of a chamber.

#### SUB-SECTION 2: JURISDICTION

### ARTICLE 61.-
The Supreme Court has jurisdiction to resolve cases that fall within its jurisdiction as determined by law.

### ARTICLE 62.-
A Plenary Chamber shall be put into operation by a decision of the President of the Supreme Court to adjudicate, among others:

- A second cassation appeal against a court decision of the Appeal Court, in accordance with the procedural provisions in force;

- A motion for revision against final judgments and court decisions, in accordance with the procedural provisions in force.

The Plenary Chamber may also be put into operation to adjudicate motions for revision or motions for re-adjudication in case there is a request for review and re-decision on any case from the King, who is the Chairperson of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, through the Minister of Justice.

The Plenary Chamber shall be convened by the President of the Supreme Court.

The Plenary Chamber of the Supreme Court is the trial body that issues decisions in the name of the Supreme Court.

When the Plenary Chamber is put into operation, this chamber shall be composed of at least 09 (nine) judges representing all chambers of the Supreme Court.

The Plenary Chamber is under the chairmanship of the President of the Supreme Court.

### ARTICLE 63.-
Mixed Chambers may be put into operation by a decision of the President of the Supreme Court on their own initiative or upon the request of any specialized chamber President, when a case involves issues falling within the jurisdiction of multiple specialized chambers or if such issues have received conflicting resolutions before different specialized chambers of the Supreme Court. Mixed Chambers shall be convened by the President of the Supreme Court.

The decision of the Mixed Chambers on jurisdiction between different specialized chambers and on legal disputes shall be binding on all specialized chambers of the Supreme Court and lower courts.

When the Mixed Chambers are established, this chamber shall be composed of at least 09 (nine) judges, divided equally between the judges representing the relevant specialized chambers. Mixed Chambers shall be under the chairmanship of the President of the Supreme Court.

The President of the Supreme Court shall issue a decision in accordance with the decision of the Mixed Chambers. The decision on jurisdictional disputes and legal disputes is final and not subject to appeal.

### ARTICLE 64.-
The Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate cassation appeals against criminal court decisions of the Appeal Court, by applying the provisions of criminal procedure in force.

### ARTICLE 65.-
The Civil Chamber of the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate cassation appeals against court decisions and appeals against court orders in civil cases of the Appeal Court, as well as cassation appeals against civil judgments of the Court of First Instance and other cases, by applying the provisions of civil procedure in force.

### ARTICLE 66.-
The Commercial Chamber of the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate cassation appeals against court decisions and appeals against court orders in commercial cases of the Appeal Court, by applying the provisions of commercial procedure.

### ARTICLE 67.-
The Labor Chamber of the Supreme Court has jurisdiction to adjudicate cassation appeals against court decisions and appeals against court orders in labor cases of the Appeal Court, by applying the provisions of labor procedure.

If the Prosecutor General Attached to the Supreme Court does not agree with the conclusions and the representative of the general prosecution who made the conclusions still refuses to follow the instructions, the Prosecutor General may assign another Deputy Prosecutor General or prosecutor or assign themselves as representative of the general prosecution at the hearing.

# CHAPTER 5: INCOMPATIBILITY

### ARTICLE 74.-
The functions of public prosecution, investigation and adjudication shall be separated. Any judge who has been in charge of a case as a representative of the prosecution or as an investigating judge cannot adjudicate or participate in the adjudication of the same case. Otherwise, the judgment or court decision shall be void.

### ARTICLE 75.-
A judge cannot participate in the adjudication of a case with one or more judges who are their spouse or relatives by blood up to the 4th (fourth) degree inclusive, or relatives by marriage up to the 3rd (third) degree inclusive, whether as a trial judge or a representative of the prosecution. This principle shall also apply to judges at a higher court regarding cases adjudicated by judges at a lower court who are their spouse or relatives by blood up to the 4th (fourth) degree inclusive, or relatives by marriage up to the 3rd (third) degree inclusive.

### ARTICLE 76.-
Judges whose spouse or relatives by blood up to the 4th (fourth) degree inclusive, or relatives by marriage up to the 3rd (third) degree inclusive, serve as a lawyer for any party in a case, cannot participate in the adjudication of that case.

### ARTICLE 77.-
A judge cannot participate in the adjudication of any case where one party is their spouse or relative by blood up to the 6th (sixth) degree inclusive, or relative by marriage up to the 3rd (third) degree inclusive.

### ARTICLE 78.-
Unless there are specific procedural provisions stating otherwise, any party to a case or any person who has an interest in the case, including the representative of the prosecution, may request for the nullification of a judgment or court decision on the grounds of non-compliance with the incompatibility rules as provided in Articles 74, 75, 76 and 77 above.

This request for nullification may be made at any time before the court during the appellate procedure by applying the procedural provisions in force. Even if the judgment or court decision has become final, a request for nullification may still be made within 60 (sixty) days from the date of discovery of the grounds as provided in Articles 74, 75, 76 and 77 above after the judgment or court decision has become final. In this latter case, the request for nullification must be filed with the court that issued the final judgment or court decision, but it must be adjudicated by a different panel of trial judges to review and decide on the nullification of that final judgment or court decision, and to continue the following procedures in accordance with the provisions in force.

# CHAPTER 6: BUDGET OF THE COURTS AND PROSECUTION OFFICES ATTACHED TO THE COURTS

### ARTICLE 79.-
Courts of First Instance and prosecution offices attached to Courts of First Instance, Appeal Courts and general prosecution offices attached to Appeal Courts, as well as the Supreme Court and the general prosecution office attached to the Supreme Court, have separate budget packages for their operation within the budget package of the Ministry of Justice.

The President of the court is the sub-authorizing officer delegated by the Minister of Justice.

The management and administration of the budget of the courts and prosecution offices attached to the courts as determined in the first paragraph above shall be determined by Sub-decree.

# CHAPTER 7: TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 81.-

### ARTICLE 82.-

### ARTICLE 83.-

### ARTICLE 84.-
In case a Court of First Instance in any province is not yet operational or cannot be put into operation, the jurisdiction to adjudicate cases in that province shall be assigned to any Court of First Instance by Royal Decree.

### ARTICLE 85.-
When the Commercial Chamber and Labor Chamber of a higher court cannot operate due to a lack of judges, the adjudication of commercial and labor cases shall be the jurisdiction of the Civil Chamber of that higher court. These matters shall be managed by the President of the higher court where the specialized chamber is located.

When the aforementioned specialized chambers are operational in accordance with this law, other specialized chambers shall transfer cases that do not fall within their jurisdiction and have not yet been processed back to the specialized chambers that have jurisdiction.

### ARTICLE 86.-
In case there are not enough judges, an investigating judge may fulfill the duties of a trial judge, but cannot adjudicate a case that they have investigated.

### ARTICLE 87.-
In case an Administrative Court is not yet operational, the jurisdiction to adjudicate administrative cases shall be assigned to the civil courts of the lower courts and the civil chambers of the higher courts.

### ARTICLE 88.-
When commercial advisors or labor advisors are not yet available, the composition for adjudicating commercial cases and labor cases shall follow the provisions of civil procedure.

### ARTICLE 89.-
When there are no provisions on commercial procedure yet, the adjudication of commercial cases shall follow the provisions of civil procedure and other legal provisions in force.

When there are no provisions on labor procedure yet, the adjudication of labor cases shall follow the provisions of civil procedure and other legal provisions in force.

When there are no provisions on administrative procedure yet, the adjudication of administrative cases shall follow the provisions of civil procedure and other legal provisions in force.

### ARTICLE 90.-
During the first 05 (five) years after this law enters into force, the appointment of leadership for the administrative units of all levels of courts may be implemented based on actual qualifications.

A chief clerk of a Court of First Instance may be appointed as director or deputy director of the Secretariat of Administration of the Court of First Instance where they are currently serving.

A chief clerk of a higher court may be appointed as Secretary-General or Deputy Secretary-General of the General Secretariat of Administration of the higher court where they are currently serving.

The Law on the Statute of Judges and Prosecutors, which was adopted by the National Assembly on 23 May 2014 during the 2nd session of its 5th legislature, and which was reviewed and approved entirely by the Senate on 12 June 2014 during the 4th session of its 3rd legislature, and which was declared constitutional by the Constitutional Council in its decision No. 147/001/2014 KBTh.Ch dated 06 July 2014, the full text of which is as follows:

# LAW ON THE STATUTE OF JUDGES AND PROSECUTORS

# CHAPTER 1
## GENERAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 1.-
This law determines the statute of judges and prosecutors as well as other principles related to judges and prosecutors for the purpose of ensuring the independence of the judiciary in accordance with the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### ARTICLE 2.-
This law has scope of application over judges and prosecutors throughout the Kingdom of Cambodia, unless there is a specific law providing otherwise.

### ARTICLE 3.-
The technical terms in this law have the following meanings:

- "Judge" refers to judges of the bench, including trial judges and investigating judges;

- "Prosecutor" refers to deputy prosecutors, prosecutors, deputy prosecutors general and prosecutors general;

- "Magistrate" refers to judges and prosecutors.

### ARTICLE 4.-
The person to be selected for fulfilling duties as a judge or a prosecutor must be competent, honest and of good moral character.

### ARTICLE 5.-
In their career, a judge may be appointed as a prosecutor and a prosecutor may be appointed as a judge.

### ARTICLE 6.-
Judges and prosecutors shall be guaranteed protection for their personal and family security in case of physical assault or threats against them.

Judges and prosecutors shall not be held liable for any criminal or civil offense regarding decisions made within the framework of their jurisdiction and duties as judges or prosecutors. This principle shall not apply in case the judge or prosecutor uses their decision to commit a serious abuse of their jurisdiction and duties assigned by law with malicious intent.

### ARTICLE 7.-
All judges and prosecutors belong to the central administrative framework of the Ministry of Justice and may be assigned to work at the Ministry of Justice. In case they are assigned to work at the Ministry of Justice, a judge must request consent from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

During the period of working at the Ministry of Justice, judges and prosecutors shall be under the statute of prosecutors and shall receive salary and functional allowances like prosecutors serving in prosecution offices attached to the courts. In case the salary and functional allowances are different, the person has the right to choose the higher salary or functional allowance.

# CHAPTER 2
## JUDGES
### SECTION 1
#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 8.-
Judges shall decide cases impartially, based on the principle of respect for the law, without receiving any pressure, threat or intimidation, direct or indirect order from any party in a case or from any person.

### ARTICLE 9.-
A judge shall be assigned to work at any court for a period of 04 (four) years.

A judge cannot be transferred to any other place without the consent of the person...

...concerned during the period of duty as provided in the first paragraph above, even if that place has a higher hierarchy. However, the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may transfer a judge to work at any other place in case the judge has been subject to a second-degree disciplinary sanction as provided in Article 55 of this law, or according to the necessary requirements for the good functioning of justice.

### SECTION 2: HIERARCHY AND CLASSES

### ARTICLE 10.-
The framework for judges consists of 03 (three) hierarchical ranks:

- Supreme Judge;

- Superior Judge;

- Junior Judge.

### ARTICLE 11.-
The classes for the hierarchical ranks of Supreme Judge, Superior Judge and Junior Judge shall be determined by Royal Decree.

### SECTION 3: UNIFORM

### ARTICLE 12.-
The uniforms of a judge include:

- The uniform for hearings and the uniform for fulfilling other duties besides hearings;

- The official uniform for various national ceremonies.

These uniforms shall be determined by Royal Decree upon the proposal of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 13.-
The uniform that all judges must wear during hearings shall be a robe.

The uniform for fulfilling other duties besides hearings shall be clothing or other insignia. The use of such clothing or insignia shall be determined by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The uniform that all judges must wear during parades or other national ceremonies where official requirements apply shall be the official uniform.

### SECTION 4: SALARY, VARIOUS ALLOWANCES, BONUSES AND LEAVE

### ARTICLE 14.-
A judge shall receive a salary based on their hierarchical rank and class, functional allowance and other allowances, as well as other bonuses in accordance with the provisions in force.

### ARTICLE 15.-
The salary, functional allowance and other allowances, such as mission allowance, housing allowance, special indemnity, relocation allowance, hospital treatment allowance and support allowance for the treatment of long-term illness of the judge, shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### ARTICLE 16.-
A judge shall be entitled to an annual leave of 30 (thirty) days in addition to the public holidays determined by the Royal Government. The working period resulting in the right to this annual leave shall be calculated starting from January 1.

A judge may request annual leave several times or all at once in a year. This leave may be extended until the end of February of the following year.

The request for annual leave must be submitted to the President of the court at least 03 (three) months before the leave.

Female judges have the right to maternity leave of 90 (ninety) days.

During the period of leave mentioned above, a judge shall receive salary, functional allowance and other allowances.

### ARTICLE 17.-
A judge who has completed 02 (two) years of service without interruption, or with interruption but has completed a total of 02 (two) years of service, may be granted leave for medical treatment for a period of up to 06 (six) months without deduction of salary.

If the leave for medical treatment continues for more than 06 (six) months up to 01 (one) year, the salary shall be reduced to half. If the leave for medical treatment must be further extended beyond 01 (one) year, that judge shall request leave without pay in accordance with the provisions of Article 45 of this law. In case the judge does not request leave without pay, the judge shall be retired on the grounds of physical disability in accordance with the provisions of Article 66 of this law.

A judge who is ill may request sick leave from the Ministry of Justice and must provide a medical certificate as evidence. The Ministry of Justice may have competent specialized doctors perform an examination at any time if deemed necessary.

### ARTICLE 18.-
For a judge who has not taken annual leave, the period of untaken leave may be added to the period of sick leave mentioned above without deduction of salary.

### SECTION 5: SELECTION OF JUDGES

### ARTICLE 19.-
Candidates who meet the following conditions shall be allowed to take the entrance examination for student judges:

1. Must have Khmer nationality by birth;

2. Be at most 35 (thirty-five) years of age for students and at most 40 (forty) years of age for civil servants, calculated up to the date of the examination;

3. Must have at least a Bachelor of Law degree.

4. Must have no criminal record for felonies or misdemeanors;

5. Must be physically fit to fulfill the duties.

### ARTICLE 20.-
The number of student judges to be selected shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 21.-
Candidates who have passed the entrance examination for student judges shall receive professional training organized by the Ministry of Justice.

The organization and operation of this professional training shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 22.-
The formalities, procedures, dates and venues, as well as the examination committee for the entrance examination for student judges, shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 23.-
Recruitment into the judge framework may be conducted through internal competitive examination among civil servants and court clerks who have at least a Bachelor of Law degree and at least 05 (five) years of experience in the legal or justice field, and who are at most 45 (forty-five) years of age calculated up to the date of the examination. Lawyers who have at least a Bachelor of Law degree and have practiced their profession for at least 05 (five) years may also register for the competitive examination according to the provisions of the first paragraph above, provided that the lawyer is at most 45 (forty-five) years of age calculated up to the date of the examination.

Persons who have passed the competitive examination for recruitment into the judge framework according to the provisions of the first paragraph above shall receive professional training organized by the Ministry of Justice.

The formalities and procedures of the internal competitive examination mentioned above and the number of recruits into the judge framework according to this article, as well as the organization and operation of the training, shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### SECTION 6: INTERNSHIP AND PERMANENT APPOINTMENT IN THE JUDGE FRAMEWORK

### ARTICLE 24.-
Student judges selected according to the provisions of Articles 19 and 23 of this law and who have successfully completed professional training shall be appointed by Royal Decree as probationary judges at the initial class of the Junior Judge hierarchical rank. A probationary judge shall undergo an internship for a period of 01 (one) year, calculated from the date of appointment.

The formalities of the internship and the power of a probationary judge shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Justice, after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The appointment as a probationary judge shall have effect on the calculation of salary and seniority of service.

### ARTICLE 25.-
A probationary judge who has successfully completed the internship shall be permanently appointed in the judge framework at the initial class of the Junior Judge hierarchical rank.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy may decide to have a probationary judge extend their internship for 01 (one) additional year if that probationary judge failed to successfully complete the internship or was absent during the internship for more than 30 (thirty) days in total.

In case a probationary judge is absent for more than 30 (thirty) days without authorized leave, or a probationary judge who failed the extended internship again, they shall be removed from the judge framework after a decision from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 26.-
A probationary judge who lacks sufficient capacity, or whose health is insufficient to fulfill the duties according to the conditions determined by law, or who commits professional misconduct or is not fully dedicated to their work, may be removed from the judge framework at any time after a decision from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The decision for removal or permanent appointment in the judge framework must be made within at most 03 (three) months after the completion of the internship.

### SECTION 7: PROMOTION IN CLASS AND HIERARCHICAL RANK

### ARTICLE 27.-
Promotion in class and hierarchical rank shall be made by merit-based selection and by seniority, in accordance with the table of classes and hierarchical ranks.

The conditions for promotion in class or hierarchical rank by merit-based selection and by seniority shall be determined by Royal Decree.

### ARTICLE 28.-
The promotion in class and hierarchical rank of a judge shall be based on, among other things:

- Strict respect for professional discipline;

- Good performance of duties;

- Good conduct and morality, and not affecting the honor of the magistracy;

- Participation in training courses or completion of studies at higher education institutions.

During the period of service, a judge who has made efforts for further studies and obtained a post-graduate degree shall be encouraged by being promoted by 01 (one) additional class.

### ARTICLE 29.-
For each class and hierarchical rank within a hierarchy, the number of judges planned for promotion in class or hierarchical rank for each year shall be determined according to the requirements of work and the national budget plan.

### ARTICLE 30.-
A judge within the same hierarchy may be promoted one class at a time.

In case of necessity, a judge may change the hierarchy through an examination.

### ARTICLE 31.-
Every year in the first week of January, each judge shall receive an individual assessment form which that judge must complete based on the questionnaire points.

The individual assessment form shall be scored with clear comments and reasons. Regarding the scoring, it shall be implemented as follows:

1. Judges of the Court of First Instance and Deputy Presidents of the Court of First Instance shall be scored by the President of the Court of First Instance;

2. Judges of the Appeal Court and Deputy Presidents of the Appeal Court shall be scored by the President of the Appeal Court;

3. Presidents of the Court of First Instance, Presidents of the Appeal Court, judges of the Supreme Court and Deputy Presidents of the Supreme Court shall be scored by the President of the Supreme Court;

4. Judges who have been transferred to fulfill duties at other institutions shall be scored by the head of those institutions.

### ARTICLE 32.-
Registration in the promotion list for a class can only be made for judges who have served after being permanently appointed and have been in their hierarchical rank or class for at least 02 (two) years, calculated up to April 13 of each year.

### ARTICLE 33.-
The composition of the Promotion Commission for class and hierarchical rank consists of:

- 01 (one) Secretary of State of the Ministry of Justice: **President**;

- 01 (one) Deputy President of the Supreme Court: **Vice-President**;

- 01 (one) Deputy Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court: **Vice-President**;

- President of the Phnom Penh Appeal Court: **Member**;

- Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Phnom Penh Appeal Court: **Member**;

- President of the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance: **Member**;

- Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Phnom Penh Court of First Instance: **Member**;

- Secretary-General of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy: **Secretary**.

The Minister of Justice may appoint a number of other members as necessary to assist this Commission.

### ARTICLE 34.-
The list of candidates proposed for promotion in class for each hierarchical rank shall be organized in order of seniority in accordance with the table of each class.

This list shall be posted for the persons concerned at the courts.

### ARTICLE 35.-
Any judge who has completed the required seniority for promotion in class or hierarchical rank but whose name is not on the aforementioned list, may file a protest to request their registration to the Minister of Justice within at most 30 (thirty) days starting from the date of receiving information about the promotion list. After this period, the protest shall be void.

### ARTICLE 36.-
The individual assessment forms of judges registered in the list of each class and those of judges who filed protests shall be submitted to the Promotion Commission for class and hierarchical rank as provided in Article 33 of this law within the second week of February of each year.

### ARTICLE 37.-
The President of the Promotion Commission for class and hierarchical rank shall convene the meetings of the Commission. This Commission must meet before the Khmer New Year.

### ARTICLE 38.-
After having reviewed the individual assessment forms, decided on the protests and heard the full explanation of the President, each member shall score each candidate judge from 0 (zero) to 20 (twenty). Registration in the candidate list shall be made in order of merit according to the total scores. In case two candidates receive equal scores, the candidate with higher age shall be registered first in the list.

### ARTICLE 39.-
The Promotion Commission for class and hierarchical rank shall organize the list according to the seniority of the judges promoted in class and hierarchical rank and according to their rank in the registration list to be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and opinion. The Secretary-General of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall organize the list of judges who received a favorable opinion from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for promotion in class and hierarchical rank to the Minister of Justice for the preparation of a draft Royal Decree for submission to the King.

### ARTICLE 40.-
Any judge who is currently under preliminary disciplinary investigation or investigation for a criminal offense shall be suspended from the right to be registered in the promotion list for a class or hierarchical rank.

### ARTICLE 41.-
In case a candidate is not promoted in class or hierarchical rank, that candidate shall have priority to be registered first in the promotion list for the following year.

### ARTICLE 42.-
The promotion in class or hierarchical rank and the increase in salary and seniority shall be calculated starting from April 13 of each year.

### SECTION 8: APPOINTMENT, TRANSFER, SECONDMENT, PLACING OUTSIDE THE FRAMEWORK, PLACING ON LEAVE AND REMOVAL OF JUDGES

### ARTICLE 43.-
The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall decide and submit proposals to the King regarding appointment, transfer, secondment, placing outside the framework, placing on leave and removal of all judges.

### ARTICLE 44.-
A judge may be allowed to be seconded or placed outside the judge framework upon the request of the person concerned or of another institution with the consent of the person concerned.

In case of necessity to fulfill duties temporarily in a public position as a Member of the National Assembly, Member of the Senate, Member of the Royal Government, Under-Secretary of State, in an elected mandate or in a position as an advisor of any rank, a judge may request to be seconded. The authorization for this secondment shall be made upon the proposal of the person concerned or another institution with the consent of the person concerned. During the secondment period, that judge shall continue to receive salary...

...as well as the right to promotion in class or hierarchical rank and the right to pension, but shall not receive functional allowances or other bonuses of their original position.

In case of necessity to fulfill duties temporarily in a state enterprise or an international organization, a judge may request to be placed outside the framework. This authorization for being placed outside the framework shall be made upon the proposal of the person concerned or another institution with the consent of the person concerned. A judge who has been placed outside the framework shall not have the right to receive salary, functional allowance and other allowances as provided in Article 15 of this law.

A judge may be removed from the judge framework in case that judge has abandoned their work or was absent for more than 30 (thirty) days by implementing Article 52 of this law, or by the person concerned requesting permanent resignation.

The resignation of a judge must be made in writing by the person concerned. This request shall be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and decision.

### ARTICLE 45.-
In case of necessity for personal or family reasons or incompatibility, a judge may request to be placed on leave without pay for a specific period upon the request of the person concerned. The authorization for leave without pay may be made for a period of 01 (one) year or 02 (two) years at a time. This leave without pay can be granted only twice and the total duration shall not exceed 04 (four) years.

The condition for being allowed to be placed on leave without pay upon the request of the person concerned is that the judge must have served in the judge framework for at least 04 (four) years, not counting judges who are subject to second-degree disciplinary sanction under point (b) of Article 55 of this law.

This request shall be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and decision.

### ARTICLE 46.-
A judge who is currently on the first leave without pay, if they intend to request an extension or to return to service, must submit a request 02 (two) months before the expiration of the period of leave without pay. Any judge who fails to submit a request for extension of leave without pay or fails to return to fulfill their duties at the latest 30 (thirty) days from the expiration of the period of leave without pay...

...shall be considered as having abandoned their work and shall be removed from the judge framework starting from that date.

### ARTICLE 47.-
A judge who has been sent for an internship to improve professional accuracy shall be considered as a judge fulfilling their duties.

### ARTICLE 48.-
In case of removal from the judge framework by implementing the provisions of this law, the person concerned has the right to receive back all the money that the pension fund has deducted from their salary in accordance with the law or regulations of the pension fund.

### ARTICLE 49.-
The President of the Supreme Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Judge of the highest class. The Deputy Presidents and all judges of the Supreme Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Judge and their number shall not exceed 25 (twenty-five) persons, including the President of the Supreme Court. However, according to the necessary requirements, the number of judges of the Supreme Court may be determined to be more than this by Royal Decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice.

The President of the Appeal Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Judge. Deputy Presidents and all judges of the Appeal Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Judge or Superior Judge.

The President of the Court of First Instance shall have the hierarchical rank of Superior Judge. Deputy Presidents and judges of the Court of First Instance shall have the hierarchical rank of Superior Judge or Junior Judge.

### SECTION 9: DUTIES OF JUDGES

### ARTICLE 50.-
A judge shall have the duty to be responsible for the work assigned and shall strictly respect their code of conduct.

In their private life, a judge shall not commit any act which can affect their own dignity and honor or the prestige of the magistracy.

In the political field, a judge must absolutely maintain a neutral attitude.

### ARTICLE 51.-
All judges shall not abandon their work or be absent without prior authorization from their immediate superior.

### ARTICLE 52.-
Abandonment of work or unauthorized absence without reason for 15 (fifteen) days or more shall be subject to disciplinary sanction of suspension of salary or being placed on leave without pay, calculated from the first day of abandonment of work or unauthorized absence. If the abandonment of work or unauthorized absence without reason exceeds 30 (thirty) days and that judge still fails to return to fulfill their duties after 02 (two) formal notices, they shall be removed from the framework.

### ARTICLE 53.-
A judge currently fulfilling duties at any court level must request prior authorization from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy before publishing or requesting the publication by any means of texts or writings as well as other drawings related to their duties.

Violation of the provisions of this article shall be subject to disciplinary sanction, without prejudice to criminal prosecution, if any.

This provision shall not apply to the provision of normal information related to the proceedings of cases at the courts under conditions that do not violate the professional secrecy determined by law. Such information shall be provided through a regular administrative official of the court.

### SECTION 10: DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS

### ARTICLE 54.-
Faults of a judge in fulfilling professional duties and acts affecting the honor, clean morality and dignity in contradiction with the code of ethics for judges and prosecutors shall result in disciplinary sanctions according to the provisions of this law. However, the exercise of discretionary power of a judge or prosecutor in the implementation of the law shall not be considered a disciplinary fault.

Disciplinary sanctions shall not be an obstacle to criminal prosecution if an offense has been committed.

A judge may be temporarily suspended from their duties by the President of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy in case that judge is currently subject to a proceeding before the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 55.-
Disciplinary sanctions consist of 02 (two) degrees:

1. First-degree disciplinary sanctions:

(a) Verbal warning;

(b) Written warning to be recorded in the personal file;

(c) Suspension from registration in the promotion list for a class or hierarchical rank for a period not exceeding 02 (two) years, or removal from the list in case the name has already been registered.

2. Second-degree disciplinary sanctions:

(a) Compulsory transfer, which may include further training;

(b) Suspension from work without salary for a period not exceeding 01 (one) year;

(c) Reduction of one or more classes or reduction of hierarchical rank;

(d) Compulsory retirement;

(e) Compulsory dismissal from duties;

(f) Dismissal from the framework.

Second-degree disciplinary sanctions shall be implemented by Royal Decree after a decision from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 56.-
In case of compulsory dismissal from duties or dismissal from the framework in accordance with the provisions of this law, the person concerned has the right to receive back all the money that the pension fund has deducted from their salary in accordance with the law or regulations of the pension fund.

### SECTION 11: JUDICIAL PROSECUTION

### ARTICLE 57.-
A judge who has committed any criminal offense may be subject to judicial prosecution.

The Minister of Justice may decide to transfer jurisdiction to another Court of First Instance besides the Court of First Instance where that judge fulfills their duties.

In case any judge has committed a criminal offense and the prosecutor has decided to prosecute, the prosecutor shall inform the Minister of Justice about this prosecution within at most 72 (seventy-two) hours.

If there is an arrest or detention of any judge, the competent authority shall inform the Minister of Justice about this arrest or detention.

The Minister of Justice shall urgently provide the above information to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 58.-
A judge who is prosecuted for a felony or misdemeanor shall be automatically suspended from their duties until there is a final court decision on the sentence. This judge shall receive their regular salary until there is a court judgment deciding that they are guilty. Such salary shall be temporarily suspended by a decision of the Minister of Justice starting from the date of the court judgment deciding that they are guilty, even if there is an appeal against the judgment.

### ARTICLE 59.-
In case a prosecuted judge receives a dismissal of charges or is finally acquitted by the court, that judge shall be allowed to return to work immediately. Their seniority, class, hierarchical rank and pension shall be calculated and they shall receive all the salary that was temporarily suspended.

This provision shall also apply to a judge who was not removed from the judge framework following the provisions of Article 61 of this law.

### ARTICLE 60.-
Notwithstanding the court's decision for dismissal of charges, acquittal or sentence, if the person concerned has committed a fault against the code of ethics for judges and prosecutors, they may be subject to further disciplinary prosecution by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 61.-
A sentence for a felony or a misdemeanor committed intentionally without suspension of sentence shall result in removal from the judge framework, starting from the date the judgment or final verdict becomes final.

### SECTION 12: RETIREMENT

### ARTICLE 62.-
All judges shall retire at the age of 60 (sixty).

All judges of the Supreme Court who are 60 (sixty) years of age or older shall retire upon their own request. However, when reaching the age of 65 (sixty-five), the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall decide on the continuation of work of all judges of the Supreme Court upon their own request.

The Minister of Justice shall submit a draft Royal Decree to the King regarding the above matters.

### ARTICLE 63.-
A judge who has completed 30 (thirty) years of service may request retirement before the age of 60 (sixty).

A judge who has served for 30 (thirty) years or more has the right to receive a seniority pension based on their last salary.

A judge who has served for 20 (twenty) years or more but less than 30 (thirty) years has the right to receive a proportional pension based on their last salary.

A judge who has served for less than 20 (twenty) years shall receive only the refund of pension contributions and other one-time allowances.

The period of service mentioned above shall also include the period of service as a public official before appointment as a judge.

The pension of a judge shall be paid at the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The pension can be received at only one place.

The implementation of the pension regime shall be determined in detail by Sub-decree.

### ARTICLE 64.-
A judge who has completed 30 (thirty) years of service shall no longer have pension contributions deducted. However, if a judge has served for less than 30 (thirty) years, pension contributions shall be deducted until they complete 30 (thirty) years of service in order to receive a seniority pension.

### ARTICLE 65.-
The seniority of service of a judge shall be calculated from the date the person concerned entered state service, in any period.

### ARTICLE 66.-
Any judge who can no longer fulfill their professional duties due to physical disability shall be retired. The decision on physical disability shall be made by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy based on a forensic medical report from specialized doctors appointed by the Ministry of Health.

In case this physical disability clearly results from the performance of their duties or from great effort in public service, that judge shall be retired and entitled to an invalidity pension equal to the seniority pension, regardless of the period of service. The judge shall receive this pension only if the illness or disability is the direct and clear cause of the invalidity or incapacity to fulfill duties.

The cause, source, type and traces of injury or illness must be certified by a forensic medical report from specialized doctors appointed by the Ministry of Health.

If a judge dies from the great effort in service mentioned above, the spouse and dependent children who are not yet married may receive an allowance equal to 12 (twelve) months of the deceased's salary.

Besides this, the State shall provide support for minor children and the spouse, unless the spouse remarries.

### ARTICLE 67.-
In case of physical disability as provided in Article 66 above, the Minister of Justice shall make a report to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and decision on retirement.

The Minister of Justice shall submit a draft Royal Decree to the King regarding the above matters after the decision of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### SECTION 13: HONORARY TITLES

### ARTICLE 68.-
Except in cases of compulsory retirement, upon retirement, a judge shall receive the title of Honorary Judge and shall be promoted to one hierarchical rank higher than the hierarchical rank held during service, provided that the judge has served in that rank for at least 01 (one) year before retirement. This honorary title shall be granted by Royal Decree.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall submit proposals to the King regarding the above matter.

### ARTICLE 69.-
A retired judge has the full right to keep the regalia of this honorary title.

This honorary title may be revoked by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice if there are acts affecting the honor or dignity of the magistracy.

### SECTION 14: OATH

### ARTICLE 70.-
Before entering service, a judge shall take an oath at the Royal Palace.
The content of the oath is attached as an annex to this law.

### ARTICLE 71.-
The aforementioned oath-taking shall be recorded in a protocol made by the official of the Ministry of Royal Palace and shall be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy to be kept in the personal file of the person concerned.

### ARTICLE 72.-
Any judge who refuses to take the oath cannot fulfill the duties of a judge and may be removed from the judge framework.

A judge who despises or insults the words of the oath they have already taken shall be subject to disciplinary sanction.

### ARTICLE 73.-
When having to testify before a court, a judge shall take an oath according to the traditions of their religion and according to the court's procedures.

### CHAPTER 3: PROSECUTORS
### SECTION 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 74.-
The General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall be led by 01 (one) Prosecutor General and assisted by Deputy Prosecutors General and a number of prosecutors as necessary. The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General and prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court are representatives of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court.

The General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall be led by 01 (one) Prosecutor General and assisted by Deputy Prosecutors General and a number of prosecutors as necessary. The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General and prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court are representatives of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court where they fulfill their duties.

The Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall be led by 01 (one) Prosecutor and assisted by a number of deputy prosecutors as necessary. The Prosecutor and deputy prosecutors of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance are representatives of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance where they fulfill their duties.

Only the prosecution office has the right to initiate public action. Prosecutors are responsible for searching for criminal offenses, conducting criminal prosecution and requesting the application of the law before the investigating jurisdictions and trial jurisdictions.

In civil cases and other cases, the representative of the prosecution office may participate in judicial proceedings and provide opinions if they consider it necessary to protect the public interest.

### ARTICLE 75.-
The key principles of the prosecution office include, among others, the following:

1. The prosecution office is a single unit where all prosecutors must be under the leadership and supervision of their hierarchy. The Minister of Justice is the hierarchical head of the prosecution office and may issue written instructions to the prosecution offices at all levels. The General Department of Prosecution and Criminal Affairs of the Ministry of Justice acts as the staff for the Minister of Justice on this matter. The Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court is the hierarchical head of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court. The prosecutors and deputy prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall work under the leadership and collective responsibility of the Prosecutor General who is their hierarchical head. The Prosecutor General of the Appeal Court is the hierarchical head of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court. The prosecutors and deputy prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall work under the leadership and collective responsibility of the Prosecutor General who is their hierarchical head. The Prosecutor of the Court of First Instance is the hierarchical head of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance. All deputy prosecutors of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall work under the leadership and collective responsibility of the Prosecutor who is their hierarchical head.

The prosecution office is indivisible, which means that prosecutors fulfilling their duties at the same prosecution office attached to the courts have common tasks in the same function and can substitute each other.

According to this principle, several representatives of the prosecution office may succeed each other in the same case according to the assignment of the hierarchical head.

3. The prosecution office is an independent body which is not under the authority of the court where it is attached. This court has no power to supervise the work of the prosecutor. Even in case the prosecutor has committed a fault in fulfilling their duties, the court shall not record a warning against the prosecutor in its decision. This prohibition applies to both criminal cases and other cases. However, the court may submit a proposal to the hierarchical head of the prosecutor regarding the prosecutor's fault.

### ARTICLE 76.-
Each year:
- The Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall make a report on offenses and their work activities to the Minister of Justice and provide a copy to the Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court having territorial jurisdiction.
- The Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall make a report on offenses, their work activities and the work activities of all prosecution offices attached to the Courts of First Instance within their territorial jurisdiction to the Minister of Justice and provide a copy to the Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court.
- The Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall make a report on the work activities of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court to the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 77.-
All prosecutors shall fulfill their duties and roles based on the principle of respect for the law without being subject to pressure, threat, or suppression, or direct or indirect orders from any party in a case or from any individual, except as provided in Article 75 of this law.

### SECTION 2: HIERARCHICAL RANK AND CLASS

### ARTICLE 78.-
The prosecutor framework has 03 (three) hierarchical ranks:

- Supreme Prosecutor;

- Superior Prosecutor;

- Junior Prosecutor.

### ARTICLE 79.-
The classes for the hierarchical ranks of Supreme Prosecutor, Superior Prosecutor and Junior Prosecutor shall be determined by Royal Decree.

### SECTION 3: UNIFORM

### ARTICLE 80.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 3 (Uniform) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### SECTION 4: SALARY, OTHER ALLOWANCES, BONUSES AND ANNUAL LEAVE

### ARTICLE 81.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 4 (Salary, Other Allowances, Bonuses and Annual Leave) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### SECTION 5: SELECTION OF PROSECUTORS

### ARTICLE 82.-
The selection and training of prosecutors shall be implemented together with the selection and training of judges in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 5 (Selection of Judges) of this law.

### SECTION 6: INTERNSHIP AND PERMANENT APPOINTMENT IN THE PROSECUTOR FRAMEWORK

### ARTICLE 83.-
Student prosecutors who have successfully completed professional training shall be appointed by Royal Decree as intern prosecutors in the first class of the Junior Prosecutor rank. Intern prosecutors shall serve an internship for a period of 01 (one) year starting from the date of appointment.

The internship of intern prosecutors shall be determined by a Proclamation of the Minister of Justice after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The appointment as an intern prosecutor shall take effect for salary organization and seniority calculation.

### ARTICLE 84.-
Intern prosecutors who have successfully completed their internship shall be permanently appointed in the prosecutor framework in the first class of the Junior Prosecutor rank. The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall submit proposals to the King regarding this permanent appointment upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice.

The Minister of Justice may decide to allow an intern prosecutor to continue their internship for 01 (one) additional year if that intern prosecutor did not successfully complete the internship or was absent for more than 30 (thirty) days in total.

In case an intern prosecutor is absent for more than 30 (thirty) days without authorization or fails to successfully complete the additional internship, they shall be removed from the prosecutor framework by implementing the provisions of Article 89 of this law.

### ARTICLE 85.-
An intern prosecutor who does not have sufficient aptitude or health to fulfill their duties according to the conditions determined by law or has committed faults in their work may be removed from the prosecutor framework at any time by implementing the provisions of Article 89 of this law.

The removal or permanent appointment in the prosecutor framework shall be made within at most 03 (three) months after the completion of the internship.

### SECTION 7: PROMOTION IN CLASS AND HIERARCHICAL RANK

### ARTICLE 86.-
The provisions from Article 27 to Article 30, Article 32 to Article 38 and Article 40 to Article 42 of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 7 (Promotion in Class and Hierarchical Rank) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### ARTICLE 87.-
Each year, in the first week of January, each prosecutor shall receive an individual assessment form which that prosecutor must fill in according to all the questions.

The individual assessment form shall be scored with clear comments and reasons.

Regarding the scoring, it shall be implemented as follows:

1. Deputy prosecutors of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall be scored by the Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance;

2. Prosecutors and Deputy Prosecutors General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall be scored by the Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court;

3. Prosecutors of the Court of First Instance, Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court, and prosecutors and Deputy Prosecutors General of the Supreme Court shall be scored by the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court;

4. Prosecutors who have been transferred to fulfill duties at other institutions shall be scored by the head of those institutions.

### ARTICLE 88.-
The Promotion Commission for Class and Hierarchical Rank shall organize the list according to the seniority of the prosecutors promoted in class and hierarchical rank and according to their rank in the registration list to be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and opinion. The Secretary-General of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall organize the list of prosecutors who received a favorable opinion from the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for promotion in class and hierarchical rank to the Minister of Justice for the preparation of a draft Royal Decree for submission to the King.

### SECTION 8: APPOINTMENT, TRANSFER, SECONDMENT, PLACING OUTSIDE THE FRAMEWORK, PLACING ON LEAVE AND REMOVAL OF PROSECUTORS

### ARTICLE 89.-
The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall submit proposals to the King regarding appointment, transfer, secondment, placing outside the framework, placing on leave and removal of all prosecutors of the Kingdom of Cambodia upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice. The Supreme Council of the Magistracy must be consulted on the aforementioned proposals.

The Minister of Justice shall submit a draft Royal Decree to the King regarding the above matters.

### ARTICLE 90.-
A prosecutor may be allowed to be seconded or placed outside the prosecutor framework upon the request of the person concerned or of another institution with the consent of the person concerned.

In case of necessity to fulfill duties temporarily in a public position as a Member of the National Assembly, Member of the Senate, Member of the Royal Government, Under-Secretary of State, in an elected mandate or in a position as an advisor of any rank, a prosecutor may request to be seconded. The authorization for this secondment shall be made upon the proposal of the person concerned or another institution with the consent of the person concerned. During the secondment period, that prosecutor shall continue to receive salary as well as the right to promotion in class or hierarchical rank and the right to pension, but shall not receive functional allowances or other bonuses of their original position.

In case of necessity to fulfill duties temporarily in a state enterprise or an international organization, a prosecutor may request to be placed outside the framework. This authorization for being placed outside the framework shall be made upon the proposal of the person concerned or another institution with the consent of the person concerned. A prosecutor who has been placed outside the framework shall not have the right to receive salary, functional allowance and other allowances as provided in Article 81 of this law.

A prosecutor may be temporarily suspended from their duties by the Minister of Justice in case that prosecutor is currently subject to a proceeding before the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

A prosecutor may be removed from the prosecutor framework in case that prosecutor has received a second-degree disciplinary sanction or in case that prosecutor has abandoned their work or was absent...

...more than 30 (thirty) days by implementing Article 52 of this law, or by the person concerned requesting permanent resignation.

The resignation of a prosecutor must be made in writing by the person concerned. This request shall be submitted to the Minister of Justice for review and processing according to the procedure.

### ARTICLE 91.-
In case of necessity for personal or family reasons or incompatibility, a prosecutor may request to be placed on leave without pay for a specific period upon the request of the person concerned. The authorization for leave without pay may be made for a period of 01 (one) year or 02 (two) years at a time. This leave without pay can be granted only twice and the total duration shall not exceed 04 (four) years. The condition for being allowed to be placed on leave without pay upon the request of the person concerned is that the prosecutor must have served in the prosecutor framework for at least 04 (four) years, not counting prosecutors who are subject to second-degree disciplinary sanction.

The request for leave without pay must be made in writing by the person concerned. This request shall be submitted to the Minister of Justice for review and processing according to the procedure.

### ARTICLE 92.-
A prosecutor who is currently on the first leave without pay, if they intend to request an extension or to return to service, must submit a request 02 (two) months before the expiration of the period of leave without pay. Any prosecutor who fails to submit a request for extension of leave without pay or fails to return to fulfill their duties at the latest 30 (thirty) days from the expiration of the period of leave without pay shall be considered as having abandoned their work and shall be removed from the framework starting from that date.

### ARTICLE 93.-
A prosecutor who has been sent for an internship to improve professional accuracy shall be considered as a prosecutor fulfilling their duties.

### ARTICLE 94.-
In case of removal from the prosecutor framework in accordance with the provisions of this law, the person concerned has the right to receive back all the money that the pension fund has deducted from their salary in accordance with the law or regulations of the pension fund.

### ARTICLE 95.-
The Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Prosecutor of the highest class. The Deputy Prosecutors General and all prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Prosecutor.

The Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Prosecutor. The Deputy Prosecutors General and all prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court shall have the hierarchical rank of Supreme Prosecutor or Superior Prosecutor.

The Prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall have the hierarchical rank of Superior Prosecutor. All deputy prosecutors of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance shall have the hierarchical rank of Superior Prosecutor or Junior Prosecutor and above.

### SECTION 9: DUTIES OF PROSECUTORS

### ARTICLE 96.-
A prosecutor shall have the duty to be responsible for the work assigned and shall strictly respect their code of conduct.

In their private life, a prosecutor shall not commit any act which can affect their own dignity and honor or the prestige of the prosecution office.

A prosecutor currently fulfilling duties at any prosecution office attached to the courts must request prior authorization from the Minister of Justice before publishing or requesting the publication by any means of texts or writings as well as other drawings which may affect the prestige of the prosecution institution or judicial institution. This provision shall not apply to public statements of the prosecutor made in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

In the political field, a prosecutor must absolutely maintain a neutral attitude.

Violation of the provisions of paragraphs 2 and 3 of this article shall be subject to disciplinary sanction, without prejudice to criminal prosecution, if any.

### ARTICLE 97.-
All prosecutors shall not abandon their work or be absent without prior authorization from their immediate superior.

The determination of the authorization for absence of the Prosecutor of the Court of First Instance, the Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court and the Prosecutor General of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court shall be decided by the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 98.-
Abandonment of work or unauthorized absence without reason for 15 (fifteen) days or more shall result in a disciplinary sanction of salary suspension or being placed on leave without pay, starting from the day of abandonment or unauthorized absence. If the abandonment of work or unauthorized absence without reason exceeds 30 (thirty) days and that prosecutor still does not return to fulfill their duties after being warned twice, they shall be removed from the framework.

### SECTION 10: DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS

### ARTICLE 99.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 10 (Disciplinary Sanctions) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### SECTION 11: JUDICIAL PROSECUTION

### ARTICLE 100.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 11 (Judicial Prosecution) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### SECTION 12: RETIREMENT

### ARTICLE 101.-
Prosecutors of both sexes shall retire at the age of 60 (sixty).

However, all prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Supreme Court who are 60 (sixty) years of age or older shall retire upon their own request. But upon reaching the age of 65 (sixty-five), the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall decide on the continuation of work of those prosecutors...

### ARTICLE 102.-
The provisions from Article 63 to Article 67 of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 12 (Retirement) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### SECTION 13: HONORARY TITLES

### ARTICLE 103.-
Except in cases of compulsory retirement, upon retirement, a prosecutor shall receive the title of Honorary Prosecutor and shall be promoted to one hierarchical rank higher than the hierarchical rank held during service, provided that the prosecutor has served in that rank for at least 01 (one) year before retirement. This honorary title shall be granted by Royal Decree.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall submit proposals to the King regarding the above matter.

### ARTICLE 104.-
A retired prosecutor has the full right to keep the regalia of this honorary title.

This honorary title may be revoked by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice if there are acts affecting the honor or dignity of the magistracy.

### SECTION 14: OATH

### ARTICLE 105.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 14 (Oath) of this law which apply to judges shall also apply to prosecutors.

### CHAPTER 4: INCOMPATIBILITY

### ARTICLE 106.-
The fulfillment of duties as a judge or prosecutor at all courts or prosecution offices attached to the courts is incompatible with the fulfillment of public duties, the duties of a Member of the National Assembly or Member of the Senate...

...Member of the Royal Government, Under-Secretary of State, in an elected mandate or in a position as an advisor of any rank, unless there are contrary provisions.

A judge or prosecutor currently fulfilling their duties at all courts or prosecution offices attached to the courts cannot fulfill the duties of a lawyer, engage in a commercial profession or engage in any other activity which results in a conflict of interest or which affects the honor of the magistracy. However, a judge and prosecutor may fulfill duties as a lecturer at educational institutions.

### ARTICLE 107.-
A judge or prosecutor who has been allowed to be placed on leave, if they intend to engage in private work, must inform the Minister of Justice. The Minister of Justice may object to such work if they consider those activities contrary to their honor or dignity, or damaging to the prestige of the office of judge or prosecutor.

In case of violation of this article, the judge or prosecutor who was placed on leave shall be subject to disciplinary sanction as provided in Article 55 of this law.

### CHAPTER 5: TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 108.-
Judges and prosecutors who received appointments before this law entered into force and who are within the framework managed by the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be entitled by law to have the framework of judges and prosecutors in accordance with the provisions of this law, some exceptions notwithstanding.

The Commission to review the criteria for integration into classes and hierarchical ranks of judges and prosecutors shall be created by Royal Decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The aforementioned integration shall be made by Royal Decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice.

### ARTICLE 109.-
The provisions of Chapter 2 (Judges), Section 12 (Retirement) and Chapter 3 (Prosecutors)...

Section 12 (Retirement) of this law shall be implemented after the integration into the framework of judges and prosecutors.

In the period waiting for the aforementioned integration, the retirement of judges and prosecutors shall be implemented in accordance with the legal norms existing before the implementation of this law.

### ARTICLE 110.-
In the first 05 (five) years after this law enters into force, the provisions of Article 49 and Article 89 of this law shall not be implemented, except for the determination of the number of judges at the Supreme Court.

In the first 05 (five) years after this law enters into force, judges of the Appeal Court and prosecutors of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the Appeal Court reaching retirement age may be allowed to continue their work upon their own request. This continuation of work shall be for 01 (one) year at a time, but shall not exceed 03 (three) times. The authorization for continuation of work shall be made by Royal Decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### CHAPTER 6: FINAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 111.-
Any provisions contrary to the provisions of this law shall be null and void.

**LAW**

**ON**

**THE ORGANIZATION AND FUNCTIONING OF THE SUPREME COUNCIL OF THE MAGISTRACY**

### CHAPTER 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS

### ARTICLE 1.-
This law has the objective to create and determine the organization and functioning of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy with the aim of assisting the King to guarantee the independence of the judicial power in accordance with the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### ARTICLE 2.-
This law applies to judges and prosecutors throughout the Kingdom of Cambodia, unless there is a separate law providing otherwise.

### ARTICLE 3.-
The technical terms used in this law shall be defined as follows:
- Judge refers to "judges of the seat" (juge assis), including but not limited to, trial judges and investigating judges.
- Prosecutor refers to deputy prosecutors, prosecutors, deputy prosecutors general and prosecutors general.
- Magistracy (Corps of the Magistracy) refers to judges and prosecutors.

### CHAPTER 2: ORGANIZATION OF THE SUPREME COUNCIL OF THE MAGISTRACY

### SECTION 1: COMPOSITION OF THE SUPREME COUNCIL OF THE MAGISTRACY

### ARTICLE 4.-
The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be under the high presidency of the King and shall be composed of:
- The Minister of Justice, as a member;
- The President of the Supreme Court, as a member;

- 3- The Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court, as a member;
- 4- One (01) member appointed by the Senate by an absolute majority of its members from among dignitaries who are former judges or former prosecutors or dignitaries with at least 15 years of professional experience in the field of law or the judiciary;
- 5- One (01) member appointed by the National Assembly by an absolute majority of its members from among dignitaries who are former judges or former prosecutors or dignitaries with at least 15 years of professional experience in the field of law or the judiciary;
- 6- One (01) member appointed by the Constitutional Council by an absolute majority of its members from among dignitaries who are former judges or former prosecutors or dignitaries with at least 15 years of professional experience in the field of law or the judiciary;
- 7- One (01) member appointed by the Minister of Justice from among all full-right prosecutors;
- 8- One (01) member who is a judge of the higher court, elected by judges of the higher court;
- 9- One (01) member who is a prosecutor of the General Prosecution Office Attached to the higher court or a prosecutor fulfilling duties at the Ministry of Justice, elected by prosecutors fulfilling duties at the General Prosecution Office Attached to the higher court and at the Ministry of Justice;
- 10- One (01) member who is a full-right judge of the Court of First Instance, elected by full-right judges of the Courts of First Instance throughout the country;
- 11- One (01) member who is a full-right prosecutor of the Prosecution Office Attached to the Court of First Instance, elected by full-right prosecutors of the Prosecution Offices Attached to the Courts of First Instance throughout the country.

All the aforementioned members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be appointed by Royal Decree. The Minister of Justice shall submit a draft Royal Decree to the King regarding this matter.

The formalities and procedures for the election of members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as provided in points 8 to 11 of this article shall be determined by a Proclamation (Prakas) of the Minister of Justice after obtaining the approval from the majority of the members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### ARTICLE 5.-
The elected members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall have a mandate of 05 (five) years and their mandate shall end when new elected members are appointed. Elected members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may stand as candidates for subsequent mandates.

The election for members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be organized 90 (ninety) days before the end of each mandate.

In case any member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy dies, resigns or loses the qualifications as provided in Article 4 above or is in a situation where they cannot fulfill their duties for any reason, that member shall be replaced by a new member for the remainder of the mandate, according to the formalities provided in Article 4 above. The Minister of Justice shall organize this matter urgently.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy may decide to remove any of its elected members for the reason of serious violation of its internal regulations or any activity which seriously affects the honor and dignity of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. The decision to remove any elected member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall require the approval of at least 08 (eight) members of the total membership. A member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy who has been sentenced to imprisonment without suspension for a felony or an intentional misdemeanor shall be automatically removed from the position. A new member who replaces them shall implement the provisions of this law.

### ARTICLE 6.-
The function of a member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy is incompatible with the fulfillment of duties as a judge or prosecutor at the courts and prosecution offices attached to the courts during their mandate as a member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, except for the President of the Supreme Court and the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court.

A member of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy whose functions are incompatible as provided in the above paragraph shall request a secondment from their duties as a judge or prosecutor at the courts and prosecution offices attached to the courts.

### ARTICLE 7.-
The President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy [The King] may appoint a representative of His Majesty to preside over the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. The representative who has been appointed to lead and manage the work of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall report regularly on the activities of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy to the President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The representative shall not participate in decision-making of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. However, in case the Minister of Justice or the President of the Supreme Court is appointed as the representative, the Minister of Justice or the President of the Supreme Court shall have the right to participate in the decision-making of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy like other members.

### SECTION 2: THE GENERAL SECRETARIAT OF THE SUPREME COUNCIL OF THE MAGISTRACY

### ARTICLE 8.-
The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall have a General Secretariat located under the central administration of the Ministry of Justice to serve as a secretariat supporting its functioning. The General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy is led by a Secretary General with a rank equivalent to Secretary of State or Minister, and some Deputy Secretaries General as necessary, who shall be appointed by Royal Decree or Sub-decree.

The Secretary General and Deputy Secretaries General of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be appointed by Royal Decree or Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice after consultation with all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. The Secretary General and Deputy Secretaries General of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be selected from among dignitaries or civil servants and shall have at least 10 (ten) years of professional experience in law, administration or finance. Civil servants working in the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be within the framework of the Ministry of Justice. The General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may have contract staff to assist in its work as necessary. The General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy is divided into departments, which are led by a director and some deputy directors as necessary.

The organization and functioning of the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### ARTICLE 9.-
The Secretary General of the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, assisted by some staff of this General Secretariat as necessary, shall serve as the secretariat for all meetings of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy and meetings of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

# Chapter 3
# Operation of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy

### Article 10._

The Minister of Justice shall convene a meeting of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy upon the Royal Command of the President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. At least 04 (four) members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may request a meeting of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. The Minister of Justice shall prepare a report to the President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy requesting a Royal Decision to convene the meeting.

### Article 11._

The meeting of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be valid only if at least 06 (six) members are present.

The decision of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be valid with at least 06 (six) votes by secret ballot. The President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall not participate in the decision-making of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

In case of urgent necessity and at the request of at least 06 (six) members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, the decision of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may be made by indirect secret ballot. The Minister of Justice shall organize this task after receiving the Royal Decision from the President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 12._

Members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, including the recording secretaries and assistant recording secretaries, shall maintain the confidentiality of the meeting.

### Article 13._

For all tasks of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy related to the proposal of draft Royal Decrees to the King, the Minister of Justice shall prepare these tasks on behalf of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The Minister of Justice is the representative of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy in relations with other individuals and the public, and has the duty to manage the administrative affairs of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, with the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as the staff.

### Article 14._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall prepare internal regulations to determine its working procedures. The internal regulations shall be adopted by at least 06 (six) votes of all members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 15._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall have its own budget package supplied by the national budget. The Minister of Justice is the authorizing officer legally empowered by the President of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 16._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall receive functional allowances, meeting allowances, and other allowances for fulfilling its functions.

Members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may choose only one functional allowance among the functional allowances they are entitled to receive.

### Article 17._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall use its own seal for its work.

# Chapter 4
# Competence of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy
## Section 1
## General Competence of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy

### Article 18._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall decide and propose to the King on the appointment, transfer, suspension from duty, placement outside the framework, placement on leave, and removal from the list of all judges.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall propose to the King on the appointment, transfer, suspension from duty, placement on leave, placement outside the framework, and removal of prosecutors.

all, upon the proposal of the Minister of Justice. The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be consulted as a necessity regarding the above proposal.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall provide advice on the promotion of grades and ranks for all judges and prosecutors, according to the procedures and conditions as stated in the Law on the Statute of Judges and Prosecutors.

### Article 19._

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be consulted as a necessity to provide advice on draft laws or proposed laws related to the organization of the judiciary and the operation of this sector. The advice of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be provided at the latest within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receiving the draft laws or proposed laws from the Minister of Justice. In case of urgency, this period may be reduced to 10 (ten) days.

The Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall review and decide on the Code of Ethics for Judges and Prosecutors.

## Section 2
## Competence of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy in Disciplinary Actions for Judges and Prosecutors

### Article 20._

In disciplinary actions against judges, the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall meet as a Disciplinary Council under the presidency of the President of the Supreme Court. In disciplinary actions against prosecutors, the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall meet as a Disciplinary Council under the presidency of the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court. In these cases, the King and the Minister of Justice shall not participate in the Disciplinary Council.

In disciplinary cases related to the President of the Supreme Court or the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court, the Disciplinary Council shall meet under the high presidency of the King or the Royal Representative. The Minister of Justice shall participate in this Disciplinary Council meeting.

The judges or prosecutors who are members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy and whose disciplinary files are involved, cannot participate in the meeting as members of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy to review and decide on those disciplinary cases.

### Article 21._

An inspection team of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be established to assist the work of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy in investigating and researching disciplinary cases. The composition of the inspection team of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be appointed by Royal Decree after receiving a decision by a majority of the members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. This inspection team shall be led by co-presidents, one of whom shall be selected from among judges with the rank of supreme judge, and the other co-president shall be selected from among prosecutors with the rank of supreme prosecutor.

The terms of reference, as well as the organization and operation of the inspection team of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, shall be determined by the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 22._

The inspection team shall perform its work as assigned by the President of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. Within the framework of its work, the inspection team may request to review various files related to the disciplinary case or which are useful for the disciplinary case. In case of necessity, the inspection team may request clarification from any individual. Judges and prosecutors who do not cooperate with the request of the inspection team shall be considered as committing a disciplinary fault, which shall be recorded in the inspection report.

### Article 23._

Disciplinary complaints related to judges and prosecutors shall be submitted to the General Secretariat of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy or to the Ministry of Justice. The Minister of Justice shall order a preliminary review and investigation of these complaints to build a disciplinary file before deciding to send the file to the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for further proceedings.

### Article 24._

After receiving the disciplinary file and if considered necessary, the President of the Disciplinary Council may order further investigation using the inspection unit of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 25._

The President of the Disciplinary Council shall convene a meeting of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy. Disciplinary files, as well as documents related to the discipline of judges and prosecutors, shall be sent to all members of the Disciplinary Council at least 10 (ten) days before the meeting. Judges or prosecutors involved in these disciplinary cases have the right to know about the disciplinary charges against them and have the right to defend themselves either personally or with the assistance of a lawyer before the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

The meeting of the Disciplinary Council shall be held in camera. The President of the Disciplinary Council may invite various relevant individuals to participate in the meeting if considered necessary. Members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, as well as individuals participating in the Disciplinary Council meeting of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy, shall maintain the confidentiality of the meeting.

The quorum for the Disciplinary Council meeting shall be the same as the quorum for the meeting of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as stated in Article 11 of this Law.

Detailed procedures for the operation of the Disciplinary Council shall be determined in the internal regulations of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy.

### Article 26._

In case a fault is found, the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall decide to impose disciplinary sanctions as stated in the Law on the Statute of Judges and Prosecutors. The decision of the Disciplinary Council shall be based on facts and law.

The decision of the Disciplinary Council shall be implemented in the same manner as the decision of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as stated in Article 11 of this Law.

The decision of the Disciplinary Council shall be submitted to the Supreme Council of the Magistracy for review and decision. The decision of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall be final and not subject to appeal.

In case a second-degree disciplinary sanction is announced, the Supreme Council of the Magistracy shall propose to the King on this matter.

# Chapter 5
# Transitional Provisions

### Article 27._

During the transitional period of organizing the new composition of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as stated in this Law

in Article 4 of this Law, the composition of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy existing before this Law enters into force shall continue its work in accordance with the provisions of this Law until the new composition is officially appointed by Royal Decree. In this case, the principle of incompatibility as stated in Article 6 of this Law shall not be applied.

### Article 28._

The Minister of Justice shall organize the election of members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as stated from points 8 to 11 of Article 4 of this Law within 03 (three) months after this Law enters into force.

The forms and procedures for the first election to select members of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy as stated in the third paragraph of Article 4 of this Law shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

### Article 29._

During the period when the inspection team of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy has not yet been appointed according to the provisions of Article 21 of this Law, the President of the Disciplinary Council of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy may assign judges or prosecutors serving in the courts and prosecution offices to conduct research and investigation into disciplinary cases.

### Article 30._

The provisions of Article 6 of this Law shall not be applied to the fulfillment of the functions of judges or prosecutors in the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia to prosecute crimes committed during the period of Democratic Kampuchea.

# Chapter 6
# Final Provisions

### Article 31._

All provisions related to the organization and operation of the Supreme Council of the Magistracy that were announced

declared by Royal Kram No. 09 NS.94 dated December 22, 1994, as well as any legal documents contrary to this Law, are null and void and shall be replaced by this Law.
