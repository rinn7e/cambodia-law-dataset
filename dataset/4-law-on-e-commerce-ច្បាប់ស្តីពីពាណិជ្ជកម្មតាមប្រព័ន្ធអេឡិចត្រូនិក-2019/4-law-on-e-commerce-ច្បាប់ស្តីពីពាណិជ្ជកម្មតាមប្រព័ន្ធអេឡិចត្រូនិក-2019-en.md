### HEREBY PROMULGATE

The Law on E-Commerce which was adopted by the National Assembly on October 08, 2019, at its 3rd session of the 6th legislature, and which the Senate has reviewed and commented on the form and legal aspects of the law in its entirety on October 18, 2019, at its 4th plenary session of the 4th legislature, with the following contents:

# LAW
# ON
# E-COMMERCE

### CHAPTER 1
### GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 1.- Purpose

The purpose of this Law is:

1. To govern electronic commerce in the Kingdom of Cambodia and internationally.

2. To establish legal certainty in commercial and civil transactions via electronic systems.

3. To provide public confidence in the use of electronic communications.

Article 2.- Objective

The objectives of this Law are as follows:

1. To determine the authenticity, integrity, and reliability of electronic forms.

2. To promote the development of legal and business frameworks for secure implementation of electronic commerce.

3. To prevent and suppress acts that cause disturbance to data and information systems.

4. To eliminate all barriers hindering electronic commerce resulting from legal uncertainty regarding requirements for written documents and signatures.

5. To facilitate the submission of documents via electronic systems with public institutions and enhance the effective service delivery of public institutions through the use of reliable electronic records.

6. To establish rules, regulations, and standards related to the authenticity and integrity of electronic records.

Article 3.- Scope

This Law shall apply to all commercial and civil activities, documents, and transactions conducted via electronic systems, except for activities, documents, and transactions related to:

1. The creation or implementation of powers of attorney.

2. The creation or signing of wills, codicils, or any other orders related to succession.

3. Any contracts for the sale, transfer, or disposal of rights over immovable property or any other interests in such property.

4. The transfer of immovable property or any interests related to such immovable property.

5. Other exceptions as determined by sub-decree.

Article 4.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Law are defined in the glossary set out in the Annex to this Law.

### CHAPTER 2
### VALIDITY OF ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

Article 5.- Legal Recognition of Electronic Communications

No person may deny the validity, legal effect, admissibility, or enforceability of electronic communications solely on the grounds that they are in electronic form or contained in electronic communications.

Article 6.- Requirements for Written Form

1. Where any provision requires information to be provided in writing or a record to be made in writing, such requirement is deemed to be satisfied if the information is in electronic form and is accessible and retrievable for future reference.

2. Where the requirement in paragraph 1 above is applied to a legal requirement that a person provides multiple copies of information or a document at the same time regarding any information or matter to another person, the person providing it may satisfy such requirement by providing only one copy in electronic form.

Article 7.- Requirements for Signature

1. Where any provision requires the signature of a person, such requirement is deemed to be satisfied by an electronic signature if the electronic signature used is created through a means that:

a. Can identify the person and indicate that person’s approval of the information or record contained in the electronic communication.

b. Is reliable as appropriate for the nature, purpose, and circumstances relevant to that electronic communication, being created or used in connection therewith.

2. Notwithstanding any provision determining requirements for a signature or simply prescribing consequences for the absence of a signature, the provisions of paragraph 1 above shall also apply.

Article 8.- Requirements for Retention of Original Information

The legal requirement to retain information that is not in electronic form may be satisfied by retaining that information in electronic form if:

1. That electronic form is a reliable means to determine the completeness of the information; and

2. The information is easily accessible for future use.

Article 9.- Formalities to be Defined

Where any provision requires information to be in a specified form, such requirement may be substituted by providing information in electronic form if that electronic form satisfies all the following conditions:

1. It contains the same or substantially the same information as required in the prescribed form;

2. It is viewable, readable, and retrievable for future use; and

3. It allows the recipient of the information to retain it.

Article 10.- Requirements for Record Retention

1. Where any provision requires certain documents, records, or information to be retained, such requirement is deemed to be satisfied if the retention satisfies all the following conditions:

a. The information contained in the electronic communication is accessible and usable for future reference;

b. The electronic communication is retained in its original form as created, sent, or received, or in a form that can demonstrate the integrity of the information; and

c. Such information is retained to enable any person to identify the origin and destination of the electronic communication, as well as the date and time it was sent or received.

2. Obligations to retain documents, records, or information under paragraph 1 do not include any information used solely for the purpose of sending or receiving the message.

Article 11.- Requirements for Evidence

No provision in the application of rules of evidence shall prevent the admissibility of electronic communications as evidence solely on the grounds that:

1. The evidence is an electronic communication; or

2. The evidence is not in its original form.

Article 12.- Formation and Validity of Contracts

1. Offers and contracts may be made through electronic means.

2. A contract in electronic form, as provided in decimal 1 above, shall be valid, legally effective, and enforceable when the offer is accepted and agreed upon.

Article 13.- Effect Between Parties

The sender and recipient of an electronic communication shall not deny the legal effect, validity, and enforceability of their declarations of intent or other statements.

Article 14.- Separate Implementation by Agreement of the Parties

1. By agreement, parties involved in the formation, sending, receiving, retaining, or processing of electronic communications may implement provisions differently from those in Chapter 2 of this Law, unless otherwise determined.

2. Notwithstanding the provisions of Chapter 2, parties to a contract or any transaction may:

a. Not use electronic records, electronic communications, or electronic signatures in any contract or transaction by agreement; or

b. Set additional requirements related to the form or authenticity of the contract or transaction by agreement.

### CHAPTER 3
### PROCESS OF ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

Article 15.- Time and Place of Sending and Receiving Information in Electronic Communications

1. Information in an electronic communication is deemed to have been sent when:

a. The information has left the information system under the control of the sender or the party sending it on behalf of the sender; or

b. In cases where the information has been sent but has not yet left the information system under the control of the sender or the party sending it on behalf of the sender, the information is deemed to have been sent when it is received at any time.

2. Information in an electronic communication is presumed to have been sent from the sender’s place of business, regardless of where the information was actually sent from.

3. The time of receipt of information is the time when the information is retrievable by the recipient at an electronic address previously specified by the recipient. The information is presumed to have been received at the recipient’s place of business.

4. If an electronic address was not provided by the recipient, the time of receipt of information is the time when the recipient becomes aware of the sending and is able to retrieve that information through any electronic address, and it is presumed to have been received at the recipient’s place of business.

5. If an electronic address was provided by the recipient, the information in the electronic communication is presumed to have been received when that information reaches the recipient’s electronic address and is deemed to have been received at the recipient’s place of business.

Article 16.- Proposal to Form a Contract

Any request to enter into a contract via electronic communication that is not addressed to any specific person shall be deemed merely an invitation to make an offer. Such request shall be deemed an offer if it clearly indicates the intent of the person making the request to be bound in case of acceptance by the other party.

Article 17.- Use of Automated Systems for Contract Formation

A contract formed by the interaction between an automated system of any person and another person or with an automated system of another person shall not be denied validity, legal effect, or enforceability solely on the grounds of the absence of human intervention in the activities of the automated system in forming the contract.

Article 18.- Errors in Inputting Information in Electronic Communications

Where a person makes an error in inputting information in an electronic communication with an automated system of another party, and that automated system does not provide an opportunity to correct the error, that person or the party acting on behalf of the person who inputted the information has the right to withdraw or remove the erroneous information in cases where:

1. The person has notified the other party of the error as soon as possible after becoming aware of the error in the electronic communication; and

2. The person has not received any benefit from the erroneous information before notifying the other party and has not caused any damage to the interests of the other party or any third person.

The provisions of this Article shall not apply to the securities sector.

### CHAPTER 4
### SECURE ELECTRONIC RECORDS AND SECURE ELECTRONIC SIGNATURES

Article 19.- Secure Electronic Records

An electronic record is deemed to be a secure electronic record if a proper security procedure has been applied to ensure that the electronic record has not been altered from a specific point in time until the time of verification.

Article 20.- Secure Electronic Signatures

1. An electronic signature is deemed to be a secure electronic signature if it complies with the following conditions:

a. It is uniquely linked to the signatory;

b. It identifies the signatory;

c. It is created using means that are under the control of the signatory;

d. It indicates the time and date of the signature; and

e. It indicates the original status of the message or electronic record to which it is linked.

2. The provisions of paragraph 1 above do not restrict the ability of any person in an electronic communication to:

a. Set additional conditions related to the reliability of the electronic signature; or

b. Provide evidence regarding the unreliability of the electronic signature.

Article 21.- Presumptions Relating to Secure Electronic Records and Signatures

1. Unless there is evidence to the contrary, in proceedings involving a secure electronic record, the secure electronic record is presumed to have not been altered since a specific point in time.

2. Unless there is evidence to the contrary, in proceedings involving a secure electronic signature, a secure electronic signature is presumed as follows:

a. The electronic signature is the signature of the person concerned; and

b. The electronic signature was affixed by that person with the intent to sign or to approve the electronic record.

3. In the absence of a secure electronic record or a secure electronic signature, no provision in Chapter 4 may create a presumption regarding the authenticity or integrity of that electronic record or electronic signature.

Article 22.- Identity Theft

No person shall dishonestly or without authorization use the identity, records, electronic signatures, electronic addresses, passwords, or identification symbols of another person in commercial or non-commercial transactions via electronic systems.

Article 23.- Competent Authorities Governing Security Procedures

1. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications is the competent institution for governing security procedures for electronic records and electronic signatures.

2. The governing of security procedures for electronic records and electronic signatures shall be determined by legal regulations.

### CHAPTER 5
### INTERMEDIARIES AND ELECTRONIC COMMERCE SERVICE PROVIDERS

Article 24.- Liability of Intermediaries and Electronic Commerce Service Providers

1. Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers shall not be civilly or criminally liable regarding information contained in an electronic record related to their services, provided that they are not the ones who created that record and in cases where:

a. The intermediary or electronic commerce service provider did not know that the information sent would cause civil or criminal liability.

b. The intermediary or electronic commerce service provider did not know of facts or circumstances from which it could be presumed that the information would cause civil or criminal liability; or

c. The intermediary or electronic commerce service provider later became aware of information, facts, or circumstances from which it could be presumed that the information

could cause civil or criminal liability, and has implemented procedures regarding illegal information and prohibitions as provided in Article 25 below.

2. Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers are not required to monitor any electronic records processed by electronic systems under their control, even if the processing of such records could constitute an offense or lead to civil liability, unless otherwise determined.

3. The provisions of this Article shall not be applied to exempt intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers from:

a. Obligations created by laws or legal regulations;

b. Obligations to comply with orders or directives of the court or other competent authorities; or

c. Contractual obligations.

Article 25.- Liability for Information or Incidents

1. Where an intermediary or electronic commerce service provider knows that information in a record could cause civil or criminal liability, the intermediary or electronic commerce service provider must take immediate measures as follows:

a. Remove the information from the information system under the control of the intermediary or electronic commerce service provider and cease providing services related to that information.

b. Retain the information as evidence and notify the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications and other relevant ministries and competent institutions regarding the facts and identity of the suspects.

2. Where an intermediary or electronic commerce service provider becomes aware of any incident or situation that could lead to civil or criminal liability, the intermediary or electronic commerce service provider must retain the information as evidence and notify the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications and other relevant ministries and competent institutions.

3. Upon becoming aware of or receiving news related to information in an electronic record that could cause civil or criminal liability, the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications and

other relevant ministries and competent institutions may order the intermediary or electronic commerce service provider to:

a. Remove the information from the information system under its control.

b. Suspend or cease providing services to that person; or

c. Suspend or cease providing services related to the electronic record.

4. Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers shall not be held civilly liable for breach of contract or tort, or as prescribed by law, for actions taken in good faith following orders from the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications or other relevant ministries and competent institutions.

5. Any person who notifies an intermediary or electronic commerce service provider of illegal activities, knowing that such notification is false or misleading, shall be held civilly or criminally liable for such acts.

Article 26.- Granting of Permits or Licenses

1. Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers must apply for permission or licenses from the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications.

2. Licenses or permits granted by the Ministry of Commerce include:

a. Permits for conducting business via electronic systems for natural persons.

b. Licenses for providing electronic commerce services for legal entities.

3. The Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications shall provide online service certificates.

4. Exemptions, classification of licenses, formalities, and procedures for granting permissions or licenses shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 27.- Code of Professional Ethics and Regulations for Intermediaries and Electronic Commerce Service Providers

1. Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers must comply with the code of professional ethics and regulations related to electronic commerce as determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications.

2. The code of professional ethics mentioned in paragraph 1 above shall be determined and drafted by the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications in cooperation with relevant ministries, institutions, and parties.

Article 28.- Tax Obligations, Incentives, and Exemptions

Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers shall be subject to existing taxation procedures and shall receive various incentives and exemptions in accordance with existing laws and regulations.

### CHAPTER 6
### CONSUMER PROTECTION

Article 29.- Minimum Information in Electronic Commerce

1. A person using electronic communications for selling goods or services to consumers shall provide accurate, clear, and understandable information, and shall at least include the following:

a. The legal name or identification of the person, registered business address, and electronic means for communication or telephone number.

b. Quick, easy, and effective forms of communication between the consumer and the seller.

c. Terms, conditions, and costs of goods or services related to commercial transactions, especially terms, conditions, and payment methods, and details regarding the withdrawal or cancellation of orders, termination, delivery, exchange of goods, and refunds. And

d. The actual goods and services offered for sale.

2. The information mentioned in paragraph 1 above must be sufficient to enable consumers to make decisions on commercial transactions and retain them as information.

3. The provisions of this Article shall not apply to the securities and insurance sectors.

Article 30.- Unsolicited Commercial Communications

Any person, whether having a place of business inside or outside the Kingdom of Cambodia, who makes unsolicited commercial communications to consumers located inside or outside the Kingdom of Cambodia through broadcast systems, via intermediaries, or telecommunications service providers, shall provide clear and easy options for consumers to opt-out or reject such unsolicited commercial communications.

Article 31.- Creation of Electronic Systems in Windows and Pop-ups

1. No person shall create an electronic system for the purpose of falsifying or causing confusion to gain benefit, or for the purpose of attracting use or transactions leading to damage for the person using it or for third persons.

2. No person shall create, provide, facilitate, distribute, or send malicious codes for dishonest purposes through electronic means into the devices or electronic systems of other persons.

Article 32.- Data Protection

1. A person who retains private information in electronic form shall use all means to ensure that such information is protected with reasonable security in all circumstances to avoid loss, access, use, alteration, distribution, or disclosure of such information, unless authorized by the owner of that information or by other parties authorized by law.

2. No person shall interfere with an electronic system, access, retrieve, copy, extract, leak, eliminate, or alter data stored by another person dishonestly or without authorization.

Article 33.- Application of Provisions Related to Consumer Protection

Persons using electronic communications in commercial activities with consumers shall also comply with various provisions related to consumer protection.

### CHAPTER 7
### GOVERNMENT ACTIVITIES AND TRANSACTIONS VIA ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS

Article 34.- Recognition of Electronic Communications by Government Institutions

1. Ministries or government institutions may use electronic forms for activities and transactions as follows:

a. Receiving the registration of documents or fulfilling requirements for creating or retaining documents;

b. Issuing licenses and permits;

c. Determining methods and procedures for payment; or

d. Other activities determined by the ministry or government institution.

2. Where a ministry or government institution decides to use electronic communications for any activity or transaction as mentioned in paragraph 1 above, that ministry or institution may specify:

a. The manner and form in which information in the electronic communication must be received, registered, created, retained, or printed;

b. The type of signature required where a signature is required in such electronic communication;

c. The manner and form in which such signature must be attached to the electronic communication;

d. Appropriate management plans and procedures to ensure the integrity, security, and confidentiality of electronic communications or payments; and

e. Any other conditions establishing the equivalence between documents or payments in electronic form and documents or payments in writing.

### CHAPTER 8
### ELECTRONIC EVIDENCE

Article 35.- General Admissibility of Electronic Evidence

No rules of evidence shall be applied to deny the general admissibility of an electronic record as evidence solely on the grounds that it is an electronic record, even if it is not in its original form.

Article 36.- Integrity of Electronic Systems

1. In legal proceedings, the validity of electronic evidence may be established through the integrity of the electronic system in which the data was recorded or stored.

2. In the absence of evidence to the contrary, an electronic system used for recording or storing electronic records is presumed to have integrity in legal proceedings under any of the following conditions:

a. There is evidence supporting the fact that the electronic system or other similar devices were operating properly at the time the electronic record was recorded or stored when it is produced as evidence, or in cases where the electronic system or other similar devices were not operating properly or were out of order but did not affect the integrity of the electronic record, and there are no reasonable grounds to challenge the integrity of that record.

b. There is evidence showing that the electronic record was recorded or stored by a party whose interests are adverse to the party using that electronic record as evidence in legal proceedings; or

c. There is evidence showing that the electronic record was recorded or stored in the ordinary course of business by a person who is not a party to the legal proceedings and who did not record or store the electronic record under the control of the party using that electronic record as evidence in the legal proceedings.

Article 37.- Integrity of Information

1. In the absence of evidence to the contrary, an electronic record recorded or stored in an electronic system is presumed to have integrity in legal proceedings if the electronic record:

a. Is complete and unaltered apart from any added metadata or insignificant changes occurring in the ordinary course of communication, storage, or display;

b. Is certified and signed in electronic form through means provided by a properly licensed security procedure provider;

c. Has integrity and content certified by a ministry or competent institution;

d. Is recorded in a storage device or other electronic means that does not allow alteration of the electronic record;

e. Has been monitored and certified for integrity by a court-appointed expert;

f. There is evidence supporting the fact that the electronic system or other similar devices were operating properly at all material times, or in cases where the electronic system or other similar devices were not operating properly or were out of order but the integrity of the record was not affected by such situation, and there are no other reasonable grounds to doubt the integrity of the record; or

g. There is evidence showing that the electronic record was recorded or stored by a party involved in the legal proceedings whose interests are adverse to the party seeking to use that electronic record.

2. Where there is evidence showing that the electronic record was recorded as a matter of course and for ordinary business purposes by a person who is not a party to the legal proceedings and who did not record or store the electronic record under the control of the party seeking to use that electronic record.

3. Where a report is recorded in an electronic record created by an electronic system and is not deemed hearsay, such reports are admissible if the conditions mentioned in paragraph 2 regarding that electronic record are satisfied.

Article 38.- Printed Documents

A document printed correctly based on the original content from an electronic record may be used as evidence in legal proceedings.

Article 39.- Burden of Proof for Authenticity of Electronic Evidence

1. Any person seeking to produce an electronic record in any legal proceedings shall bear the burden of proving the authenticity of the electronic record with evidence supporting the produced record.

2. Where any provision protects persons by preferential treatment, including consumers and children, and determines the burden of proof in a way that benefits those persons more than this Law, that provision shall prevail over this Article.

Article 40.- Standards for Recording or Storing Electronic Records

To determine whether an electronic record should be admitted or not under other provisions, the production of evidence may be based on standards, procedures, use, or practice of recording or storing electronic records, considering the type of business, nature, and purpose of that electronic record.

Article 41.- Issuance of Certificates of Authenticity

1. Where any person intends to prove the authenticity of an electronic record as evidence, that person may request such evidence from an expert related to the authenticity of that electronic record in the form of a certificate.

2. Certificates of authenticity mentioned in paragraph 1 above shall be issued by:

a. A competent ministry or institution, or a person responsible for the operation or management of a specific security procedure generator, or a security procedure generator recognized by the court; or

b. An expert appointed or recognized by the court.

3. Competent persons for issuing certificates of authenticity, as mentioned in paragraph 2 above, shall not falsely issue certificates of authenticity for records that are fraudulent electronic recordings that are not true.

Article 42.- Other Procedures for Establishing Electronic Evidence

In addition to the means of producing evidence mentioned in this Law, electronic evidence may be established through other techniques and procedures such as testimony from witnesses or competent institutions, electronic records that do not allow alteration, and forensic results on electronic systems as found by court experts.

Article 43.- Abuse of Coding

No person shall insert codes in electronic commercial communications as evidence that could lead to prosecution, or insert codes on data or evidence in electronic systems related to an offense.

Article 44.- Admissibility of Electronic Records from Abroad

Electronic evidence certified by competent ministries or institutions of foreign states shall be admissible as evidence if the electronic system recording or storing that electronic record has integrity in accordance with the standards set out in Chapter 8 of this Law.

Article 45.- Admissibility of Electronic Information from Abroad

To determine whether information in electronic form is admissible or not, or admissible to what extent, the location where such information was created or used, or the place of business created abroad, shall not be considered, provided that the electronic record is accessible and retrievable within the jurisdiction of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### CHAPTER 9
### PAYMENTS AND/OR FUND TRANSFERS VIA ELECTRONIC SYSTEMS

Article 46.- Payment Transactions via Electronic Systems

1. Any person who operates a payment system, provides payment services, issues electronic payment instruments, or provides means for payment, or performs any transaction deemed to be providing payment to the public via electronic systems shall apply for prior permission or a license from the National Bank of Cambodia.

2. The National Bank of Cambodia is the competent institution for establishing regulations related to payments via electronic systems.

Article 47.- Liability of Payment Service Providers

1. A payment service provider shall not provide a consumer with a payment instrument that the consumer has not requested, except where the payment card is an expired card or is due for replacement.

2. Before performing an electronic fund transfer, the service provider shall clearly verify the consumer’s identity and double-check the accuracy of the electronic fund transfer. The payment service provider shall be liable in the following cases:

a. Transactions occurring without authorization from the consumer;

b. Transactions occurring after knowing of fraud, loss, or theft of the electronic fund transfer instrument;

c. Transactions occurring after notification from the consumer as provided in Article 48 below;

d. Failure to comply with the consumer’s order or performing a transaction different from the consumer’s order; or

e. Irregularities in the technical system, errors in using the technical system, or deficiencies in the electronic fund transfer instrument.

3. A payment service provider may be exempted from liability only in cases of force majeure or errors made by the consumer themselves.

4. Where the payment service provider is liable, the payment service provider shall compensate the consumer for damages within no more than 30 (thirty) days from the date of receiving notification from the consumer as provided in Article 48 of this Law, for the value of the lost transaction, fees, and penalties for late payment arising from this transaction, without yet including other damages that may occur to the consumer.

5. No person shall issue a payment card or alter a payment card into any form different from its original form without authorization for dishonest purposes, fraud, or forgery leading to activity against another person.

Article 48.- Liability of Consumers

Consumers shall notify the payment service provider of transactions occurring without authorization, errors, or other inaccuracies in their accounts.

2. Consumers shall notify the payment service provider or institutions authorized by the payment service provider of loss or theft of electronic transfer instruments/means or data theft for use with payment instruments. Such notification may be made in writing or through any electronic means accepted by the payment service provider, and must be sent as soon as possible or within no more than 2 (two) days after becoming aware of the theft or loss of the payment instrument.

3. Where there is sufficient evidence proving that the loss or damage in the cases mentioned above was the fault of the consumer, the payment service provider has the right to hold the consumer fully liable for such loss or damage.

4. Where there is no evidence proving the fault of the consumer, the payment service provider shall be held fully and unconditionally liable for such loss or damage.

### CHAPTER 10
### COMPETENCE, COMPLAINTS, AND DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURES

Article 49.- Competence

Written warnings, suspension of business activities or operations, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of permits or licenses, and the imposition of penalties are within the competence of the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications.

In cases where a person fails to comply with the measures above, the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications may file a lawsuit in a competent court.

Article 50.- Complaint Procedures

1. Any person dissatisfied with a written warning, suspension of business activities or operations, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of a permit or license, and the imposition of penalties by the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications has the right to file a complaint to the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Posts and Telecommunications.

2. The Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Posts and Telecommunications shall decide within no more than 30 (thirty) days.

3. Any person dissatisfied with the decision of the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Posts and Telecommunications has the right to file an appeal to the competent court of the Kingdom of Cambodia within no more than 30 (thirty) days from the date of receiving notification of that decision.

Article 51.- Disciplinary Procedures

The procedures for imposing penalties, payment of fines, management of fine receipts, and allocation of revenue from penalties as provided in this Law shall be determined by inter-ministerial prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance, the Minister of Commerce, and the Minister of Posts and Telecommunications.

### CHAPTER 11
### PENAL PROVISIONS

Article 52.- Sanctions

Sanctions under this Law include written warnings, suspension of business activities or operations, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of permits or licenses, fines, imprisonment, and monetary penalties.

Article 53.- Offenses of Identity Theft

Any person who violates the provisions of Article 22 shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 3 (three) years and a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 6,000,000 (six million) Riels.

Article 54.- Offenses of Non-Reporting of Information or Incidents

Intermediaries and electronic commerce service providers who violate the provisions of paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of Article 25 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Riels to 2,000,000 (two million) Riels.

Article 55.- Offenses of Providing False Information

Any person who commits acts as provided in paragraph 5 of Article 25 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Riels to 2,000,000 (two million) Riels.

Article 56.- Operating Without Permission

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 26 shall be ordered to cease business activities or operations and shall be fined no more than 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels.

In case of recidivism after a final decision as mentioned in paragraph 1 above, the offender shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 6,000,000 (six million) Riels.

Article 57.- Offenses of Non-Compliance with Minimum Information Requirements in Electronic Commerce

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of Article 29 shall be given a written warning.

In case of recidivism after a written warning as mentioned in paragraph 1 above, the offender shall face suspension or revocation of their license or permit as an electronic commerce service provider.

Article 58.- Offenses of Making Unsolicited Commercial Communications

Any person who violates the provisions of Article 30 shall be given a written warning.

In case of recidivism after a written warning as mentioned in paragraph 1 above, the offender shall face suspension or revocation of their license or permit as an intermediary or electronic commerce service provider, or the termination of the broadcast system or electronic means of the sender.

Article 59.- Offenses of Creating Electronic Systems in Windows and Pop-ups

Any person who violates the provisions of Article 31 shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 3 (three) years and a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 6,000,000 (six million) Riels.

Article 60.- Offenses of Non-Compliance with Data Protection Obligations

Any person retaining private information in electronic form who violates the provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 32 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels.

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 2 of Article 32 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 2 (two) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels.

Article 61.- Offenses of Violating the Issuance of Certificates of Authenticity

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 3 of Article 41 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Riels to 2,000,000 (two million) Riels.

Article 62.- Offenses of Violating Coding Laws

Any person who violates the provisions of Article 43 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 6,000,000 (six million) Riels.

Article 63.- Offenses of Performing Payment Transactions via Electronic Systems

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 1 of Article 46 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels.

Article 64.- Offenses of Altering Payment Cards or Electronic Fund Transfers

Any person who violates the provisions of paragraph 5 of Article 47 shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 6,000,000 (six million) Riels.

Article 65.- Criminal Liability of Legal Entities

1. A legal entity may be declared criminally liable under the conditions provided in Article 42 (Criminal Liability of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code for offenses provided in Articles 53 to 55, paragraph 2 of Article 56, and Articles 59 to 64 of this Law.

2. Legal entities shall be punished by a double fine, as well as one or more of the following additional penalties:

a. Dissolution as procedures provided in Article 170 (Dissolution and Liquidation of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code;

b. Placement under judicial supervision as procedures provided in Article 171 (Placement under Judicial Supervision) of the Criminal Code;

c. Prohibition from performing one or more activities as procedures provided in Article 172 (Prohibition from Activities) of the Criminal Code;

d. Public display of the sentence as procedures provided in Article 180 (Public Display of Decision) of the Criminal Code;

e. Publication of the sentence in written media, electronic media, or through all means of audiovisual telecommunications as procedures provided in Article 181 (Publication of Decision through Audiovisual Telecommunications Means etc.) of the Criminal Code.

### CHAPTER 12
### FINAL PROVISIONS

Article 66.- Abrogation

Any provisions contrary to this Law shall be abrogated.

Article 67.- Implementation

After becoming effective, this Law shall be disseminated for a period of 6 (six) months before starting its implementation.

### ANNEX
### DEFINITIONS

The key terms used in this Law shall be defined as follows:

1. Access means any entry into a computer program, computer system, or electronic network for the purpose of communicating, sending, storing, or retrieving data, information, or other documents within the electronic system through all means.

2. Addressee means a person who receives the electronic communication as intended by the originator, but does not include an intermediary involved in making the electronic communication.

3. Automated System means a computer program, computer system, electronic network, or other electronic means used for reporting or responding automatically without intervention by any natural person.

4. Card means a bank card, credit/debit card, smart card, or payment card, even if in electronic form that stored value and has payment functions.

5. Contaminant means any electronic program created for the purpose of:

(a) altering, damaging, recording, or sending data or programs in an electronic system; or

(b) interfering by any means with the ordinary operation of the system or electronic network.

6. Cardholder means a person whose name is inscribed on the card and who receives benefit from using such payment card.

7. Communication means tokens, statements, claims, notifications, requests, offers, or acceptances that a party makes for the purpose of decision making regarding the formation or performance of a contract.

8. Counterfeit card means any card created through fake presentation or description, altered, forged, or cards containing any illegal components.

9. Data means a set of numbers, letters, signs, messages, images, sound, video, information, or electronic programs prepared in an appropriate form for use in a database or electronic system.

10. Electronic means relating to technology having electrical, digital, magnetic, optical, biometric, electrochemical, wireless, electromagnetic, or other functional similar properties.

11. Electronic Address means a number or address used for sending or receiving notifications, documents, information, or communications by electronic means.

12. Authenticity means the accuracy and reliability of an electronic communication.

13. Electronic Commerce means the activity of buying, selling, renting, or exchanging goods or services, including commercial and civil activities, as well as government activities and transactions performed via electronic systems.

14. Electronic Payment Instrument means a payment card or any other payment instrument in electronic form that has payment functions.

15. Electronic Communication means information that is communicated, processed, recorded, displayed, created, stored, received, or sent through electronic means.

16. Electronic Device means any device created to control, direct, and operate via an electronic system.

17. Electronic Evidence means information, data, or documents created, stored, sent, or received in electronic form or communication for use as a reference regarding any facts in legal proceedings, and where such information, data, or documents have authenticity in accordance with the Law on Electronic Commerce.

18. Electronic Form means a format of information, data, or documents created, sent, received, or stored in an electronic system.

19. Electronic Fund Transfer means any transaction initiated through an electronic fund transfer instruction or through other electronic instruments or similar devices to instruct a bank or financial institution to debit or credit an account.

20. Electronic record means a record created, communicated, received, stored, or processed in an electronic system or for transmission from one electronic system to another.

21. Electronic Commerce service provider means a person using electronic means to supply goods and/or services, excluding insurance institutions.

22. Electronic Signature means any signature created by electronic means for identifying the signatory, including digital signatures, biometric signatures, and other signatures.

23. Electronic system means an electronic device or a group of interconnected devices communicating through electronic programs, which can perform automated processing of data, information, or documents, including electronic devices for storing such data, information, or documents.

24. Information system means a system for creating, sending, receiving, storing, or processing electronic records.

25. Intermediary means a person who provides services for sending, receiving, transmitting, or storing electronic communications temporarily or permanently, or who provides other services related to electronic communications, including:

1. Persons representing the originator, addressee, transmitter, or storer;

2. Telecommunications service providers;

3. Network service providers;

4. Internet service providers;

5. Search engine service providers;

6. Online payment service providers;

7. Online auction service providers;

8. Online marketplace and e-commerce platform service providers.

26. Issuer means a bank, financial institution, or any person who issues payment cards and has received authorization.

27. Malicious code means a program or a hidden function of a program that infects data, information, or documents with or without leaving traces of such infection in the infected electronic documents, and which can further replicate itself within the system autonomously or with human intervention.

28. Originator means a party or a representative who initiated the creation, storage, or transmission of data by electronic means, but does not include an intermediary.

29. Payment Service Provider means a bank, financial institution, or other person who has received authorization or a license from the National Bank of Cambodia to operate a payment system, issue electronic payment instruments or electronic payment means, or perform any transaction deemed to be providing electronic payment to the public.

30. Place of business means:

a. The location indicated by the originator or the addressee, unless a party shows that the person making such indication does not have a place of business at that location;

b. If a party has not indicated a specific place of business and has more than one place of business, the place of business is that which has the closest relationship to the relevant contract, considering circumstances known to or contemplated by the parties at any time before or at the time of the conclusion of the contract;

c. For a natural person who does not have a place of business, the place of business means their habitual residence.

The following shall not by itself be considered a place of business of a party:

- A location where equipment or technical devices of an information system are used;

- An electronic address or domain name connected to the party’s location.

31. Record means information, data, or documents recorded, stored, or inputted in physical form or stored in electronic form, code, or other formats, and which can be retrieved in a perceivable form.

32. Service provider means:

(a) A person providing information and communication services, including sending, receiving, storing, or processing electronic communications or providing other electronic services;

(b) A person who owns, occupies, operates, manages, or controls a Public Switched Network or a person providing telecommunications services; or

(c) Any other person operating data storage for electronic communication services, or a user of such services.

33. Security procedure provider means a person who creates or provides security procedures.

34. Security Procedure means a procedure determined by law or contract or decided by any party, used for the purpose of:

(a) verifying that a signature, communication, or transaction in an electronic system is indeed that of a specific person;

(b) detecting errors or alterations in a communication or in the storage place of electronic records since a specific time.

The security procedure mentioned above may involve the use of algorithms or codes, passwords for identification, encryption, secret questions, or recognition of any procedure or similar security devices.

35. Writing/Written means information or other records that are written, inscribed, or printed on paper or other materials.

36. E-mail or Electronic Message means information created, sent, or received from an electronic system, including attachments in text, images, sound, video, and any other electronic records that can be sent with the message.

37. Will means the transfer of rights and obligations from a deceased person to one or more heirs according to their wishes or by law.

38. Acceptance means an expression of will by which a person gives their consent to an offer made by another person for the purpose of forming a contract.
