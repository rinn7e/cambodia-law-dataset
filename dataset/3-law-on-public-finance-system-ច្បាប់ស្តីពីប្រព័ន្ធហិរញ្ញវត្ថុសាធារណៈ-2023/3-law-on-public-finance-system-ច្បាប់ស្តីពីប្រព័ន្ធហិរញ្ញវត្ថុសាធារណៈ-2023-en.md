# Promulgates

The Law on the Public Finance System, which was adopted by the National Assembly on February 08, 2023, during the 6th session of its 6th legislature, and which was reviewed in its entirety by the Senate on February 20, 2023, during the 4th session of its 4th legislature, with the following full text:

# Law

# On

# Public Finance System

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

### Article 1 .-

This law defines the fundamental principles for the management of the public finance system and the preparation of financial laws, especially each stage of the process of preparation, approval, and execution of the budget, and the monitoring of budget results, as well as the definition of functions and responsibilities of relevant actors to achieve budget credibility, financial accountability, linking budget to policy, and performance accountability, with the aim of ensuring effectiveness, transparency, accountability, and sustainability of public finance management in supporting macroeconomic stability, economic growth, and social equity.

### Article 2 .-

This law is the basic law defining the management of the public finance system and has the scope of application to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, public administrative establishments, and other legal entities that benefit from public finance, as well as the relationships and interactions between national public finance, international public finance, national private finance, and international private finance.

### Article 3 .-

Key terms used in this law are defined in the Glossary, which is an annex to this law.

# Chapter 2
# Fundamental Principles, Budget, and State Resources and Charges

## Section 1
## Fundamental Principles

### Article 4 .-

The budget must have 10 (ten) fundamental principles that must be strictly implemented as follows:

1- Authoritativeness.

2- Credibility.

3- Unity.

4- Universality.

5- Specialization.

6- Balance.

7- Accountability.

8- Effectiveness.

9- Transparency.

10- Performance.

### Article 5 .-

Financial laws include:

1- Annual Financial Law.

2- Revised Financial Law.

3- Budget Settlement Law.

### Article 6 .-

The Annual Financial Law plans and authorizes, for a one-year fiscal period, the sources of state resources and charges, surplus or deficit, and various financings, by ensuring budget balance and maintaining macroeconomic and financial stability, and may also define necessary provisions related to the management of public finance to serve the policy agenda of the Royal Government in socio-economic development.

### Article 7 .-

The Revised Financial Law authorizes amendments to the provisions of the Annual Financial Law if there are necessary requirements to respond to changing circumstances, such as disasters, sudden changes in the macroeconomic situation, or other urgent needs of substantial relevance to the national interest that were not anticipated in the fiscal year.

### Article 8 .-

The Budget Settlement Law is adopted annually on the final results of the execution of the budget and other state finances, as well as non-financial information related to the execution of the financial law in previous fiscal years.

### Article 9 .-

The Prime Minister and the Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue legal regulations under the law within their competence to ensure the effective implementation of this law and the laws as stated in Article 5 above.

### Article 10 .-

In addition to the provisions stated in this law, the management of finance and properties of sub-national administrations is subject to a separate law.

### Article 11 .-

The fiscal year begins on January 1 and ends on December 31.

### Article 12 .-

Relationships and interactions between national public finance, international public finance, national private finance, and international private finance refer to the direct or indirect relations between national public finance, international public finance, national private finance, and international private finance that occur through state management or monitoring, either directly or indirectly, to ensure macroeconomic and public financial sustainability and socio-economic development.

Such relationships and interactions include, among others, financial policy instruments, borrowing and lending operations, state guarantees, investment in financial assets, joint ventures both domestic and abroad, signing of bilateral and multilateral free trade agreements, public-private partnership projects, issuance of financial instruments, establishment of state-private joint ventures, membership contributions to international or intergovernmental institutions, fiscal and non-fiscal concessions, use of state financial reserve funds, and the implementation of special programs of the Royal Government to intervene in solving socio-economic crises.

The relationships and interactions mentioned above must be managed by the provisions of this law, as well as applicable legal provisions and regulations.

### Article 13 .-

The management, preparation, execution, monitoring, and auditing of revenue and expenditure implementation, and financial and accounting operations of the state budget, as well as state property management, must use and be reflected in a single information technology system for public finance management. Common rules and procedures for using the information technology system for public finance management shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance may produce physical or electronic documents, including the draft annual financial law and its attached documents, and the draft budget settlement law and its attached documents, in written form for up to 5 (five) copies. Additionally, it shall be in digital form for the Office of the Council of Ministers for the plenary session of the Council of Ministers, relevant ministries, institutions, and the legislative bodies.

### Article 14 .-

Public finance professional officials of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, and public administrative establishments whose duty is to implement public finance tasks must have a professional framework according to each type of job position. The professional qualifications for each type of job position shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance and other applicable regulations.

## Section 2
## Budget

### Article 15 .-

Budget refers to the state's financial plan approved in the financial law, including the planning of revenue and expenditure, borrowing, and financing to fill the budget balance to achieve planned performance.

All revenues must be allocated for all expenditure items.

The budget approved in the Annual Financial Law is the basis for ordering expenditures.

All revenues and expenditures must be recorded in the Treasury Single Account.

### Article 16 .-

The national budget or state budget includes the national level budget, sub-national level budget or sub-national administration budget, annex budget, and special accounts of the Treasury.

### Article 17 .-

National level budget refers to the budget of ministries and institutions, which includes the budget of the central administration, the budget of public administrative establishments, and the budget of specialized departments under their supervision, as well as the budget of similar public entities.

The national level budget must be planned, authorized, and executed according to the rules and procedures defined in this law.

### Article 18 .-

Sub-national administration budget refers to the budget of capital and provincial administrations, the budget of municipality, district and khan administrations, and the budget of commune and sangkat administrations, as well as other relevant sub-national administration budgets in accordance with applicable regulations.

The sub-national administration budget must be planned, authorized, and executed according to the rules and procedures defined in this law and laws related to the management of finance and properties of sub-national administrations.

### Article 19 .-

Annex budget at the national level applies to state entities with legal personality that perform operations primarily of an industrial and commercial nature, by allowing separate revenue for specific expenditures. Annex budgets must be created and abolished by financial law. Operations of the annex budget must be planned, authorized, and executed under the same conditions as the national level budget. Reallocation of budget credits between the annex budget and other types of budgets shall not be permitted. Rules and procedures for managing the annex budget shall be determined by Sub-decree.

Annex budget at the sub-national administration level applies to state entities with legal personality that perform public service operations of an industrial, commercial, and administrative nature, by allowing separate revenue for specific expenditures. Annex budgets at the sub-national administration level must be created and abolished by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance. Operations of the annex budget at the sub-national administration level must be planned, authorized, and executed under the same conditions as the sub-national administration budget.

Reallocation of budget credits between the annex budget and other types of budgets at the sub-national administration level shall not be permitted. Rules and procedures for managing the annex budget at the sub-national administration level shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 20 .-

Special accounts of the Treasury apply to state entities without legal personality that perform primarily administrative operations, to serve the implementation of policies or specific missions of the Royal Government, by allowing separate revenue for specific expenditures. Special accounts of the Treasury must be created and abolished by financial law.

Operations of the special accounts of the Treasury must be planned, authorized, and executed under the same conditions as the national level budget. Reallocation of budget credits between the special accounts of the Treasury and other types of budgets shall not be permitted.

Rules and procedures for managing special accounts of the Treasury shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 21 .-

Public Administrative Establishments (PAEs) are legal entities under public law with an autonomous budget. PAE budgets must be prepared in accordance with the policy objectives and programs defined by the ministry, institution, or sub-national administration, and must be reflected as an annex to the budget of the ministry, institution, or sub-national administration that is the technical guardian.

Regular sources of resources for PAEs include self-generated revenue and occasional balancing subsidies from the state budget and other revenues.

PAEs must be accountable to the respective ministry, institution, or sub-national administration that is the technical guardian, and to the Ministry of Economy and Finance which is the financial guardian, in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

Budget operations of PAEs at the national level must be planned, authorized, and executed generally in the same pattern as those of the national level budget. Budget operations of PAEs at the sub-national level must be planned, authorized, and executed generally in the same pattern as those of the sub-national administration budget.

Rules and procedures for planning revenue and expenditure, authorization, and execution of PAE budgets shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 22 .-

The classification of the state budget contents includes economic classification, program classification, administrative classification, functional classification, project classification, and fund classification.

Detailed rules and implementation of the above budget content classification shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

## Section 3
## State Resources and Charges

### Article 23 .-

State resources and charges include budget resources and charges and financing resources and charges.

Budget resources and charges must be shown in the state budget in the form of revenue and expenditure.

Financing resources and charges must be shown in the form of financing requirements and financing sources.

### Article 24 .-

Budget resources include the following:

1- Tax revenue, including domestic tax revenue, tax revenue on international trade, and other tax revenues.

2- Non-tax revenue, including revenue from the management of state property, revenue from public enterprises, public shares and other financially autonomous entities, revenue from public services, revenue from fines and penalties, and other non-tax revenues as determined by law and applicable regulations.

3- Other revenues, including revenue from long-term financial assets, grants, repayment of loans, and various other revenues.

Each resource must be classified into chapters, accounts, sub-accounts, and if necessary, further detailed by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 25 .-

The creation, modification, abolition, levy, determination of rates, determination of assessment bases, exemptions, and concessions of taxes and duties, as well as rules for resolving tax disputes, must be determined by law.

No legal provision or provision under the law of any type, and no international agreement, can result in changes leading to the loss of tax revenue. Such cases can only occur if implemented in accordance with applicable legal forms after obtaining consent from the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The collection and audit of tax revenue shall be under the responsibility of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

### Article 26 .-

The management of non-tax revenue shall be determined by the Law on Non-Tax Revenue Management. The collection of non-tax revenue shall be under the audit of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations that collect non-tax revenue must pay all such non-tax revenue into the Treasury Single Account, without using any portion of the revenue for direct offset against expenditures or for any special expenditure, except where authorized in principle by the Royal Government upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 27 .-

Revenue from the management of state property refers to revenue obtained from the management, business operations, proceeds from concessions on state property, and other revenues from state property as determined by law and applicable regulations.

The management, use, and disposal of state property must comply with the Law on the Management, Use, and Disposal of State Property.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance performs the function as the state property management authority and as the staff of the Royal Government in the management, use, and disposal of state property. Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, Public Administrative Establishments, and other public legal entities are the state property holding authorities.

### Article 28 .-

Revenue from public enterprises, public shares, and other financially autonomous entities includes revenue obtained from the payment of dividends or budget surplus, dividends from capital, as well as the payment of cash and cash equivalents after the enterprise or entity is dissolved. The payment of revenue into the national budget by public enterprises, public shares, and other financially autonomous entities shall be determined by law and applicable regulations.

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to decide to require public enterprises and other financially autonomous entities to pay a portion or all of the excess capital into the national budget, in case it is found that the public enterprise or other financially autonomous entity has no necessary need for investment or development of said public enterprise or other financially autonomous entity.

### Article 29 .-

Revenue from public services refers to revenue collected from the payment of fees for using public services from natural or legal persons, for state-paid services.

Public services must be implemented by ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, and Public Administrative Establishments with the aim of serving general interests. Under the authorization of the Royal Government, public services may be provided by private parties through contracts signed between the private party and the ministry, institution, or similar public entity, or between the private party and the sub-national administration, or between the private party and the Public Administrative Establishment. Such contracts must have the consent of the Minister of Economy and Finance or an entity authorized by the Minister of Economy and Finance, in accordance with applicable regulations.

Public service fees are subject to joint Prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the respective Minister or Head of the institution.

### Article 30 .-

Legal instruments creating non-tax revenue, including contracts, agreements, and/or other relevant legal instruments, must be under the audit and consensus of the Minister of Economy and Finance in accordance with applicable regulations; otherwise, they shall be considered null and void.

### Article 31 .-

Other revenues, as described in point 3 of Article 24, excluding revenues belonging to the categories of tax revenue and non-tax revenue mentioned above, must be managed and implemented under the determination of laws and applicable legal regulations.

### Article 32 .-

State budget charges include current expenditure and capital expenditure, which are divided into 6 types as follows:

1- Personnel charges, including expenditures on salaries, allowances, and various subsidies, social contributions, social quotas, and other benefits.

2- Operating expenditure, including expenditures on the purchase of goods, maintenance and repairs, and service rentals to obtain means and various services to serve state activities.

3- Financial charges, including interest payments and other relevant charges on domestic and international public debt.

4- Investment expenditure, including expenditures on the acquisition of long-term assets, both tangible and intangible, as well as long-term financial assets.

5- Transfers:

- Intervention expenditure, including expenditures on providing social benefits, social intervention, economic intervention, and cultural intervention.

- Subsidies with conditions and without conditions to sub-national administrations.

- Subsidies to Public Administrative Establishments.

- Subsidies to public enterprises.

- Donations.

- Other subsidies.

6- Other expenditures, including taxes and duties paid by the state, unallocated expenditures, unforeseen expenditures, and other expenditures.

Each type of expenditure must be classified into chapters, accounts, and sub-accounts by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 33 .-

Financing resources and charges that help ensure the state's financial balance are the results of the following operations:

1- Movements of state reserve deposits.

2- Management of depositors' accounts.

3- Borrowing, similar debt, and cooperation financing.

4- Other cash operations.

### Article 34 .-

The operations mentioned in Article 33 of this law must comply with a number of fundamental principles as follows:

1- Investment of state reserves must be carried out according to general annual authorization or any special authorization defined by the annual financial law.

2- Depositors' accounts must not have a debit balance.

3- Budgets of sub-national administrations, Public Administrative Establishments, and similar public entities must be deposited into accounts at the National Treasury, except where authorized by the Minister of Economy and Finance.

4- Borrowing, similar debt, and cooperation financing must be implemented according to general annual authorization or any special authorization defined by the annual financial law.

In addition to the financing resources and charges that must be managed according to applicable law, rules and procedures for managing other financing resources and charges mentioned above shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 35 .-

The Financial Reserve Fund is the state's savings fund. The sources of this Financial Reserve Fund include:

- Annual surplus of the national level budget, in the amount of 2 (two) percent to 4 (four) percent of the implementation result.

- National budget implementation revenue from one year prior to the current budget year, as defined in the annual financial law.

- Possible surplus from the annual national level budget implementation, which must be determined by the Budget Settlement Law.

- Other sources as determined in the Financial Law.

The Financial Reserve Fund is saved for the purpose of intervening to solve matters of vital importance when the country faces economic, financial crises, or various urgent emergencies, such as the spread of contagious diseases and natural disasters.

The Financial Reserve Fund may be invested in the short, medium, and long term.

The Financial Reserve Fund must be recorded in the Treasury Single Account. The balance of the Financial Reserve Fund shall be carried forward from year to year.

The use of the Financial Reserve Fund is subject to approval in the Financial Law. The Financial Reserve Fund shall be under the management of the Royal Government. The Minister of Economy and Finance is the staff of the Royal Government in the management and disposal of the Financial Reserve Fund. The Minister of Economy and Finance must report to the National Assembly and the Senate annually on the management, use, and disposal of the Financial Reserve Fund.

Rules and procedures for managing the Financial Reserve Fund shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 36 .-

The Medium-Term Public Finance Framework (MTPFF) estimates state resources and budget charges, determines budget policy steps, determines expenditure ceilings for the national level and sub-national level, determines financing requirement ceilings, and evaluates public finance risks for the medium term based on the macroeconomic outlook and policy priorities of the Royal Government to ensure public financial sustainability.

The Medium-Term Budget Framework (MTBF) determines the budget expenditure ceilings for ministries and institutions by linking the budget to the priority policies of the ministry or institution, and must be consistent with the Medium-Term Public Finance Framework.

The Medium-Term Budget Framework for sub-national administrations determines the budget expenditure ceilings for capital and provincial administrations and other relevant sub-national administrations based on the Medium-Term Public Finance Framework.

Both of the above medium-term budget frameworks must be used as the basis for preparing the Budget Strategic Plan (BSP) and the annual Financial Law to achieve planned performance.

### Article 37 .-

The annual Financial Law must allocate budget credits by ministry, institution, and similar public entity, by type of expenditure, and must register the national level annex budget, Treasury special accounts, and national level Public Administrative Establishments.

The annual Financial Law must allocate budget credits to capital and provincial administrations by program and type of expenditure, and as a lump sum budget to municipality, district, khan, and commune, sangkat administrations, and must register the sub-national level annex budget and Public Administrative Establishments at the sub-national level.

### Article 38 .-

Budget credits approved in the Financial Law must be allocated to ministries, institutions, and similar public entities by program and type of expenditure to contribute to achieving the policy objectives of the ministry, institution, or similar public entity and the priority policies of the Royal Government.

The allocation of budget credits to ministries, institutions, and similar public entities at the program level and chapter level of the budget classification shall be determined by Sub-decree.

The detailed allocation of budget credits by sub-program and cluster of activities and the economic classification of the budget classification shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Minister of Economy and Finance must issue a Prakas to put into use the Budget Book on the allocation of budget credits below the program level and below the chapter level of the budget classification by January 1st of the budget implementation year.

Unallocated expenditures with specific subjects mentioned in point 6 of Article 32 of this law are expenditures clearly defined by the annual Financial Law and are subject to transfer by Sub-decree to the expenditure objectives when those budget credits are approved.

Unforeseen expenditures mentioned in point 6 of Article 32 of this law shall not be allocated to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations. Budget credits for unforeseen expenditures are authorized for any expenditures that cannot be specifically determined in advance, such as emergencies or expenditures related to disasters and various accidental events, for reallocation to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations when those budget credits are approved.

### Article 39 .-

There are two types of budget credits: limited credits and estimated credits.

Limited credits are provided as high-limit commitments and authorizations, where this type of expenditure is determined in advance. Such expenditures can only be created through a single Supplementary Finance Law.

Estimated credits are authorized for necessary expenditures and in cases where the planning is insufficient when...

public, membership contributions to international or inter-governmental institutions, social protection interventions, and other expenditures as determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Budget credits for salaries and allowances of personnel may be authorized at a detailed level exceeding the plan within the limit of the number of personnel for each ministry, institution, similar public entity, and sub-national administration as determined in the annual Financial Law. Increasing the budget credits for salaries and allowances due to an increase in the number of personnel during the budget implementation year is not permitted. The personnel ceilings for ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations must be determined by the annual Financial Law. In case of special, necessary, and urgent needs, the Royal Government may increase the personnel ceilings and increase the budget credits for salaries and allowances of personnel in accordance with the increase in the number of personnel, which increase is subject to approval in the Budget Settlement Law of the budget implementation year.

### Article 40 .-

Current expenditure credits must be divided into credits for the implementation of approved programs and credits for the implementation of new measures of those programs.

Credits for the implementation of approved programs include credits determined in the previous year's operating expenditure minus the baseline and minus credits for work activities that will not recur in the budget planning year, or plus credits for modifications according to the actual situation.

Credits for the implementation of approved programs must be at the minimum necessary level required for the operation of necessary work activities under the conditions of the previous year.

Modifications according to the actual situation must take into account effects that occurred in the previous year and that may occur in subsequent years, within the medium-term budget framework.

New program measures are measures related to any decisions that lead to additional financial impacts on the budget.

Budget credits for program implementation must have explanations of the necessity, suitability, and calculation basis towards achieving planned performance.

Rules and procedures for the allocation of current expenditure credits shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 41 .-

Capital expenditure credits are investment credits divided into commitment credits and payment credits.

Investment credits determined for investment projects or programs initiated in the year and credits must be set appropriately, being consistent with the medium-term investment project plan.

Commitment credits are limited credits authorized in the annual Financial Law for the Royal Government to guarantee the entire expenditure for the planned investment project or program without allowing additional credits for its completion. This commitment credit must be consistent with the medium-term budget framework and ensure public financial management sustainability and macroeconomic stability.

Payment credits must be used to issue payment orders that are state obligations according to the payment terms of the investment project contract and within the limit of the commitment credits.

Commitment credits and payment credits must be placed under the Authorizing Officer for expenditures necessary for the implementation of investment projects as determined in the annual Financial Law.

### Article 42 .-

Commitment credits can be of unlimited duration. These credits are carried forward from year to year until de-committed. Payment credits not used when closing the management (year-end) shall expire and new credits may be authorized for the following year.

### Article 43 .-

Investment projects or programs of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital and provincial administrations that are not completed in the implementation year, the unused capital expenditure payment credits at the close of management (year-end) may be authorized to be carried forward as payment credits for the following year to complete necessary investment projects or programs. This carry-forward to the following year must be decided by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance, at the latest by January 31st of the following year.

Payment credits authorized to be carried forward to the following year must be limited not lead to more than 10 (ten) percent respectively of the annual national level investment expenditure credits and the annual capital and provincial administration investment expenditure credits. Payment credits authorized to be carried forward from the previous year that are not used in the following year must be nullified in the Budget Settlement Law of the following year.

Specifically, the carry-forward of payment credits to the following year for investment projects or programs of municipality, district, and khan administrations shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Credits authorized for carry-forward as mentioned above are subject to recording in the Budget Settlement Law of the following year.

Rules, criteria, and procedures for authorizing the carry-forward of payment credits to the following year shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 44 .-

Investment credits through foreign financing are estimated credits and cannot be subject to any credit transfer measures.

### Article 45 .-

No legal provision, regulation, or administrative measure can be approved, recognized, or decided upon if it requires the expenditure of new charges that will occur, without being evaluated, forecasted, and authorized before and during the planning and determination by the annual Financial Law.

### Article 46 .-

Revenue must be collected, recorded, and paid in full into the Treasury Single Account and must not be used for direct offset against any expenditure.

The entire revenue must be allocated for all expenditure items. However, some revenues may be allocated only for specific expenditure items in the form and under conditions determined by law. Such allocations must be organized in the form of an annex budget or special accounts of the Treasury.

Increasing the amount of approved budget credits by the Authorizing Officer based on any special revenue is strictly prohibited.

## Section 4
## Authority and Responsibility

### Article 47 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the duty of responsibility for the management of budget revenue and expenditure, borrowing, lending, and state guarantees approved by law.

The objectives, ceilings, and financial conditions of borrowing, lending, and state guarantees must be determined in the annual Financial Law.

### Article 48 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance is the staff of the Royal Government in the preparation, implementation, monitoring, and management of the macroeconomic policy framework and the public finance policy framework.

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the duty to provide reports on the situation of the macroeconomic and public finance framework to the National Assembly and the Senate twice a year: at mid-year and during the discussion for the approval of the draft annual Financial Law.

### Article 49 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the duty to lead the compliance and maintenance of public financial discipline, as well as to strengthen budget efficiency both at the allocation level and the implementation level through the preparation and implementation of the Medium-Term Public Finance Framework, the Medium-Term Budget Framework, compliance with the Financial Law, and the management of budget implementation with mechanisms for regular monitoring, audit, and evaluation of budget management.

### Article 50 .-

National and sub-national administrations have the duty of responsibility in the preparation of the Budget Strategic Plan, budget management, and monitoring of their budget implementation, especially being accountable for the determination and results of policy implementation within their sectors and jurisdictions that are consistent with the policies of the Royal Government.

# Chapter 3
# Budget Preparation and Approval

## Section 1
## Budget Preparation Framework

### Article 51 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance must issue a circular on the detailed calendar for the annual budget preparation in mid-January.

The Minister of Economy and Finance must prepare the draft circular of the Royal Government on the preparation of the Budget Strategic Plan (BSP) and the annual budget draft based on the Medium-Term Public Finance Framework and the Medium-Term Budget Framework determined by the Royal Government, to be submitted to the Prime Minister for review and decision in early May, specifying the forms, formats, and procedures for budget draft preparation, as well as relevant documents to be attached.

Every Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital and Provinces must prepare the Budget Strategic Plan and the annual budget draft in accordance with the above-mentioned circular of the Royal Government and submit them to the Minister of Economy and Finance during mid-June.

The draft annual Financial Law must be submitted to the Council of Ministers for review and decision during mid-October. After review and decision, the Royal Government must submit the draft annual Financial Law and other relevant documents to the National Assembly, at the latest by the first week of November for review and approval, before submitting this draft law to the Senate at the latest by the first week of December for review and recommendation before December 20th, and promulgated by Royal Decree, as well as having all implementation procedures completed at the latest by December 31st.

In case of a request from the relevant specialized committees of the National Assembly, under the coordination of the commission in charge of finance of the National Assembly, members of the Royal Government in charge of leading and managing ministries, institutions, and similar public entities have the duty to lead relevant technical officials to provide clarifications on the objectives, policies, and programs, as well as the performance reports of programs under the jurisdiction of the ministry, institution, and entity.

The draft annual Financial Law must be published to the public within 1 week after being submitted to the National Assembly, and published to the public within 2 weeks after this annual Financial Law enters into force.

The Medium-Term Public Finance Framework and the Medium-Term Budget Framework must be submitted to the National Assembly and published in April, after obtaining the decision from the Council of Ministers.

The Medium-Term Public Finance Framework and the Medium-Term Budget Framework may be updated before being submitted as an attachment to the draft annual Financial Law to the National Assembly.

## Section 2
## Budget Preparation and Approval Process

### Article 52 .-

Budget preparation must comply with the provisions mentioned in Article 4, Article 6, and Article 15 of this law.

### Article 53 .-

Revenue planning must be based on state budget resource sources coming from tax revenue, non-tax revenue, and other revenues by:

1- The Minister of Economy and Finance must directly plan tax revenue, non-tax revenue, and other revenues that are subject to collection under his/her jurisdiction.

2- Every Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats that have a share in the management of revenue under their jurisdiction must submit to the Minister of Economy and Finance a list of all revenue items and all revenue estimation lists for recording in the budget revenue plan completely and accurately. These revenues are an inseparable part of the total state budget revenue.

### Article 54 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance must plan financing resources based on sources coming from:

1- Issuance and sale of state securities.

2- Borrowing from financial institutions and development partners.

3- Deposits of the Royal Government.

4- Financing from various other sources in accordance with applicable regulations.

### Article 55 .-

Current expenditure and investment expenditure planning must be prepared by the national level administration and sub-national administration to be consistent with the program level and the entire work activities.

### Article 56 .-

The planning of budget requirements must be prepared carefully and clearly based on legal principles, strategic goals of the Royal Government, the National Strategic Development Plan, sectoral policies and strategies, as well as policy objectives, programs, and expected results of the relevant ministry, institution, similar public entity, and sub-national administration.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall establish norms and documentation for all various work activities, as well as for goods and services, by cooperating with relevant ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations.

### Article 57 .-

Investment expenditure planning must be prepared for individual investment projects or programs that have prior approval in accordance with applicable regulations on public investment management and that are prepared to achieve the objectives determined in the policy agenda of the Royal Government. All investment projects or programs are the basis for evaluating the total investment expenditure of the project or program and for determining the amount of commitment credits and payment credits for each stage in the year.

### Article 58 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to plan the revenue and expenditure of national and sub-national administrations that fail to submit their budget plans on time, based on the results of the previous implementation years, to ensure compliance with the calendar for the preparation and approval of the draft annual Financial Law as mentioned in Article 51 of this law.

### Article 59 .-

The draft annual Financial Law is divided into two parts:

Part 1: Provisions of the draft annual Financial Law, including:

1- Authorization to collect all state budget resources and determination of those resources as a total amount.

2- Mentioning provisions related to state budget resources, including tax revenue, non-tax revenue, and other revenues.

3- Determination of budget charges as the maximum credits approved for the benefit of the state budget.

4- Mentioning other provisions related to state budget charges, including ceilings for lending.

5- Determination of budget balance / balance of resources and budget charges.

6- Determination of financing resource ceilings.

7- Mentioning provisions related to financing resources and charges, including ceilings for borrowing and state guarantees.

8- Mentioning provisions related to the state's Financial Reserve Fund.

9- Mentioning other financial provisions.

### Part 2: Annex Tables of the draft annual Financial Law, including:

1- Table of national level budget revenue, divided by type, chapter, account, sub-account, and by ministry, institution, and similar public entity.

2- Table of budget revenue of capital and provincial administrations, divided by type, chapter, account, sub-account, and by capital and province.

3- Table of expenditure, divided by ministry, institution, and similar public entity, by type of expenditure.

4- Table of expenditure by program.

5- Table of investment expenditure, divided by type of expenditure for ministries, institutions, and similar public entities, divided into commitment credits and payment credits.

6- Table of personnel ceilings for ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations.

7- Table of subsidy expenditures to Public Administrative Establishments.

8- Table of revenue and expenditure for annex budgets and special accounts of the Treasury (national level).

9- Table of budget expenditure of capital and provincial administrations (by type of expenditure, by program, and by capital and province).

10- Financial balance table reflecting budget operations as the balance of resources and budget charges, as well as reflecting financing requirements and financing sources.

### Article 60 .-

The draft annual Financial Law to be submitted to the National Assembly and the Senate must be accompanied by the following documents:

1- Explanatory Note of this draft law.

2- The Medium-Term Public Finance Framework and the Medium-Term Budget Framework or updates to these documents.

3- Annex on annual performance projects of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital and provincial administrations.

In addition to the above documents, the Royal Government may provide other relevant documents as an attachment to the draft annual Financial Law for additional information to the National Assembly and the Senate.

### Article 61 .-

In case the draft annual Financial Law is not approved before January 1st of the fiscal year, the following measures shall be legally implemented:

1- Tax revenue, non-tax revenue, and other revenues are authorized and continued for collection under the conditions and provisions determined in the previous Financial Law and within the legal framework as well as existing regulations.

2- Current expenditures of the state budget according to the previous year's budget credit framework, using the one-twelfth (1/12) method temporarily each month until the new budget is approved.

3- Investment expenditures within the limit of payment credits remaining from the previous year's budget credit framework, and which can exceed this in necessary cases, while not allowing the payment to exceed the commitment credit limit approved in the previous year's budget.

4- Counterpart expenditure for implementing public investment projects through foreign financing and public debt charges, including principal repayment, interest, and other relevant charges in accordance with existing agreements.

5- All expenditures that are obligations related to Cambodia's membership in international and inter-governmental institutions.

## Section 3
## Authority and Responsibility

### Article 62 .-

The annual Financial Law must be approved under the conditions mentioned in this law as other separate laws, except in the following cases:

1- Resources, charges, and state budget balance which must be approved as a package, including:

- National level budget resources and charges

- Sub-national administration resources and charges

- Resources, charges, and state budget balance / equilibrium

- Provisions related to financing resources and charges.

2- The summary list of annex budgets and special accounts of the Treasury which must be approved as a package.

3- Other provisions which must be approved by chapter.

4- The entire draft annual Financial Law which is subject to final approval.

### Article 63 .-

No modification of any provision of the draft Financial Law can take place if the modification aims to reduce resources or create or increase state budget charges that significantly affect the planned budget balance, except in the following cases:

1- Creating new expenditures made by canceling or reducing any other expenditures to maintain the budget balance.

- Proposing new expenditures made by attaching requests for reallocation from any expenditure categories already proposed, to maintain the budget balance.

- Proposing new expenditures made by attaching requests to create new revenue items or increase revenue to maintain the budget balance.

Any modification of any provision of the draft Financial Law must have an evaluation of the magnitude of the impact during the implementation year and, if necessary, in subsequent years.

Any modification of any provision of the draft Financial Law must have proper reasons and justification.

A request to modify any provision of the draft Financial Law that is contrary to the content of this article shall not be taken for review and approval.

### Article 64 .-

The allocation of budget credits to ministries and institutions at the chapter level and program level of the budget classification must be implemented in accordance with Article 38 of this law. This allocation must not lead to the modification of budget credits determined by law.

# Chapter 4
# Budget Implementation

## Section 1
## Budget Implementation Framework

### Article 65 .-

The transfer of budget credits from one another between ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital and provincial administrations must be determined by the Financial Law.

In case of necessity to strengthen the institutional structure of the Royal Government, the transfer of budget credits from one another between ministries, institutions, and similar public entities may be carried out by Sub-decree under the condition that this transfer of budget credits does not modify the expenditure subject and the allocation of budget credits by chapter of the economic classification of the budget classification and has the same objective of activity.

In case of necessity to transfer functions from the national level administration to sub-national administrations, the transfer of budget credits from the parent ministry, institution, or similar public entity to the relevant sub-national administration may be carried out by Sub-decree under the condition that this transfer of budget credits does not modify the expenditure subject and the allocation of budget credits by chapter of the economic classification of the budget classification and has the same objective of activity.

In case of necessity to strengthen the institutional structure of sub-national administrations, the transfer of budget credits from one another between sub-national administrations may be carried out by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

under the condition that this transfer of budget credits does not modify the expenditure subject and the allocation of budget credits by chapter of the economic classification of the budget classification and has the same objective of activity.

### Article 66 .-

Modifying budget credits from one chapter to another in the budget of a ministry, institution, or similar public entity shall be determined by Sub-decree.

Modifying budget credits from one program to another in the budget of a ministry, institution, or similar public entity is permitted to a maximum level of 5 (five) percent of each program's credit. This modification of budget credits shall be determined by Sub-decree.

The Prime Minister may delegate the decision-making authority at any level to the Minister of Economy and Finance for the modification of budget credits as mentioned in the above two paragraphs. In case of such delegation, the modification of budget credits shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Modifying budget credits from one chapter to another in sub-national administrations shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance. The Minister of Economy and Finance may delegate to sub-national administrations the decision-making authority to perform reallocations of their budget credits to solve needs in necessary cases.

Modifying budget credits from one program to another in the budget of capital and provincial administrations is permitted to a maximum level of 5 (five) percent of each program's credit. This modification of budget credits shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Rules and procedures for the modification of budget credits in municipality, district, and khan administrations shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 67 .-

Modifying budget credits from one account to another in the same chapter and from one sub-program to another in the same program shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance. The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas to delegate the authority for this modification of budget credits to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital and provincial administrations.

Modifying budget credits from one sub-account to another in the same account and from one activity cluster to another in the same sub-program shall be made by Prakas of the Minister of the ministry or by decision of the Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital and Provinces, except for some expenditure items that have risks in budget management. The exempted expenditure items shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance, which must be updated regularly according to actual needs.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance must study, review, and continuously modify the rights to modify budget credits in the chapter and program level for ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital and provincial administrations in accordance with the principle of budget decentralization implementation.

Rules and procedures for the modification of budget credits in the chapter and program level shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 68 .-

The allocation of budget credits recorded in unallocated expenditure with subjects and unforeseen expenditure, under the additional budget credit package for the benefit of various programs and chapters of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations, shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 69 .-

During the fiscal year, budget credits for items that are no longer needed for use may be canceled by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 70 .-

When the evolution of revenue and expenditure shows a budget deficit, the Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas to freeze part or all of some types of budget credits approved in the Financial Law. In case the deficit is severe, the Royal Government may issue a Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance to cancel part or all of some types of budget credits approved in the annual Financial Law. Such credit cancellation is subject to approval in the Budget Settlement Law.

### Article 71 .-

In case of necessity to intervene to solve challenges caused by emergencies and disasters that significantly affect national interests, based on the report of the Minister of Economy and Finance, the Prime Minister may issue a Sub-decree to:

- Transfer budget credits from one another between ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations, and transfer credits within the budget of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations by authorizing the modification of expenditure subjects and the reallocation of these transferred budget credits.

- Create budget credits for use as necessary for each case.

Such transfer and creation of additional budget credits are subject to approval in the Supplementary Finance Law when the actual situation of the case is concluded.

Notwithstanding the provisions of the above paragraph, if this Supplementary Finance Law cannot be enacted within the fiscal year in which the events occurred, the transfer and creation of such additional budget credits shall be subject to regularization in the Budget Settlement Law of the implementation year.

### Article 72 .-

Various modifications to the annual Financial Law may be done during the fiscal year through a Supplementary Finance Law which must be submitted for approval by the National Assembly and the Senate, in the same manner as the approval of the annual Financial Law.

### Article 73 .-

The Supplementary Finance Law must be prepared based on the full format identical to the annual Financial Law. The Supplementary Finance Law has the following annexes:

1- Report showing the reasons and objectives of the budget credit modification, reflecting the implementation situation and budget forecast.

2- Detailed explanatory annex on the budget credit requirements requested for modification.

3- Summary table on the movement of budget credits leading to modifications in the implementation year.

## Section 2
## Budget Implementation Process

### Article 74 .-

The implementation of state financial and accounting operations is the duty of the Authorizing Officer and the Public Accountant.

The functions of Authorizing Officer and Public Accountant are incompatible.

The Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats is the Principal Authorizing Officer of his/her budget. The function of Authorizing Officer for other heads of sub-national administrations must be implemented in accordance with applicable regulations.

The Principal Authorizing Officer is the one who orders state budget revenue and expenditure for the budget credits allocated to the parent ministry, institution, similar public entity, and sub-national administration.

The Principal Authorizing Officer must delegate authority to the heads of budget entities under his/her supervision as Delegated Authorizing Officers for the implementation of budget operations within the scope of allocated budget credits to achieve planned performance. The authority and responsibility of the budget entity shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Principal Authorizing Officer or Delegated Authorizing Officer may appoint subordinates as Assistant Authorizing Officers in accordance with applicable regulations.

The roles, duties, and responsibilities of the Authorizing Officer, as well as the delegation of authority to the Delegated Authorizing Officer and Assistant Authorizing Officer, shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 75 .-

State budget implementation is subject to budget control by the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance shall establish budget control units alongside ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations, and delegate functions to these units to implement budget control. The Ministry of Economy and Finance may delegate authority to any unit located near or at the budget implementation level to implement the aforementioned budget control. The delegation of functions and authority for budget control shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Budget control may be implemented through light, normal, or rigorous procedures, depending on the type of expenditure or the budget implementation unit, as determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Rules and procedures for the implementation and control of the budget, as well as the functions and duties of the budget control unit, shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 76 .-

All state budget expenditures for purchasing, construction, repair, and service leasing must comply with rules and procedures determined in laws and other legal instruments related to public procurement in force.

## Section 3
## Monitoring and Evaluation of Budget Implementation

### Article 77 .-

Within the framework of performance-based budgeting, the monitoring and evaluation of budget implementation must be carried out based on measurement and evaluation tools and methods.

Monitoring shall review the quality of program preparation, program implementation and performance, and resource usage. Specifically, evaluation shall review the effectiveness and performance achieved annually and multi-annually of the programs of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and sub-national administrations.

Under the accountability framework, the Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, and Khans must prepare an annual performance report in accordance with the principles and methods determined in the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Minister of Economy and Finance reviews and confirms compliance and provides recommendations on the performance reports of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital, provincial, municipal, district, and khan administrations to be used as input for improving performance plans in the following year.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may initiate and lead necessary or selective evaluations to measure the effectiveness and efficiency of programs.

The framework, methodology, mechanism, and procedures for monitoring and evaluation within the performance-based budgeting framework shall be determined by Sub-decree.

## Section 4
## Authority and Responsibility

### Article 78 .-

Subject to the conditions and limits of authority granted in the Financial Law and under the authority of the Prime Minister, ONLY the Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to borrow money, including the issuance of state securities, the receipt of grants, investment in financial assets, the provision of guarantees and financial commitments, and the right to lend money, both domestically and internationally. Beyond this, no public official or citizen holding a public mandate through any election has the right to borrow money or lend money or issue guarantee letters or perform any other actions, directly or indirectly, that could lead to debts or other financial burdens, directly or indirectly, to the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The borrowing of money and the creation of financial guarantee obligations of public enterprises must comply with the conditions determined in legal provisions and legal instruments related to public enterprise management and as determined by the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Subject to the limits determined in the Financial Law and under the authority of the Prime Minister, ONLY the Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to prepare programs for settling debts owed by the state or debts owed to the state.

### Article 79 .-

The Minister, Head of institution, and Head of similar public entity, as well as the Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats, have the obligation to be responsible for budget implementation and the achievement of performance planned within their respective jurisdictions, within the framework determined by law.

### Article 80 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance is responsible for managing public procurement and issuing legal instruments to guide the implementation of public procurement for ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, and public administrative establishments, in accordance with the law and other legal instruments related to public procurement in force.

### Article 81 .-

The Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats, Head of public enterprise, and Director of public administrative establishment must ensure full compliance, responsibility, transparency, and accountability in the implementation of the law and other legal instruments related to public procurement in force.

### Article 82 .-

The Program Manager has the duty to be responsible to the Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, or Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital and Provinces for budget preparation, coordination, monitoring, and promotion of various operations at the program level, including budget implementation and achievement of planned performance.

The Head of the Budget Entity has the duty to be responsible for various operations within the framework of his/her authority and responsibility, including preparation, budget implementation, and performance planned to the Program Manager and the Principal Authorizing Officer.

The authority, duties, and responsibilities of the Program Manager and Head of the Budget Entity shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 83 .-

Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and capital, provincial, municipal, district, and khan administrations shall have a financial unit as a department or equivalent unit, responsible for financial technical work and for coordinating and supporting various program operations, including primarily budget strategic planning, annual budget preparation, budget implementation, control, and monitoring and evaluation, as well as their budget implementation results, including budget implementation reports and performance reports.

The roles, duties, authority, and responsibility, as well as the qualifications of the Head of the Financial Unit, shall be determined by Sub-decree.

# Chapter 5
# Accounting and Budget Settlement

## Section 1
## Accounting Framework

### Article 84 .-

In accordance with the principle of accountability, the Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, and Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats, Head of public enterprise, and Director of public administrative establishment must prepare and provide to the Minister of Economy and Finance reports related to their jurisdiction, including state budget revenue and expenditure reports...

performance reports, project implementation reports related to borrowing, lending, state guarantees, and grants, and reports related to revenue from investment in financial assets, as well as other information upon request by the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall determine the types, conditions, dates of provision, and templates of the reports as mentioned in the above paragraphs.

### Article 85 .-

State budget financial and accounting operations must be implemented by the Public Accountant. The centralization of figures must be carried out by the Treasury Accountant, who is a public accountant at the National Treasury.

State budget financial and accounting operations must be verified for accuracy in accordance with public accounting rules and cash management by the Public Accountant.

Every state budget financial and accounting operation must have supporting documents as evidence and be implemented in electronic or digital form in the Information Technology system. Rules and procedures for determining supporting documents and electronic and digital forms shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 86 .-

Public accounting is a system of recording, classifying, measuring, summarizing, and presenting budget and financial information that allows for the control of budget and accounting transaction data and the presentation of financial reports and budget implementation reports, as well as the calculation and analysis of actual revenue and costs of activities or services, and serves as the basis for performance evaluation.

Types of public accounting include budget accounting, general accounting, and analytical accounting. The Minister of Economy and Finance may determine other types of public accounting as necessary for the purpose of ensuring effective and efficient public finance management.

Public accounting shall be applied to the state budget, except for the budget of public enterprises.

The maintenance of accounting books and the preparation of public accounting reports must be in Khmer language and in Riel currency.

Each accounting period shall be determined for 12 (twelve) months, starting from January 1st until the accounting closing date on December 31st of the same year.

### Article 87 .-

Budget accounting records, monitors, and reports on the use of budget credits, both revenue and expenditure, as determined by the Financial Law.

Budget accounting must be prepared on a cash basis. The recording of state budget revenue and expenditure must comply with the following principles:

- It must be recognized as budget revenue in the budget implementation year when that revenue is actually received in cash by the Public Accountant.

- It must be recognized as budget expenditure in the budget implementation year when that expenditure is actually paid in cash by the Public Accountant.

Revenue payment slips and payment orders in the budget implementation fiscal year may continue to be received until January 31st of the following year, and must be recorded with additional treasury slips as of December 31st of the implementation year.

In case a Supplementary Finance Law is approved in the budget implementation year or there is a Sub-decree adding budget credits in accordance with Article 71 of this law, budget revenue and expenditure planned in this Supplementary Finance Law or Sub-decree can be implemented during the complementary period and must be recorded in the accounting books of the annual budget implementation fiscal year. The complementary period for budget implementation in this case shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 88 .-

State resources and financing charges must be recorded according to public accounting standards and according to each type of cash transaction. Budget revenue and expenditure transactions arising from cash transactions must be recorded according to revenue and expenditure items, in accordance with the conditions determined in Article 87 of this law.

### Article 89 .-

General accounting records all financial and accounting transactions and prepares financial reports that reflect the reality and accuracy of the financial situation and financial results.

General accounting must be prepared on an accrual basis and must implement Cambodia's Public Accounting Standards.

State budget financial and accounting transactions are recognized in the accounting books in the period when those transactions occurred, regardless of the receipt or payment of cash.

General accounting must ensure the quality of accounts, financial and accounting data, and financial reports. Rules and procedures for the implementation of Public Accounting Standards shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance, in accordance with the strategy of the Royal Government.

### Article 90 .-

Analytical accounting measures the cost of producing products, providing public services, or other state interventions by unit, function, sector, program, or project, to serve as an additional basis for decision-making on public administration management.

Analytical accounting shall be prepared based on data from budget accounting and general accounting.

### Article 91 .-

General regulations on public accounting shall determine all conditions that enable financial and accounting transactions to be recorded, analyzed, and managed for budget implementation with accountability, effectiveness, and efficiency.

General regulations on public accounting shall be determined by Sub-decree.

## Section 2
## Budget Settlement

### Article 92 .-

Budget settlement is the subject of the Budget Settlement Law.

### Article 93 .-

The draft Budget Settlement Law must be prepared by the Minister of Economy and Finance, based on revenue, expenditure data, and other financial information reported by the Public Accountant and according to the budget implementation performance reported by the Principal Authorizing Officer.

The draft Budget Settlement Law is divided into two parts:

Part 1: Provisions of the draft Budget Settlement Law, including:

1- Final confirmation of the amount of revenue, expenditure, and budget results implemented during the budget fiscal year.

2- Final confirmation of the amount of financing resources and charges that ensure the financial balance of the budget implementation year and shown in the financial balance table.

3- Approval of the financial situation and the results of the fiscal year determined in Article 89 of this law on general accounting.

4- Recognition of the regularization of budget credits, including transfers, creation of budget credits, and cancellation of budget credits in cases occurring during the budget fiscal year.

5- Cancellation of unused budget credits, except where there are provisions allowing for the carry-forward of those budget credits.

6- Provisions related to other necessary financial information.

### Part 2: Annex Tables of the draft Budget Settlement Law, including:

1- Financial balance table and budget operations table.

2- Budget revenue table, including:

- National level budget revenue table by type, chapter, account, sub-account, and by ministry and institution.

- Budget revenue table of capital and provincial administrations by type, chapter, account, sub-account, and by capital and province.

3- Budget expenditure table reflecting final results of budget expenditure as mentioned in point 4 of Part 1 above, including:

- Expenditure table by ministry, institution, and similar public entity, by type of expenditure.

- Expenditure table by program.

- Investment expenditure table with domestic and foreign financing, by type of expenditure for ministry, institution, and similar public entity.

- Table of subsidy expenditures to Public Administrative Establishments.

- Budget expenditure table of capital and provincial administrations (by type of expenditure, by program, and by capital and province).

4- Table of financial situation and results.

5- Annex budget revenue and expenditure table and special accounts of the Treasury (national level).

### Article 94 .-

The draft Budget Settlement Law to be submitted to the National Assembly and the Senate must be accompanied by the following documents:

1- Explanatory Note of this draft law.

2- Annex on financial reports.

3- Annex on annual performance reports of each ministry, institution, similar public entity, and capital and provincial administration.

4- Annex on canceled credits and credits authorized for carry-forward to the next year, in accordance with Article 43 of this law.

5- Annex on personnel count by ministry, institution, similar public entity, and sub-national administration.

6- Annex on revenue and expenditure of municipality, district, khan, and commune, sangkat administrations.

In addition to the above documents, the Royal Government may provide other relevant documents as an attachment to the draft Budget Settlement Law for additional information to the National Assembly and the Senate.

### Article 95 .-

The draft Budget Settlement Law must be submitted to the Council of Ministers for review and decision during mid-October. After the review and decision, the Royal Government must transmit the draft Budget Settlement Law and other relevant documents, simultaneously with the draft annual Financial Law, to the National Assembly for review and approval before transmitting this draft law to the Senate at the latest in the first week of December for review and providing comments before December 20th and promulgated by Royal Decree at the latest by December 31st.

The draft Budget Settlement Law must be published within 1 week after submission to the National Assembly and published within 2 weeks after this Budget Settlement Law comes into force.

### Article 96 .-

The Ministry of Economy and Finance must transmit the financial reports to the National Audit Authority during August for the National Audit Authority to review and provide audit opinions on these financial reports in a timely manner during mid-October, concurrently with the Royal Government's submission of the draft Budget Settlement Law to the National Assembly.

## Section 3
## Authority and Responsibility

### Article 97 .-

The Public Accountant is responsible for receiving and managing revenue, paying expenditure, and keeping funds. The Public Accountant has the duty to verify the accuracy of budget implementation in accordance with general regulations of public accounting and cash management. ONLY the Public Accountant has the authority to implement operations and solve all problems related to cash management. The Public Accountant is responsible for maintaining accounting books and preparing financial reports by ensuring respect for principles and general regulations of public accounting, and ensuring the fairness of accounting entries and respect for financial procedures in force within his/her jurisdiction.

Within this jurisdiction, the Public Accountant shall have personal and financial responsibility.

### Article 98 .-

The Public Accountant shall be appointed by Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance. The Authorizing Officer and other Public Accountants must recognize the Public Accountant who has been appointed.

### Article 99 .-

All state budget revenue and expenditure implementations must be carried out in accordance with the instructions of the Minister of Economy and Finance and the regulations in force.

All recordings of state budget revenue and expenditure must comply with the budget classification and the chart of public accounts. Every state budget revenue and expenditure report and financial report must be transparent and published in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to assign subordinates to carry out financial inspections of public finance management, including all financial and accounting transactions, at ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, public administrative establishments, and other public legal entities that implement state budget transactions to ensure the collection of accurate information and data for the preparation of the draft Budget Settlement Law.

# Chapter 6
# Internal Control System, Internal Audit, and Financial Inspection

## Section 1
## Framework and Process

### Article 100 .-

Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, and public administrative establishments must organize and operate internal control and internal audit systems in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

Rules and procedures for organizing and operating internal control and internal audit systems shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 101 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance is the sole technical official of the Royal Government in financial inspection of public finance management and has the duty to carry out financial inspections at ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, public administrative establishments, and other legal entities that receive benefits from public finance, based on risks, cases of suspicion, and complaints.

Rules and procedures for carrying out financial inspections shall be determined by Sub-decree.

## Section 2
## Authority and Responsibility

### Article 102 .-

Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, Governor of the Board of Governors...

Administrations must assign internal auditors to audit the compliance and operational efficiency of the internal control system over public finance management of their subordinate units in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

The internal audit unit and related individuals or units have the obligation to cooperate, provide, and allow access to documents, information, bank accounts, officials, staff, assets, operations, and information technology systems, as well as to cooperate in implementing audit procedures and other works to develop the internal audit function without obstruction, with high responsibility and efficiency, in accordance with relevant legal provisions in force and as permitted by the Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes, Sangkats, Head of public enterprise, and Director of the respective public administrative establishment.

The internal audit unit of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, and public administrative establishments must implement improvements based on internal audit recommendations in a timely manner to maintain high efficiency in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

### Article 103 .-

The Minister of Economy and Finance has the right to assign subordinates to carry out financial inspections of public finance management, including the implementation of state budget revenue and expenditure and the management of state assets, in accordance with relevant financial inspection rules and procedures in force.

Entities subject to financial inspection have the obligation to cooperate, clarify, providing supporting documents, data, accounting books, bank accounts, state assets, and information technology systems sufficiently and accurately for the purpose of financial inspection without obstruction, with high responsibility and efficiency.

Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, public administrative establishments, and other legal entities that receive benefits from public finance and are subject to financial inspection must implement financial inspection measures of the Ministry of Economy and Finance with high efficiency in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

# Chapter 7
# Penalties

### Article 104 .-

Ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, public enterprises, public administrative establishments, and other legal entities that receive benefits from public finance that violate the provisions of this law and other relevant regulations in force shall be subject to financial measures to be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Financial measures against other legal entities that receive benefits from public finance as mentioned above shall not be an obstacle to criminal prosecution and sentencing, as well as civil liability, in accordance with the law and other relevant regulations in force.

### Article 105 .-

Public officials and citizens holding a public mandate through elections who violate the provisions of this law and other relevant regulations in force in the implementation of their functions and duties shall be subject to administrative sanctions in accordance with the law and other relevant legal instruments in force. Such administrative sanctions shall not be an obstacle to criminal prosecution and sentencing, as well as civil liability, in accordance with the law and other relevant regulations in force.

### Article 106 .-

Public officials and citizens holding a public mandate through elections who are found to have caused loss of public funds or damage to state assets, regardless of the value of the loss or damage, including interest on the value of the loss or damage and other compensations, shall become a personal debt of the concerned individual, who has the obligation to reimburse and pay back into the state budget. Such financial responsibility shall not be an obstacle to criminal prosecution and sentencing, as well as civil liability, in accordance with law and other relevant regulations in force.

Rules and procedures related to this financial responsibility shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the proposal of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 8
# Transitional Provisions

### Article 107 .-

The Royal Government must prepare and put into implementation legal instruments to ensure the fundamental implementation of performance-based budgeting by 2026 at the latest.

### Article 108 .-

All laws and legal instruments related to the implementation of the Law on Public Finance System promulgated by Royal Decree No. NS/RKM/0508/016 dated May 13, 2008, and related amendment laws currently in force, shall continue to be effective until new laws and legal instruments are enacted to replace them in accordance with the provisions of this law.

# Chapter 9
# Final Provisions

### Article 109 .-

The Law on Public Finance System promulgated by Royal Decree No. NS/RKM/0508/016 dated May 13, 2008, and any provisions contrary to this Law are hereby nullified.

# Annex of the Law on Public Finance System
# Glossary

## K

**Program**: refers to the collection of credits of a ministry, institution, similar public entity, or capital and provincial administration, municipality, district, or khan that contributes to the implementation of public policy under its management. Each program has a clear and measurable objective with specific indicators. A program is the basis for determining sub-programs and clusters of activities.

**Borrowing**: refers to the mobilization of financing resources through the issuance of state securities and borrowing from financial institutions, development partners, and other legal entities in accordance with regulations in force.

**State Guarantee**: refers to the assumption of financial obligations in the event that the party receiving the state guarantee is unable to pay back the other party to the agreed contract.

**Centralization of figures**: refers to the collection of financial and accounting transaction figures, budget reports, and financial reports from relevant units by a Treasury Accountant to prepare consolidated budget and financial reports of the state.

**Lending**: refers to the provision of loans using the state budget and/or using financing borrowed by the state, in accordance with regulations in force.

**Medium-Term Public Finance Framework (MTPFF)**: refers to a strategy document providing estimates of state budget resources and charges, determining budget policy steps, determining national and sub-national budget expenditure ceilings, and determining financing requirement ceilings and evaluating public finance risks for the medium term, based on macroeconomic outlook and the policy priorities of the Royal Government to ensure public finance sustainability.

**Medium-Term Budget Framework (MTBF)**: refers to the establishment of an institutional mechanism for prioritization to show the policy priorities of the Royal Government by setting out expenditure and resource ceilings for the medium term for ministries, institutions, and sub-national administrations. The MTBF is linked to the MTPFF and the Budget Strategic Plan (BSP) through the integration of policy-based planning and three-year medium-term budget estimation.

**Ministry**: refers to a ministry established in accordance with the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers and led by a member of the Royal Government who is the Principal Authorizing Officer of the parent ministry's budget.

## C (Phonetic K in Khmer order)

**Treasury Single Account (TSA)**: refers to the system of managing and centralizing bank accounts or cash of the Royal Government under the sole movement and management of the Ministry of Economy and Finance through a Public Accountant. The Treasury Single Account is used to centralize state resources to ensure the efficiency of payment of state expenditures.

**Treasury Accountant**: refers to a Public Accountant who performs functions at the General Department of National Treasury. Treasury Accountants include the Central Treasury Accountant and Capital and Provincial Treasury Accountants. The Treasury Accountant is responsible for the financial and accounting work of the national and sub-national administrations, and has the function of centralizing figures of financial and accounting transactions, budget reports, and financial reports of other units to prepare consolidated budget and financial reports of the state.

**Public Accountant**: refers to a state agent responsible for receiving and managing revenue, paying expenditure, keeping and managing cash, maintaining accounting books, and preparing budget and financial reports of the state. Public Accountants include Treasury Accountants, Accountants of Public Administrative Establishments, Annex Budget Accountants, Special Treasury Account Accountants, and other Accountants implementing state budget accounting.

**Accountability**: refers to the responsibility of ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, and other legal entities for functions and duties related to public finance through a reporting system. National budget implementation agents, including Authorizing Officers and Public Accountants, must be responsible for their decisions under the authority provided by law.

## Ch

**Sustainability**: refers to the balance between revenue and expenditure that must be within a magnitude that the budget can support. In cases of budget sustainability, financing or debt borrowing must be considered at a level that can be repaid in the long term.

**Domestic tax revenue**: refers to revenue received from payment of taxes, duties, and penalties from the implementation of tax laws and regulations.

**International trade tax revenue**: refers to revenue received from payment of taxes, duties, and other fees on imports and exports.

## D

**Development Partner**: refers to a country or institution that provides financing in the form of loans, grants, or technical assistance to the Kingdom of Cambodia for social and economic development and capacity building.

## T

**Transparency**: refers to all laws and legal instruments, as well as information, principles, and procedures related to budget, both financial and non-financial, which must be published in a timely manner to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, sub-national administrations, and the public in accordance with relevant regulations in force.

**Budget Balance**: refers to the balance between budget resources and charges in the annual Financial Law and the Supplementary Finance Law.

**Financial Balance**: refers to the balance between state resources and charges in the annual Financial Law and the Supplementary Finance Law. Financial balance is ensured through the relationship between the budget balance and state financing. In case of a budget surplus, the state can use that surplus to address financing needs, or in case of a budget deficit, the state must use financing sources to fill the budget deficit.

## Tr (Phonetic Tr)

**Asset**: refers to resources controlled by the entity as a result of past events and from which the entity expects future economic benefits or service potential to flow.

**Financial Asset**: refers to any asset that is (a) cash, (b) an equity instrument of another entity, and (c) a contractual right to receive cash or another financial asset, or to exchange financial instruments with another entity to obtain benefit.

## B (Phonetic P)

**Program Manager**: refers to a leader appointed by the Minister, Head of institution, Head of similar public entity, or Governor of the Board of Governors of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, or Khans, who is the Principal Authorizing Officer, to be responsible for budget preparation, coordination, monitoring, and promoting operations at the program level, including budget implementation to achieve planned performance results.

**Budget Entity Manager**: refers to an operator responsible for operations within his/her jurisdiction of authority and responsibility, including budget preparation, budget implementation, and planned performance before the Program Manager and the Principal Authorizing Officer.

**Internal Control System**: refers to the control measures or processes established and implemented by the entity itself to provide reasonable assurance in achieving the effectiveness and efficiency of operations, reliability and integrity of information, as well as compliance with principles, plans, laws, and regulations in force.

**Efficiency**: refers to the proper allocation of budget and alignment with policy priorities within an appropriate volume, and expenditures must be implemented to ensure value for money.

## P (Phonetic P)

**Citizen holding a public mandate through elections**: refers to members of the Senate, the National Assembly, the Capital Council, Provincial Councils, Municipal Councils, District Councils, Khan Councils, and Commune/Sangkat Councils, as well as other citizens holding a public mandate through elections to perform other public functions.

## Ph (Phonetic Ph)

**Authority of the Law**: refers to the Financial Law which must be strictly respected and cannot be violated by any public institution. This law has supremacy over other laws regarding public finance aspects.

**Reliability**: refers to budget information or data that must be comprehensive, accurate, and consistent. Furthermore, financial and non-financial information must be sincere and honest, without bias or hiding any revenue or expenditure.

## R

**Sub-national Administration**: refers to the administration of the Capital, Provinces, Municipalities, Districts, Khans, and Communes/Sangkats, which are public legal entities with autonomy in managing their administration within the jurisdiction of their respective Councils, which have decision-making power, except where specifically provided otherwise.

**National Administration**: refers to ministries, institutions, similar public entities, and specialized departments under their supervision, except where specifically provided otherwise.

**Policy Agenda**: refers to strategies for sectoral decision-making of the Royal Government reflected in state plans, including the National Strategic Development Plan (NSDP) and sectoral and sub-sectoral policies of ministries and institutions.

**Medium-Term**: refers to a period of no less than 3 years and no more than 5 years.

## V

**Public Finance Sector**: refers to the respect and implementation of principles, regulations, and procedures in force in public finance management, as well as respect for key performance indicator conditions determined in the Medium-Term Public Finance Framework to improve the efficiency of budget framework preparation and strengthen the link between implementation strategies to ensure public finance sustainability and macroeconomic stability.

**Scope**: refers to revenue and expenditure which are subjects that must show clear detailed calculation bases in the Financial Law. Revenue must be shown by major categories, while expenditure must be shown by purpose and...

(Continued from page 44)... the purpose of budget credit use and having clear targets of use, while remaining within the determined maximum limits. In this sense, the budget must clearly show the purpose and targets of all public activities, requiring the collection of all revenues and the payment of all expenditures of the state budget.

## S

**Universality**: refers to all types of revenue that must be used for all types of expenditure without condition. All types of revenue must be collected, recorded, and paid in full into the Treasury Single Account and must not be directly offset against any expenditure.

**Performance**: refers to the results of budget implementation, both financial and non-financial, which can be measured in the form of key indicators, for use as a basis for assessing the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of public finance management.

**Internal Audit**: refers to independent, objective assurance and consulting activity using a systematic approach with proper skills in assessing and increasing the efficiency of risk management, internal control systems, and governance to add value and improve operations that allow the entity to achieve its objectives as determined.

**Public Enterprise**: refers to a legal entity with financial autonomy whose capital is entirely or mostly state-owned. In addition to the provisions of this law, the management of public enterprises is the subject of other relevant laws.

**Institution**: refers to an institution other than a ministry, established by the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia or established by law, led by a head who is the Principal Authorizing Officer of the respective institution's budget.

## H

**Public Finance**: refers to state resources and charges, including revenue, expenditure, assets, and liabilities that the state must manage, use, and dispose of with sustainability, stability, and efficiency in the medium and long term for the country's social and economic development.

## O (Phonetic A)

**Public Official**: refers to (a) an individual serving in a legislative, executive, or judicial body who is appointed by a legal instrument, whether permanent or temporary, whether receiving or not receiving salary, regardless of rank or age; (b) an individual performing public functions, including public agents or public enterprises, and other public institutions mentioned in the laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Budget Control Unit**: refers to a unit established by the Ministry of Economy and Finance to be attached to a ministry, institution, or similar public entity, and at sub-national administrations, or can be a unit located close to or at the budget implementation level, which is delegated by the Minister of Economy and Finance to perform financial control functions, including control of public procurement implementation and control/promotion of non-tax revenue collection.

**Budget Entity**: refers to a unit to which the respective ministry, institution, similar public entity, or sub-national administration has provided a clear mandate to implement work activities to produce specific, measurable results and contribute to achieving the policy objectives of the ministry, institution, similar public entity, or sub-national administration within the allocated budget credits. A budget entity can be a General Department or an equivalent unit, or a department, with a clear management structure, work volume, personnel count, and appropriate budget size.

**Financial Unit**: refers to a single unit under the management of the Principal Authorizing Officer of a ministry, institution, similar public entity, or capital and provincial, municipality, district, or khan administration, which has the role and duty to lead, instruct, support, and coordinate with all budget entities in budget strategic planning, annual budget preparation, budget implementation, and budget implementation review and monitoring, as well as consolidated reporting. The Financial Unit can be a General Secretariat, a General Department, a Department, a Division, or an Office in charge of financial affairs, which is responsible for the budget and financial work of the ministry, institution, similar public entity, or capital and provincial, municipal, district, or khan administration.

**Entity without Legal Personality**: refers to a unit under a ministry, institution, similar public entity, or sub-national administration that is assigned functions or established to implement an Annex Budget, based on criteria determined by relevant legal instruments.

**Similar Public Entity**: refers to a public entity other than a ministry or institution, which is not a subordinate unit of any ministry or institution. A similar public entity must be established by a legal instrument in force and receives funding entirely or partially from the national budget, led by a head who is the Principal Authorizing Officer of the respective public entity's budget.

**Budget Implementation Unit**: refers to a budget entity and other units under a ministry, institution, similar public entity, or sub-national administration that implement the budget.

**Financial Inspection**: refers to the review, research, investigation, and verification of state financial transactions, including the implementation of legal instruments related to public finance, to contribute to ensuring accountability in public finance management, in accordance with laws and relevant legal instruments in force.

**Authorizing Officer**: refers to a person with sufficient legal authority to order revenue and expenditure transactions of a ministry, institution, similar public entity, or sub-national administration within his/her budget credits. Authorizing Officers include Principal Authorizing Officers, Delegated Authorizing Officers, and Acting Authorizing Officers.

**Principal Authorizing Officer**: refers to the Minister of a ministry, the Head of an institution, the Head of a similar public entity, or the Governor of the Board of Governors of a Capital, Province, Municipality, District, or Khan, who has full authority to order revenue and expenditure transactions by him/herself, while respecting the limits of his/her authority and functions.

**Acting Authorizing Officer**: refers to a subordinate leader who is assigned by the Principal Authorizing Officer or a Delegated Authorizing Officer to represent or act on their behalf, based on general regulations of public accounting, during the absence or busyness of the Principal Authorizing Officer or the Delegated Authorizing Officer.

**Delegated Authorizing Officer**: refers to the head of a budget entity who has received authority from the Principal Authorizing Officer to implement budget transactions within the allocated budget credits to achieve planned performance results.

## E (Phonetic Ae)

**Unity**: refers to all revenues and expenditures that must be planned together in a single document, which is the annual Financial Law. In public finance management, unity also refers to a single budget system that is unified and consistent and must be implemented through a clear, single-framework mechanism.
