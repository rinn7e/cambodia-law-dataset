#### Message from

### H.E. ANG VONG VATHANA

#### Minister of Justice

This Code of Criminal Procedure is the result of nearly 10 years of work by the Ministry of Justice, prepared with the assistance of French cooperation. Since 1994, the Ministry of Justice has begun the process of revising the criminal procedure to meet the needs of Cambodia, which desires a clear and complete Code of Criminal Procedure, as criminal procedure is a primary legal text and an important means for criminal courts.

In preparing the Code, it was necessary to gather various scattered texts to make them easy to understand by organizing them in a unified and consistent manner. The Ministry of Justice has adopted the principles of the law dated March 8, 1951, concerning criminal procedure, which aims to ensure the institutional continuity in accordance with the legal traditions of the Kingdom of Cambodia and provides investigative judges with a primary role in the investigation and search for evidence. This Code clearly states the principles to ensure efficiency in the search for offenders, prosecution, and trial, as well as respect for individual freedoms, in accordance with the principles set by the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia and international conventions to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party, including: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights of 1948, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights of 1966...).

This Code of Criminal Procedure was adopted by the National Assembly with 83/83 votes on June 7, 2007, and was adopted by the Senate with 51/51 votes on July 24, 2007. The adoption of this code is a testimony to the firm will in building the legal framework in accordance with the policy of legal and judicial system reform of the Royal Government of Cambodia under the wise leadership of Samdech Techo Prime Minister Hun Sen.

In our Cambodian society today, almost every citizen understands that it is essential to have an understanding of the legal rules to ensure the effectiveness of their application. This is because every modern society must face the understanding and dissemination of laws to lead and manage social peace, ensure safety in commercial transactions both nationally and internationally. And on the occasion where I and my colleagues from the Ministry of Justice represented the Royal Government to defend the draft Code of Criminal Procedure at the National Assembly during the 6th session of the 3rd legislature, many representatives of the people raised the importance and necessity of legal education and dissemination to ensure the effectiveness of application by requesting the Ministry of Justice to educate and disseminate this Code to law enforcement officers and citizens extensively.

As a description, the regular publication and dissemination of legal texts at every time they are adopted is a stage that allows for the building of a democratic system and the rule of law in accordance with the spirit of the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Recognizing the necessity and importance of legal education and dissemination in ensuring the effectiveness of law enforcement, raising public awareness, and strengthening the rule of law, the Ministry of Justice, serving the Royal Government in this field, has arranged for the publication of this Code of Criminal Procedure with the support of French cooperation to distribute to legal professionals, courts, and law enforcement officers extensively.

The Ministry of Justice firmly believes that this Code of Criminal Procedure will become an important tool for training and legal education and dissemination, as well as contributing to raising the right to access legal information and the right to justice in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Phnom Penh, August 06, 2007

# CODE OF CRIMINAL PROCEDURE

# BOOK 1

## CRIMINAL ACTION AND CIVIL ACTION

### TITLE 1

#### GENERAL PRINCIPLES

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### GENERAL PRINCIPLES

#### Article 1.- Purpose of the Code of Criminal Procedure

The Code of Criminal Procedure aims to define all rules that must be strictly respected and followed in order to clearly determine the existence of any criminal offense.

The provisions of this Code shall apply to criminal cases when there are no separate rules specified by a specific special law.

#### Article 2.- Criminal Action and Civil Action

A criminal action and a civil action are two different actions.

A criminal action aims to examine the existence of an offense, to demonstrate the guilt of the offender, and to punish that person according to the law.

A civil action aims to provide compensation for damages caused by the offense to the victim, and for this purpose, to allow the victim to receive compensation commensurate with the actual loss suffered.

### TITLE 2

#### CRIMINAL ACTION

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### CRIMINAL ACTION

#### Article 3.- Subject of Criminal Action

A criminal action shall be applied to every person, both natural persons and legal entities, regardless of race, color, sex, language, belief, religion, political tendency, national origin

social status, resources, or any other situation.

#### Article 4.- Commencement of Criminal Action by the Prosecutor

A criminal action shall be exercised in the name of the general interest by the prosecutor.

The prosecutor shall conduct criminal charges and request the application of the law before the investigating jurisdiction and the trial jurisdiction.

#### Article 5.- Initiation of Criminal Action by the Victim

Victims of a felony or misdemeanor may file a complaint by establishing themselves as a civil party before the investigating judge. A complaint with the establishment as a civil party has the effect of referring the criminal action to the investigating judge under the conditions stipulated in Article 139 (Transfer of complaint to the Royal Prosecutor) and Article 140 (Payment of security deposit) of this Code.

The criminal jurisdiction may also receive complaints from civil servants or other public agents who have been granted such power by separate laws.

#### Article 6.- Filing of Simple Complaint by the Victim

Every person who declares themselves a victim of an offense may file a complaint. A simple complaint does not have the effect of initiating criminal charges.

In the event that the complaint remains unanswered or is filed without action by the Royal Prosecutor, the victim may appeal to the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal, in accordance with the procedure provided for in Article 41 (Filing of a case without action) of this Code.

#### Article 7.- Extinguishment of Criminal Action

The causes for the extinguishment of a criminal action are as follows:

1- Death of the offender.

2- Prescription of the action.

3- General amnesty.

4- Repeal of the criminal law.

5- Res judicata.

When a criminal action has been extinguished, criminal charges can no longer be initiated or must be terminated.

#### Article 8.- Other Causes of Extinguishment of Criminal Action

When a separate law specifically provides for it, a criminal action may also be extinguished by:

1- Compromise with the State.

2- Withdrawal of the complaint, in cases where the complaint is a necessary condition for criminal prosecution.

3- Payment of a lump sum fine or a settlement fine.

#### Article 9.- Offenses without Statutory Limitation

Genocide, crimes against humanity, and war crimes are not subject to any statute of limitations.

#### Article 10.- Statutory Limitation periods for Offenses

Except for the provisions of Article 9 (Offenses without statutory limitation) of this Code, the limitation periods for criminal action are as follows:

- 15 (fifteen) years for felonies;

- 5 (five) years for misdemeanors;

- 1 (one) year for petty offenses.

#### Article 11.- Interruption of Commencement of Limitation Period

The limitation period shall begin from the date the offense was committed. The limitation period shall be interrupted by any act of prosecution or investigation. Each act of prosecution or investigation shall cause a new limitation period to begin, according to the categories in Article 10 (Statutory limitation periods for offenses) of this Code. The new period can be invoked against any person involved in the case.

#### Article 12.- Res Judicata

In application of the principle of res judicata, any person who has been definitively acquitted by a court may not be prosecuted again for the same act, even under a different legal characterization.

### TITLE 3

#### CIVIL ACTION

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### CIVIL ACTION

#### Article 13.- Civil Action and Damage

A civil action shall be exercised by the victim of an offense.

To be compensable, the damage must:

- Be a direct consequence of the offense;

- Be personal damage;

- Have occurred and be current.

Damage may be material, physical, or mental.

#### Article 14.- Reparation of Damage

Damage may be repaired by the payment of compensation, by the restitution to the victim of lost property, or by the restoration to its original state of property that has been destroyed or damaged.

The compensation must be commensurate with the damage suffered.

#### Article 15.- Civil Party

A civil action may be brought on behalf of the victim by their legal representative when the victim is a minor or an incapacitated person under a legal protection regime.

#### Article 16.- Civil Action of Succesors of the Victim

In the event of the death of the victim, a civil action may be brought or continued by their successors.

#### Article 17.- Associations Fighting against Sexual Violence, Domestic Violence, or Violence against Children

Any properly registered association that has existed for at least 3 (three) years prior to the date of the offense and whose statutory purpose includes fighting against sexual violence, domestic violence, or violence against children, may exercise the rights granted to a civil party for the following types of offenses:

- Intentional attacks on life;

- Physical attacks on the person;

- Sexual attacks.

#### Article 18.- Associations Fighting against Kidnapping, Human Trafficking, and Sexual Exploitation

Any properly registered association that has existed for at least 3 (three) years prior to the date of the offense and whose statutory purpose includes fighting against kidnapping, human trafficking, or sexual exploitation, may exercise the rights granted to a civil party for offenses provided by law relating to the fight against kidnapping, human trafficking, and sexual exploitation.

#### Article 19.- Associations Fighting against Racism and Discrimination

Any properly registered association that has existed for at least 3 (three) years prior to the date of the offense and whose statutory purpose includes fighting against racism and providing assistance to victims of discrimination based on national origin, ethnicity, race

religion, may exercise the rights granted to a civil party for the following types of offenses when the offense was committed because of the national origin of a person or because that person is a member of a real or supposed ethnic group, race, or religion:

- Discrimination;

- Intentional attacks on life or physical integrity;

- Destruction and damage.

#### Article 20.- Admissibility of Complaints from Associations

In the cases provided for in Article 17 (Associations fighting against sexual violence, domestic violence, or violence against children) to Article 19 (Associations fighting against racism and discrimination) of this Code, the complaint of an association is admissible only if there is evidence showing that the consent of the victim or their legal representative has been obtained. In the event of an attack on life, the complaint of an association is admissible only if the association can show evidence that the consent of the victim's successors has been obtained.

#### Article 21.- Defendants in a Civil Action

A civil action may be brought against all persons who must repair the damage resulting from the offense, including:

- Perpetrators and co-perpetrators of the offense;

- Instigators and accomplices;

- Persons civilly liable.

#### Article 22.- Relationship between the Jurisdiction of Criminal Courts and Civil Courts

A civil action may be brought at the same time as a criminal action before a criminal trial court.

A civil action may also be brought before a civil trial court. In such a case, the civil action shall be stayed as long as no definitive decision has been rendered on the criminal action.

#### Article 23.- Prior Declaration of Guilt

The criminal court may rule on the reparation of damage only if it has previously examined the elements of the criminal offense and declared the accused guilty.

#### Article 24.- Death of the Accused

A civil party may request reparation of damage from the successors of the accused if the accused dies during the course of the criminal proceedings. Although the criminal action is extinguished, the criminal court remains competent to rule on the civil action brought against those successors.

#### Article 25.- Waiver and Withdrawal of Complaint by the Victim

The victim may waive their right to bring a civil action or withdraw their complaint. The waiver or withdrawal of the complaint by the victim cannot terminate or stay the exercise of the criminal action, except for the provisions in point 2 of Article 8 (Other causes of extinguishment of criminal action) of this Code.

#### Article 26.- Prescription of Civil Action

A civil action shall be subject to the statute of limitations according to the rules of the Civil Code. However, this civil action can no longer be brought before a criminal court after the expiration of the limitation period for the criminal action.

# BOOK 2

## AUTHORITIES IN CHARGE OF PROSECUTION, INVESTIGATION, AND INQUIRY

### TITLE 1

#### PROSECUTION SERVICE

### CHAPTER 1

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 27.- Role of the Prosecution Service

The Prosecution Service shall conduct criminal charges and request the application of the law by the court. The Prosecution Service shall ensure the enforcement of criminal decisions and shall ensure careful attention to the issuance and circulation of arrest warrants.

In the performance of their duties, prosecutors have the right to directly request the assistance of the public force.

The prosecutor must be present during criminal hearings.

#### Article 28.- Prosecutors

Prosecutors include:

1- The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General, and Prosecutors attached to the Supreme Court.

2- The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General, and Prosecutors attached to the Court of Appeal.

3- The Royal Prosecutor and Deputy Royal Prosecutors attached to the Court of First Instance.

#### Article 29.- Hierarchy of the Prosecution Service

The Minister of Justice may report to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal or the Royal Prosecutor at the Court of First Instance any offenses of which he has knowledge. He may issue written instructions, which must be included in the file, directing the Prosecutor General or the Royal Prosecutor to initiate prosecutions or to make such submissions as the Minister deems appropriate.

The Minister of Justice may not issue instructions to the Prosecutor General or the Royal Prosecutor to file any criminal case without action.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal has authority over all prosecutors within his territorial jurisdiction. He may issue instructions to prosecute or have prosecutions directed, or to make such submissions as he deems appropriate.

The Royal Prosecutor has authority over prosecutors within his territorial jurisdiction. He may issue instructions to prosecutors to prosecute or have prosecutions directed, or to make such submissions as he deems appropriate.

#### Article 30.- Oral Submissions

Every prosecutor must comply with the instructions of his superiors in accordance with the hierarchy.

But at the time of the hearing, the prosecutor shall be free to make such oral observations as he deems appropriate in accordance with his conscience. No disciplinary action may be taken against a prosecutor for observations made orally during a hearing.

### CHAPTER 2

#### FUNCTIONS OF THE PROSECUTOR GENERAL AT THE SUPREME COURT

#### Article 31.- Representation of the Prosecution Service at the Supreme Court

The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General, and Prosecutors attached to the Supreme Court shall represent the Prosecution Service at that court.

#### Article 32.- Role of the Prosecutor General at the Supreme Court

The Prosecutor General at the Supreme Court shall participate in ensuring respect for the law in cases involving appeals on points of law (cassation), review of cases, or other petitions submitted to the Supreme Court for examination.

### CHAPTER 3

#### FUNCTIONS OF THE PROSECUTOR GENERAL AT THE COURT OF APPEAL

#### Article 33.- Representation of the Prosecution Service at the Court of Appeal

The Prosecutor General, Deputy Prosecutors General, and Prosecutors attached to the Court of Appeal shall represent the Prosecution Service at that court.

#### Article 34.- Role of the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall monitor the application of the law within the territorial jurisdiction of the Court of Appeal.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal may conduct inspections of all prosecution services within his territorial jurisdiction.

In the event of a serious offense, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal must report to the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 35.- Power of the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall monitor and supervise the judicial police.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal may summon the heads of judicial police units to discuss issues relating to the functioning of those units.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal may assign tasks to all judicial police officers or agents to perform all duties that he deems useful for the management or processing of judicial police warrants.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal may conduct inspections of judicial police units at any time. The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal may participate in interrogations and may supervise the implementation of detention, specifically respect for legal forms and the management of detention facilities.

All heads of judicial police units, judicial police officers, and judicial police agents must comply with the instructions of the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

### CHAPTER 4

#### FUNCTIONS OF THE ROYAL PROSECUTOR AT THE COURT OF FIRST INSTANCE

#### SECTION 1

##### PROSECUTION SERVICE AT THE COURT OF FIRST INSTANCE

#### Article 36.- Representation of the Prosecution Service at the Court of First Instance

The Royal Prosecutor and Deputy Royal Prosecutors attached to the Court of First Instance shall represent the Prosecution Service at that court.

#### Article 37.- Power of the Royal Prosecutor

The Royal Prosecutor shall lead and coordinate the activities of all judicial police officers and agents within his territorial jurisdiction. However, when executing a rogatory commission, judicial police officers shall be under the authority of the investigating judge.

The Royal Prosecutor shall exercise all powers recognized by this Code for judicial police officers in investigations.

The Royal Prosecutor may go to the scene of an investigation and give all useful instructions to judicial police officers and agents. In cases of special necessity, the Royal Prosecutor may withdraw the right to investigate from judicial police officers and agents and arrange for their replacement.

The Royal Prosecutor may conduct inspections of judicial police units at any time. The Royal Prosecutor may participate in interrogations and may supervise the implementation of detention measures, specifically respect for legal forms and the management of detention facilities.

#### Article 38.- Power of Deputy Royal Prosecutors

Deputy Royal Prosecutors, who are under the authority of the Royal Prosecutor, shall exercise all powers held by the Royal Prosecutor in the investigation and prosecution of offenses.

#### Article 39.- Territorial Jurisdiction of the Royal Prosecutor

The competent Royal Prosecutor is:

- The Royal Prosecutor of the place where the offense was committed;

- The Royal Prosecutor of the place of residence of the person suspected of having committed the offense;

- The Royal Prosecutor of the place of arrest of the person suspected of having committed the offense.

#### SECTION 2

##### EXERCISE OF THE CRIMINAL ACTION

#### Article 40.- Prosecutorial Discretion

The Royal Prosecutor shall decide on the action to be taken on complaints and reports that he receives directly or that are transmitted to him by judicial police officers.

The Royal Prosecutor may decide to file the case without action or to initiate criminal prosecution. Before making a decision, the Royal Prosecutor may open a preliminary investigation or order additional inquiries.

In the event of a serious offense, the Royal Prosecutor shall report to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal, who shall in turn report to the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 41.- Filing of the Case Without Action

When a case is filed without action, the Royal Prosecutor must notify the complainant of his decision as soon as possible, and at the latest within 2 (two) months from the date the complaint was recorded in the register provided for in Article 50 (Complaints Register) of this Code.

The decision to file a case without action must be based on legal and factual grounds. The decision to file a case without action does not have the effect of res judicata.

The Royal Prosecutor may revoke his decision as long as the criminal action has not been extinguished.

If the complainant is not satisfied with the decision to file the case without action, he may appeal to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

The appeal must be made within 2 (two) months from the date of notification of the decision to file the case without action. The appeal shall be made by an ordinary petition to the relevant prosecution service. The file shall then be immediately transmitted to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal by the prosecution service at the Court of First Instance.

If the appeal is found to be well-founded, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall issue instructions to the Royal Prosecutor at the Court of First Instance to initiate prosecution. The Prosecutor General shall issue instructions by written guidance. Otherwise, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall confirm the decision of the Royal Prosecutor. The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal must notify the appellant of this decision.

#### Article 42.- Obligation to Report Felonies or Misdemeanors

Every public authority or official who, in the performance of their duties, acquires knowledge of a felony or misdemeanor, must immediately inform the Royal Prosecutor or a judicial police officer, and must transmit all information, reports, documents, and evidence relating to the offense.

No exception may be made except for professional secrecy in cases where the law specifically provides for it.

#### Article 43.- Initiation of Prosecution

Criminal prosecution may be initiated by:

- Opening an investigation;

- Direct referral for trial; or

- Procedures for immediate appearance.

#### Article 44.- Opening an Investigation

In felony cases, the Royal Prosecutor must open an investigation.

An investigation shall be opened by an introductory submission sent to the investigating judge. An investigation may be opened against one or more named persons or against an unknown person.

The introductory submission shall contain:

- A summary of the facts;

- The legal characterization of the offense;

- The legal provisions that define and punish the offense; and

- The name of the person against whom the investigation is requested, if possible.

The introductory submission must be dated and signed.

These formal requirements are mandatory; otherwise, the introductory submission shall be null and void.

#### Article 45.- Initiation of Prosecution in Misdemeanor Cases

In misdemeanor cases, the Royal Prosecutor may:

- Open an investigation by following the provisions of Article 44 (Opening an investigation) of this Code;

- Refer the accused directly for trial before the Court of First Instance by following the provisions of Article 46 (Direct referral for trial) of this Code; or

- Order the immediate appearance of the accused before the Court of First Instance by following the provisions of Article 47 (Immediate appearance) and Article 48 (Procedure for immediate appearance) of this Code.

#### Article 46.- Direct Referral for Trial

A direct referral for trial is an order to the accused to appear before the Court of First Instance. The direct referral order shall contain:

- The identity of the accused;

- A summary of the facts;

- The legal characterization of the offense.

- Legal provisions that define and punish the offense.

The direct referral order shall specify the court, the place, the date, and the time of the hearing. The order must state that the accused may be assisted by a lawyer.

#### Article 47.- Immediate Appearance

The Royal Prosecutor may order the immediate appearance of the accused before the Court of First Instance if all the following conditions are met:

- The offense is flagrant, as defined in Article 86 (Definition of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor) and Article 88 (Acts Deemed Flagrant Felonies or Misdemeanors) of this Code;

- The offense is punishable by imprisonment of at least 1 (one) year and at most 5 (five) years;

- The accused is of age (an adult);

- The case is ready for trial.

#### Article 48.- Procedure for Immediate Appearance

When deciding to use the procedure for immediate appearance, the Royal Prosecutor shall:

- Verify the identity of the person brought before him;

- Inform the person of the facts alleged against them and the legal characterization of the offense;

- Receive the person's statement, if they wish to make one;

- Prepare a report on the order for immediate appearance.

The Royal Prosecutor shall inform the accused that they have the right to be assisted by a lawyer of their choice or a court-appointed lawyer, in accordance with the Law on the Statute of Lawyers.

The chosen or appointed lawyer shall be informed immediately. The lawyer may examine the case file and communicate with the accused.

A record of these formalities must be included in the report; otherwise, the procedure shall be null and void.

The Court of First Instance shall be seized by the report on the order for immediate appearance.

The accused shall be kept in custody by the public force until they appear before the court, which must hold the hearing on the same day.

At the appearance, the court, after verifying the identity of the accused and providing a summary of the facts alleged against them, shall inform the accused that they have the right to time to prepare their defense.

If the accused requests time, or if the court considers that the case is not ready for trial, the case shall be postponed to a subsequent hearing.

The court may order the provisional detention of the accused by a reasoned judgment. In this judgment, the court must refer to the conditions set forth in Article 205 (Reasons for Provisional Detention) of this Code. The court shall issue a detention order.

The judgment on the merits must be rendered within a period not exceeding 2 (two) weeks from the date of the appearance before the court. The provisional detention shall end by operation of law after the expiration of 2 (two) weeks.

If the court seized by the procedure for immediate appearance finds that the conditions of Article 47 (Immediate appearance) of this Code are not met, or that the complexity of the case requires further investigation, the court shall return the file to the Royal Prosecutor for the opening of an investigation. The accused must be brought before an investigating judge on the same day; otherwise, the accused must be released.

#### Article 49.- Direct Referral for Trial in Petty Offense Cases

In petty offense cases, the Royal Prosecutor shall summon the accused to appear directly before the trial court, in accordance with the provisions of Article 46 (Direct referral for trial) of this Code.

#### Article 50.- Complaint Register

At each Court of First Instance, a prosecution register shall be established for recording complaints made directly to the Royal Prosecutor or transmitted by judicial police officers.

Every complaint must be recorded in the register with the following information:

- Identity of the complainant;

- Date the complaint was received by the prosecution service;

- Source of the complaint: whether it was made directly to the Royal Prosecutor or transmitted by a judicial police officer;

- Nature of the facts reported;

- Action taken on the complaint: decision to file without action or to initiate criminal prosecution.

The complaint register shall be maintained by the prosecution clerk under the supervision of the Royal Prosecutor. The register may be inspected by all judicial authorities, notably the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

### TITLE 2

#### INVESTIGATING JUDGE

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### INVESTIGATING JUDGE

#### Article 51.- Appointment by the President of the Court

When an investigating judge is unable to perform his duties due to leave, illness, or any other reason, another investigating judge of the same court shall be temporarily appointed by the President of the Court to perform the investigative functions.

In the event that there is no investigating judge, another judge of the same court may be temporarily appointed by the President of the Court to perform the investigative functions.

The President of the Court shall issue an appointment order which is not subject to appeal.

Furthermore, the provisions of Article 26 (Distribution of Cases) of the Code of Civil Procedure must be respected.

#### Article 52.- Distribution of Cases by the President of the Court

When there are several investigating judges in a court, cases shall be distributed among them by the President of the Court.

#### Article 53.- Withdrawal of a Case from an Investigating Judge

The withdrawal of a case from one investigating judge to be assigned to another investigating judge may be requested from the President of the Court in the interest of the proper administration of justice. This request may be made by a reasoned application from the Royal Prosecutor, acting on his own initiative or at the request of a party.

The grounds for withdrawing a case from an investigating judge must follow the provisions of Article 556 (Grounds for Challenge of Judges) of this Code.

#### Article 54.- Incompatibility Resulting in Nullity

An investigating judge may not participate in the trial of a criminal case in which he has acted as the investigating judge; otherwise, the trial shall be null and void.

The investigating judge shall exercise the powers provided for in this Code.

### TITLE 3

#### INVESTIGATING CHAMBER

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### INVESTIGATING CHAMBER

#### Article 55.- Special Chamber of the Court of Appeal

In the Court of Appeal, there shall be a chamber known as the Investigating Chamber. The Investigating Chamber has the jurisdiction to rule on appeals brought against decisions of the investigating judge.

Members of the Investigating Chamber may not participate in the trial of a criminal case in which they have acted as the investigating judge; otherwise, the judgment shall be null and void.

### TITLE 4

#### JUDICIAL POLICE

### CHAPTER 1

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 56.- Mission of the Judicial Police

The judicial police are assistants to the judicial power.

The judicial police have the duty to investigate felonies, misdemeanors, and petty offenses, to identify and arrest offenders, and to collect evidence.

#### Article 57.- Composition of the Judicial Police

The judicial police include:

1- Judicial police officers;

2- Judicial police agents;

3- Other officials and public agents who are specifically empowered by law to investigate certain offenses within their jurisdiction.

#### Article 58.- Coordination of the Activities of the Judicial Police

The Royal Prosecutor shall lead and coordinate the activities of all judicial police officers and agents within his territorial jurisdiction.

The Royal Prosecutor has authority over other officials and public agents mentioned in Article 82 (Empowerment of other officials and public agents) of this Code when those officials perform their duties as judicial police.

When executing a rogatory commission, judicial police officers shall be under the authority of the investigating judge.

#### Article 59.- Supervision of the Judicial Police

The judicial police shall be under the monitoring and supervision of the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall exercise disciplinary power over the judicial police.

### CHAPTER 2

#### JUDICIAL POLICE OFFICERS

#### SECTION 1

##### QUALIFICATIONS AS JUDICIAL POLICE OFFICER

#### Article 60.- Judicial Police Officers

The following persons have the status of judicial police officer:

1- National police officers with the rank of at least sub-lieutenant who have served for at least 2 (two) years in the national police and have obtained a higher diploma in judicial police;

2- Officers of the Royal Gendarmerie who have served for at least 2 (two) years in the Royal Gendarmerie and have obtained a higher diploma in judicial police;

3- Persons with the following ranks and functions:

a- Governors and Vice-Governors of provinces and municipalities; Governors and Vice-Governors of districts and sections; and Chiefs and Vice-Chiefs of communes and quarters (Sangkat).

b- Directors and Deputy Directors of the Central Judicial Police Department, Directors and Deputy Directors of the Central Security Department, and Directors and Deputy Directors of the Central Public Order Department of the General Commissariat of National Police.

c- Directors and Deputy Directors of Departments, Directors and Deputy Directors of Offices

Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections in charge of criminal affairs under the Central Judicial Police Department, the Central Security Department, and the Central Public Order Department;

d- Directors and Deputy Directors of Departments, Directors and Deputy Directors of Offices, Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections of:

- Border Police Department "for cross-border offenses";

- Anti-Drug Department;

- Tourism Police Department;

- Special Commissariat for Heritage Protection.

e- Police Commissioners and Deputy Police Commissioners of provinces and municipalities;

f- Directors and Deputy Directors of Offices, Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections in charge of criminal affairs attached to provincial or municipal police commissariats, such as:

- Felonies Crime Office and Misdemeanors Crime Office;

- Anti-Human Trafficking and Juvenile Protection Office;

- Economic Police Office;

- Scientific and Technical Criminal Police Office;

- Anti-Drug Office;

- Anti-Terrorism Office; and

- Foreigner Office.

g- Inspectors and Deputy Inspectors, Directors and Deputy Directors of Criminal Sections of district or section police inspectorates;

h- Directors and Deputy Directors of the Traffic Police Office, Directors and Deputy Directors of the Traffic Police Section "for traffic offenses"; Directors and Deputy Directors of the Weapons and Explosives Management Office, Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections of the Weapons and Explosives Management Office "for weapons and explosives offenses"; Directors and Deputy Directors of Units, Directors and Deputy Directors

Tourism Police Office and Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections of the Tourism Police Office.

i- Chiefs and Deputy Chiefs of commune or quarter (Sangkat) administrative police posts "for criminal offenses".

j- Commanders and Deputy Commanders of the Royal Gendarmerie in charge of criminal affairs.

k- Commanders and Deputy Commanders of the Provincial or Municipal Royal Gendarmerie in charge of criminal affairs.

l- Commanders and Deputy Commanders of the District or Section Royal Gendarmerie in charge of criminal affairs.

m- Directors and Deputy Directors of Offices, Directors and Deputy Directors of Sections of the Royal Gendarmerie in charge of criminal affairs.

n- Directors and Deputy Directors of other units in charge of criminal investigation that are created in case of necessity.

The Minister who is the supervisory authority of police officers or officers of the Royal Gendarmerie mentioned in items 1, 2, and 3 of this Article shall appoint the police officers or Royal Gendarmerie officers who are to perform the duties of judicial police officer by a joint proclamation (Prakas) with the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 61.- Higher Diploma in Judicial Police

The Higher Diploma in Judicial Police shall be awarded after passing a professional examination consisting of the following basic subjects:

1- Criminal Law;

2- Criminal Procedure;

3- Humanitarian duties as set forth in the Code of Conduct established by the United Nations.

The diploma must be jointly signed and sealed by the Minister of Justice and the relevant Minister.

#### Article 62.- Board of Examiners

The Board of Examiners for the examination shall be presided over by the Prosecutor General at the Supreme Court or a prosecutor at the Supreme Court designated for this purpose.

1- For National Police officers, the Board of Examiners shall consist of:

- One investigating judge appointed by the President of the Supreme Court;

- One prosecutor appointed by the Prosecutor General at the Supreme Court;

- Two representatives of the Ministry of Interior.

2- For Royal Gendarmerie personnel, the Board of Examiners shall consist of:

- One investigating judge appointed by the President of the Supreme Court;

- One prosecutor appointed by the Prosecutor General at the Supreme Court;

- Two representatives of the Ministry of National Defense.

The procedures for issuing the diploma shall be determined by a joint proclamation (Prakas) of the relevant Ministers.

#### Article 63.- Oath of Judicial Police Officers

Every judicial police officer must take an oath to perform their duties faithfully. The oath shall be taken before the Court of Appeal. The oath does not need to be retaken when subsequent qualifications as a judicial police officer are obtained. The formula of the oath shall be determined by a proclamation (Prakas) of the Minister of Justice.

#### SECTION 2

##### MISCONDUCT IN THE PERFORMANCE OF DUTIES

#### Article 64.- Disciplinary Procedure

All misconduct committed by a judicial police officer in the performance of their duties must be reported by the Royal Prosecutor or the investigating judge to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal. Depending on the case, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall declare...

and notify the Minister of Interior or the Minister of National Defense to initiate disciplinary proceedings. The Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall be informed of the subsequent steps of the disciplinary procedure.

#### Article 65.- Disciplinary Sanctions

Disciplinary sanctions imposed by the Minister of Interior or the Minister of National Defense do not preclude criminal prosecution if an offense has been committed.

When a judicial police officer commits an offense in the performance of their duties, the investigative or trial jurisdiction seized of the case may be dessaised (withdrawn) in favor of another jurisdiction by decision of the Supreme Court, in the interest of the proper administration of justice. The unit to which the officer belongs shall be immediately informed.

Such a request for withdrawal of jurisdiction shall be made by the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 66.- Prohibition from Performing the Duties of Judicial Police Officer

When the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal considers that the seriousness of the misconduct committed by a judicial police officer is incompatible with the performance of their duties, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal has the power to:

1- Temporarily prohibit the officer from performing the duties of judicial police officer for a maximum period of 5 (five) years;

2- Permanently prohibit the officer from performing the duties of judicial police officer.

Before making a decision, the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal shall summon the judicial police officer and record their explanations. The officer may be assisted by a lawyer or an advisor of their choice.

The decision of prohibition must be reasoned. This decision shall be notified to the Minister of Interior or the Minister of National Defense, as the case may be.

This decision may be appealed to the Minister of Justice, who may cancel the prohibition decision or reduce the duration of the prohibition imposed by the Prosecutor General.

#### SECTION 3

##### TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION OF JUDICIAL POLICE OFFICERS

#### Article 67.- Adminstrative District of Assignment

Each judicial police officer has jurisdiction within the territorial boundaries of the unit to which they are assigned.

In Phnom Penh, judicial police officers have jurisdiction over the entire city, regardless of where their specific unit is located.

The decision to appoint a judicial police officer must specify the administrative district where the officer is assigned. This decision must be notified to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal.

#### Article 68.- Extension of Territorial Jurisdiction

In cases of flagrant felony or misdemeanor as defined in Article 86 (Definition of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor) and Article 87 (Arrest in case of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor) of this Code, and if there is urgency, the Royal Prosecutor may authorize judicial police officers to operate throughout the national territory. Permission may be given orally, but such permission must be noted in the investigation report.

In the context of an investigation, when an investigating judge issues a rogatory commission, in case of urgency, the judge may authorize judicial police officers to operate throughout the national territory.

When operating outside their assigned district, judicial police officers must request the assistance of judicial police officers with territorial jurisdiction.

The judge who authorized the extension of jurisdiction must inform the Royal Prosecutor with territorial jurisdiction by any means.

#### Article 69.- Consequences of Non-Compliance with Jurisdictional Rules

The rules of territorial jurisdiction provided for in the preceding articles are mandatory. Failure to comply with them shall result in the nullity of the acts performed by the judicial police officer.

#### Article 70.- Orders of the Judicial Authority

In the performance of their mission, judicial police officers shall receive or request orders only from the judicial authority.

#### SECTION 4

##### MISSION OF JUDICIAL POLICE OFFICERS

#### Article 71.- Reporting of Offenses

Judicial police officers shall receive complaints and reports. They shall investigate offenses.

Judicial police officers conduct investigations into flagrant offenses and preliminary investigations under the conditions of Article 84 (Difference in powers of judicial police officers according to investigation) and Article 110 (Power of reasoning of the judge) of this Code.

When an investigation has been opened, judicial police officers execute rogatory commissions from the investigating judge under the conditions set forth in Article 173 (Definition of rogatory commission) and Article 184 (Reports prepared by judicial police officers) of this Code.

#### Article 72.- Reports

Every complaint received by a judicial police officer must be recorded in a report.

The report shall accurately record the statements of the complainant. If necessary, the judicial police officer may call upon an interpreter who shall take an oath, according to their beliefs or religion, to interpret faithfully. In no case may the interpreter be chosen from among the police or Royal Gendarmerie personnel who are part of the operating force.

The report of the filing of a complaint must contain the following information:

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer;

- The unit to which the judicial police officer belongs;

- The date.

Each page must be signed by the judicial police officer and the complainant.

Any crossings-out, additions, or references must be validated by the signatures of the judicial police officer and the complainant in the margin.

Before signing or affixing their thumbprint, the complainant must reread the report. If necessary, the judicial police officer shall read the contents of the report aloud.

#### Article 73.- Complaint Register

At each judicial police unit, a complaint register must be established. Whenever a complaint is filed, the judicial police officer must record in the register:

- The identity of the complainant;

- The date the complaint was filed;

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer who prepared the complaint report;

- The nature of the alleged acts;

- The action taken on the complaint, such as referral to the Royal Prosecutor or opening of an investigation.

The complaint register must be permanently maintained and available to judicial authorities upon request.

#### Article 74.- Action Following a Complaint

The judicial police officer who receives a complaint must immediately either open an investigation or transmit the complaint report to the Royal Prosecutor for action.

Before opening an investigation, the judicial police officer may request instructions from the Royal Prosecutor.

When receiving a report that provides clear and reasonable grounds for suspicion, the judicial police officer must inform the Royal Prosecutor and request instructions.

#### Article 75.- Interference with Judicial Action

In no case may a judicial police officer file a criminal case without action, even if a settlement has been reached between the offender and the victim or if the complaint has been withdrawn.

A judicial police officer who intentionally withholds a report or evidence, or conceals the existence of a report or evidence from the judicial authorities, shall be deemed to have committed an offense punishable under the Criminal Code provisions relating to the investigation for evidence.

### CHAPTER 3

#### JUDICIAL POLICE AGENTS

#### Article 76.- Qualifications as Judicial Police Agent

The following persons have the status of judicial police agent:

1- Police officers and deputy police officers who do not have the status of judicial police officer.

2- Other National Police agents;

3- Officers of the Royal Gendarmerie who do not have the status of judicial police officer;

4- Other personnel of the Royal Gendarmerie.

#### Article 77.- Oath of Judicial Police Agents

Judicial police agents must take an oath to perform their duties honestly. The oath shall be taken before the Court of First Instance. The oath does not need to be retaken when subsequent qualifications as a judicial police agent are obtained. The formula of the oath shall be determined by a proclamation (Prakas) of the Minister of Justice.

#### Article 78.- Mission of Judicial Police Agents

Judicial police agents have the mission to:

1- Investigate offenses, especially petty offenses;

2- Assist judicial police officers in the performance of their duties.

For investigations, judicial police agents prepare reports. These reports have the value of simple information only.

In no case may judicial police agents perform acts that are reserved exclusively for judicial police officers. Any acts performed in violation of this prohibition shall be null and void.

#### Article 79.- Misconduct in the Performance of Duties

All misconduct committed by a judicial police agent in the performance of their duties must be reported by the Royal Prosecutor or the investigating judge to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal. Depending on the case, the Prosecutor General shall notify the Minister of Interior or the Minister of National Defense to initiate disciplinary proceedings. The Prosecutor General shall be informed of the subsequent steps of the disciplinary procedure.

#### Article 80.- Disciplinary Sanctions

Disciplinary sanctions imposed by the Minister of Interior or the Minister of National Defense do not preclude criminal prosecution if an offense has been committed.

When a judicial police agent commits an offense in the performance of their duties, the Supreme Court may withdraw jurisdiction in favor of another jurisdiction in the interest of the proper administration of justice.

Such a request for withdrawal of jurisdiction shall be made by the Royal Prosecutor.

### CHAPTER 4

#### JURISDICTION OF THE NATIONAL POLICE AND THE ROYAL GENDARMERIE

#### Article 81.- Determination of Jurisdiction

In the field of judicial police, the National Police and the Royal Gendarmerie have the jurisdiction set forth in Article 56 (Mission of the Judicial Police) of this Code.

Furthermore, for military offenses, only the Royal Gendarmerie has the status of judicial police.

The operations and cooperation between National Police officers and Royal Gendarmerie officers in the performance of their duties as judicial police shall be determined by a sub-decree.

Judicial police units with national jurisdiction may be created by a royal decree.

### CHAPTER 5

#### OTHER OFFICIALS AND PUBLIC AGENTS WITH SPECIAL POWER TO INVESTIGATE CERTAIN OFFENSES

#### Article 82.- Empowerment of Other Officials and Public Agents

Other officials and public agents who are specifically empowered by law to investigate offenses shall be under the authority of the Royal Prosecutor when performing those duties.

The forms and procedures for granting empowerment shall be determined by a joint proclamation (Prakas) of the Ministry of Justice and the relevant Ministry.

Other officials and public agents must take an oath to perform their duties honestly. The oath shall be taken before the Court of First Instance. The formula of the oath shall be determined by a proclamation (Prakas) of the Ministry of Justice.

For investigations, these officials and agents prepare reports. These reports have the value of simple information only.

All misconduct committed by other officials or public agents in the performance of their duties must be reported by the Royal Prosecutor or the investigating judge to the Prosecutor General at the Court of Appeal. The Prosecutor General shall notify the Minister to whom the official or agent is subordinate to initiate disciplinary proceedings. The Prosecutor General shall be informed of the subsequent steps of the disciplinary procedure. Disciplinary sanctions do not preclude criminal prosecution if an offense has been committed.

## BOOK 3

### INVESTIGATION

### TITLE 1

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 83.- Secrecy of the Investigation

The investigation must be secret. All persons participating in the investigation, particularly prosecutors, lawyers, clerks, police, Royal Gendarmerie, officials, experts, interpreters, doctors, and persons with the jurisdiction mentioned in Article 99 (Technical or Scientific Examination) of this Code, are bound by professional secrecy.

However, professional secrecy is not an obstacle to the right of defense.

Furthermore, the Royal Prosecutor is authorized to make public statements when he considers that information published about a case is inaccurate.

The violation of the secrecy of the investigation is an offense punishable under the criminal law in force.

#### Article 84.- Distinction of Powers of Judicial Police Officers according to Investigation

The powers granted to judicial police officers vary depending on whether the officer is acting in the context of a flagrant investigation or a preliminary investigation.

### TITLE 2

#### FLAGRANT INVESTIGATION

### CHAPTER 1

#### FLAGRANT FELONIES OR MISDEMEANORS

#### Article 85.- Powers of Judicial Police Officers in Flagrant Investigation

In cases of flagrant felonies or misdemeanors, judicial police officers must comply with the provisions of this Title.

#### Article 86.- Definition of Flagrant Felonies or Misdemeanors

A flagrant felony or misdemeanor is:

- An offense that is being committed;

- An offense that has just been committed.

An offense is also deemed to be a flagrant felony or misdemeanor when, shortly after the commission of the offense:

- The suspected person is chased by public clamor;

- A person is found in possession of objects, or has marks or clear clues indicating...

that lead to the conclusion that the person committed or participated in the commission of the offense.

#### Article 87.- Arrest in case of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor

In case of a flagrant felony or misdemeanor, any person may arrest the offender and bring them before the nearest judicial police officer.

#### Article 88.- Cases Deemed Flagrant Felonies or Misdemeanors

An offense is also deemed to be a flagrant felony or misdemeanor, even if it does not fall within the cases mentioned in Article 86 (Definition of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor) above, for any felony or misdemeanor committed in a place where the occupant requests the Royal Prosecutor or a judicial police officer to investigate, or a judicial police agent if there is no judicial police officer at that location. In the latter case, the judicial police agent must immediately report to a judicial police officer.

### CHAPTER 2

#### INVESTIGATIVE MEASURES

#### Article 89.- Initial Measures in case of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor

In case of a flagrant felony or misdemeanor, the judicial police officer must immediately inform the Royal Prosecutor.

The judicial police officer shall proceed to the scene to investigate the offense. The judicial police officer shall ensure the preservation of any clues or evidence that might disappear.

The judicial police officer may prohibit any person from leaving the scene of the offense until the completion of their operations.

If he considers it useful, the Royal Prosecutor shall proceed to the scene and direct the investigation.

In case of urgency, the Royal Prosecutor may authorize any judicial police officer to operate throughout the national territory, in compliance with the conditions set forth in Article 68 (Extension of Territorial Jurisdiction) of this Code.

#### Article 90.- Report on Search and Examination

The judicial police officer shall prepare a report on their searches and examinations.

#### Article 91.- Searches

Judicial police officers may conduct searches. In this case, the judicial police officer must first obtain authorization from the Royal Prosecutor, even if given orally.

The search must be conducted in the presence of the occupant of the premises or, if there is no occupant, in the presence of 2 (two) witnesses. The witnesses shall be designated by the judicial police officer. Witnesses may not be police or Royal Gendarmerie personnel who are part of the operating force for the search.

Judicial police officers may not start a search before 06:00 (six) A.M. or after 18:00 (eighteen) P.M., except:

- In the cases mentioned in Article 86 (Definition of Flagrant Felony or Misdemeanor) of this Code;

- In the cases mentioned in Article 88 (Cases Deemed Flagrant Felonies or Misdemeanors) of this Code;

- In case of a call for help from within the premises;

- In case of a search in a place open to the public during opening hours;

- In case of a search in any place where drugs are produced, stored, trafficked, distributed, or used.

The judicial police officer shall prepare a search report, which shall include:

- The authorization of the Royal Prosecutor, stating the date and time it was given;

- The identity of the occupant of the premises or of each witness.

Judicial police officers may not conduct searches in a lawyer's office. Only the Royal Prosecutor or the investigating judge may conduct such a search, in the presence of the Bâtonnier (President of the Bar Association) or a representative of the Bâtonnier, or the lawyer concerned.

Judicial police officers may conduct searches in a press building, a print media enterprise, or an audiovisual media enterprise only in the presence of the Royal Prosecutor or an investigating judge who shall ensure that the search does not affect the free exercise of the profession of journalist and does not lead to an improper delay in the dissemination of information.

#### Article 92.- Sealing of Evidence

Judicial police officers may seize objects as evidence. The judicial police officer must place seals on the seized objects.

After showing the seized items to the occupant of the premises or to the 2 (two) witnesses, the judicial police officer shall prepare a seizure report including an inventory of the seized items. The seizure report must be signed or thumbprinted by the occupant of the premises or by the two witnesses.

#### Article 93.- Report on Interrogation

Judicial police officers may summon and bring to their unit any person suspected of having participated in the offense. The judicial police officer shall interrogate such persons.

Each interrogation must be recorded in a report.

The report shall accurately record the statements of the person being interrogated. If necessary, the judicial police officer may call upon an interpreter who shall take an oath to tell the truth according to their belief or religion. The interpreter may not be chosen from among the police or Royal Gendarmerie personnel or persons involved in the same case.

Each page of the report must be signed or thumbprinted by the person who made the statements.

Before signing or thumbprinting, the person must reread the report. If necessary, the judicial police officer shall read the contents of the report aloud.

#### Article 94.- Summons to Appear in Flagrant Investigation

Judicial police officers may summon and interrogate any person capable of providing information about the facts.

The summoned person is required to appear. In case of refusal, the judicial police officer shall inform the Royal Prosecutor, who may issue a summons to appear. This summons must identify the person concerned, be dated, and be signed and sealed by the Royal Prosecutor. The summons to appear authorizes the judicial police officer to use public force to compel the person to appear. Paragraphs 2 to 5 of Article 93 (Report on Interrogation) of this Code shall apply.

#### Article 95.- Technical or Scientific Examination

If technical or scientific examinations that cannot be delayed are required, the judicial police officer may call upon any person with the requisite skills. If the person is not listed on the national list of experts mentioned in Article 163 (National List of Experts) of this Code, they must take an oath, according to their religion or belief, to assist the court faithfully.

### CHAPTER 3

#### POLICE CUSTODY (GARDE À VUE)

#### Article 96.- Police Custody

To meet the needs of the investigation, the judicial police officer may place in custody any person suspected of having participated in the offense. The judicial police officer may also place in custody any person capable of providing information about the facts if the following condition is met:

- The person capable of providing information refuses to provide information;

- The Royal Prosecutor has given written consent for the custody.

The judicial police officer must immediately inform the Royal Prosecutor and provide all evidence justifying the custody. The duration of police custody is 48 (forty-eight) hours. This duration starts from the time the person arrives at the police or Royal Gendarmerie unit.

For felonies, when there are clues showing the culpability of the person in custody, the judicial police officer may extend the duration of custody if it is necessary for the proper conduct of the investigation. This extension must be requested from the Royal Prosecutor, who shall verify the grounds. The written and reasoned authorization for the extension of custody shall be included in the case file. The extension is an exceptional measure. This extension may not exceed 24 (twenty-four) hours, excluding the time necessary for transport.

An extension may not be granted if the person in custody is a minor.

For felonies, a minor between 14 and under 16 years of age may not be held in custody for more than 36 hours.

For misdemeanors, a minor between 14 and under 16 years of age may not be held in custody for more than 24 hours.

For felonies, a minor between 16 and under 18 years of age may not be held in custody for more than 48 hours.

For misdemeanors, a minor between 16 and under 18 years of age may not be held in custody for more than 36 hours.

Minors under 14 years of age may not be placed in police custody.

#### Article 97.- Report on Police Custody

When a person is placed in custody, the judicial police officer must immediately notify the person of the decision and the reasons for it. The judicial police officer shall also inform the person of the rights set forth in Article 98 (Assistance of a Lawyer during Police Custody) of this Code.

the judicial police officer may call upon an interpreter. The judicial police officer shall immediately prepare a custody report, which shall include:

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer who ordered the custody;

- The identity of the person placed in custody;

- The reasons for the custody;

- The date and time the custody began;

- The notification of the rights provided for in Article 98 (Assistance of a Lawyer during Police Custody) of this Code;

- If applicable, the name of the interpreter.

The report must be signed or thumbprinted by the person in custody after being read, or if the person is illiterate, after the judicial police officer has read the report aloud. If the person refuses to sign or thumbprint, the judicial police officer shall note this. The report shall be attached to the case file.

#### Article 98.- Assistance of a Lawyer during Police Custody

After 24 (twenty-four) hours from the start of the custody, the person in custody may request to speak with a lawyer or any person of their choice, provided that person is not involved in the same case. That person shall be informed of the request by any means and without delay. That person may go to the place of custody and communicate with the person in custody for 30 (thirty) minutes under conditions that ensure the confidentiality of the conversation. After the conversation, the person may make written observations that shall be attached to the case file.

#### Article 99.- Assistance of a Doctor during Police Custody

The Royal Prosecutor or the judicial police officer may request a doctor to examine the person in custody at any time. The doctor shall verify whether the health of the person in custody is compatible with the custody.

If the doctor considers that the health status of the person is incompatible with the custody, the judicial police officer shall immediately report to the Royal Prosecutor. The doctor shall issue a medical certificate which shall be attached to the case file. The judicial police officer shall note in the report the name of the doctor and the date and time of the medical examination.

The Royal Prosecutor may proceed directly to the scene to verify the conditions of the custody.

#### Article 100.- Minors in Police Custody

When a minor is placed in custody, the judicial police officer must inform the father, mother, legal representative, or person in charge of the minor by any means.

#### Article 101.- Custody Register

In each National Police or Royal Gendarmerie unit that holds persons in custody, a custody register must be established.

Every placement in custody must be recorded in the register with the following information:

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer who ordered the custody;

- The identity of the person placed in custody;

- The date and time the custody began and ended;

- The name and rank of the judge who authorized the extension of custody, if applicable;

- The name of the doctor who examined the person in custody, if applicable;

- The nature of the decision taken by the judicial authority at the end of the custody.

The register must be permanently maintained and available to judicial authorities upon request.

#### Article 102.- Summary Report on Police Custody

Every placement in custody must be recorded in a summary report.

The summary report shall include:

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer who ordered the custody;

- The identity of the person placed in custody;

- The reason for the custody;

- The date and time the custody began;

- The procedures for informing the Royal Prosecutor;

- The procedures for informing relatives as per Article 100 (Minors in Police Custody) of this Code;

- The name of the doctor who examined the person in custody;

- The identity of the person who communicated with the person in custody as per Article 98 (Assistance of a Lawyer during Police Custody) of this Code;

- The name and rank of the judge who authorized the extension of custody, if applicable;

- The duration of each interrogation and the duration of the rest periods between each interrogation;

- The date and time the custody ended;

- The nature of the decision taken by the judicial authority at the end of the custody;

- The conditions under which the custody took place and any incidents that occurred.

The summary report on police custody shall be attached to the case file.

### CHAPTER 4

#### PRESENTATION OF THE PERSON

#### Article 103.- Presentation of the Person in Custody

At the end of the period of police custody, the person in custody must be:

- Presented to the Royal Prosecutor; or

- Released.

This decision shall be made by the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 104.- Time for Presentation

When a person in custody must be presented to the Royal Prosecutor, they must be brought before the Royal Prosecutor immediately.

If the presentation of the person in custody cannot take place on the same day due to exceptional reasons resulting from transport difficulties or distance, additional time may be granted by the Royal Prosecutor.

In all cases, the presentation of the person in custody must be carried out as quickly as possible.

The reasons for the delay must be recorded in the report submitted to the Royal Prosecutor by the judicial police officer.

### CHAPTER 5

#### VARIOUS PROVISIONS RELATING TO FLAGRANT INVESTIGATION

#### Article 105.- Prohibition of Unauthorized Eavesdropping

Judicial police officers have no power to order the listening to or recording of telephone conversations. Judicial police officers have no power to order the interception and recording of transmissions by any means of telecommunication, such as faxes or emails.

#### Article 106.- Duration of Flagrant Investigation

The duration of a flagrant investigation may not exceed 7 (seven) days from the commission of the offense. Investigative acts must be performed continuously during this period. If the investigation is not completed within 7 (seven) days, the judicial police officer must seek instructions from the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 107.- Transmission of Reports to the Royal Prosecutor

Upon completion of the investigation, the judicial police officer must transmit all reports and evidence to the Royal Prosecutor.

When a person is presented to the Royal Prosecutor, the transmission of reports and evidence must take place at the same time as the presentation of the person.

#### Article 108.- Information to be Included in Reports

Reports prepared by judicial police officers must state:

- The name and rank of the judicial police officer;

- The unit to which the officer belongs;

- The date.

Each page must be signed by the judicial police officer.

Any deletions or references must be validated by the signature of the judicial police officer in the margin.

Interrogation reports or custody reports must include the identity of the person concerned.

#### Article 109.- Nullity for Non-Compliance with Provisions

The rules and forms prescribed in:

- Article 90 (Report on Search and Examination)

- Article 91 (Searches)

- Article 92 (Sealing of Evidence)

- Article 93 (Report on Interrogation)

- Article 94 (Summons to Appear in Flagrant Investigation)

- Article 95 (Technical or Scientific Examination)

- Article 96 (Police Custody)

- Article 97 (Report on Police Custody)

- Article 98 (Assistance of a Lawyer during Police Custody)

- Article 99 (Assistance of a Doctor during Police Custody)

- Article 100 (Minors in Police Custody)

- Article 105 (Prohibition of Unauthorized Eavesdropping)

- Article 106 (Duration of Flagrant Investigation); and

- Article 108 (Information to be Included in Reports)

of this Code are mandatory. Failure to comply with these rules shall result in the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 110.- Power of Reasoning of the Judge

In principle, reports have the value of simple information only. However, reports from judicial police officers are considered "prima facie evidence," meaning they are presumed to be true until proven otherwise. Contrary evidence may be presented to the judge by any legal means.

The judge freely assesses the value of reports prepared by judicial police officers in the context of a flagrant investigation.

### TITLE 3

#### PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATION

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATION

#### Article 111.- Opening of Preliminary Investigation

When aware of acts that may constitute a felony, misdemeanor, or petty offense, judicial police officers may conduct a preliminary investigation either on their own initiative or at the request of the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 112.- Rules Applicable to Preliminary Investigation

The provisions of:

- Article 90 (Report on Search and Examination)

- Article 95 (Technical or Scientific Examination)

- Article 105 (Prohibition of Unauthorized Eavesdropping)

- Article 107 (Transmission of Reports to the Royal Prosecutor); and

- Article 108 (Information to be Included in Reports)

of this Code shall apply to preliminary investigations.

#### Article 113.- Searches

When a preliminary investigation concerns a felony or misdemeanor, judicial police officers may conduct searches and seizures of evidence.

The judicial police officer must first obtain the express and certain consent of the occupant of the premises.

This consent must be in writing and handwritten by the occupant. If the occupant is illiterate, it must be clearly stated in the report that the occupant is illiterate and has consented to the search.

If the occupant is absent or refuses the search, the President of the competent Court of First Instance may issue a search warrant at the request of the Royal Prosecutor. The Royal Prosecutor must personally direct the search. The search must be conducted in the presence of the occupant or, if absent, in the presence of 2 (two) witnesses. The witnesses shall be designated by the Royal Prosecutor. Witnesses may not be police or Royal Gendarmerie personnel who are part of the operating force for the search. The search may not begin before 06:00 (six) A.M. or after 18:00 (eighteen) P.M.

The provisions of Article 92 (Sealing of Evidence) of this Code shall apply to seizures.

#### Article 114.- Summons to Appear in Preliminary Investigation

Judicial police officers may summon and interrogate any person suspected of having participated in the offense or capable of providing information about the facts.

The summoned person is required to appear. In case of refusal, the judicial police officer shall inform the Royal Prosecutor, who may issue a summons to appear. This summons must identify the person concerned, be dated, and be signed and sealed by the Royal Prosecutor.

The summons to appear authorizes the judicial police officer to use public force to compel the person to appear.

#### Article 115.- Report on Interrogation in Preliminary Investigation

Every interrogation must be recorded in a report.

The report must accurately record the statements of the person concerned. If necessary, the judicial police officer may call upon an interpreter who has taken an oath according to their religion or belief to translate faithfully. The interpreter may not be chosen from among the police, Royal Gendarmerie personnel, or persons involved in the same case.

#### Article 116.- Police Custody of Suspects

When a preliminary investigation concerns a felony or misdemeanor, the provisions of Chapter 3 (Police Custody) and Chapter 4 (Presentation of the Person) of Title 2 (Flagrant Investigation) of this Code shall apply.

#### Article 117.- Nullity for Non-Compliance with Provisions

In the context of a preliminary investigation, the rules and forms prescribed in:

- Article 96 (Police Custody)

- Article 97 (Report on Police Custody)

- Article 100 (Minors in Police Custody)

- Article 105 (Prohibition of Unauthorized Eavesdropping)

- Article 107 (Transmission of Reports to the Royal Prosecutor)

- Article 113 (Searches)

- Article 114 (Summons to Appear in Preliminary Investigation)

of this Code are mandatory. Failure to comply with these rules shall result in the nullity of the proceedings.

#### Article 118.- Power of Reasoning of the Judge

In principle, reports have the value of simple information only. However, reports from judicial police officers are considered "prima facie evidence," meaning they are presumed to be true until proven otherwise. Contrary evidence may be presented to the judge by any legal means.

The judge freely assesses the value of reports prepared by judicial police officers in the context of a preliminary investigation.

### TITLE 4

#### RESTITUTION OF SEIZED ITEMS DURING INVESTIGATION

### SINGLE CHAPTER

#### RESTITUTION OF SEIZED ITEMS DURING INVESTIGATION

#### Article 119.- Competent Authority to Order Restitution

The Royal Prosecutor is competent to order the restitution of objects seized during the investigation to their owner, provided that these objects are not necessary for the manifestation of the truth and if the ownership of the objects is not seriously contested.

However, objects that are dangerous to persons or property, such as weapons or explosives, as well as objects whose possession is illegal, may not be restored. These objects shall be confiscated by the State or destroyed.

In case of refusal of restitution, the applicant may appeal to the Prosecutor General.

The Royal Prosecutor and the Prosecutor General are no longer competent to order restitution when a prosecution has been initiated before an investigating or trial jurisdiction. The Royal Prosecutor and the Prosecutor General regain their competence when the judicial investigation has ended with a final order of non-suit.

#### Article 120.- Restitution of Confiscated Items to the Owner

When the court that received the case has issued a final decision and has not ruled on the restitution of the objects to the owner, the Royal Prosecutor or the Prosecutor General, depending on the case, is competent to decide on the application for restitution on their own initiative, provided that the ownership of the objects is not seriously contested.

### BOOK 4

#### JUDICIAL INVESTIGATION (INSTRUCTION)

### TITLE 1

#### THE INVESTIGATING JUDGE

### CHAPTER 1

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 121.- Secrecy of Judicial Investigation

Judicial investigations must be secret.

Any person contributing to the judicial investigation, especially the investigating judge, lawyer, clerk, police, Royal Gendarmerie personnel, civil servants, experts, interpreters, doctors, and competent persons mentioned in Article 95 (Technical or Scientific Examination) of this Code, must respect professional secrecy.

However, professional secrecy may not hinder the exercise of the rights of the defense.

Furthermore, the Royal Prosecutor may make public statements when they consider that information disseminated regarding a case is inaccurate.

Violation of the secrecy of the investigation is an offense punishable under the Penal Code in force.

#### Article 122.- Opening of Judicial Investigation

A judicial investigation is mandatory for felonies. For misdemeanors, a judicial investigation is optional.

#### Article 123.- Territorial Jurisdiction

The competent investigating judges are:

- The investigating judge of the place where the offense was committed;

- The investigating judge of the residence or place of stay of the person suspected of...

committed the offense.

- The investigating judge of the place where the suspected person was arrested.

In case of conflict between several investigating judges belonging to different Courts of First Instance and being seized of the same facts, the conflict shall be resolved by the President of the Investigation Chamber.

Jurisdiction disputes between several Investigation Chambers shall be resolved by the President of the Supreme Court.

These decisions are not subject to appeal.

#### Article 124.- Introductory Submission

A judicial investigation is opened by an introductory submission from the Royal Prosecutor in accordance with Article 44 (Opening of Judicial Investigation) of this Code.

The judicial investigation may be opened against a named person or persons or against an unknown person, in accordance with the second paragraph of Article 44 (Opening of Judicial Investigation) of this Code.

The investigating judge may not perform any investigative act without an introductory submission from the Royal Prosecutor.

When an investigating judge receives a complaint with a civil party application, the investigating judge shall proceed in accordance with Article 139 (Transmission of the Complaint to the Royal Prosecutor) and Article 140 (Deposit of Security) of this Code.

When the investigating judge receives a simple complaint, they shall transmit it to the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 125.- Scope of the Case

The investigating judge is seized of the facts mentioned in the Royal Prosecutor's introductory submission. The investigating judge shall investigate only those facts.

If new facts that may constitute a criminal offense appear during the investigation, the investigating judge shall inform the Royal Prosecutor. The Royal Prosecutor may request the investigating judge to investigate these new facts by means of a supplementary submission.

without a supplementary submission from the Royal Prosecutor, the investigating judge has no power to investigate those new facts.

However, the investigating judge does not need a supplementary submission if the new facts constitute an aggravating circumstance of the facts mentioned in the initial introductory submission.

#### Article 126.- Placing Under Examination

The investigating judge must place under examination any person named in the introductory submission.

The investigating judge has the power to place under examination any person against whom there exists serious and consistent evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of the offense, even if they are not named in the introductory submission.

The investigating judge may place the person under examination as a perpetrator, an instigator, or an accomplice.

#### Article 127.- Investigation for Charge and for Discharge

The investigating judge shall perform all investigative acts that they consider useful for the manifestation of the truth in accordance with the law.

The investigating judge has the duty to investigate for both charge and discharge.

#### Article 128.- Assistance of the Clerk

The investigating judge must always be assisted by a clerk. In no case may the clerk perform acts that fall within the competence of the investigating judge.

#### Article 129.- Functions of the Clerk

The case file shall be kept by the clerk. The clerk shall duplicate each document of the case file to the extent possible. These copies must be certified as true copies of the original by the clerk. The copies shall be kept in a duplicate case file.

Every page of the case file must be numbered by the clerk in chronological order.

The case file and the duplicate case file shall be deposited in the office of the clerk at the investigating judge's office or in a room of the court that provides sufficient security.

The lawyer or their secretary is authorized by the investigating judge to make copies at their own expense and under the supervision of the clerk.

#### Article 130.- Proceeding to the Scene by the Investigating Judge and Clerk

The investigating judge may proceed to the scene with the clerk within the territorial jurisdiction of the Court of First Instance to perform any investigative act they consider useful, especially material examinations, searches, and seizures.

The investigating judge must inform the Royal Prosecutor of this proceeding. In this case, the Royal Prosecutor may accompany the investigating judge.

If the needs of the investigation require it, the investigating judge may proceed to the scene with the clerk anywhere in the national territory. However, the judge must first inform the Royal Prosecutor of the Court of First Instance of the territory to which they are traveling.

The investigating judge shall prepare a report on these operations.

#### Article 131.- Letters Rogatory

The investigating judge may request another judge or a judicial police officer or a judicial police unit to perform certain acts by means of letters rogatory, under the conditions set forth in Article 173 (Letters Rogatory) to Article 184 (Report Prepared by Judicial Police Officer) of this Code.

#### Article 132.- Request for Acts by the Royal Prosecutor

At any time during the investigation, the Royal Prosecutor may request the investigating judge to perform any investigative act they consider useful.

If the investigating judge refuses to comply with the Royal Prosecutor's request, the judge must issue an order of refusal within 15 (fifteen) days. This order must be reasoned and notified to the Royal Prosecutor without delay.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within 15 (fifteen) days, the Royal Prosecutor may appeal directly by means of an application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 133.- Request for Acts by the Charged Person

At any time during the investigation, the charged person may request the investigating judge to interrogate them, to hear a civil party or a witness, to conduct a confrontation, or to proceed to the scene. The request must be in writing and reasoned.

If the investigating judge refuses to comply with the request, the judge must issue an order of refusal within 1 (one) month of receiving the request. This order must be reasoned and notified to the Royal Prosecutor and the charged person.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within the period of 1 (one) month, the charged person may appeal directly by means of an application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 134.- Request for Acts by the Civil Party

At any time during the investigation, the civil party may request the investigating judge to hear them, to hear a witness, to interrogate the charged person, to conduct a confrontation, or to proceed to the scene. The request must be in writing and reasoned.

If the investigating judge refuses to comply with the request, the judge must issue an order of refusal within 1 (one) month of receiving the request. This order must be reasoned and notified to the Royal Prosecutor and the civil party.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within the period of 1 (one) month, the civil party may appeal directly by means of an application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 135.- Transmission of the Case File to the Royal Prosecutor

At any time, the Royal Prosecutor may examine the case file or request its transmission for examination, provided that the file is returned within 24 (twenty-four) hours.

#### Article 136.- Presence of the Royal Prosecutor during Investigative Acts

The Royal Prosecutor may be present at all investigative acts, especially during the interrogation of charged persons, confrontations, and the hearing of parties.

#### Article 137.- Civil Party Application by way of Intervention

Once a judicial investigation has been opened, any person who claims to have suffered injury may apply to be a civil party before the investigating judge at any time.

The civil party application by way of intervention is not subject to any specific form.

When the civil party application is made in writing, the document shall be included in the case file. When the application is made orally, the investigating judge shall prepare a report.

The investigating judge shall notify the Royal Prosecutor and the charged person of the civil party application.

#### Article 138.- Complaint with Civil Party Application

Any person who claims to have been injured by a felony or misdemeanor may file a complaint with a civil party application before the competent investigating judge. The complaint may be filed by a lawyer on behalf of the victim.

#### Article 139.- Transmission of the Complaint to the Royal Prosecutor

The investigating judge shall issue an order recording the filing of the complaint with the civil party application. The investigating judge shall transmit this complaint to the Royal Prosecutor.

After examining the complaint with the civil party application, the Royal Prosecutor shall request the investigating judge to proceed by means of an introductory submission. This introductory submission may be directed against an unknown person, even if the complaint with the civil party application names one or more persons.

The Royal Prosecutor may request the investigating judge not to investigate if the public action is extinguished or if the facts do not constitute a criminal offense.

If the investigating judge decides not to investigate, they shall issue a reasoned order, which must be immediately notified to the civil party.

If the investigating judge decides to investigate despite the Royal Prosecutor's request, they shall issue a reasoned order, which must be immediately notified to the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 140.- Deposit of Security

Based on the resources of the civil party and the foreseeable difficulties of the case, the investigating judge shall issue an order determining the amount of security to be deposited by the civil party and the time limit for this deposit.

If the civil party is unable to deposit the security, the investigating judge may exempt them from it. This order shall be immediately notified to the civil party.

In case of deposit, the civil party shall receive a receipt signed by the President of the Court, the Royal Prosecutor, and the clerk.

If the security is not deposited within the time limit set by the investigating judge, the complaint with the civil party application shall be declared inadmissible. The investigating judge shall decide by an order, which must be immediately notified to the civil party.

The security shall be returned to the party concerned at the end of the proceedings, unless the provisions of Article 141 (Abusive or Dilatory Complaint) of this Code are applied.

The security shall be deposited in a special bank account or kept in a place that provides solid security. This place shall be chosen by agreement between the President of the Court and the Royal Prosecutor.

#### Article 141.- Abusive or Dilatory Complaint

If the judicial investigation opened following a complaint with a civil party application is closed by an order of non-suit, and if the investigating judge considers that the proceedings were abusive or dilatory, the judge...

may decide by an order to sentence the civil party to a civil fine. The amount of the fine may not exceed the amount of the security. The order to pay the fine shall be immediately notified to the person concerned.

#### Article 142.- Action for Damages

When a judicial investigation opened following a complaint with a civil party application is closed by a final order of non-suit, the person named in the complaint may sue for damages against the complainant if the proceedings were abusive or dilatory.

The claim for damages shall be filed before the Court of First Instance where the investigation was conducted. The court may request the transmission of the case file.

### CHAPTER 2

#### INVESTIGATIVE ACTS BY THE INVESTIGATING JUDGE

### SECTION 1

#### NOTIFICATION OF PLACING UNDER EXAMINATION

#### Article 143.- Notification of Placing Under Examination

At the first appearance, the investigating judge shall verify the identity of the charged person, inform them of the facts alleged against them and the legal qualification of the offense. The investigating judge shall inform the charged person of their right to make statements or to remain silent. Mention of this notification shall be included in the report of the first appearance.

If the charged person wishes to make statements, the investigating judge shall receive them immediately.

The investigating judge shall inform the charged person of their right to choose a lawyer or to request the assignment of a lawyer in accordance with the Law on the Statute of Lawyers.

A minor charged person must always be assisted by a lawyer. If the charged person does not choose one, a lawyer shall be assigned under the conditions set forth by the Law on the Statute of Lawyers.

After the first appearance, the charged person who remains at liberty must declare their address to the investigating judge. The charged person is informed that:

- They must inform the investigating judge of any change of address;

- Any notification made to the last address declared by the charged person shall be deemed to have been made to the person concerned.

The notification and the declaration regarding the change of address shall be recorded in the report of the first appearance.

#### Article 144.- Assistance of an Interpreter

If necessary, the investigating judge shall call upon an interpreter. The interpreter shall take an oath according to their religion or belief to assist the court and to translate accurately all statements. In no case may the interpreter be chosen from among judges, clerks, police, Royal Gendarmerie personnel, parties, or witnesses.

### SECTION 2

#### INTERROGATION OF THE CHARGED PERSON

#### Article 145.- Presence of the Lawyer during Interrogation

When the charged person is assisted by a lawyer, the investigating judge must summon the lawyer at least 5 (five) days before the interrogation. During this period, the lawyer may examine the case file.

The charged person may only be interrogated in the presence of their lawyer. However, if the lawyer, having been duly summoned, does not appear on the day and at the time set, the investigating judge may interrogate the charged person without the lawyer's presence. Mention of this absence shall be included in the interrogation report.

In exceptional cases, the investigating judge may interrogate the charged person without summoning the lawyer if the charged person explicitly waives the presence of their lawyer.

This waiver must be recorded in a separate report from the interrogation report and must be signed by the charged person.

In case of urgency, the investigating judge may also interrogate the charged person without summoning the lawyer. The urgency must result from a risk of death or the fear of losing evidence. The nature of the urgency must be mentioned in the report.

The investigating judge may call upon an interpreter as set forth in Article 144 (Assistance of an Interpreter) of this Code.

#### Article 146.- Questions with the Permission of the Investigating Judge

During the interrogation of the charged person, the Royal Prosecutor and the lawyer may also ask questions with the permission of the investigating judge.

In case of refusal, the refusal shall be mentioned in the report.

#### Article 147.- Confrontations

The provisions of Article 145 (Presence of the Lawyer during Interrogation) and Article 146 (Questions with the Permission of the Investigating Judge) of this Code shall apply to confrontations between charged persons.

#### Article 148.- Minimum Period for Interrogation

When a period of 4 (four) months has elapsed since the interrogation at the first appearance and the charged person has not been interrogated or confronted, the investigating judge must receive their statements if the charged person so requests. If the investigating judge does not summon the charged person within 1 (one) month of the request, the charged person may appeal directly to the Investigation Chamber, which shall hear the person. A report shall then be transmitted to the investigating judge.

#### Article 149.- Rights of the Defense of the Charged Person

The lawyer of a detained charged person may communicate freely with their client in prison or in the detention center. Conversations between the lawyer and the charged person may not be overhead or recorded. The lawyer may read the documents of the case file to their client. Conversely, the lawyer may not provide photocopies of documents from the case file to their client.

### SECTION 3

#### HEARING THE CIVIL PARTY

#### Article 150.- Hearing the Civil Party

The civil party may be assisted by a lawyer.

In this case, the investigating judge must summon the civil party's lawyer at least 5 (five) days before the hearing. During this period, the lawyer may examine the case file.

The civil party may only be heard in the presence of their lawyer. However, if the lawyer, having been duly summoned, does not appear on the day and at the time set, the investigating judge may hear the civil party without the lawyer's presence. Mention of this absence shall be included in the report.

In exceptional cases, the investigating judge may hear the civil party without summoning the lawyer if the civil party explicitly waives the presence of their lawyer. This waiver must be recorded in a separate report from the hearing report and must be signed by the civil party.

In case of urgency, the investigating judge may also hear the civil party without summoning the lawyer. The urgency must result from a risk of death or the fear of losing evidence. The nature of the urgency must be mentioned in the report.

The investigating judge may call upon an interpreter as set forth in Article 144 (Assistance of an Interpreter) of this Code.

#### Article 151.- Questions with the Permission of the Investigating Judge

During the hearing, the Royal Prosecutor and the lawyer may also ask questions with the permission of the investigating judge. In case of refusal, the refusal shall be mentioned in the report.

#### Article 152.- Confrontations

The provisions of Article 150 (Hearing the Civil Party) and Article 151 (Questions with the Permission of the Investigating Judge) of this Code shall apply to confrontations involving the civil party.

### SECTION 4

#### HEARING WITNESSES

#### Article 153.- Hearing Witnesses

The investigating judge may hear any person whose statements may be useful for the manifestation of the truth.

The investigating judge shall hear witnesses separately, without the presence of the charged person or the civil party. The investigating judge may also conduct confrontations between charged persons, civil parties, and witnesses.

Any person summoned by the investigating judge to appear as a witness must appear.

In case of refusal, the investigating judge may request public force to compel the witness to appear. The investigating judge shall issue a summons to appear. This summons must identify the witness, be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

#### Article 154.- Oath of Witnesses

Before answering questions, each witness shall take an oath according to their religion or belief to tell the truth. The form of the oath shall be as set forth in the Annex to this Code.

#### Article 155.- Assistance of an Interpreter

The investigating judge may call upon an interpreter for hearing witnesses as set forth in Article 144 (Assistance of an Interpreter) of this Code.

#### Article 156.- Witnesses Exempted from the Oath

The following witnesses shall be heard without taking an oath:

1- The father, mother, and any ascendant of the charged person;

2- The son, daughter, and any other descendant of the charged person;

3- The brother or sister of the charged person;

4- The brother-in-law or sister-in-law of the charged person;

5- The husband or wife of the charged person, even if divorced;

6- Children under the age of 14.

#### Article 157.- Inadmissibility of Certain Persons as Witnesses

To respect the rights of the defense, the investigating judge may not hear as a witness any person against whom there exists evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of the offense. In this case, the judge shall apply the procedure of Article 143 (Notification of Placing Under Examination) of this Code.

#### Article 158.- Proceeding to the Residence of a Witness

If a witness is ill or unable to travel, the investigating judge and the clerk may proceed to the residence or place where the witness is located to receive their statements.

### SECTION 5

#### SEARCHES AND SEIZURES

#### Article 159.- Rules Applicable to Searches

The investigating judge may conduct searches.

The search must be conducted in the presence of the occupant of the premises or, if absent, in the presence of 2 (two) witnesses designated by the judge. Witnesses may not be police or Royal Gendarmerie personnel who are part of the operating force for the search.

The search may not begin before 06:00 (six) A.M. or after 18:00 (eighteen) P.M., except:

- Searches in places open to the public;

- Searches anywhere drugs are manufactured, stored, traded, distributed, or used.

Searches in a lawyer's office must be conducted in the presence of the Bâtonnier (President of the Bar Association) or their delegate.

The investigating judge shall prepare a search report signed by themselves, the clerk, and the occupant or the 2 (two) witnesses.

The report must mention the identity of the occupant or each witness. When the search is conducted in a lawyer's office, the report must mention the presence of the Bâtonnier or their delegate.

#### Article 160.- Sealing of Evidence

The investigating judge may seize evidence. The investigating judge must seal the seized items.

After exhibiting the seized items to the occupant or the 2 (two) witnesses, the investigating judge shall prepare a seizure report including an inventory of the seized items. The seizure report must be signed by the investigating judge, the clerk, and the occupant or the 2 (two) witnesses. The report must mention the identity of the occupant or each witness.

#### Article 161.- Restitution of Seized Items by the Investigating Judge

As long as the closing order has not been issued, the investigating judge is competent to order the restitution of seized objects to their owner, provided that ownership is not seriously contested. The investigating judge shall decide by a reasoned order, after obtaining the opinion of the Royal Prosecutor. The order shall be immediately notified to the applicant and their lawyer, if any.

Restitution shall not be ordered if it may hinder the manifestation of the truth.

Objects dangerous to persons or property, such as weapons or explosives, and objects whose possession is illegal, may not be returned to the person concerned. These objects shall be confiscated for the state.

### SECTION 6

#### EXPERTISE

#### Article 162.- Necessity of Expertise

When a technical issue arises, the investigating judge may order an expertise on their own motion or at the request of the Royal Prosecutor, the charged person, or the civil party.

If the investigating judge refuses to comply with a request for expertise, they must issue a reasoned order. The order must be issued within 5 (five) days if the request is made by the Royal Prosecutor, and within 1 (one) month if the request is made by the charged person or the civil party. This order shall be immediately notified to the applicant.

#### Article 163.- National List of Experts

A national list of experts is established. This list shall be prepared by the Minister of Justice. The procedures for inclusion in or removal from the list shall be determined by a Prakas.

Experts included in the national list shall take an oath before the Court of Appeal of Phnom Penh, according to their religion or belief, to assist the court honestly in the search for justice. Experts included in the list are not required to take a new oath each time they are appointed to perform an expertise.

#### Article 164.- Appointment of Experts not included in the National List

The investigating judge shall normally choose an expert included in the national list of experts.

However, the investigating judge may, by a specially reasoned order, appoint an expert who is not included in the national list. In this case, the expert shall take an oath, according to their religion or belief, to assist the court honestly in the search for justice.

#### Article 165.- Order of Appointment of Experts

Experts are appointed by an order of the investigating judge. The order shall specify the mission of the expert and the time limit for its completion.

The mission may only concern technical issues.

If appropriate, the investigating judge shall hand over some or all of the seized items to the expert. The investigating judge shall prepare a report on the delivery of the seized items. The expert may break the seals to conduct the expertise. If the expertise is likely to damage or destroy the seized items, the expert must inform the investigating judge and obtain their permission before proceeding.

#### Article 166.- Oversight of the Expert's Mission by the Investigating Judge

The expert shall perform their mission under the oversight of the investigating judge. The expert must inform the investigating judge of the progress of their mission and especially of any difficulties they may encounter.

If the expert does not respect the time limit set by the investigating judge, the judge may appoint another expert in their place. However, if circumstances so require, the investigating judge may grant an extension by means of an order.

#### Article 167.- Acts Necessary for the Performance of the Expert's Mission

To perform their mission, the expert may hear any person other than the charged person. Statements received by the expert have the value of simple information. Persons heard by the expert are not required to take an oath.

If the expert considers it necessary to hear the charged person, they must obtain permission from the investigating judge. The charged person may request that the expert hear them in the presence of their lawyer.

However, when the investigating judge appoints a doctor or a psychologist to examine the charged person, the medical or psychological examination shall be conducted without the presence of the lawyer.

#### Article 168.- Expert Report

When the expertise operations are completed, the expert shall prepare a report in which they clearly describe their operations and make conclusions. The expert must certify that they personally performed the operations described in the report. The report must be dated and signed.

When the expert has broken the seals to examine the seized items, they must mention this in their report.

When the expert submits their report, they must return the seized items to the investigating judge. If the seals were broken, the investigating judge must reseal the items. A report on the return of the items and the resealing must be prepared.

If the expertise has caused damage or destruction to the seized items, the expert must mention in their report the permission obtained from the investigating judge, as set forth in the 3rd paragraph of Article 165 (Order of Appointment of Experts) of this Code.

Whenever possible, the expert shall provide copies of the report to the investigating judge.

#### Article 169.- Appointment of Multiple Experts

If circumstances so require, the investigating judge may appoint several experts.

In this case, if the experts have different opinions, each must write down their own opinion or their disagreement.

#### Article 170.- Notification of the Conclusions of the Expertise

When the expert report is completed, the investigating judge shall inform the Royal Prosecutor.

The expert report shall be included in the case file.

The investigating judge shall summon the charged person and their lawyer to inform them of the conclusions of the expertise.

The investigating judge may also summon the civil party and their lawyer to inform them of the conclusions of the expertise.

The investigating judge shall set a time limit for the Royal Prosecutor, the charged person, or the civil party to request a supplementary expertise or a counter-expertise. This time limit may not be less than 10 (ten) days. During this period, the case file, including the expert report, may be examined by the lawyers.

Any request for supplementary expertise or counter-expertise must be in writing and reasoned.

If the investigating judge refuses to comply with a request for supplementary expertise or counter-expertise, they must issue a reasoned order. The order must be issued within 5 (five) days if the request is made by the Royal Prosecutor, and within 1 (one) month if the request is made by the charged person or the civil party. The order shall be immediately notified to the applicant.

Any request for supplementary expertise or counter-expertise made after the time limit set by the investigating judge shall be included in the case file.

If the investigating judge has not ruled on a request for expertise, supplementary expertise, or counter-expertise within the time limits set forth in the 2nd paragraph of Article 162 (Necessity of Expertise) of this Code or in this Article, the applicant may appeal directly by means of an application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 171.- Costs of Expertise

The costs of the expertise shall be borne by the applicant.

When the expertise, supplementary expertise, or counter-expertise is requested by the investigating judge, the Royal Prosecutor, or a judicial police officer, the costs shall be borne by the State.

### SECTION 7

#### INTERCEPTION OF TELECOMMUNICATIONS

#### Article 172.- Interception of Telecommunications Ordered by the Investigating Judge

If strictly necessary for the manifestation of the truth, the investigating judge may order the interception and recording of telephone conversations. The investigating judge may also order the interception and recording of any correspondence by telecommunication means, such as faxes or emails.

The investigating judge may request any public entity or specialized official to install technical equipment and perform the recording. The investigating judge shall specify in their order the type of communication to be intercepted and the duration of the mission.

The appointed public entity or official must comply with the investigating judge's request. During the performance of the mission, they must inform the investigating judge of its progress and especially of any difficulties encountered. When the mission is completed, the public entity or official shall transcribe the recorded sounds. The transcription must accurately reflect the content of the recorded sounds. The recordings shall be delivered to the investigating judge and sealed.

At any time, the investigating judge may proceed to the place where the interception and recording are conducted. Access to these places may not be denied to the investigating judge on any grounds, even if they are military locations.

The investigating judge may also perform these acts by means of a letter rogatory.

### SECTION 8

#### LETTERS ROGATORY

#### Article 173.- Letters Rogatory

By means of a letter rogatory, the investigating judge may delegate to a judge of their court or of another court, or to a judicial police officer or a judicial police unit, the task of performing investigative acts.

#### Article 174.- Content of Letters Rogatory

Letters rogatory shall specify the nature of the acts to be performed. These acts must be directly related to the alleged offense. Letters rogatory may not be general; they must be clearly directed.

The investigating judge shall set the time limit for the execution of the letter rogatory.

Letters rogatory must be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

The investigating judge may withdraw the letter rogatory at any time.

#### Article 175.- Letters Rogatory Issued to Another Judge

When letters rogatory are issued to a judge, that judge shall exercise all the powers of the investigating judge.

#### Article 176.- Letters Rogatory Issued to Judicial Police Officers

When letters rogatory are assigned to a judicial police officer, that officer shall exercise the powers set forth in Articles 178 (Report of Searches and Examinations) to 184 (Report Prepared by Judicial Police Officers) of this Code.

For the execution of the letter rogatory, the judicial police officer is under the authority of the investigating judge and shall report only to that judge.

#### Article 177.- Execution of Letters Rogatory by Judicial Police Officers

A judicial police officer shall perform the acts specified in the letter rogatory within their territorial jurisdiction, subject to the provisions of the 2nd and 3rd paragraphs of Article 68 (Extension of Territorial Jurisdiction) of this Code.

#### Article 178.- Report of Searches and Examinations

The judicial police officer shall prepare a report on their searches and examinations.

#### Article 179.- Rules Governing the Hearing of Witnesses by Judicial Police Officers

Judicial police officers may summon and hear witnesses.

Before answering questions, each witness shall take an oath according to their religion or belief to tell the truth. However, witnesses listed in Article 156 (Witnesses Exempted from the Oath) are exempted from the oath.

To respect the rights of the defense, judicial police officers may not hear as a witness any person against whom there exists evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of the offense. If the hearing of a witness reveals such evidence, the judicial police officer must stop the hearing and report to the investigating judge.

Judicial police officers may call upon an interpreter who has taken an oath according to their religion or belief to assist the court and to translate accurately.

Any person summoned as a witness must appear. In case of refusal, the judicial police officer shall report to the investigating judge, who may issue a summons to appear. This summons must identify the witness, be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge. The summons to appear allows the judicial police officer to use public force to compel the witness to appear before them.

The 2nd to 5th paragraphs of Article 93 (Report on Hearings) of this Code shall apply to the report on the hearing of a witness.

#### Article 180.- Acts Prohibited to Judicial Police Officers

Judicial police officers may not interrogate the charged person or hear the civil party.

#### Article 181.- Searches and Seizures by Judicial Police Officers

Judicial police officers may conduct searches and seizures under the conditions set forth in Article 91 (Searches) and Article 92 (Sealing of Evidence) of this Code. However, the permission required by the 2nd paragraph of the 1st section of Article 91 (Searches) of this Code must be granted by the investigating judge.

#### Article 182.- Police Custody within the Framework of Letters Rogatory

The provisions of Articles 96 (Police Custody) to 102 (Final Report on Police Custody) regarding police custody shall apply within the framework of the execution of letters rogatory. However, the powers granted by these articles to the Royal Prosecutor shall be exercised by the investigating judge. For the application of Article 103 (Presentation of the Held Person) and Article 104 (Time Limit for Presentation) of this Code, at the end of the police custody, the held person must be presented before the investigating judge.

#### Article 183.- Interception of Telecommunications within the Framework of Letters Rogatory

When letters rogatory provide for the interception and recording of telephone conversations or the interception and recording of any correspondence by telecommunication means, such as faxes or emails, the judicial police officer shall exercise the powers granted to the investigating judge by the 2nd paragraph of the 1st section and the 2nd section of Article 172 (Interception of Telecommunications Ordered by the Investigating Judge) of this Code.

#### Article 184.- Reports Prepared by Judicial Police Officers

Reports prepared by judicial police officers within the framework of the execution of letters rogatory shall be subject to the provisions of Article 108 (Mentions to be Included in the Report) of this Code.

### CHAPTER 3

### COERCIVE MEASURES

#### SECTION 1

#### ORDERS

#### Article 185.- Issuance of Orders

The investigating judge may issue summons to appear, orders to bring, arrest warrants, and detention orders.

#### SECTION 2

#### SUMMONS TO APPEAR

#### Article 186.- Summons to Appear

The investigating judge may issue a summons to appear.

A summons to appear is an order to a person to appear before the investigating judge.

A summons to appear may be issued to the charged person or to any person against whom there exists evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of an offense.

#### Article 187.- Mentions to be Included in a Summons to Appear

A summons to appear shall include the following mentions:

- Identity of the person concerned;

- The offense alleged and the legal provisions defining and punishing it;

- The date, month, year, time, and place where the person must appear before the investigating judge;

- The name and title of the judge who issued the summons.

The summons to appear must be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

#### Article 188.- Notification of a Summons to Appear

A summons to appear shall be notified to the person concerned by a judicial police officer, a judicial police agent, or a bailiff.

The person concerned shall receive a copy of the summons and shall sign the original, which shall be returned to the investigating judge.

The judicial police officer, judicial police agent, or bailiff shall inform the investigating judge of any difficulties encountered in the performance of their mission.

#### SECTION 3

#### ORDERS TO BRING

#### Article 189.- Issuance of an Order to Bring by the Investigating Judge

The investigating judge may issue an order to bring.

#### Article 190.- Order to Bring

An order to bring is an order to the public force to arrest and bring a person before the investigating judge.

An order to bring may be issued against the charged person or any person against whom there exists evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of an offense.

#### Article 191.- Mentions to be Included in an Order to Bring

An order to bring shall include the following mentions:

- Identity of the person concerned;

- The offense alleged and the legal provisions defining and punishing it;

- The name and title of the judge who issued the order.

The order to bring must be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

#### Article 192.- Execution of an Order to Bring

The execution of an order to bring shall be assigned to a judicial police officer.

In case of urgency, the order to bring shall be disseminated to police or Royal Gendarmerie units by all means. The original order shall be immediately delivered to the judicial police officer in charge of its execution. The judicial police officer may not enter the residence of the person concerned before 06:00 (six) A.M. or after 18:00 (eighteen) P.M. The judicial police officer shall inform the investigating judge of any difficulties encountered in the performance of their mission.

#### Article 193.- Presentation after an Order to Bring

If, due to circumstances, the person concerned cannot be brought immediately before the investigating judge after their arrest, they shall be taken to a police or Royal Gendarmerie unit, a detention center, or a prison. This person must be presented before the investigating judge or their substitute no later than the following day. If the presentation does not take place by the following day, the person concerned shall be released.

#### Article 194.- Inclusion of Detention Time in Provisional Detention

When a person arrested under an order to bring is detained, the duration of this detention shall be included in the duration of provisional detention, if any.

#### SECTION 4

#### ARREST WARRANTS

#### Article 195.- Issuance of an Arrest Warrant by the Investigating Judge

The investigating judge may issue an arrest warrant.

#### Article 196.- Arrest Warrant

An arrest warrant may be issued against the charged person or any person against whom there exists evidence making it likely that they participated in the commission of an offense.

The investigating judge may only issue an arrest warrant if:

1- The offense alleged is a felony or a misdemeanor punishable by imprisonment;

2- The person concerned is in hiding or is outside the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

An arrest warrant is an order:

- To the public force to search for, arrest and bring that person to

prison or a detention center;

- To the head of the prison or detention center to receive and detain that person.

#### Article 197.- Arrest Warrant and Opinion of the Royal Prosecutor

Before issuing an arrest warrant, the investigating judge must obtain the opinion of the Royal Prosecutor. The investigating judge shall issue a reasoned order after receiving the opinion of the Royal Prosecutor.

The Royal Prosecutor shall ensure the dissemination of the arrest warrant.

#### Article 198.- Mentions to be Included in an Arrest Warrant

An arrest warrant shall include the following mentions:

- Identity of the person concerned;

- The offense alleged and the legal provisions defining and punishing it;

- The name and title of the judge who issued the warrant.

The arrest warrant must be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

#### Article 199.- Execution of an Arrest Warrant

The execution of an arrest warrant shall be assigned to a judicial police officer.

In case of urgency, the arrest warrant shall be disseminated to police or Royal Gendarmerie units by all means. The original warrant shall be delivered as soon as possible to the judicial police officer in charge of its execution.

The judicial police officer may not enter the residence of the person concerned before 06:00 (six) A.M. or after 18:00 (eighteen) P.M. The judicial police officer shall inform the investigating judge of any difficulties encountered in the performance of their mission.

#### Article 200.- International Dissemination of an Arrest Warrant

If necessary, an arrest warrant may be disseminated internationally through the Ministry of Justice using any effective means.

#### Article 201.- Presentation after an Arrest Warrant

The head of the prison or detention center must receive and detain the person who is the subject of an arrest warrant. The head of the prison or detention center shall keep the original or a certified copy of the warrant.

The person concerned must be brought before the investigating judge or their substitute as soon as possible, provided the investigation is still ongoing.

If the presentation requires the transfer of the person to another prison or detention center, the transfer shall be organized by the prosecution.

If the person concerned is not brought before the investigating judge or their substitute by the day following the arrest, they must be heard by the Royal Prosecutor of the place of detention. The Royal Prosecutor shall prepare a report on the hearing, which shall be sent to the investigating judge.

#### Article 202.- Inclusion of Detention Time in Provisional Detention

When a person arrested under an arrest warrant is detained, the duration of this detention shall be included in the duration of provisional detention, if any.

#### SECTION 5

#### PROVISIONAL DETENTION

#### Article 203.- Principle of Provisional Detention

As a principle, the charged person shall remain free. In exceptional cases, the charged person may be placed in provisional detention under the conditions set forth in this section.

#### Article 204.- Cases for Provisional Detention

Provisional detention may only be ordered in cases of a felony or a misdemeanor for which the law provides for a term of imprisonment of 1 (one) year or more.

#### Article 205.- Grounds for Provisional Detention

Provisional detention may be ordered when it is necessary:

1- To stop the offense or to prevent its recurrence;

2- To prevent pressure on witnesses or victims, or to prevent collusion between the charged person and their accomplices;

3- To preserve evidence or material clues;

4- To ensure the presence of the charged person during the proceedings;

5- To protect the safety of the charged person;

6- To preserve public order from any trouble caused by the offense.

#### Article 206.- Observations of the Charged Person and Statement of Grounds for the Order

An investigating judge who intends to order provisional detention, either on their own motion or following a final submission by the Royal Prosecutor, shall inform the charged person and receive their observations. If the charged person is assisted by a lawyer, the lawyer shall present the defense's arguments.

The investigating judge shall order provisional detention by a reasoned order. In the order, the judge shall refer to the provisions of Article 205 (Grounds for Provisional Detention) of this Code. The provisional detention order shall be immediately notified to the Royal Prosecutor and the charged person.

The investigating judge shall issue a detention order as set forth in Article 220 (Definition of Detention Order), Article 221 (Mentions to be Included in a Detention Order), and Article 222 (Execution of a Detention Order) of this Code.

#### Article 207.- Order Refusing Provisional Detention

When the investigating judge, seized by a final submission from the Royal Prosecutor requesting provisional detention, does not comply with the request, the judge

shall issue, within 5 (five) days, an order refusing provisional detention, which does not need to be reasoned. The order shall be immediately notified to the applicant.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within 5 (five) days, the Royal Prosecutor may appeal by means of an application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 208.- Duration of Provisional Detention for Felonies

For adults charged with a felony, provisional detention may not exceed 6 (six) months. However, at the expiration of this period, the investigating judge may extend the provisional detention once for a further 6 (six) months by a properly and clearly reasoned order.

The investigating judge may order the extension of provisional detention no more than 2 (two) times.

#### Article 209.- Duration of Provisional Detention for Misdemeanors

For adults charged with a misdemeanor, provisional detention may not exceed 4 (four) months. However, at the expiration of this period, the investigating judge may extend the provisional detention once for no more than 2 (two) months by a properly and clearly reasoned order.

The total duration of provisional detention may not exceed half of the minimum sentence provided by law.

#### Article 210.- Duration of Provisional Detention for Crimes Against Humanity, etc.

In case of charges for crimes against humanity, genocide, or war crimes, provisional detention may not exceed 1 (one) year for each offense. However, at the expiration of this period, the investigating judge may extend the provisional detention once for 1 (one) year by a properly and clearly reasoned order.

The investigating judge may order the extension of provisional detention no more than 2 (two) times.

#### Article 211.- Extension of Provisional Detention

When the investigating judge decides to extend provisional detention, they shall inform the charged person and receive their observations. If the charged person is assisted by a lawyer, the lawyer shall present the defense's arguments for their client.

The investigating judge shall extend provisional detention by a reasoned order. In the order, the judge shall refer to the provisions of Article 205 (Grounds for Provisional Detention) of this Code. This order shall be immediately notified to the charged person.

#### Article 212.- Decision Regarding Minors Under 14 (Fourteen) Years of Age

A minor under 14 (fourteen) years of age may not be placed in provisional detention. The investigating judge may decide to entrust the minor to their parents, a guardian, or, in the absence of a guardian, to a provisional educational care center pending the court's decision.

#### Article 213.- Duration of Provisional Detention for Felonies Involving Minors from 14 (Fourteen) to Under 18 (Eighteen) Years of Age

For minors from 14 (fourteen) to under 18 (eighteen) years of age charged with a felony, the duration of provisional detention is as follows:

1- Provisional detention may not exceed 4 (four) months when the minor is under 16 (sixteen) years of age.

2- Provisional detention may not exceed 6 (six) months when the minor is from 16 (sixteen) to under 18 (eighteen) years of age.

#### Article 214.- Duration of Provisional Detention for Misdemeanors Involving Minors from 14 (Fourteen) to Under 18 (Eighteen) Years of Age

For minors from 14 (fourteen) to under 18 (eighteen) years of age charged with a misdemeanor, the duration of provisional detention is as follows:

1- Provisional detention may not exceed 2 (two) months when the minor is under 16 (sixteen) years of age.

2- Provisional detention may not exceed 4 (four) months when the minor is from 16 (sixteen) to under 18 (eighteen) years of age.

The total duration of provisional detention under points 1 and 2 above may not exceed half of the minimum sentence provided by law for such minor.

#### Article 215.- Release of the Charged Person by the Investigating Judge

The investigating judge may order the release of the charged person at any time.

When the investigating judge intends to release the charged person, they shall inform the Royal Prosecutor to obtain their opinion and transmit the case file to the Royal Prosecutor for examination. The Royal Prosecutor shall provide their opinion as soon as possible. The investigating judge shall rule within a maximum period of 5 (five) days after transmission of the file to the Royal Prosecutor.

In case of urgency, the investigating judge may order the immediate release of the charged person without waiting for the Royal Prosecutor's opinion. The investigating judge shall state the reasons for the urgency in their order.

#### Article 216.- Release at the Request of the Royal Prosecutor

The Royal Prosecutor may request the release of the charged person at any time. The investigating judge shall rule within 5 (five) days.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within 5 (five) days, the Royal Prosecutor may appeal by application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

An order refusing release must be reasoned.

#### Article 217.- Release at the Request of the Charged Person

The charged person may apply for release at any time. The investigating judge shall immediately transmit the application to the Royal Prosecutor for their opinion and transmit the case file for examination. The Royal Prosecutor shall provide their opinion as soon as possible. The investigating judge shall rule within a maximum period of 5 (five) days after transmission of the file to the Royal Prosecutor.

The charged person may file a new application for release with the investigating judge or the Investigation Chamber 1 (one) month after the decision rejecting the previous application. The investigating judge or the Investigation Chamber shall rule on this application within 5 (five) days after receiving the application.

An order refusing release must be reasoned.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within 5 (five) days, the charged person may appeal directly to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 218.- Notification of a Release Order

A release order must be immediately notified to the Royal Prosecutor and to the head of the prison or detention center. The provisions of Article 276 (Release of the Detained Person) of this Code shall apply.

An order refusing release must be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor and the charged person.

When the investigating judge orders the release of the charged person, the judge may place them under judicial supervision under the conditions set forth in Articles 223 (Obligations of Judicial Supervision) to 230 (Release of the Charged Person from Obligations of Judicial Supervision) of this Code.

#### SECTION 6

#### DETENTION ORDERS

#### Article 219.- Issuance of a Detention Order by the Investigating Judge

The investigating judge may issue a detention order.

#### Article 220.- Definition of a Detention Order

A detention order is an order to the head of a prison or detention center to receive and detain the charged person.

The investigating judge may only issue a detention order if the charged person is the subject of a provisional detention order.

#### Article 221.- Mentions to be Included in a Detention Order

A detention order shall include the following mentions:

- Identity of the charged person;

- Nature of the offense alleged and the legal provisions defining and punishing it;

- Name and title of the judge who issued the order.

The detention order must be dated, signed and sealed by the investigating judge.

#### Article 222.- Execution of a Detention Order

The head of the prison or detention center must receive and detain the person for whom a detention order is issued until notified of their release. The head of the prison or detention center shall keep the original or a certified copy of the detention order.

#### SECTION 7

#### JUDICIAL SUPERVISION

#### Article 223.- Obligations of Judicial Supervision

The investigating judge may place the charged person under judicial supervision at any time, provided the offense alleged is punishable by imprisonment.

Judicial supervision consists of an order to the charged person who remains free to comply with one or several of the following obligations:

1- To not leave the territorial limits determined by the investigating judge;

2- To not change residence without the permission of the investigating judge;

3- To not go to certain places determined by the investigating judge;

4- To present themselves periodically before a police or Royal Gendarmerie unit designated by the investigating judge;

5- To answer summons from any person designated by the investigating judge;

6- To hand over all identity documents to the clerk's office;

7- To not drive a vehicle;

8- To not receive or meet with certain persons designated by the investigating judge;

9- To deposit a bail amount, the amount and payment period of which shall be determined by the investigating judge based on the resources of the charged person;

10- To not possess or carry weapons. Any weapons in the possession of the charged person shall be handed over to the clerk's office;

11- To undergo medical examination and, if necessary, to be placed under a treatment regimen in a hospital;

12- To not engage in certain professional activities.

For the application of point 12 above, the investigating judge may not prohibit the exercise of an electoral mandate or trade union activities.

#### Article 224.- Non-application of Judicial Supervision to Minors Under 14 (Fourteen) Years of Age

A minor under 14 (fourteen) years of age may not be placed under judicial supervision.

#### Article 225.- Issuance of Receipts

In cases provided for in points 6, 9 and 10 of Article 223 (Obligations of Judicial Supervision) above, the charged person shall receive a receipt for the delivery of identity documents, bail, or weapons. The receipt for identity documents must be signed by the clerk.

The receipt for bail or weapons must be signed by the president of the court, the Royal Prosecutor, and the clerk.

The bail amount shall be deposited in a special account at a bank or kept in a secure place. This place shall be chosen by agreement between the president of the court and the Royal Prosecutor.

Weapons shall be kept in the same secure place as mentioned above.

#### Article 226.- Order for Judicial Supervision

The investigating judge shall place the charged person under judicial supervision, either on their own motion or upon the final submission of the Royal Prosecutor. The investigating judge shall specify in their order the obligations imposed on the charged person.

The order placing the person under judicial supervision shall be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor and to the charged person.

When the investigating judge, seized of a final submission from the Royal Prosecutor requesting that the charged person be placed under judicial supervision, does not comply with the request, the judge must issue an order refusing the request within 5 (five) days.

This order shall be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within 5 (five) days, the Royal Prosecutor may appeal by application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 227.- Modification of Judicial Supervision on the Investigating Judge's Own Motion

The investigating judge may at any time modify the content of the obligations, cancel them, or add new obligations.

The investigating judge may also terminate the judicial supervision.

The investigating judge shall decide by order, which shall be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor and to the charged person.

#### Article 228.- Request for Modification of Judicial Supervision by the Royal Prosecutor

The Royal Prosecutor may request the termination or modification of judicial supervision at any time. The investigating judge shall rule within 5 (five) days. This order shall be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within this period, the Royal Prosecutor may appeal by application to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

An order refusing to comply with the request of the Royal Prosecutor must be reasoned.

#### Article 229.- Request for Modification of Judicial Supervision by the Charged Person

The charged person may apply for the termination or modification of judicial supervision at any time. The investigating judge shall immediately transmit the application to the Royal Prosecutor for their opinion and transmit the case file for examination. The investigating judge shall rule within a maximum period of 5 (five) days from the date of transmission of the case file to the Royal Prosecutor. This order shall be notified as soon as possible to the Royal Prosecutor and to the charged person.

When a new application for termination or modification of judicial supervision is filed before the investigating judge or the Investigation Chamber has ruled on a previous application, the 5 (five) day period shall start from the date of the decision on the previous application. An order refusing the application must be reasoned.

If the investigating judge has not ruled within these 5 (five) days, the charged person may appeal directly to the Investigation Chamber, which shall rule in place of the investigating judge.

#### Article 230.- Release of the Charged Person from Obligations of Judicial Supervision

If the charged person intentionally evades the obligations of judicial supervision, the investigating judge may order their provisional detention, regardless of the duration of the imprisonment sentence incurred, and even if the charged person has already been held in provisional detention for the maximum duration provided by Articles 208 (Duration of Provisional Detention for Felonies) to 210 (Duration of Provisional Detention for Crimes Against Humanity, etc.), and Article 213 (Duration of Provisional Detention for Felonies Involving Minors from 14 (Fourteen) to under 18 (Eighteen) Years of Age).

and Article 214 (Duration of Provisional Detention for Misdemeanors Involving Minors from 14 (Fourteen) to under 18 (Eighteen) Years of Age) of this Code.

When the investigating judge orders provisional detention for the grounds mentioned above, the judge shall inform the charged person and receive their observations. If the charged person is assisted by a lawyer, the lawyer shall present the defense's arguments for their client.

The investigating judge shall order provisional detention by a reasoned order. This order shall be immediately notified to the Royal Prosecutor and to the charged person.

Provisional detention ordered under this article may not exceed 4 (four) months for adults and 2 (two) months for minors.

### CHAPTER 4

### SUMMONS AND NOTIFICATIONS

#### SECTION 1

#### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 231.- Calculation of Time Limits

Any time limit provided for in this Title for the performance of an act or formality shall expire on the last day at midnight (24:00). A time limit that would normally expire on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday shall be extended until the first working day.

In all cases where the signature of a person is required on any act, the signature may be replaced by a fingerprint if the person is unable to sign.

#### Article 232.- Methods of Summoning

The charged person who remains free, the civil party, and witnesses shall be summoned by mail, by administrative means, or through a police or Royal Gendarmerie unit.

The summons to the charged person shall be sent to the last address declared as set forth in Article 143 (Notification of Placing under Investigation) of this Code.

In case of necessity, the summons may be made by any means.

#### Article 233.- Summons to the Charged Person Who is Detained

The detained charged person shall be summoned through the head of the prison or detention center.

#### Article 234.- Summons to the Lawyer

The lawyer shall be summoned by mail or by administrative means.

In case of necessity, the summons may be made by any means.

#### Article 235.- Mentions of Summons in the Case File

The method and date of the summons shall be recorded in the case file by the clerk.

#### Article 236.- Notification of Orders to the Lawyer

Whenever an order must be notified to the charged person, the investigating judge shall also notify the lawyer, if the charged person is assisted by one.

Whenever an order must be notified to the civil party, the investigating judge shall also notify the lawyer, if the civil party is assisted by one.

#### Article 237.- Notification of Orders to the Royal Prosecutor

Orders of the investigating judge shall be notified orally to the Royal Prosecutor.

The clerk shall record the date of the notification on the order. The Royal Prosecutor shall sign.

The Prosecutor General shall be notified of the investigating judge's orders by the Royal Prosecutor in accordance with the procedures determined by the Prosecutor General.

#### Article 238.- Notification of Orders to the Charged Person

Orders of the investigating judge shall be notified to the detained charged person as follows:

- Orally, or

- Through the head of the prison or detention center.

Orders of the investigating judge shall be notified to the charged person who remains free, the civil party, and the lawyer as follows:

- Orally;

- By administrative means; or

- Through a police or Royal Gendarmerie unit.

When notification is made orally, the clerk shall record it on the order. The charged person or the civil party shall sign.

In other cases, notification shall be made by delivery of a copy of the order, with a signed acknowledgment of receipt by the recipient.

#### Article 239.- Delivery of Summons or Other Orders

Any civil servant, administrative authority, police officer, or Royal Gendarmerie officer requested by the investigating judge to deliver a summons or other order must comply with such request.

The person, unit, or authority designated by the investigating judge must deliver the summons or order to the person concerned, with a signed acknowledgment of receipt. The document with the acknowledgment of receipt shall be returned to the investigating judge as soon as possible.

#### SECTION 2

#### SPECIAL RULES

#### Article 240.- Mandatory Mentions in the Investigating Judge's Records

All records of the investigating judge must include the following:

- Name of the investigating judge;

- Name of the clerk;

- Number and date of the case file;

- Date of the order for judicial investigation;

- Nature of the offense mentioned in the order for judicial investigation.

- Date of the record.

Each page of the record must be signed by the investigating judge and the clerk.

The record shall not contain any blank lines. Erasures or references must be approved by the signatures of the investigating judge and the clerk in the margin.

#### Article 241.- Signatures on the Record of First Appearance

Each page of the record of the first appearance, as set forth in Article 143 (Notification of Placing under Investigation) of this Code, must be signed in the margin by the investigating judge, the clerk, and the person concerned.

When the charged person gives answers, these answers shall be accurately transcribed into the record.

#### Article 242.- Rules for Writing Records

Every interrogation, hearing, or confrontation must be recorded. The record shall accurately reflect the questions and the answers, as well as spontaneous statements.

Each page of the record of interrogation of the charged person must be signed by the charged person and, if applicable, by the interpreter.

Each page of the record of hearing of the civil party must be signed by the civil party and, if applicable, by the interpreter.

Each page of the record of hearing of a witness must be signed by the witness and, if applicable, by the interpreter.

Each page of the record of confrontation must be signed by all persons confronted and, if applicable, by the interpreter.

Before signing, the charged person, the civil party, or the witness shall read the record. If any person is unable to read, the clerk shall read the record aloud. The interpreter shall translate. If any person refuses to sign, the investigating judge shall record the refusal in the record.

#### Article 243.- Mandatory Mentions in the Investigating Judge's Orders

Every order of the investigating judge must include the following:

- Name of the investigating judge;

- Name of the clerk;

- Number and date of the case file;

- Date of the order for judicial investigation;

- Nature of the offense mentioned in the final submission;

- Identity of the charged person, date and place of birth, and place of residence, if available;

- Number and date of the order.

Each page of the order must be signed by the investigating judge and the clerk.

The order shall not contain any blank lines. Erasures or references must be approved by the signatures of the investigating judge and the clerk in the margin.

#### Article 244.- Orders of Transmission

When the investigating judge transmits any document of the case file to the Royal Prosecutor for examination, the judge shall issue a transmission order.

When the investigating judge transmits the entire case file to the Royal Prosecutor for examination, the judge shall issue a transmission order.

The provisions of Article 243 (Mandatory Mentions in the Investigating Judge's Orders) of this Code shall apply to transmission orders.

#### Article 245.- Applications Filed by a Lawyer

Whenever this Code allows the charged person to file an application with the investigating judge, such application may be filed by the charged person's lawyer.

Whenever this Code allows the civil party to file an application with the investigating judge, such application may be filed by the civil party's lawyer.

Any application for recovery of seized items by a person who is not a party to the judicial investigation may be made by a lawyer.

## CHAPTER 5

## CLOSURE OF THE JUDICIAL INVESTIGATION

#### Article 246.- Final Submission of the Royal Prosecutor

When the investigating judge considers the investigation to be completed, the judge shall inform the Royal Prosecutor, the charged person, the civil party, and the lawyers.

2 (two) days later, the investigating judge shall transmit the case file to the Royal Prosecutor for examination.

If the Royal Prosecutor considers that further investigative acts are necessary, the Royal Prosecutor shall proceed in accordance with Article 132 (Request for Acts by the Royal Prosecutor) of this Code.

Within a period of 15 (fifteen) days if the charged person is detained, and 1 (one) month if the charged person is free, the Royal Prosecutor shall return the case file to the investigating judge with a final submission. This period shall run from the date when the Royal Prosecutor receives the file.

If the Royal Prosecutor agrees with the investigating judge that the investigation should be closed, the Royal Prosecutor shall make a written and reasoned final submission. The Royal Prosecutor may request the investigating judge to order the transmission of the charged person to the trial court or to issue a non-suit order.

#### Article 248.- Return of Seized Items

In the settlement order, the investigating judge shall rule on the return of seized items to their owners. The provisions of the second paragraph of Article 119 (Authorities Competent to Order the Return of Items to Owners) of this Code shall apply.

#### Article 249.- Decision on Provisional Detention and Judicial Supervision in a Settlement Order

A settlement order terminates provisional detention. In this case, the provisions of Article 276 (Release of the Detained Person) of this Code shall apply.

However, by a separate decision in the settlement order, the investigating judge may keep the charged person in provisional detention until they appear before the trial court. In this order, the investigating judge shall refer to the conditions of Article 205 (Grounds for Provisional Detention) of this Code.

The decision to keep the charged person in provisional detention shall remain effective for a period of 4 (four) months. If the charged person has not appeared before the trial court within these 4 (four) months, they must be released on the court's own motion.

The settlement order terminates judicial supervision.

When the investigating judge has required the charged person to deposit bail, to hand over identity documents, or to hand over weapons not related to the offense, the bail, identity documents, or weapons shall be returned to the person concerned by the clerk against a signed receipt.

However, by a separate decision in the settlement order, the investigating judge may keep the charged person under judicial supervision until they appear before the trial court.

#### Article 250.- Transmission for Trial

If the investigating judge issues an order of transmission for trial, the judge shall immediately transmit the case file to the president of the court to set the date for the trial.

#### Article 251.- Reopening of Investigation Based on New Charges

After a non-suit order of the investigating judge or a non-suit judgment of the Investigation Chamber has become final, if new charges appear, the investigation may be reopened on the application of the Royal Prosecutor.

### CHAPTER 6

### NULLITY OF THE INVESTIGATION

#### Article 252.- Nullity for Non-compliance with Provisions

The rules and formalities provided for in the following articles regarding general provisions of the judicial investigation are mandatory and must be complied with, otherwise the proceedings shall be null and void:

- Article 122 (Opening of the Judicial Investigation);

- Article 123 (Territorial Competence);

- Paragraph 3 of Article 124 (Order for Judicial Investigation);

- Paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 125 (Scope of the Complaint); and

- Article 128 (Assistance of the Clerk) of this Code.

Nullity shall also be incurred when the violation of a rule or mandatory formality provided for by this Code or by other provisions of criminal procedure harms the interests of the party concerned. Mandatory rules and formalities include those intended to ensure the respect of the rights of the defense.

#### Article 253.- Appeal to the Investigation Chamber

Only the Investigation Chamber has competence to rule on the nullity of procedural acts.

If the investigating judge considers that a procedural act is null and void, the judge shall seize the Investigation Chamber by a reasoned order and shall inform the Royal Prosecutor, the charged person, and the civil party.

If the Royal Prosecutor considers that a procedural act is null and void, the Royal Prosecutor shall file a reasoned application with the Investigation Chamber and shall inform the investigating judge.

If the charged person or the civil party considers that a procedural act is null and void, they shall file a reasoned application with the Investigation Chamber and shall inform the investigating judge. The application may be filed by the lawyer of the charged person or of the civil party.

The application provided for in this article shall be recorded at the clerk's office of the Investigation Chamber. At once, the clerk shall request the investigating judge to transmit the procedural file.

Orders that may be appealed may not be the subject of a nullity application.

#### Article 254.- Waiver of the Right to Invoke Nullity

When the violation of a rule or mandatory formality harms the interests of a party, that party may waive the right to invoke the nullity and thereby regularize the procedure. Such waiver must be recorded by the investigating judge. If the party is assisted by a lawyer, the investigating judge shall summon the lawyer.

at least 5 (five) days before the date of recording. During this period, the lawyer may examine the procedural file.

#### Article 255.- Continuation of Investigation in Case of Nullity Application

When the Investigation Chamber is seized of a nullity application, the investigating judge may continue the investigation, unless the president of the Investigation Chamber decides otherwise. This decision is not subject to appeal.

#### Article 256.- Regularization of Nullity by Settlement Order

A final settlement order regularizes any procedural nullities, if any. No nullity of the prior procedure may be raised before the trial court.

## TITLE 2

## THE INVESTIGATION CHAMBER

### CHAPTER 1

### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 257.- Register of Appeals and Applications

A register of appeals and applications shall be kept at the clerk's office of the Investigation Chamber. Upon receipt of an appeal or application, the clerk of the Investigation Chamber must immediately inform the investigating judge.

When the Investigation Chamber is directly seized by application, the clerk of the Investigation Chamber shall request the clerk of the investigating judge to transmit the procedural file or the duplicate file.

#### Article 258.- Notification of the Hearing Date

The president of the Investigation Chamber shall examine the file to see if it is in order and then set the date for the hearing. The president of the Investigation Chamber shall orally inform the Prosecutor General of the hearing date.

Appeal. The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal shall inform the parties and their lawyers of the hearing date.

Notification to the detained charged person shall be made as follows:

- Orally; or

- Through the head of the prison or detention center.

Notification to the charged person who remains free, the civil party, and the lawyer shall be made as follows:

- Orally;

- By administrative means; or

- Through a police or Royal Gendarmerie unit.

When notification is made orally, the clerk shall record the date of notification in the margin of the order. The charged person, the civil party, or the lawyer shall sign.

In other cases, notification shall be made by delivery of a copy of the order with a signed acknowledgment of receipt.

#### Article 259.- Consultation of Case File and Submissions

The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal and the lawyers may examine the case file until the hearing begins.

The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal shall deliver their written submissions to the clerk no later than 1 (one) day before the hearing.

The parties and the lawyers may deliver their written arguments to the clerk.

Written submissions and arguments shall be initialed by the clerk, dated, and immediately included in the case file.

The parties and their lawyers are allowed to file arguments until the start of the hearing.

#### Article 260.- Conduct of the Hearing

The hearing shall be held in the deliberation room.

After the report by the president of the Investigation Chamber, the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal and the lawyers of the parties shall present their brief observations.

The Investigation Chamber may order the parties as well as the detained charged person to appear in person and to produce items of evidence.

When the hearing is closed, the Investigation Chamber shall deliberate in the absence of the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal, the parties, and the lawyers.

The judgment shall be delivered in a private hearing on the same day or at a subsequent hearing. The judgment must be reasoned and must contain the elements necessary for review by the Supreme Court. The judgment shall be signed by the president of the Investigation Chamber.

The judgment shall be notified orally as soon as possible to the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal. The clerk shall record the date of notification in the margin of the judgment. The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal shall sign the receipt.

The judgment shall be notified to the parties and their lawyers as set forth in Article 238 (Notification of Orders to the Charged Person) of this Code.

#### Article 261.- Review of the Regularity of Proceedings

Whenever it is seized of an appeal, the Investigation Chamber shall review the regularity of the proceedings and their proper conduct.

If the Investigation Chamber finds a cause of nullity, it may on its own motion declare the entire proceeding or any part thereof null and void. The Investigation Chamber shall proceed as set forth in Article 280 (Effects of Nullity) of this Code.

#### Article 262.- Additional Investigative Acts

The Investigation Chamber may order any additional investigative acts that it considers useful.

To perform these acts, the Investigation Chamber shall appoint one of its members or any investigating judge to whom it delegates its powers.

The investigating judge assigned to complete these tasks shall exercise the powers of an investigating judge within the limits determined by the Investigating Chamber.

When the investigating tasks are completed, the procedural file shall be returned and filed at the registry of the Investigating Chamber. The President of the Investigating Chamber shall set a date for a new hearing. The provisions of Article 258 (Consultation of the case file and judgment) of this Code shall apply.

#### Article 263.- Extension of investigation to related offenses

The Investigating Chamber, on its own motion or upon the application of the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal, may order the extension of an investigation to offenses related to the offenses specified by the investigating judge, when such offenses appear from the procedural file.

Related offenses are:

- When offenses have been committed at the same time by several persons together;
- When offenses have been committed by different persons following an agreement, even if the acts were committed at different times or in different places; or
- When objects stolen, misappropriated or obtained from any offense are concealed by other persons.

#### Article 264.- Extension of investigation to other persons

The Investigating Chamber may order the prosecution of persons other than those specified by the investigating judge, except for the provisions of Article 12 (Res Judicata) of this Code.

#### Article 265.- Re-opening of investigation upon discovery of new evidence

After the order of the investigating judge or the judgment of the Investigating Chamber dismissing the charges has become final, if new evidence appears, the investigation may be re-opened upon the initiative of the Prosecutor.

### Chapter 2
### Appeal against Orders of the Investigating Judge

#### Article 266.- Appeal against the decision of the investigating judge by the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal and the Prosecutor

The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal and the Prosecutor may appeal against any decision of the investigating judge.

#### Article 267.- Appeal against the order of the investigating judge by the charged person

The charged person may appeal against the following orders:

- The order refusing a request to perform an investigating act, as provided for in the second paragraph of Article 133 (Request to perform a task by the charged person);

- The order refusing the return of seized objects to the owner, as provided for in Article 161 (Return of seized objects to the owner by the investigating judge) and Article 248 (Return of seized objects to the owner);

- The order refusing a request for an expert opinion, as provided for in the second paragraph of Article 162 (Necessity of an expert opinion);

- The order refusing a request for an additional expert opinion or a counter-expert opinion, as provided for in the seventh paragraph of Article 170 (Notification of the conclusions of the expert opinion); and

- The order concerning pre-trial detention and judicial control, as provided for in Section 5 concerning pre-trial detention and Section 7 concerning judicial control of Chapter 3 concerning interim measures in Book 1 of this Code, and Article 249 (Decision of the closing order regarding pre-trial detention and judicial control) of this Code.

#### Article 268.- Appeal against the order of the investigating judge by the civil party

The civil party may appeal against the following orders:

- The order refusing a request to perform an investigating act, as provided for in the second paragraph of

#### Article 134 (Request to perform a task by the civil party);

- The order refusing to investigate as provided for in the fourth paragraph of Article 139 (Forwarding the complaint to the Prosecutor);

- The order setting the amount of the deposit as provided for in the first paragraph of Article 140 (Payment of the deposit);

- The order declaring the complaint of the civil party inadmissible as provided for in the fourth paragraph of Article 140 (Payment of the deposit) of this Code;

- The order imposing a fine on the civil party as provided for in Article 141 (Abusive or dilatory complaint);

- The order refusing the return of seized objects to the owner as provided for in Article 161 (Return of seized objects to the owner by the investigating judge) and Article 248 (Return of seized objects to the owner);

- The order refusing a request for an expert opinion as provided for in the second paragraph of Article 162 (Necessity of an expert opinion);

- The order refusing a request for an additional expert opinion or a counter-expert opinion as provided for in the seventh paragraph of Article 170 (Notification of the conclusions of the expert opinion);

- The closing order as provided for in Article 247 (Closing order) of this Code.

#### Article 269.- Appeal against the order refusing the return of seized objects to the owner

Any person who is not a party to the investigation, who in accordance with Article 161 (Return of seized objects to the owner by the investigating judge) of this Code has requested the return of seized objects, may appeal against the order of the investigating judge refusing such request.

#### Article 270.- Time limit for appeal

The appeal of the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal must be made within 1 (one) month at the latest from the date the order was issued.

The appeal of the Prosecutor must be made within 5 (five) days from the date of notification of the order.

The appeal of the charged person, the civil party and any person referred to in Article 269 (Appeal against the order refusing the return of seized objects to the owner) of this Code must be made within 5 (five) days from the date of notification of the order.

If the notification is made by delivering a copy of the order with an acknowledgement of receipt, the time limit for appeal shall run from the date of such acknowledgement.

#### Article 271.- Jurisdictions of the Investigating Chamber

The appeal shall be brought before the Investigating Chamber of the Court of Appeal.

#### Article 272.- Form of the declaration of appeal

The appeal shall be made by a declaration at the registry of the Court of First Instance. The appeal shall be recorded in the register of appeals of the court.

The appeal may be made by the lawyer for the charged person, the civil party, or the person referred to in Article 269 (Appeal against the order refusing the return of seized objects to the owner) of this Code.

The appeal of a charged person in detention shall be made by a declaration to the head of the prison or the detention center. The head of the prison or the detention center shall immediately deliver a copy of the appeal to the registry of the Court of First Instance. This document shall be recorded in the register of appeals.

#### Article 273.- Forwarding the file to the Investigating Chamber

Upon receipt of the appeal, the registry of the Court of First Instance shall immediately notify the investigating judge.

The registrar of the investigating judge shall prepare and forward the case file to the Investigating Chamber, with a copy of the declaration of appeal attached. This copy shall be made by the registrar of the Court of First Instance according to the information recorded in the register of appeals.

The file must be forwarded to the registry of the Investigating Chamber within 5 (five) days of the appeal, except in case of force majeure.

#### Article 274.- Protective file

When a duplicate file has been prepared in accordance with Article 128 (Functions of the registrar) of this Code, the investigating judge shall keep the original file. The duplicate file shall be forwarded to the Investigating Chamber.

#### Article 275.- Continuation of investigation in case of appeal

In case of an appeal against an order other than a closing order, the investigating judge may continue the investigation, unless otherwise decided by the President of the Investigating Chamber.

This decision is not subject to appeal.

#### Article 276.- Release of the charged person in detention

When the investigating judge orders the release of a charged person in pre-trial detention, the charged person shall remain in prison until the time limit for an appeal by the Prosecutor has expired, unless the Prosecutor agrees to the immediate release of the charged person. A written agreement from the Prosecutor shall be placed in the procedural file.

The same applies to a non-suit order that results in the release of a charged person in detention.

In case of an appeal by the Prosecutor against an order for release or a non-suit order, the charged person in detention shall remain in prison until the Investigating Chamber has ruled on the appeal.

#### Article 277.- Appeal against a non-suit order

When the Investigating Chamber is seized of an appeal against a non-suit order, it shall rule in accordance with the provisions of point 3 of Article 281 (Consequences of nullity) of this Code.

### Chapter 3
### Pre-trial detention

#### Article 278.- Judgment ruling on pre-trial detention

In matters of pre-trial detention, the judgment of the Investigating Chamber must be issued within 15 (fifteen) days from the receipt of the file at the registry of the Investigating Chamber. If this time limit expires, the charged person shall be released, unless verifications have been ordered or in case of unforeseeable and insurmountable circumstances which prevent the judgment from being issued within this time limit.

When the Investigating Chamber orders pre-trial detention, it must state the reasons for its judgment in accordance with the provisions of Article 205 (Reasons for pre-trial detention) of this Code. The President of the Investigating Chamber shall issue a detention order.

The file shall be immediately returned to the investigating judge after the judgment has been executed.

### Chapter 4
### Nullities

#### Article 279.- Inadmissibility of an application for nullity

The Investigating Chamber may declare an application for nullity inadmissible in the following cases:

- The application is not reasoned;

- The application concerns an order that can be appealed;

- The application is manifestly ill-founded.

The decision of the Investigating Chamber is not subject to appeal.

When the application for nullity is declared inadmissible, the case file shall be immediately returned to the investigating judge.

#### Article 280.- Scope of nullity

When a request for nullity of an act or document of the proceedings is brought before the Investigating Chamber, if the Chamber grants the request, it shall decide whether the nullity extends to all or part of the subsequent proceedings.

The acts or documents which have been annulled shall be removed from the case file and filed at the registry of the Investigating Chamber.

#### Article 281.- Consequences of nullity

After having pronounced the nullity, the Investigating Chamber may:

- Return the case file to the investigating judge;

- Withdraw the case from the investigating judge and assign it to another investigating judge; or

- Continue the investigation itself.

#### Article 282.- Power to continue the investigation by the Investigating Chamber

When the Investigating Chamber decides to continue the investigation itself, it shall assign the task to one of its members who shall exercise the powers of an investigating judge, except for the powers provided for in Chapter 5 concerning the closing of an investigation in Book 1 of this Code.

When the investigation appears to be completed, the procedural file shall be filed at the registry of the Investigating Chamber. The President of the Investigating Chamber shall set a date for the hearing. The provisions of Article 258 (Consultation of the file and judgment) of this Code shall apply.

The Investigating Chamber shall close the investigation by a closing order. The provisions of Articles 247 (Closing order) to 250 (Forwarding for trial) concerning the closing order of the investigating judge of this Code shall apply to the closing order of the Investigating Chamber. When the Investigating Chamber orders the release of a charged person who

is in detention, this decision shall be executed immediately. The provisions of Article 276 (Release of the charged person in detention) of this Code shall not apply.

### Chapter 5
### Personal powers of the President of the Investigating Chamber

#### Article 283.- Powers of the President of the Investigating Chamber

The President of the Investigating Chamber shall ensure the smooth operation of the investigating offices of the investigating judges. He shall supervise, in particular, the conditions for the application of the provisions concerning pre-trial detention, warrants, commissions rogatory and expert opinions.

He shall ensure that the proceedings are not subject to unreasonable delay.

The President of the Investigating Chamber may carry out inspections in the investigating offices.

#### Article 284.- List of cases

Every investigating judge shall, at the end of each quarter, prepare a list of all cases with a notation of the last investigating act performed.

#### Article 285.- Inspection of the investigating offices

A report on any inspection of the investigating offices by the President of the Investigating Chamber must be sent to the President of the Court of Appeal.

#### Article 286.- Application for closing of investigation

When 1 (one) year has elapsed since the date of the charge, the charged person may request the investigating judge to close the investigation. The investigating judge must rule on this request within 1 (one) month from its receipt. If the investigating judge does not rule within this time limit, the charged person may apply to the President of the Investigating Chamber, who shall order the investigating judge to rule.

When 1 (one) year has elapsed since the date of filing a complaint as a civil party, the civil party may request the investigating judge to close the investigation. The investigating judge must rule on this request within 1 (one) month from its receipt. If the investigating judge does not rule within

this time limit, the civil party may apply to the President of the Investigating Chamber, who shall order the investigating judge to rule.

The powers recognized by this Article for the charged person and the civil party may be exercised by their lawyer.

## Book 5
## Judgments

### Title 1
### Judgments of the Court of First Instance

#### Chapter 1
#### Jurisdiction and Composition of the Court

#### Section 1
#### General Provisions

#### Article 287.- Territorial organization of courts

In the chief town of each province or municipality, there is a court of first instance whose jurisdiction extends over the whole territory of the province or municipality, called "Salah Domboung" (Court of First Instance).

#### Article 288.- Incompatibility of judicial functions

The functions of judge and prosecutor or deputy prosecutor are absolutely incompatible.

Any judge who has performed the functions of prosecutor, deputy prosecutor or investigating judge in a case may not participate in the trial of that case, otherwise the judgment shall be null and void.

#### Section 2
#### Material and Territorial Jurisdiction

#### Article 289.- Jurisdiction of the Court of First Instance

The Court of First Instance shall try felonies, misdemeanors and petty offenses. The Court of First Instance shall sit as a collegial body of 3 (three) judges for felonies and for related misdemeanors and petty offenses.

The Court of First Instance shall sit with a single judge for misdemeanors and petty offenses.

#### Article 290.- Jurisdictional conflict between Courts of First Instance

The territorially competent courts are:

- The court of the place where the offense was committed;

- The court of the residence of the accused; or

- The court of the place of arrest of the accused.

When two or more courts within the jurisdiction of the same Court of Appeal are seized of the same case, the President of the Court of Appeal shall designate which court shall hear the case.

Territorial jurisdictional conflicts between courts of equal rank shall be resolved by the President of the higher court.

This decision is not subject to appeal.

The jurisdiction over the accused shall extend to all co-perpetrators, instigators and accomplices.

If the court seized finds that it has no territorial jurisdiction, it shall declare its lack of jurisdiction by a judgment and order the file to be returned to the Prosecutor so that the Prosecutor can forward the case to the court with territorial jurisdiction. If necessary, this court shall rule on the continuation of the detention or judicial control of the accused.

#### Section 3
#### Seizing the Court

#### Article 291.- Mode of seizing the court

In criminal matters, the Court of First Instance shall be seized by:

- A forwarding order from the investigating judge or a forwarding judgment from the Investigating Chamber;

- A direct citation order from the Prosecutor;

- A record of immediate appearance prepared by the Prosecutor.

Any person brought before the court by one of the procedures mentioned above shall be referred to as "the accused."

The victim of an offense may file a complaint as a civil party before the court, if he has not already done so before the investigating judge.

The person referred to as the "civilly liable party" is any person who, in accordance with the law, must repair the damage caused to the victim of the offense.

#### Article 292.- Setting the hearing date

Except for the procedure of immediate appearance, the date of the hearing shall be set by the President of the Court.

In cases where the court is seized by a direct citation, a forwarding order from the investigating judge or a forwarding judgment from the Investigating Chamber, the President of the Court must take into account the time limits provided for in Article 487 (Time limit between delivery of summons, direct citation and order to appear) and Article 466 (Time limit between delivery of summons and order to appear) of this Code to set the hearing date.

The date shall be notified orally to the Prosecutor.

#### Article 293.- Common time limit

Any time limit provided for by this Code for the performance of an act or formality shall expire on the last day at midnight (24:00). In general, where a time limit expires on a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday, it shall be extended until the next working day.

In all cases where the law requires the signature of a person on an act, the signature may be replaced by a fingerprint if the person is unable to sign.

#### Article 294.- Summons to parties following a forwarding order or judgment

In cases where the court is seized by a forwarding order from the investigating judge or a forwarding judgment from the Investigating Chamber, the Prosecutor shall summon to appear at the hearing:

- The accused;

- The civil party;

- The victim, if he has not yet filed a complaint as a civil party before the investigating judge;

- The witnesses;

- The experts;

- The interpreters;

- The civilly liable party according to the information provided by the accused or the civil party.

The summons to appear at the hearing shall be made in accordance with the provisions of Title 2 of Book 7 of this Code. A copy of the forwarding order of the investigating judge or the forwarding judgment of the Investigating Chamber must be attached to the summons to be delivered to the accused.

#### Article 295.- Summons to parties following a direct citation

In cases where the court is seized by a direct citation, the Prosecutor shall summon to appear at the hearing:

- The victim;

- Witnesses;

- Interpreters;

- Civilly liable party according to the information provided by the accused or the civil party.

The summons to appear at the hearing shall be made in accordance with Title 1 of Book 7 of this Code.

#### Article 296.- Summons to parties in case of immediate appearance

In the procedure of immediate appearance, the Prosecutor shall summon to appear at the hearing:

- The victim;

- The witnesses;

- The interpreters;

- The civilly liable party according to the information provided by the accused or the civil party.

The summons to appear at the hearing shall be made in accordance with Title 1 of Book 7 of this Code. However, in case of emergency, the Prosecutor may summon the victim, witnesses, interpreters and the civilly liable party by any means, including orally.

#### Article 297.- Summons to witnesses

The prosecution witnesses who have not yet been confronted with the accused must be summoned to appear at the hearing.

#### Article 298.- Summons to witnesses by the accused and the civil party

The accused and the civil party may bring to the hearing any witnesses whom the Prosecutor has not summoned, at their own expense. Such summons shall be made in accordance with the formalities provided for in Title 3 of Book 7 of this Code.

#### Article 299.- joinder of proceedings

When the court is seized of several proceedings related to several facts which are connected, the court may order the joinder of these proceedings.

### Chapter 2
### Appearance of parties

#### Section 1
#### Appearance of the accused

#### Article 300.- Appearance of the accused

The accused must appear in person at the court hearing.

The accused shall be assisted by a lawyer of his choice. The accused may also request the appointment of a lawyer under the conditions provided for in the law on the bar.

#### Article 301.- Assistance of a lawyer

The assistance of a lawyer is mandatory in the following cases:

1. In case of a felony;

2. When the accused is a juvenile. If no lawyer has been chosen by the accused, a lawyer shall be appointed by the President of the Court under the conditions provided for in the law on the bar.

#### Article 302.- Voluntary appearance of the accused

In case of a direct citation, the accused shall appear voluntarily at the hearing.

#### Article 303.- Appearance of the accused under guard

In the procedure of immediate appearance, the accused shall be kept under guard until the moment he appears before the court.

The court may order the pre-trial detention of the accused as provided for in Article 48 (Procedure of immediate appearance) of this Code.

The judgment on the merits of the case must be pronounced within a time limit not exceeding 2 (two) weeks from the date of appearance of the accused before the court.

The pre-trial detention shall end by operation of law upon the expiry of the period of 2 (two) weeks.

#### Article 304.- Procedure of immediate appearance

When deciding to use the procedure of immediate appearance, the Prosecutor shall:

- Verify the identity of the person brought before him;

- Inform him of the facts alleged against him and the legal classification of the offense;

- Receive the statements of the person if he wishes to speak;

- Prepare a record of the immediate appearance.

The Prosecutor shall inform the accused of his right to the assistance of a lawyer of his choice or an appointed lawyer in accordance with the law on the bar.

The chosen or appointed lawyer shall be immediately informed. The lawyer may consult the file and communicate with the accused.

Mention of these formalities must be recorded in the record, otherwise the proceedings shall be null and void.

The Court of First Instance shall be seized by the record of the immediate appearance.

The accused shall be kept under guard until he appears before the court, which appearance must take place on the same day.

Upon appearance, after having verified the identity of the accused and having read a summary report of the facts alleged against him, the court shall inform the accused that he is entitled to a period of time to prepare his defense.

If the accused requests a period of time, or if the court finds that the case is not ready for trial, it shall adjourn the case to a subsequent hearing.

The court may order the pre-trial detention of the accused by a reasoned judgment. In the judgment, the court shall refer to the conditions of Article 205 (Reasons for pre-trial detention) of this Code. The court shall issue a detention order.

The judgment on the merits must be pronounced within a time limit not exceeding 2 (two) weeks from the date of the appearance before the court. The pre-trial detention shall end by operation of law upon the expiry of the period of 2 (two) weeks.

If the court seized by the procedure of immediate appearance finds that the conditions of Article 47 (Immediate appearance) of this Code are not met or that the complexity of the case requires further investigation, the court shall return the file to the Prosecutor to open an investigation. The accused must be brought before an investigating judge on the same day, otherwise the accused shall be released automatically.

#### Article 305.- Appearance of the accused following a forwarding order or judgment

In cases where the court is seized by a forwarding order from the investigating judge or a forwarding judgment from the Investigating Chamber, the accused shall appear voluntarily, unless the investigating judge or the Investigating Chamber has ordered pre-trial detention.

The Prosecutor shall take any necessary measures to ensure that the accused in detention is brought before the court by the public force.

As provided for in Article 249 (Decision of the closing order regarding pre-trial detention and judicial control) of this Code, the decision to maintain the accused in pre-trial detention shall expire at the end of 4 (four) months. If the accused has not appeared before the court within this time limit, the accused must be released automatically.

The judgment on the merits of the case must be rendered within a reasonable time.

An accused person who appears at the hearing in detention remains in detention until the judgment on the merits is pronounced, unless the court orders his release.

#### Article 306.- Ex-officio release of the accused in detention

At any time, the court may order the release of the accused person in detention or the continuation of the detention of the accused person referring to Article 205 (Reasons for pre-trial detention) of this Code.

The court may decide after hearing the accused, his lawyer and the Prosecutor.

#### Article 307.- Application for release by the accused person in detention

The accused person in detention may apply to the court for release, either orally at the hearing or by a letter sent to the clerk.

An application for release may be made by the lawyer of the accused person in detention, either orally at the hearing or by a letter sent to the clerk.

Where the application is made orally, the clerk shall record it in the record of the hearing. Where the application is made by letter, the clerk shall note the date of receipt on the letter and forward it immediately to the President of the Court.

The court shall decide after hearing the accused, his lawyer and the Prosecutor. The court must decide as soon as possible and at the latest within 10 (ten) days from the oral request or from the receipt of the written application.

#### Article 308.- Temporary detention pending the Prosecutor's appeal period

When the court orders a release, the accused person in detention shall be kept in prison until the expiry of the period for the Prosecutor to appeal, unless the Prosecutor agrees to the immediate release of the accused person in detention. The written decision of the Prosecutor to agree or not to agree must be included in the file of the proceedings.

In case of an appeal by the Prosecutor against the judgment ordering the release of the accused person in detention, the accused shall be kept in prison until the Court of Appeal has decided on this appeal.

#### Article 309.- Accused unable to appear

If, for health reasons or for a serious reason, the accused is unable to appear before the court, the President of the hearing may order that the accused be interrogated at the place where the accused is currently located.

The court shall set the date for the interrogation.

The interrogation shall be conducted by the President of the hearing in the presence of the Prosecutor, the clerk and the lawyer for the accused, unless the accused has expressly waived the presence of his lawyer.

A record of this interrogation must be prepared.

#### Article 310.- Arrest warrant issued by the court

In matters of felonies and misdemeanors, if the accused does not appear, the court may order the stay of proceedings and issue an arrest warrant. The court must specify the date of the new hearing.

The arrest warrant shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the accused;

- The offense charged and the legal provisions defining and punishing this offense;

- The name and title of the judge who issued the warrant.

The arrest warrant must be dated and signed by the President of the hearing and bear the official seal.

The provisions of Article 192 (Execution of the arrest warrant) of this Code shall apply.

The accused shall be taken to a police department, a Gendarmerie unit, a detention center or a prison until the moment of his appearance before the court. The accused may request to be heard by the Prosecutor. The Prosecutor shall prepare a record of this hearing.

#### Section 2
#### Appearance of the civil party

#### Article 311.- Filing a civil party complaint during the hearing

During the hearing, a civil party complaint is filed by a simple declaration made before the clerk. This declaration shall be recorded in the record of the hearing.

A civil party complaint may no longer be filed after the Prosecutor has made his submissions on the merits of the case.

A victim who has already filed a civil party complaint during the investigation is not required to repeat this formality before the court.

#### Article 312.- Incompatibility between the status of civil party and witness

A civil party may not be heard as a witness.

#### Article 313.- Assistance and representation of the civil party

The civil party may be assisted by a lawyer of his choice. The civil party may also be represented by a lawyer, a spouse or a direct blood relative. A representative who is not a lawyer must have a written power of attorney.

#### Section 3
#### Appearance of the civilly liable party

#### Article 314.- Appearance of the civilly liable party

The civilly liable party may be assisted by a lawyer. The civilly liable party may also be represented by a lawyer, a spouse or a direct blood relative. A representative who is not a lawyer must have a written power of attorney.

#### Section 4
#### Appearance of witnesses

#### Article 315.- Appearance of witnesses

Witnesses must appear before the court when summoned. The court may use the public force to compel witnesses to appear.

### Chapter 3
### Trial proceedings

#### Section 1
#### Publicity of the trial and conduct of the hearing

#### Article 316.- Publicity and camera hearings

The trial shall take place in public hearing.

However, if the court considers that publicity represents a danger to public order or morality, it may order, by a reasoned decision, that the trial take place in camera, in whole or in part. The court shall decide by a separate judgment or by a specific decision in the judgment on the merits.

The court's decision ordering a camera hearing is not subject to appeal.

#### Article 317.- Pronouncement of the judgment

In all cases, the judgment must be pronounced in public hearing.

#### Article 318.- Maintenance of order during the hearing

The President of the hearing shall conduct the trial. The President must ensure that the rights of the defense are fully respected. However, the President may exclude from the trial

anything that has the purpose of uselessly prolonging the trial or does not contribute to the revelation of the truth.

The President of the hearing shall ensure order during the hearing.

The President of the hearing may prohibit some or all juveniles from entering the courtroom.

The President of the hearing may order the expulsion of any person whose behavior disturbs the orderly conduct of the trial.

In the performance of his duties, the President of the hearing may call upon the public force.

#### Article 319.- Consultation of the file

Before the hearing, lawyers may consult the case file at the clerk's office under the supervision of the clerk.

The lawyer or his secretary may be authorized by the President of the Court to make copies of documents in the file at their own expense and under the supervision of the clerk.

#### Article 320.- Offenses committed during the hearing

If an offense occurs during the hearing, the President of the hearing shall prepare a record of the facts.

The President of the hearing may order the immediate arrest and the delivery of the offender to the Prosecutor to decide on the measures to be taken.

When the offense is a petty offense or a misdemeanor, the court may try the offender immediately if the Prosecutor so requests and if the offender agrees.

#### Section 2
#### Rules of evidence

#### Article 321.- Evaluation of evidence by the court

Unless otherwise provided by law, evidence in criminal matters may be established by any means.

The court shall assess freely the value of the evidence submitted for its examination according to its deep conviction.

The court may base its decision only on the evidence contained in the file or which has been produced during the hearing.

A confession, like any other evidence, is subject to the court's assessment. A statement obtained through physical or psychological coercion has no evidentiary value.

Communications between the accused and his lawyer may not be used as evidence.

#### Section 3
#### Conduct of the trial

#### Article 322.- Rules concerning the parties present at the hearing

The clerk shall call the names of the accused, the civil party, the civilly liable party, the victim, the witnesses and the experts and verify their identities.

When the civil party is represented by a spouse or a direct blood relative, the clerk shall verify the identity of the representative and the validity of the power of attorney. The same applies to the civilly liable party. The clerk shall consult the President of the hearing if necessary.

Each party shall sit in the place assigned to them in the courtroom.

The accused may not communicate with each other.

Experts and witnesses shall withdraw to a waiting room reserved for them, from which they can neither see nor hear what is happening in the courtroom.

During the hearing and in the waiting room, witnesses may not communicate with each other.

#### Article 323.- Challenges to the regularity of the summons

Any challenge to the regularity of a direct citation, a record of immediate appearance or a summons to appear at the hearing must be raised before any interrogation of the accused on the merits, otherwise it shall be inadmissible.

#### Article 324.- Hearing of witnesses not summoned

At the opening of the trial, parties may request the court to hear as witnesses persons present in the courtroom who have not been regularly summoned. The hearing of these witnesses is subject to the permission of the President of the hearing. The clerk shall record their identity and invite them to withdraw to the waiting room.

#### Article 325.- Interrogation of the accused

The President of the hearing shall inform the accused of the acts alleged against him and shall interrogate him. The President of the hearing shall ask any questions he considers useful for the revelation of the truth. The President of the hearing has the duty to interrogate the accused on both incriminating and exculpatory evidence.

After the President of the hearing has finished his interrogation, the Prosecutor, the lawyers and the parties may be authorized to ask questions of the accused. Any question must be authorized by the President of the hearing. Except for questions from the Prosecutor and the lawyers, other questions shall be asked through the President of the hearing. In case of objection, the President of the hearing shall decide impartially whether the question should be asked.

#### Article 326.- Hearing of the parties

The President of the hearing shall hear the civil party, the civilly liable party, the victim, the witnesses and the experts in the order he considers useful. The President of the hearing may hear as witnesses the judicial police officers and agents who participated in the investigation.

The Prosecutor, the lawyers and the parties may be authorized to ask questions. Any question must be authorized by the President of the hearing. Except for questions from the Prosecutor and the lawyers, other questions shall be asked through the President of the hearing. In case of objection, the President of the hearing shall decide impartially whether the question should be asked.

#### Article 327.- Objection to the hearing of witnesses

The Prosecutor, the lawyers and the parties may object to the hearing of a witness whose testimony they consider useless for the revelation of the truth. In case of objection, the President of the hearing shall decide impartially whether or not the witness should be heard.

#### Article 328.- Oath of witnesses

The President of the hearing shall ask each witness whether they are a blood relative, an affine or an employee of the accused, the civil party or the civilly liable party.

Before answering questions, each witness must take an oath to tell only the truth in accordance with their religion or beliefs.

After testifying, each witness must remain available to the court. The court may authorize the witness to withdraw if their presence is no longer necessary.

The provisions of Article 156 (Witnesses exempted from taking oath) of this Code shall also apply.

#### Article 329.- Informants

An informant who has received a reward for their information may also be heard by the court for information purposes.

#### Article 330.- Assistance and oath of the interpreter

If necessary, the President may call upon an interpreter. The interpreter must take an oath, in accordance with their religion or beliefs, to assist the court and to translate faithfully. In no case may an interpreter be chosen from among the judges, clerks, police officers, gendarmerie units, parties or witnesses.

The provisions of Article 156 (Witnesses exempted from taking oath) of this Code shall also apply.

#### Article 331.- Deaf and mute persons

When questions must be asked of a deaf or mute person, the clerk shall write down the questions and have the person read them and answer in writing. If the person is illiterate, the President of the hearing shall call upon an interpreter under the conditions provided for in Article 330 (Assistance and oath of the interpreter) of this Code.

The President of the hearing may call upon any person accustomed to communicating with the person to interpret. This person must take an oath, in accordance with their religion or beliefs, to assist the court and to translate faithfully.

#### Article 332.- Production of evidence

The President of the hearing may order any person to produce evidence.

#### Article 333.- Search for truth in the absence of the accused

Even in the absence of the accused, the court must seek the truth, hear the other parties and witnesses, and examine the evidence.

#### Article 334.- Submissions until the close of the trial

Until the close of the trial, the accused, the civil party and the civilly liable party may file written submissions and provide any documents they consider useful for the revelation of the truth.

The written submissions shall be initialed by the President of the hearing and the clerk and attached to the file.

#### Article 335.- Right of the parties to speak after the close of the trial

After the close of the trial, the President of the hearing shall allow the following persons to speak successively:

- The civil party, the civilly liable party and the accused, to make brief observations;

- The lawyer for the civil party, to make closing arguments;

- The Prosecutor, to make his closing submissions;

- The lawyer for the civilly liable party and the lawyer for the accused, to make their closing arguments for the defense.

The civil party and the Prosecutor may reply. However, the accused and his lawyer shall always have the last word.

The record of these formalities must be made in the record of the hearing and in the judgment.

#### Article 336.- Oral submissions of the Prosecutor

The Prosecutor shall make oral submissions that he considers appropriate in the interests of justice.

#### Article 337.- Deliberation of the court

The court shall withdraw to the deliberation room to make its decision. No further questions may be submitted to the court. No further arguments may be raised.

The Prosecutor and the clerk are not authorized to participate in the deliberation.

#### Article 338.- Site visit by the court

The court may visit any place in the national territory in the interest of revealing the truth.

The court must always be accompanied by the Prosecutor and the clerk. The accused person, the civil party, the civilly liable party and their lawyers may also accompany the court if they so request.

A record must be prepared of these operations.

#### Article 339.- Further investigation ordered by the court

If it appears that further investigation is necessary, the court may order a further investigation by an interlocutory judgment.

The persons competent to conduct this further investigation are:

- The President of the hearing;

- A judge of the trial panel, when the court sits as a collegiate body;

- Any other judge of the first instance court.

The judgment ordering a further investigation shall appoint the judge in charge of this task. Under the same conditions as the investigating judge, this judge may:

- Proceed to any part of the court's jurisdiction or to any part of the national territory;

- Hear witnesses;

- Conduct searches;

- Seize evidence;

- Order expert examinations.

To implement this further investigation, the judge may also issue a rogatory commission.

#### Article 340.- Adjournment of the case

If the trial is not completed during the hearing, the court may adjourn it to a later hearing on a date set by the court.

#### Article 341.- Preparation of the record of the hearing

During the hearing, the clerk shall prepare a record of the hearing intended to provide the Court of Appeal with the means to review the legality of the proceedings and to understand the trial.

The clerk shall endeavor to report carefully the conduct of the hearing and accurately record the questions asked and the answers given by the accused, the civil party, the civilly liable party, witnesses and experts.

The record of the hearing shall be signed by the clerk and initialed after verification by the President of the hearing within 10 (ten) days from the day of pronouncement of the judgment.

Any clerk who is negligent in preparing the record of the hearing shall be subject to disciplinary action.

#### Section 4
#### Exceptions

#### Article 342.- Competence of the court regarding exceptions

The court seized of a public prosecution has the competence to decide on all exceptions raised by the parties, unless otherwise provided by law.

The court has the competence, in particular, to decide on exceptions concerning the nullity of the proceedings submitted for its examination, except when the court is seized by a forwarding order from an investigating judge or a forwarding judgment from the Investigating Chamber.

#### Article 343.- Interlocutory issues and stay of proceedings

The court must stay its decision when a party raises an interlocutory issue the examination of which falls within the exclusive jurisdiction of another court.

An interlocutory issue is admissible only if it is likely to remove the criminal nature of the acts alleged against the accused.

In civil matters, issues concerning property rights, real estate rights and personal status fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of the civil court.

#### Article 344.- Raising exceptions

Exceptions must be raised before any defense on the merits, otherwise they shall be inadmissible.

#### Article 345.- Admissibility of interlocutory issues

The court may declare an interlocutory issue inadmissible only by a reasoned judgment. When an exception is not accepted, the trial shall proceed.

When an interlocutory issue is raised and accepted by the court, the court shall stay the proceedings and set a time limit for the interested party to seize the competent court.

Upon expiry of this time limit, if the interested party provides evidence that they have seized the competent court, the court shall set a new time limit to obtain a decision on the issue. The court seized of the case shall be informed of this time limit by the Prosecutor.

If no decision is rendered within the time limit set by the court, the criminal proceedings shall resume if the delay is caused by the negligence of the interested party.

#### Article 346.- Judgment on exceptions

The court shall decide on exceptions by a separate judgment from the judgment on the merits.

However, the court may also join the incidents to the merits and decide by a single judgment on both the exceptions and the merits.

### Chapter 4
### Decision

#### Section 1
#### Pronouncement of the judgment

#### Article 347.- Pronouncement of the judgment

The judgment shall be rendered at the hearing where the trial took place or at a later hearing. In the latter case, the President of the hearing shall inform the parties present of the date on which the judgment will be pronounced.

#### Article 348.- Limits of the court's jurisdiction over the facts

The court may decide only on the acts set out in the forwarding judgment, the forwarding order, the direct citation or the record of immediate appearance.

Where the court sits as a collegiate body and finds that the acts complained of constitute a misdemeanor or a petty offense, the court remains competent to try the case.

Where the court sits as a single-judge court and finds that the acts complained of constitute a felony, the court shall forward the file to the Prosecutor to open an investigation.

#### Article 349.- Limits of the court's jurisdiction over persons

The court may only try persons who are brought before it in the capacity of accused.

If a person summoned to appear before the court as a witness is likely to be charged as a principal or an accomplice, that person may be tried only after having been charged as provided for in Article 43 (Charging).

#### Article 350.- Declaration of guilt

The court shall examine:

- Whether the acts constitute a felony, a misdemeanor or a petty offense;

- Whether the accused has committed the acts alleged against him.

If the court finds the accused guilty, it shall pronounce the penalty provided by law.

If the court considers that the acts do not constitute an offense or that the accused is not guilty, the accused shall be acquitted.

#### Article 351.- Absence of the accused

The benefit of the doubt shall always go to the accused.

In the event of the absence of the accused, the court shall pronounce the penalty it considers appropriate if it is convinced that the accused has indeed committed the offense as charged.

#### Article 352.- Termination of judicial control

Judicial control shall terminate upon the pronouncement of the judgment.

#### Article 353.- Committal order and arrest warrant issued by the court

If the court pronounces a sentence of imprisonment of at least 1 (one) year without suspension, the court may, by a specific and reasoned decision, issue during the hearing:

- A committal order against the accused person present;

- An arrest warrant against the absent accused person.

The committal order and the arrest warrant shall be immediately enforceable.

These warrants shall contain:

- The identity of the convicted person;

- The nature of the penalty and the legal provisions on which the conviction is based;

- The name and title of the judge who issued the warrant.

The warrant must be dated and signed by the judge and bear the official seal.

A convicted person arrested in execution of an arrest warrant must be brought as soon as possible before the Prosecutor of the place of arrest, who shall inform the person of the warrant and proceed with the incarceration.

When the judgment is rendered in absentia, the convicted person who decides to file an opposition must appear as soon as possible before the court that rendered the judgment. The convicted person may apply for release. The court must decide as provided for in Article 307 (Application for release by the accused person in detention) of this Code.

#### Article 354.- Restoration of seized items

The court is competent to decide on the restoration of seized items.

#### Article 355.- Decision on civil interests

In the same judgment as the criminal case, the court shall decide on the civil interests. The court shall rule on the admissibility of the civil party complaint

and decide on the civil party application against the accused and the civilly liable party. When the court is unable to decide on the civil interests, it may adjourn this decision to a later hearing and may order an interim payment.

Persons convicted of the same offense are solidarily liable for the payment of damages.

#### Article 356.- Absence of the civil party at the hearing

If the civil party has filed a request for damages before the hearing but is absent from the hearing, and if the court finds the accused guilty, the court must nevertheless award damages to the victim based on the elements of the file.

#### Article 357.- Drafting of the judgment: Reasons and Operative part

Every judgment must contain two parts:

- The reasons, which are the logic of facts and law that led the court to its decision;

- The operative part, which is the court's decision.

The facts must be clear and beyond doubt. The court must examine each charge and the arguments raised during the hearing.

In the reasons, the court must respond to the written submissions of the parties.

The operative part shall specify the offenses of which the accused has been found guilty, the legal provisions applied and the civil sanctions.

#### Article 358.- Mentions to be included in the judgment

The judgment shall be signed by the President and the clerk.

The judgment must contain the following information:

- The date of the hearing;

- The date of the pronouncement of the judgment;

- The name and title of the presiding judge;

- The name and title of the representative of the Prosecution;

- The name and title of the clerk;

- The name, address, date and place of birth, and occupation of the accused, the civil party and the civilly liable party;

- The names of the lawyers.

After being signed by the President and the clerk, the original judgment shall be deposited at the clerk's office at the latest 8 (eight) days after its pronouncement. The clerk's office must always keep the original judgment. The clerk may provide copies of the judgment, in particular in case of appeal.

#### Article 359.- Pronouncement of the judgment in public hearing

Every judgment must be pronounced in public hearing. The operative part shall be read aloud by the President of the hearing.

#### Section 2
#### Types of judgments

#### Article 360.- Judgment after trial

If the accused appears at the hearing, the judgment is a judgment after trial. A judgment after trial does not need to be served on the accused. A judgment after trial is subject to appeal.

The judgment remains a judgment after trial even if the accused leaves the courtroom.

The judgment is also a judgment after trial when the accused has been interrogated under the conditions provided for in Article 309 (Accused unable to appear) of this Code.

#### Article 361.- Judgment deemed to be after trial

If the accused does not appear at the hearing, but has been notified of the direct citation or has received the summons, the judgment shall be deemed to be a judgment after trial against the accused.

The judgment deemed to be after trial must be served on the accused. This judgment is subject to appeal.

If the absent accused provides a reason for his absence and the court finds it valid, the court may adjourn the case to a later hearing.

#### Article 362.- Judgment in absentia

If the accused does not appear at the hearing, and if there is no evidence that he has been notified of the forwarding order or has received the summons, the judgment shall be pronounced in absentia against the accused.

The judgment in absentia against the accused must be served on the accused. This judgment is subject to opposition.

#### Article 363.- Judgment concerning the civil party

If the civil party appears at the hearing or is duly represented, the judgment is a judgment after trial concerning the civil party. A judgment after trial concerning the civil party does not need to be served. This judgment is subject to appeal.

If the civil party does not appear at the hearing and is not duly represented, the judgment shall be pronounced in absentia against the civil party. This judgment in absentia must be served on the civil party. This judgment is subject to opposition.

#### Article 364.- Judgment concerning the civilly liable party

If the person summoned in the capacity of civilly liable party appears at the hearing or is duly represented, the judgment is a judgment after trial

concerning the civilly liable party. A judgment after trial concerning the civilly liable party does not need to be served. This judgment is subject to appeal.

If the person summoned in the capacity of civilly liable party does not appear at the hearing and is not duly represented, the judgment shall be pronounced in absentia against the civilly liable party. This judgment in absentia must be served on the civilly liable party. This judgment is subject to opposition.

#### Section 3
#### Opposition to judgments in absentia

#### Article 365.- Opposition to judgments in absentia

The convicted person may file an opposition to a judgment pronounced in his absence.

#### Article 366.- Form and admissibility of the opposition

The opposition must be made by a written application:

- At the clerk's office of the court that rendered the judgment;

- Before the Prosecutor, a police unit or the gendarmerie units, or before the director of the prison or the detention center.

The convicted person may be represented by a lawyer. The lawyer must have a power of attorney to file an opposition. However, a convicted minor may be represented by his father, mother or guardian. In this case, the representative does not need a power of attorney.

When an opposition is received, the Prosecutor, the police unit, the gendarmerie units, or the director of the prison or detention center shall immediately forward the opposition to the clerk's office of the court that rendered the judgment.

If the person filing the opposition cannot sign, he shall affix his thumbprint.

#### Article 367.- Register of oppositions

The court clerk shall maintain a register of oppositions. The parties and their lawyers may consult this register freely.

#### Article 368.- Time limit for filing an opposition

The opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days from:

- The day the judgment in absentia was served, if the judgment was served on the convicted person in person; or

- The day the convicted person received actual knowledge of the judgment, if the judgment was not served in person.

#### Article 369.- Opposition limited to the civil part of the decision

The opposition may be limited to the civil interests awarded in the judgment.

#### Article 370.- Effect of the opposition

When the opposition is filed against the entire judgment, the judgment shall be null and void. The execution of the judgment shall be stayed.

However, the arrest warrant remains effective. The court remains competent to decide on the release of the accused.

When the opposition is limited to the civil part, only that part of the judgment shall be null and void. The execution of the civil part shall be stayed.

#### Article 371.- Judgment on the opposition

The case shall be brought back before the same court. All parties are summoned at the initiative of the Prosecutor.

After examining the admissibility of the opposition, the court shall decide again on the merits.

If the person who filed the opposition was duly summoned but does not appear at the hearing, the court shall declare the opposition null and void. The judgment being opposed cannot be modified. That judgment regain its full effect against all parties. The person who filed the opposition still has the right to appeal under the conditions provided for in Article 382

(Time limit for appeal by the convicted person, the civil party, and the civilly liable party) of this Code.

#### Article 372.- Opposition filed by the civil party or the civilly liable party

The civil party may also file an opposition to a judgment rendered in his absence. This opposition shall be limited to the civil part of the judgment.

The opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days from:

- The day the judgment in absentia was served, if it was served on the civil party in person; or

- The day the civil party received actual knowledge of the judgment, if it was not served in person.

The opposition must be in writing and filed at the clerk's office of the court that rendered the judgment.

The civil party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse or a direct blood relative. The representative must have a power of attorney to file an opposition.

The provisions of this Article shall also apply to the person declared civilly liable by the court.

### Part 2
### Appeal against Judgments

### Chapter 1
### Competence of the Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal

#### Article 373.- Competence of the Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal

The Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal has the competence to decide on appeals filed against decisions of the first instance courts within its jurisdiction in criminal matters.

#### Article 374.- Incompatibility of certain judges

Any judge who has performed an act of prosecution or investigation, or who has participated in the trial at first instance, may not participate in the trial at the Court of Appeal as President or judge in that same case.

### Chapter 2
### Admissibility of the appeal

#### Article 375.- Persons who may file an appeal

An appeal may be filed by:

- The Prosecutor of the first instance court and the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal;

- The convicted person;

- The civil party, for civil interests;

- The civilly liable party, for civil interests.

#### Article 376.- Form of the appeal filed by the Prosecutor, the convicted person, the civil party and the civilly liable party

The appeal by the Prosecutor, the convicted person, the civil party and the civilly liable party shall be filed at the clerk's office of the court that rendered the judgment.

The convicted person may be represented by a lawyer. The lawyer must have a power of attorney to file an appeal. However, a convicted minor may be represented by his father, mother or guardian. In this case, the representative does not need a power of attorney.

The civil party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse or a direct blood relative. The representative must have a power of attorney to file an appeal.

The civilly liable party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse or a direct blood relative. The representative must have a power of attorney to file an appeal.

The clerk shall record the appeal in a special register kept at the court's registry. The notice of appeal shall be signed by the clerk and the appellant or his representative. The power of attorney shall be attached to the notice of appeal.

#### Article 377.- Declaration of the appeal by a convicted person in detention
 
A convicted person who is in detention may express his/her will to appeal before the director of the prison or detention center. This convicted person shall complete an appeal application which shall be dated and signed.
 
The appeal application shall be recorded by the director of the prison or detention center in a special register. The convicted person shall sign the counterfoil of this register.
 
The director of the prison or detention center must send the appeal application as soon as possible to the clerk's office of the court which is required to record a mention of this appeal in the court's appeal register.
 
#### Article 378.- Illiterate appellant
 
If an appellant is unable to sign, he/she shall affix his/her thumbprint.
 
#### Article 379.- Form of the appeal filed by the Prosecutor General
 
The appeal by the Prosecutor General shall be filed at the clerk's office of the Court of Appeal.
 
The clerk shall record the notice of appeal in a special register kept at the clerk's office of the Court of Appeal. The notice of appeal shall be signed by the clerk and the Prosecutor General.
 
#### Article 380.- Access to the appeal register
 
The appeal registers of the first instance court and the Court of Appeal may be freely consulted by the parties and their lawyers.
 
#### Article 381.- Time limit for appeal by the Prosecutor and the Prosecutor General
 
An appeal by the Prosecutor must be filed within 1 (one) month.
 
An appeal by the Prosecutor General must be filed within 3 (three) months.
 
The time limit for filing an appeal starts from the date of the announcement of the judgment.

### Chapter 3
### Procedure before the Court of Appeal
 
#### Article 386.- Sending the case file to the Court of Appeal
 
In the event of an appeal, the clerk of the first instance court shall prepare the case file to be sent to the Court of Appeal.
 
The clerk must send, as soon as possible, to the clerk's office of the Court of Appeal:
 
- The case file of the proceedings;
 
- A copy of each notice of appeal with certification of conformity to the original;
 
- A copy of the judgment with certification of conformity to the original.
 
In the event of an appeal against a court decision regarding the detention of the accused, the case file must be sent within 10 (ten) days at most from the date of the appeal, unless there are insurmountable circumstances which must be mentioned in the letter of referral.
 
#### Article 387.- Determination of the hearing date and duration of trial
 
When the case file reaches the Court of Appeal, the President of the Criminal Chamber shall personally examine whether the formalities of the appeal are correct and determine the date of the hearing.
 
The Court of Appeal must decide within a reasonable time. In the event of an appeal against a decision of the first instance court regarding the detention of the accused, the Court of Appeal must decide as soon as possible and at the latest within 15 (fifteen) days from the date of receipt of the case file.
 
#### Article 388.- Notification and summons to appear at the hearing
 
The President of the Criminal Chamber notifies the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal of the date of the hearing.
 
The Prosecutor General summons the accused, the civil party and the civilly liable party as provided for in Title 2 regarding the summons of the accused to appear at the trial.

#### Article 388.- (Continued)
 
... and Title 3 regarding the summons of persons other than the accused to appear at the trial, of Book 7 regarding Summons, Sending to Immediate Trial, Summons and Notification of this Code.
 
#### Article 389.- Transfer of the detained accused
 
An accused who is in detention must be transferred, as soon as possible, by order of the Prosecutor General, to the prison or detention center which is closest to the seat of the Court of Appeal.
 
#### Article 390.- Appeal report
 
The President of the Criminal Chamber shall appoint a reporting judge. This reporting judge may be the President or an associate judge.
 
The report's purpose is to describe the proceedings and the judgment referred to the Court of Appeal. The report must be made in writing and included in the case file. The report must be sufficiently comprehensive to enable the court to be fully informed.
 
#### Article 391.- Access to the case file and submission of briefs
 
The Prosecutor General and the lawyers may consult the case file until the time of the hearing.
 
The parties and the lawyers may submit briefs to the clerk. The brief shall be initialed by the clerk, dated, and immediately inserted into the case file.
 
#### Article 392.- Public nature and confidentiality of the debates
 
The debates shall take place in a public hearing.
 
However, if the court considers that the public nature of the hearing is dangerous for public order or for good morals, the court may, by a reasoned decision, order that the debates take place entirely or partially in camera. The court decides by a separate judgment from the judgment on the merits or by a special provision within the judgment on the merits.

#### Article 392.- (Continued)
 
The court decision ordering the debates to take place in camera is not subject to appeal.
 
#### Article 393.- Examination of the accused
 
After the report is read, the President shall examine the accused.
 
The Prosecutor General, the lawyers and all parties may be authorized to ask questions to the accused. All questions must be authorized by the President of the hearing. Apart from questions from the prosecution and the lawyers, all other questions must be asked through the President of the hearing. In case of objection, the President decides at his/her own discretion whether the question shall be asked or not.
 
#### Article 394.- Hearing the civil party, the civilly liable party, the experts or witnesses
 
After the examination of the accused, the President hears the civil party and the civilly liable party in the order he/she deems useful.
 
Witnesses and experts shall only be heard if the court orders their hearing.
 
#### Article 395.- Order of speaking
 
The Prosecutor General and the lawyers speak in the order of their call.
 
In all cases, the accused speaks last. The defense lawyer of the accused is authorized to make additional brief observations.
 
#### Article 396.- Extension of rules applicable to the first instance court to the Court of Appeal
 
Furthermore, the rules applicable to the court of first instance shall also apply to the Court of Appeal.

### Chapter 4
### Effect of appeal
 
#### Article 397.- Devolutive effect of appeal
 
The case is referred to the Court of Appeal within the limits determined by the notice of appeal and according to the status of the appellant.
 
#### Article 398.- Effect of appeal on the enforcement of the judgment
 
During the time limit for filing an appeal, the enforcement of the judgment shall be stayed.
 
However, an accused in detention who has appeared before the court of first instance shall remain in prison during the time limit for the appeal of the Prosecutor and, in the event of an appeal against the criminal decision, the accused shall remain in prison until the Court of Appeal decides. In the event that the first instance court has decided to acquit the accused or has sentenced him to a term of imprisonment less than or equal to the period of provisional detention, the Prosecutor may agree to release the accused before the expiration of the time limit for filing an appeal.
 
The time limit for the appeal of the Prosecutor General is not an obstacle to the enforcement of the sentence.
 
#### Article 399.- Effect of an appeal filed only by the accused
 
When the Court of Appeal receives an appeal only from the accused, it cannot increase the sentence for that person. The court may only modify the judgment in a way that is favorable to this person. The Court of Appeal cannot add additional penalties to the main sentence.
 
The Court of Appeal may substitute the legal qualification of the offense determined by the court of first instance with another legal qualification, but it cannot increase the sentence of the accused.
 
If the court of first instance has forgotten to pronounce any mandatory additional penalty, the Court of Appeal shall annul the judgment and decide on the sentence anew.
 
When it has received an appeal only from the accused, the Court of Appeal cannot increase the amount of compensation for damages awarded to the civil party.

#### Article 400.- Effect of the appeal filed by the Prosecutor and the Prosecutor General
 
The appeal filed by the Prosecutor and by the Prosecutor General brings back into consideration the entire criminal decision of the first instance court.
 
Consequently, the Court of Appeal may annul a judgment of acquittal or increase the sentence pronounced by the court of first instance. The Court of Appeal may always decide to acquit the accused if it considers that the guilt of this person cannot be proven.
 
The Court of Appeal may pronounce any mandatory additional penalty that the first instance court has forgotten to pronounce.
 
#### Article 401.- New legal qualification of the facts by the Court of Appeal
 
The Court of Appeal may modify the legal qualification of the facts determined by the court of first instance, but it must not add any new elements which were not submitted to the court of first instance for decision.
 
#### Article 402.- Effect of the appeal filed by the civil party or the civilly liable party
 
The appeal of the civil party and the civilly liable party may only be filed regarding the civil interests.
 
In front of the Court of Appeal, the civil party cannot make a new claim that was not submitted to the first instance court for consideration.
 
#### Article 403.- Form and signature of the judgment
 
The rules regarding the form and signature of judgments of the first instance court shall apply to the judgments of the Court of Appeal.
 
#### Article 404.- Inadmissible appeal
 
When the Court of Appeal considers that the appeal is filed out of time or in incorrect conditions, the Court of Appeal declares the appeal inadmissible.

#### Article 405.- Annulment of the judgment
 
When the Court of Appeal considers that there is no guilt on the part of the accused or that the act does not constitute a crime, the Court of Appeal annuls the judgment and acquits the accused.
 
#### Article 406.- Evocation power of the Court of Appeal
 
When the Court of Appeal finds that the judgment of the first instance court is null and void, the Court of Appeal may decide on the merits of the case anew like a court of first instance.
 
#### Article 407.- Arrest or detention warrant issued by the Court of Appeal
 
If the Court of Appeal pronounces a sentence of imprisonment of at least 1 (one) year without suspension, and if security measures are required, the Court of Appeal may, by a special and reasoned decision, issue at the time of the hearing:
 
- A detention warrant against the accused who is present;
 
- An arrest warrant against the accused who is absent.
 
Detention warrants and arrest warrants are immediately enforceable.
 
These warrants shall contain the following details:
 
- Identity of the convicted person;
 
- Type of penalty and the legal provisions which are the basis for this penalty;
 
- Name and status of the judge who issues the warrant.
 
These warrants must be dated and signed by this judge and shall be stamped.
 
A convicted person who is arrested in execution of an arrest warrant shall be brought as soon as possible before the Prosecutor General at the place of arrest, who shall inform the person of this warrant and detain that person.
 
When a judgment has been rendered in absentia, a convicted person who decides to file an opposition must appear as soon as possible before the Court of Appeal that rendered the judgment. The convicted person may apply for release from detention. In such cases, the Court of Appeal decides

#### Article 407.- (Continued)
 
according to Article 307 (Complaint for release of an accused who is detained) of this Code.
 
#### Article 408.- Types of judgments
 
The judgments pronounced by the Court of Appeal are judgments rendered in court (contradictory), judgments considered to have been rendered in court (deemed contradictory) or judgments rendered in absentia (by default), following the same classification as applied to judgments rendered by the first instance court.
 
### Chapter 5
### Opposition to a judgment rendered in absentia
 
#### Article 409.- Opposition to a judgment rendered in absentia
 
A convicted person may file an opposition against a judgment that has been rendered in his/her absence.
 
#### Article 410.- Form of opposition
 
An opposition can be filed:
 
- Through a written complaint at the clerk’s office of the Court of Appeal that rendered the judgment; or
 
- Through a written complaint before the Prosecutor General, police unit, gendarmerie, or the director of the prison or detention center.
 
The convicted person may be represented by a lawyer. The lawyer must have an authorization to file an opposition. However, a minor convicted person may be represented by the father, mother, or guardian. In this case, the representative does not need a power of attorney.
 
When the written complaint of opposition is received, the Prosecutor General, police unit, and gendarmerie, as well as the director of the prison or detention center, shall immediately send the case file to the Court of Appeal which rendered the judgment.
 
If the opponent is unable to sign, he/she shall affix his/her thumbprint.

#### Article 411.- Register of opposition
 
The clerk of the Court of Appeal shall maintain a register of opposition. The parties and their lawyers may freely consult it.
 
#### Article 412.- Time limit for opposition
 
An opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days from:
 
- The date of notification of the default judgment, when the judgment has been personally served to the convicted person.
 
- The date when the convicted person certainly has knowledge of the judgment, when the judgment has not been personally served to the convicted person.
 
#### Article 413.- Opposition limited to the civil decision
 
An opposition can be limited only to the civil decision of the judgment.
 
#### Article 414.- Opposition against the criminal or civil decision of the judgment
 
When an opposition is filed against the entire judgment, that judgment shall be null and void. The enforcement of the judgment is stayed. However, an arrest warrant remains effective. The Court of Appeal has the jurisdiction to decide on the release.
 
When an opposition is filed against the civil decision, the decision regarding this part shall be null and void and the enforcement shall be stayed.
 
#### Article 415.- Judgment rendered on opposition
 
The case shall be brought back for trial before the same Court of Appeal.
 
The parties are summoned at the initiative of the Prosecutor General.
 
After deciding on the admissibility of the opposition, the court shall decide anew on the merits.

#### Article 415.- (Continued)
 
If the opponent who has been duly summoned does not appear at the hearing, the court shall declare the opposition null and void. The judgment being opposed cannot be modified. This judgment has full effect on the parties. However, the convicted person may file a cassation appeal to the Supreme Court.
 
#### Article 416.- Opposition filed by the civil party or civilly liable party: Time limit, form, representation
 
The civil party may also file an opposition against a default judgment. This opposition is limited only to the civil decision of the judgment.
 
An opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days from:
 
- The date of notification of the default judgment, when the judgment has been personally served to the civil party.
 
- The date when the civil party certainly has knowledge of the judgment, when the judgment has not been personally served to the civil party.
 
The opposition may be filed in writing at the clerk’s office of the Court of Appeal that rendered the judgment.
 
The civil party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse, or a direct blood relative. The representative must have a written power of attorney authorizing the filing of the opposition.
 
The provisions of this article shall apply to the person declared civilly liable by the Court of Appeal.

### Book 6
### Supreme Court
 
### Title 1
### Cassation appeal
 
### General Chapter
### Cassation appeal
 
#### Article 417.- Decisions subject to cassation appeal
 
The judgments of the Investigation Chamber, including those relating to extradition, as well as the judgments rendered in last resort by the Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal, are subject to cassation appeal.
 
However, this is not applicable to the judgments of the Investigation Chamber which order the referral of the accused to the trial court.
 
#### Article 418.- Persons who may file a cassation appeal
 
A cassation appeal may be filed by:
 
- The Prosecutor General attached to the Supreme Court;
 
- The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal;
 
- The accused or the convicted person;
 
- The person whose extradition is requested in extradition proceedings;
 
- The civil party;
 
- The civilly liable party.
 
Cassation appeals are heard by the Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court.
 
#### Article 419.- Grounds for cassation appeal
 
The Supreme Court may quash a decision in the following cases:
 
- Illegal composition of the court;
 
- Lack of jurisdiction of the court;
 
- Abuse of power.

- Violation of the law or incorrect application of the law;

- Violation or omission of procedural forms which are prescribed by law to be null;

- Failure to decide on a motion or application of the prosecutor or a party, when such motion or application is made in writing and is clearly expressed;

- Distortion of facts;

- Lack of reasoning;

- Contradiction between the reasoning and the decision.

#### Article 420.- Time period for filing a complaint in cassation

A complaint in cassation shall be filed within the following time periods:

- 1 (one) month for judgments of the Criminal Chamber of the Court of Appeal;

- 15 (fifteen) days for judgments of the Investigating Chamber of the Court of Appeal;

- 5 (five) days for the opinion of the Investigating Chamber expressed in extradition cases.

When the judgment against which the complaint in cassation is filed is a contradictory judgment, the time period shall start from the date of the announcement of the judgment.

When the judgment against which the complaint in cassation is filed is a judgment deemed to be contradictory, the time period shall start from the date of notification.

When the judgment is rendered in absentia, the time period shall start from the expiry of the period for filing an opposition.

#### Article 421.- Complaint in cassation against interlocutory judgments

When the Court of Appeal renders an interlocutory judgment, the complaint in cassation shall be immediately admissible if such judgment terminates the proceedings. In the opposite case, the interlocutory judgment may only be submitted to the Supreme Court for review together with the judgment on the merits.

#### Article 422.- Form of declaring a complaint in cassation

The complaint in cassation shall be made by an application at the registry of the Court of Appeal which rendered the decision. This application shall be recorded in a special register kept at the registry of this court.

The accused, the convict, and the person whose extradition is requested may be represented by a lawyer. The lawyer shall have an authorization to file a complaint in cassation. However, the accused or convict who is a minor may be represented by his/her father, mother, or legal guardian. In this case, the representative does not need to have an authorization.

The civil party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse, or a direct blood relative. The representative shall have an authorization to file a complaint in cassation.

The civilly liable party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse, or a direct blood relative. The representative shall have an authorization to file a complaint in cassation.

#### Article 423.- Signing of the complaint in cassation

The complaint in cassation shall be signed by the clerk and the appellant or his/her representative. When the appellant does not know how to sign, the person or his/her representative shall affix a thumbprint. The authorization shall be attached to the application for cassation.

In Case of the absence of the signature of the clerk or the absence of a date, the application for cassation may be admissible if no fraud is detected. The clerk shall be subject to a fine of 5,000 (five thousand) Riels. This fine shall be announced by the Supreme Court in its judgment.

#### Article 424.- Transmission of the case file to the Supreme Court

Within the shortest time period, the clerk of the Court of Appeal shall prepare the case file and transmit it to the clerk of the Supreme Court.

#### Article 425.- Registration of the case file at the Supreme Court

When the case file reaches the Supreme Court, the clerk shall register it.

#### Article 426.- Appointment of a lawyer

The parties may appoint a lawyer from the time of registration of the case file and at the latest until 1 (one) day before the date of the hearing. The parties shall transmit the name of their lawyer in writing to the clerk of the Supreme Court.

#### Article 427.- Time period for writing a memorandum

The clerk of the Supreme Court shall notify the parties of the registration of the case file.

Within 10 (ten) days after this notification, the clerk shall notify the appellant that he/she has a period of 20 (twenty) days to send to the Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court a memorandum in which he/she shall state the legal grounds. If the appellant has appointed a lawyer and has sent the lawyer's name to the clerk, this notification shall also be sent to the lawyer.

If the appellant has submitted a memorandum, the clerk shall notify the other parties and, if applicable, the lawyer, who shall then have a period of 20 (twenty) days to respond to the appellant's memorandum.

#### Article 428.- Placing the case file at disposal for examination

The case file shall be placed at the disposal of the lawyer for examination as needed and for copying at the party's own expense.

#### Article 429.- Extension of the time period for writing a memorandum

When the 20 (twenty) day period is insufficient, a party may request the President of the Criminal Chamber for an extension of time. This extension may not exceed 10 (ten) days.

#### Article 430.- Appointment of a reporting judge

Upon the expiry of the time period for submitting the memorandum, the President shall appoint a reporting judge and transmit the case file to this judge, along with the memorandum.

#### Article 431.- Writing of the report

The written report shall include descriptions of:

- The procedure and the facts;

- The legal points raised by the parties;

- The solution proposed by the reporting judge.

The report shall be included in the case file.

#### Article 432.- Transmission of case file to the Prosecutor General and written submissions

The case file, with the memorandum and the report attached, shall be transmitted to the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court.

The Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court shall prepare written submissions to be included in the case file.

The case file shall be sent back to the President of the Criminal Chamber.

#### Article 433.- Notification of trial date to the parties

The President of the Criminal Chamber shall set the trial date and verbally notify the Prosecutor General of this trial date. The Prosecutor General shall notify the parties and lawyers of this date.

Notification to an accused person in detention, to a convict in detention, or to a person whose extradition is requested, shall be made as follows:

- Verbally; or

- Through the director of the prison or detention center.

Notification to a convict or an accused person at liberty, to a civil party, to a civilly liable party, and to the lawyers, shall be made as follows:

- Verbally;

- By administrative means; or

- Through the police or gendarmerie.

When the notification is made verbally, the clerk shall make a note of the notification date in the case file. The convict, the accused, the civil party, the civilly liable party, or the lawyer shall sign it.

In other cases, the notification shall be made with an acknowledgment of receipt to be kept for reference.

#### Article 434.- Public nature of the hearing

The hearing shall be held in public.

The parties shall not be heard by the Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court, unless the President orders them to appear.

The reporting judge shall make a report. The Prosecutor General shall make submissions. The lawyer shall make defense submissions.

After the hearing, the Criminal Chamber shall retire for deliberation and draft its judgment. The judgment may be announced on the same day or at a subsequent hearing date set by the court.

In all cases, the Supreme Court may not deliberate and decide on any case without holding a hearing.

All parties may submit a memorandum or additional memoranda until the close of the hearing.

#### Article 435.- Suspensive effect of a complaint in cassation

During the time period for filing a complaint in cassation, the execution of the judgment of the Court of Appeal shall be suspended.

A complaint in cassation has a suspensive effect. The execution of the decision being appealed shall be suspended while the Supreme Court has not yet rendered its decision.

However, the execution of imprisonment shall not be suspended in cases where the accused appeared before the Court of Appeal as a person in detention.

Arrest warrants shall remain in effect.

Pre-trial detention shall continue.

A person detained in the context of extradition proceedings shall remain in detention.

In the case of an acquittal, the accused shall be released.

#### Article 436.- Decision on points of law

The Supreme Court shall decide on the points of law raised by the appellant and described in the memorandum.

#### Article 437.- Withdrawal of a complaint in cassation

The accused, the convict, the person whose extradition is requested, the civil party, and the civilly liable party may withdraw their complaint in cassation. This withdrawal shall be recorded by the President of the Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court.

#### Article 438.- Time period for rendering a decision

The Supreme Court shall render its decision within a maximum of 6 (six) months from the date of receipt of the case file, except in cases of force majeure.

#### Article 439.- Types of decisions of the Supreme Court

The Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court may:

- Reject the complaint in cassation in whole or in part;

- Quash the judgment of the Court of Appeal under appeal in whole or in part.

If the complaint in cassation is rejected, the decision being appealed shall become final (res judicata).

The appellant is not allowed to file a new complaint in cassation against the same judgment.

When the Supreme Court quashes the decision being appealed, the Supreme Court shall remand the case and the parties to the jurisdiction of another Court of Appeal or to the same Court of Appeal, but with a different composition of judges.

#### Article 440.- Quashing without remand

When the alleged acts do not constitute an offense, the Supreme Court shall declare the judgment of the Court of Appeal quashed without remanding the case.

#### Article 441.- Quashing with remand

When the Supreme Court considers that the alleged acts constitute an offense different from the one in the decision being appealed, but this offense is subject to the same penalty, the Supreme Court, without necessarily declaring the decision quashed, may reclassify the facts while maintaining the penalty previously announced and keeping the decision on the civil part unchanged.

When the Supreme Court considers that the alleged acts constitute an offense different from the one in the decision being appealed, and this offense is subject to a different penalty, the Supreme Court shall quash the judgment of the Court of Appeal and order the case to be remanded to a competent court for trial.

#### Article 442.- Plenary session judgment

If the court to which the case was remanded does not comply with the first judgment of the Supreme Court, and if the grounds raised for the second complaint in cassation are the same, the Supreme Court sitting in plenary session shall decide on the facts and the law by issuing a final judgment.

### CONTENT 2
### REVISION

### MAIN CHAPTER
### REVISION

#### Article 443.- Motion for revision

A motion for revision is a remedy against final judgments and decisions which have become res judicata.

The Plenary Session of the Supreme Court has jurisdiction over motions for revision.

#### Article 444.- Motion for revision in criminal matters

A motion for revision can be made in criminal matters, regardless of which court rendered the decision and regardless of what penalty was announced.

#### Article 445.- Grounds for opening a revision

A motion for revision may only be made in the following cases:

1- When, after a conviction for homicide, sufficient evidence is produced to establish that the victim is still alive;

2- When two persons are convicted for the same offense by decisions that are irreconcilable with each other;

3- When a witness is convicted of giving false testimony against the accused person;

4- When a new fact, a new document, or new evidence arises that leads to doubt about the guilt of the convict.

#### Article 446.- Persons who may apply for revision

The right to apply for revision belongs to:

1- The Minister of Justice;

2- The convict or his/her legal representative if the convict is incapacitated;

3- The spouse, father, mother, children, and generally any person with a material or moral interest in the cancellation of the sentence, in cases where the convict has died or disappeared.

The Minister of Justice, before applying to the Supreme Court, may request the territorially competent Prosecutor General to conduct additional investigations.

#### Article 447.- Registration of a motion for revision and access to the case file

The application for revision shall be recorded in a register by the clerk of the Supreme Court.

The case file of the motion for revision may be examined at the registry office of the Supreme Court by the applicant and his/her lawyer. The lawyer may copy the case file.

When the application is made by the Minister of Justice, the case file may be examined by the convict and his/her lawyer. In case the convict has disappeared, the case file may be examined by his/her spouse, father, mother, or children.

#### Article 448.- Procedure for revision: Time period

The clerk shall notify the applicant that he/she has 30 (thirty) days to complete his/her application. Exceptionally, this period may be extended by the President of the Supreme Court.

During this period, the President of the Supreme Court shall appoint a reporting judge. When the report is completed, the case file shall be transmitted to the Prosecutor General of the Supreme Court. The Prosecutor General shall make written submissions within 30 (thirty) days.

#### Article 449.- Decision on suspension of execution of sentence

The Criminal Chamber of the Supreme Court, having received the motion for revision, may decide to suspend the execution of the sentence if there are clear grounds.

#### Article 450.- Transmission of the revision motion to the Supreme Court

When the Criminal Chamber decides that the revision motion is admissible, the Chamber shall transmit the case file of the revision motion to the Supreme Court for trial in Plenary Session and for a decision on the facts and the law by a final judgment.

#### Article 451.- Notification of trial date and public nature of the hearing

The President of the Supreme Court shall set the trial date and verbally notify the Prosecutor General of the trial date. The Prosecutor General shall notify the applicant and the lawyer of the trial date as provided for in Article 433 (Notification of trial date to the parties) of this Code. If the revision motion was made by the Minister of Justice, the Prosecutor General shall notify the convict or his/her family of the trial date.

The hearing shall be held in public.

The Court shall hear the report of the reporting judge and then hear the lawyer of the applicant. When the revision motion is made by the Minister of Justice, the Court shall hear the lawyer of the convict or his/her family. If it is deemed useful, the Court shall hear the convict's testimony.

The Court shall hear the submissions of the Prosecutor General. The applicant's lawyer may speak again.

#### Article 452.- Additional investigation

If the Supreme Court finds that the case is ready for judgment, the Supreme Court shall decide on the revision motion by a judgment that cannot be appealed.

In the opposite case, the Supreme Court shall order an additional investigation. The Supreme Court shall appoint one of its members to perform this task. The appointed judge shall have the same powers as an investigating judge, except for pre-trial detention and judicial supervision.

When the investigation is completed, the results of the investigation shall be notified to the applicant and his/her lawyer.

The President shall set the trial date as provided for in Article 451 (Notification of trial date and public nature of the hearing) of this Code.

#### Article 453.- Reasoning of the judgment

The judgment must contain reasoning. This judgment shall be announced in public hearing.

#### Article 454.- Decision on suspension of execution of sentence

After receipt of the revision case, the Supreme Court sitting in plenary session may order the suspension of the execution of the sentence at any time. The Supreme Court shall decide by a reasoned judgment.

#### Article 455.- Effects of pardon and amnesty on motion for revision

Pardon and amnesty are not obstacles to a motion for revision.

### BOOK 7
### SUMMONS TO APPEAR FOR TRIAL, SUMMONS TO WITNESSES AND NOTIFICATIONS

### CONTENT 1
### SUMMONS TO APPEAR FOR TRIAL

### MAIN CHAPTER
### SUMMONS TO APPEAR FOR TRIAL

#### Article 456.- Summons to appear for trial: Form

As already provided for in Article 46 (Summons to appear for trial) of this Code, a summons to appear for trial is an order to the accused to appear before the Court of First Instance that decides on criminal matters.

The original summons, which records the date, place of birth, and residence of the accused, must be dated and signed by the Prosecutor.

#### Article 457.- Time period between delivery of summons to appear for trial and the date set for appearance

The time period to be observed between the delivery of the summons to appear for trial to the accused and the date set for the appearance before the court shall be determined as follows:

- 15 (fifteen) days, if the accused resides within the territorial jurisdiction of the Court of First Instance;

- 20 (twenty) days, if the accused resides elsewhere within the national territory;

- 2 (two) months, if the accused resides in a country bordering the Kingdom of Cambodia;

- 3 (three) months, if the accused resides elsewhere.

#### Article 458.- Delivery of the summons to appear for trial by the Prosecutor

A summons to appear for trial may be delivered directly to the accused by the Prosecutor.

In this case, the Prosecutor shall hand a copy of the summons to appear for trial to the accused. The Prosecutor shall make a note of the date and place of delivery on the original summons. The accused shall sign the original summons.

#### Article 459.- Delivery of the summons to appear for trial by a bailiff

A summons to appear for trial may also be delivered by a bailiff. The bailiff shall use all means to deliver this summons directly to the accused.

#### Article 460.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the accused

If the bailiff meets the accused at his/her home or anywhere else, the bailiff shall deliver a copy of the summons to the person concerned. The bailiff shall write on the original summons that a copy of the summons has been delivered directly to the accused. The bailiff shall make a note of the date and place of delivery. The accused shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be immediately returned to the Prosecutor.

#### Article 461.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff in cases of absence of the accused

If the accused is absent from his/her residence, the bailiff shall deliver a copy of the summons to any adult person present at this residence.

The bailiff shall write on the original summons that a copy of the summons has been delivered to a person present at the residence. The bailiff shall make a note of the identity of that person and the date of delivery. The person who receives the copy of the summons shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be immediately returned to the Prosecutor.

The person who receives the summons shall promise to deliver this summons to the accused as soon as possible.

#### Article 462.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief

The bailiff shall deliver a copy of the summons to the commune chief or sangkat chief, or to the deputy commune chief or deputy sangkat chief, in the following cases:

- The accused refuses to receive the summons to appear for trial;

- No person is present at the residence of the accused;

- No person present at the residence of the accused agrees to receive the summons.

The bailiff shall make a note on the original summons regarding the identity and position of the person who received the copy of the summons, as well as the date of delivery. The person who receives the copy of the summons shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be returned to the Prosecutor immediately.

The person who receives the summons shall promise to deliver this summons to the accused as soon as possible.

#### Article 463.- Other methods of delivering a summons to appear for trial

A summons to appear for trial may also be delivered by:

- A court clerk;

- Administrative channels;

- The director of a prison or detention center, if the accused is detained for another offense;

- Through the police or gendarmerie units.

Court clerks, administrative authorities, prison authorities, national police officers, or gendarmerie requested by the Prosecutor to deliver the summons to appear for trial shall comply with the Prosecutor's order.

This task shall be performed as provided for in Article 460 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the accused) to Article 462 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief) of this Code.

#### Article 464.- Accused person with no known residence or place of abode

If the accused has no known residence or place of abode, or resides outside the national territory, the Prosecutor may also prosecute the accused by means of a summons to appear for trial. In this case, the date of issuance of the summons shall be deemed to be the date of its delivery.

### SUMMONING THE ACCUSED TO THE HEARING

#### Article 465.- Form of summons to the hearing

As provided for in Article 294 (Summoning parties to the hearing by a referral order or decision) of this Code, in case of a referral order from an investigating judge or a referral decision from the Investigating Chamber, the Prosecutor shall summon the accused to the hearing.

A summons to the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the accused;

- The date and place of birth of the accused;

- The residence of the accused;

- A reference to the referral order or referral decision;

- The court having jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- The date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the accused may be assisted by a lawyer.

#### Article 466.- Time period between summons and date of appearance

The time period between the delivery of the summons to the accused and the date set for the appearance before the court shall be determined as follows:

- 15 (fifteen) days, if the accused resides within the territorial jurisdiction of the Court of First Instance;

- 20 (twenty) days, if the accused resides elsewhere within the national territory;

- 2 (two) months, if the accused resides in a country bordering the Kingdom of Cambodia;

- 3 (three) months, if the accused resides elsewhere.

However, if the accused is in detention, no strictly required time period is required between the delivery of the summons to the accused and the date set for the appearance before the court.

#### Article 467.- Delivery of the summons personally by the Prosecutor

A summons may be delivered directly to the accused by the Prosecutor.

In this case, the Prosecutor shall hand a copy of the summons to the accused. The Prosecutor shall note the date and place of delivery on the original summons. The accused shall sign the original summons.

#### Article 468.- Delivery of the summons personally by a bailiff

A summons may also be delivered by a bailiff.

The bailiff shall use all means to deliver the summons directly to the accused.

#### Article 469.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the accused

If the bailiff meets the accused at his/her residence or anywhere else, the bailiff shall deliver a copy of the summons to the accused.

The bailiff shall write on the original summons that a copy of the summons has been delivered directly to the accused. The bailiff shall note the date and place of delivery of the summons. The accused shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be returned to the Prosecutor immediately.

#### Article 470.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff in case of the accused person's absence

The bailiff shall write on the original summons that a copy of the summons was delivered to a person present at the residence. The bailiff shall note the identity of that person and the date of delivery. The person who receives the copy of the summons shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be immediately returned to the Prosecutor.

The person who receives the summons shall promise to deliver it to the accused person as soon as possible.

#### Article 471.- Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief

The bailiff shall deliver a copy of the summons to the commune chief or sangkat chief, or to the deputy commune chief or deputy sangkat chief, in the following cases:

- The accused refuses to receive the summons;

- No person is present at the residence of the accused;

- No person present at the residence of the accused agrees to receive the summons.

The bailiff shall note the identity and position of the person who received the copy of the summons and the date of delivery on the original summons. The person who receives the summons shall sign the original summons. The original summons shall be immediately returned to the Prosecutor.

The person who receives the summons shall promise to deliver this summons to the accused as soon as possible.

#### Article 472.- Other methods of delivering a summons

A summons may also be delivered:

- By a court clerk;

- By administrative channels;

- By the director of a prison or detention center, if the accused is detained for another matter;

- Through the police or gendarmerie units.

Court clerks, administrative authorities, prison authorities, national police officers, or gendarmerie requested by the Prosecutor to deliver the summons shall comply with the Prosecutor's order.

This task shall be performed in accordance with Article 469 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the accused) to Article 471 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief) of this Code.

#### Article 473.- Accused person with no known residence or place of abode

If the accused has no known residence or place of abode, or if the accused is outside the national territory, the Prosecutor may also summon the accused to attend the hearing. In this case, the date of issuance of the summons shall be deemed to be the date of its delivery.

#### Article 474.- Documents attached to a copy of the summons

In all cases, a copy of the order or decision referring the case to the trial court shall be attached to the copy of the summons.

### CONTENT 3
### SUMMONING PERSONS OTHER THAN THE ACCUSED TO THE HEARING

### MAIN CHAPTER
### SUMMONING PERSONS OTHER THAN THE ACCUSED TO THE HEARING

#### Article 475.- Summons of the civil party

A summons to a civil party to attend the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the civil party;

- The residence of the civil party;

- The name of the accused and the offense charged;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- Place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- Date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the person concerned is summoned in the capacity of civil party.

#### Article 476.- Summons of the victim

A summons of the victim to attend the hearing, when he/she has not filed a complaint as a civil party before the investigating judge. The summons shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the victim;

- The residence of the victim;

- The name of the accused and the offense charged;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- The date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the person concerned is summoned in the capacity of victim.

#### Article 477.- Summons of witnesses

A summons to a witness to attend the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the witness;

- The residence of the witness;

- The name of the accused;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- The date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that:

- The person concerned is summoned in the capacity of a witness;

- Failure to appear at the hearing, refusal to provide information as a witness, or providing false information may result in punishment according to the law.

#### Article 478.- Summons of experts

A summons to an expert to attend the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the expert;

- The residence of the expert;

- The name of the accused;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- The date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the person concerned is summoned in the capacity of an expert.

#### Article 479.- Summons of interpreters

A summons to an interpreter to attend the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the interpreter;

- The residence of the interpreter;

- The name of the accused;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- The date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the person concerned is summoned in the capacity of an interpreter.

#### Article 480.- Summons of the person with civil liability

A summons to a person with civil liability to attend the hearing shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the person with civil liability;

- The residence of the person with civil liability;

- The name of the accused;

- The court that has jurisdiction for the trial;

- The place, date, and hour of the hearing;

- Date and signature of the Prosecutor.

The summons shall state that the person concerned is summoned in the capacity of the person with civil liability.

#### Article 481.- General provisions

Summonses to civil parties, victims, witnesses, experts, interpreters, and persons with civil liability to attend the hearing shall be subject to the provisions of Article 467 (Delivery of the summons personally by the Prosecutor) to Article 472 (Other methods of delivering a summons) of this Code.

### CONTENT 4
### NOTIFICATION OF COURT DECISIONS

### MAIN CHAPTER
### NOTIFICATION OF COURT DECISIONS

#### Article 482.- Subject of the notification

A notification is a document that provides information about a court decision which the law requires to be notified to the parties concerned so that they are informed of that decision.

A notification shall be made on the initiative of the Prosecutor, the Prosecutor General, or a party.

#### Article 483.- Information to be recorded in the notification

A notification shall contain the following information:

- The type and reference of the decision to be notified;

- The identity and residence of the party who is to receive the notification of the decision;

- The date and signature of the person making the notification.

The notification shall state the available means of appeal against the decision being notified.

A copy of the decision to be notified shall be attached to the notification of the court decision.

#### Article 484.- Notification by the Prosecutor

Notification to the concerned parties may be made by the Prosecutor.

In this case, the Prosecutor shall hand a copy of the court decision to the concerned party.

The Prosecutor shall record the date and place of delivery of the copy on the original decision. The concerned party shall sign the original decision.

#### Article 485.- Notification by a bailiff

Notification may also be made by a bailiff.

The bailiff shall use all means to notify the person concerned of the court decision.

#### Article 486.- Delivery of a copy of the decision to the concerned party by a bailiff

If the bailiff meets the concerned party at his/her residence or anywhere else, the bailiff shall hand a copy of the decision to the party.

The bailiff shall write on the original decision that a copy has been delivered personally to the concerned party. The bailiff shall note the date and place of delivery of the copy. The concerned party shall sign the original decision. The original decision shall be returned to the Prosecutor immediately.

#### Article 487.- Absence of the concerned party

If the concerned party is absent from his/her residence, the bailiff shall hand a copy of the decision to any adult person present at the residence.

The bailiff shall write on the original decision that a copy of the decision has been delivered to a person present at the residence. The bailiff shall note the identity of that person and the date of delivery of the copy of the decision.

The person who receives the document shall sign the original decision. This original decision shall be returned to the Prosecutor immediately.

The person who receives the copy of the decision shall promise to deliver it to the concerned party as soon as possible.

#### Article 488.- Delivery of the decision to the commune or sangkat chief

The bailiff shall deliver the copy of the decision to the commune chief or sangkat chief, or to any deputy commune chief or deputy sangkat chief, in the following cases:

- The concerned party refuses to receive the decision;

- No person is present at the residence of the concerned party;

- No person present at the residence of the concerned party agrees to receive the decision.

The bailiff shall record the identity and position of the person who received the copy of the decision, as well as the date of delivery, on the original decision. The person who receives the copy shall sign the original decision. The original decision shall be returned to the Prosecutor immediately.

The person who receives the copy of the decision shall promise to deliver it to the concerned party as soon as possible.

#### Article 489.- Other methods of notification

A notification may also be delivered:

- By a court clerk;

- By administrative channels;

- By the director of a prison or detention center, if the accused is detained for another offense;

- Through a police or gendarmerie unit.

Court clerks, administrative authorities, prison authorities, national police officers, or gendarmerie requested by the Prosecutor to notify the court decision shall comply with the Prosecutor's order.

This task shall be performed in accordance with Article 486 (Delivery of a copy of the decision to the concerned party by a bailiff) to Article 488 (Delivery of the decision to the commune or sangkat chief) of this Code.

#### Article 490.- Concerned party with no known residence or place of abode

If the concerned party has no known residence or place of abode, or if the party resides outside the national territory, the court decision may also be notified to the person concerned. In this case, the date of the court decision shall be deemed the date of notification.

### CONTENT 5
### GENERAL PROVISIONS

### MAIN CHAPTER
### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 491.- Consequences of non-compliance with prescribed time periods

In case the time periods prescribed by Article 457 (Time period between delivery of summons to appear for trial and the date set for appearance) and Article 466 (Time period between summons and date of appearance) of this Code are not respected, the procedure shall be as follows:

- If the accused does not appear, the court shall examine the nullity of the summons to appear for trial or the summons to appear. The court shall order the issuance of a new summons to appear for trial or a new summons to appear, unless the public action is barred by the statute of limitations.

- If the accused appears at the hearing, the court may examine...

...examine the case, if the accused explicitly agrees to be tried immediately. If the accused does not agree to be tried immediately, the court shall order the case to be postponed to a later hearing.

#### Article 492.- Nullity of the summons to appear for trial and the summons

The nullity of a summons to appear for trial may only be declared when the summons has the effect of harming the interests of the accused.

The nullity of a summons to a hearing delivered to the accused, or to a civil party, or to a person with civil liability may only be declared when the summons has the effect of harming the interests of the party concerned.

#### Article 493.- Information to be recorded on the summons to appear for trial or the summons

When a summons to appear for trial or a summons to appear is delivered by a bailiff, by a court clerk, through administrative channels, by the director of a prison or detention center, or through a police or gendarmerie unit, the summons shall contain the following information:

- The identity and position of the person who delivered the summons;

- The signature of the person who delivered the summons.

#### Article 494.- Information to be recorded on the envelope

In the cases provided for in Article 461 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff in cases of absence of the accused), Article 462 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief), Article 470 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff in case of the accused person's absence), and Article 471 (Delivery of a copy of the summons by a bailiff to the commune or sangkat chief) of this Code, the copy of the summons shall be placed in a sealed envelope with the following information recorded:

- The name and address of the person concerned who is to receive the summons on the front of the envelope;

- The seal of the person delivering the summons on the back of the envelope.

This provision shall be applied in cases of notification of court decisions.

#### Article 495.- Signature and fingerprint

In all cases where the signature of any person who receives any document is required, this signature may be replaced by a fingerprint if that person is unable to sign.

### BOOK 8
### EXECUTION PROCEDURES

### CONTENT 1
### GENERAL PROVISIONS

### MAIN CHAPTER
### GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 496.- Execution of sentences and civil penalties

The execution of sentences and physical coercion shall be carried out on the initiative of the prosecution.

The execution of civil penalties shall be carried out on the initiative of the civil party.

#### Article 497.- Final decision

The prosecution shall execute the sentences once the court's decision has become final.

The time period for appeal by the Prosecutor General is not an obstacle to the execution of the sentence.

#### Article 498.- Collection of fines and procedural costs

The collection of fines and procedural costs shall be carried out by tax officials under the direction of the prosecution. Fines and procedural costs collected shall be paid into the state budget.

Every final convicting decision has the value of an order to pay the fine and the procedural costs.

#### Article 499.- Request for the assistance of the public force

Prosecutors and Prosecutors General may request the assistance of the public force to ensure that the execution of the sentence is not obstructed.

#### Article 500.- Difficulties relating to the execution of sentences

All difficulties related to the execution of a sentence shall be referred for resolution to the court of first instance or the higher court that pronounced the sentence.

The court shall be seized by a petition from the prosecution or a concerned party. The court shall decide in a public hearing after hearing the prosecution, the concerned party and, if applicable, his/her lawyer.

The execution of the sentence may be postponed by the court.

#### Article 501.- Petition for merger of sentences

A petition for the merger of sentences shall be brought before the court that pronounced the last decision.

The court shall be seized by a petition from the prosecution or a concerned party. The court shall decide in a public hearing after hearing the prosecution, the concerned party and, if applicable, his/her lawyer.

### CONTENT 2
### IMPLEMENTATION OF PROVISIONAL DETENTION AND SENTENCES DEPRIVING LIBERTY

### CHAPTER 1
### RULES RELATING TO DETENTION

#### Article 502.- Calculation of the duration of imprisonment

A sentence of one (1) day of imprisonment has a duration of twenty-four (24) hours.

A sentence of one (1) month of imprisonment has a duration of thirty (30) days.

A sentence of more than one (1) month of imprisonment shall be calculated from a given date of the first month to the corresponding date of the following month.

A convict who has completed his/her sentence shall be released between 6:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.

#### Article 503.- Deduction of the duration of provisional detention

The duration of provisional detention shall be deducted in its entirety from the duration of the sentence pronounced or, if applicable, from the total duration of the sentences to be served after merger.

#### Article 504.- Organization of prisons

The organization and internal regulations of prisons and detention centers shall be determined by a Prakas of the Ministry in charge of prisons.

This Prakas shall also determine the conditions for the distribution of convicts among the various prisons and detention centers, the procedures for implementing sentences depriving liberty, and the detention regime.

#### Article 505.- Register of detained persons

Every prison and detention center shall maintain a register in which the identity of the detained person, the date of incarceration, and the date of release are recorded. This register shall be regularly inspected by the prosecution. Each page shall be initialed by the prosecution.

Every prison and detention center administrative official shall present the register to judges upon request, as well as to judicial police officers acting under a mandate from the judicial authority.

#### Article 506.- Incarceration document

No prison administrative official may receive or detain any person without a document issued by the judicial authority.

Any prison administrative official who receives or detains any person without a document issued by the judicial authority shall be guilty of unlawful confinement.

#### Article 507.- Unlawful detention

Every judge who receives a complaint regarding the unlawful detention of any person shall immediately carry out the necessary verification.

#### Article 508.- Presentation of detained persons upon request

Every prison administrative official shall present the detained person to the judge upon request, as well as to the judicial police officer acting under a mandate from the judicial authority.

#### Article 509.- Prison inspection

The Prosecutors General attached to the Appeal Courts, the Prosecutors, the Presidents of the Investigating Chambers, and the investigating judges shall regularly inspect the prisons.

#### Article 510.- Conversations of a detained person with his/her lawyer

A detained person shall have the right to converse privately with his/her lawyer.

#### Article 511.- Notification to the Prosecutor in case of serious incidents

Every serious incident in a prison or detention center shall be immediately notified to the Prosecutor.

In case a prisoner escapes from a prison or detention center, the Prosecutor shall issue a search warrant and take immediate measures to execute the search warrant in accordance with the provisions of Article 196 (Arrest Warrant) and following articles of this Code.

### CHAPTER 2
### CONDITIONAL RELEASE

#### Article 512.- Conditions relating to behavior

Every convict sentenced to one or more terms of imprisonment may be released on condition if the convict has demonstrated good behavior during detention and shows a capacity for social reintegration.

#### Article 513.- Conditions relating to the portion of the sentence already served

Conditional release may be granted to convicts who have served terms of imprisonment of:

- Half of the sentence, if the sentence is less than or equal to one (1) year;

- Two-thirds of the sentence in other cases.

A convict sentenced to life imprisonment may be granted conditional release if the convict has already served at least twenty (20) years of imprisonment.

#### Article 514.- Competent authority for granting conditional release

Conditional release shall be granted by the President of the Court of First Instance of the place of detention. This President shall decide after receiving the opinion of the National Commission meeting at the Ministry of Justice.

The National Commission shall consist of:

- Two (2) members appointed by the Minister of Justice, one of whom shall be the President;

- The prison director or his/her representative as a member.

#### Article 515.- Opinion of the National Commission

After receiving a petition for conditional release, the President of the Court of First Instance shall send the following to the National Commission:

- The petition;

- The convicting judgment or decision;

- If applicable, all judgments and decisions if the convict has received several sentences;

- Bulletin No. 1 of the criminal records;

- The Prosecutor's opinion;

- All useful documents.

The National Commission shall provide its opinion within the shortest possible time. This opinion shall be in writing and justified, and shall be sent immediately to the President of the Court of First Instance.

#### Article 516.- Decisions relating to conditional release

The President of the Court of First Instance shall not be bound by the opinion of the National Commission.

The President shall decide by a reasoned order.

The decision of the President of the Court of First Instance may be appealed to the President of the Appeal Court within five (5) days. This appeal may only be filed by the Prosecutor General attached to the Appeal Court or the Prosecutor of the original court.

When the President of the Court of First Instance orders numerical conditional release, this decision shall not be enforceable during the five (5) day period mentioned in the third paragraph of this Article. If an appeal is filed, the decision shall not be enforceable until the order of the President of the Appeal Court is issued.

The President of the Appeal Court shall decide by a reasoned order and no appeal may be made against it.

#### Article 517.- Procedures for implementation and conditions of conditional release

The decision granting conditional release shall determine the procedures for implementation and the conditions for the release of the convict.

This decision shall determine the probation period, which may not exceed the remaining duration of the sentence that has not yet been served.

#### Article 518.- Revocation of the decision

In case of a new conviction or in case of non-compliance with the conditions set by the order, the President of the Court of First Instance may revoke his/her decision.

#### Article 519.- Arrest of a released convict

In case of urgency, the Prosecutor of the original court may order the arrest of the released convict. The Prosecutor shall inform the President of the Court of First Instance immediately.

#### Article 520.- Effects of the revocation of conditional release

In case of revocation of conditional release, the convict shall serve the remainder of his/her sentence.

The remaining duration of the sentence shall be calculated from the date on which the convict received the decision granting conditional release.

If the convict is arrested in accordance with Article 519 (Arrest of a released convict) of this Code, the period of detention from the arrest until the decision to revoke conditional release shall be deducted from the remaining duration of the sentence.

#### Article 521.- Consequences of non-revocation

If conditional release is not revoked during the probation period, once that period as determined by Article 517 (Procedures for implementation and conditions of conditional release) of this Code has expired, the release becomes final.

In this case, the sentence shall be deemed to have been served since the date of conditional release.

#### Article 522.- Prakas of the Ministry of Justice

The methods for monitoring, follow-up, control, and social reintegration of convicts released on condition shall be determined by a Prakas of the Ministry of Justice. This Prakas may entrust public or private institutions with the task of ensuring the social reintegration of convicts.

### CONTENT 3
### PHYSICAL COERCION

### MAIN CHAPTER
### PHYSICAL COERCION

#### Article 523.- Cases of implementation of physical coercion

Physical coercion shall be applied against persons convicted by a criminal court who have not paid:

- Fines;

- Procedural costs;

- Damages and civil compensation to be paid to the civil party.

#### Article 524.- Implementation by operation of law

Physical coercion shall be applied by operation of law. Physical coercion does not need to be pronounced by a court.

#### Article 525.- Physical coercion for non-payment of fines or procedural costs

If the convict fails to pay the fine or procedural costs, the tax administration shall inform the Prosecutor of the Court of First Instance.

The Prosecutor shall set a final deadline for the convict to pay the amount and notify the convict that if the amount is not paid, he/she may be subjected to physical coercion. This period may not be less than ten (10) days.

The notification shall be made:

- Orally; or

- Through administrative channels; or

- Through a police or gendarmerie unit.

A report shall be drawn up on this notification. This report shall be signed by the convict. If the convict is unable to sign, he/she shall affix his/her fingerprint.

#### Article 526.- Order to pay and to incarcerate

Upon expiration of the set period, if the convict has not paid the amount, the Prosecutor shall issue an order to pay and to incarcerate. The Prosecutor may suspend this decision for humanitarian reasons, such as the health status of the convict.

The order to pay and to incarcerate shall contain the following information:

- The identity of the convict;

- The type and reference of the penalty;

- Reference to the notification referred to in Article 525 (Physical coercion for non-payment of fines or procedural costs) of this Code;

- The amount that has not been paid;

- Duration of physical coercion.

This order shall be signed by the Prosecutor and shall be sealed.

The Prosecutor shall request the assistance of the public force to arrest and detain the convict.

#### Article 527.- Challenge to the order to pay and to incarcerate

If, at the moment of arrest or incarceration or later, the convict objects to the Prosecutor's decision, he/she shall be brought before the President of the Court.

The President of the Court shall examine the legality of the order to pay and to incarcerate. In case of a violation of the law, the President shall cancel the order.

The President shall decide by a reasoned order.

The decision of the President of the Court of First Instance may be appealed to the President of the Appeal Court within two (2) days. This appeal may be filed by the Prosecutor General, the Prosecutor of the Court of First Instance, or the convict.

When the President of the Court of First Instance cancels the order to pay and to incarcerate, this decision shall not be enforceable during the two (2) day period mentioned in the fourth paragraph of this Article. If an appeal is filed, the decision shall not be enforceable until the order of the President of the Appeal Court is issued.

The President of the Appeal Court shall decide by a reasoned order and no appeal may be made against it.

#### Article 528.- Release

In case the order to pay and to incarcerate is cancelled, the convict shall be released.

#### Article 529.- Same conditions as for imprisonment

Physical coercion is subject to the same conditions as imprisonment in a prison.

#### Article 530.- Duration of physical coercion

The duration of physical coercion shall be determined as follows:

- Ten (10) days when the amount that has not been paid does not exceed 200,000 (two hundred thousand) Riels;

- Twenty (20) days if the amount is from 250,001 (two hundred fifty thousand and one) Riels to 500,000 (five hundred thousand) Riels;

- One (1) month if the amount is from 500,001 (five hundred thousand and one) Riels to 1,000,000 (one million) Riels;

- Two (2) months if the amount is from 1,000,001 (one million and one) Riels to 2,500,000 (two million five hundred thousand) Riels;

- Three (3) months if the amount is from 2,500,001 (two million five hundred thousand and one) Riels to 5,000,000 (five million) Riels;

- Six (6) months if the amount is from 5,000,001 (five million and one) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels;

- One (1) year if the amount is from 10,000,001 (ten million and one) Riels to 20,500,000 (twenty million five hundred thousand) Riels;

- Eighteen (18) months if the amount is from 20,500,001 (twenty million five hundred thousand and one) Riels to 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels;

- Two (2) years if the amount exceeds 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels.

When physical coercion ensures the collection of several claims, the duration of such coercion shall be determined by taking into account the total amount of the penalties.

#### Article 531.- Immunity from physical coercion

Physical coercion cannot be applied against a convict who was a minor at the time of the offense, against a woman aged sixty-five (65) or over, or against a man aged seventy (70) or over.

Physical coercion may not be applied simultaneously against a husband and wife, even if this application is made to collect separate penalties.

#### Article 532.- Remaining liability for debt

A convict who has undergone physical coercion remains liable for the amount due. However, this convict may not be subjected to physical coercion again for the same debt.

#### Article 533.- Physical coercion upon request by the civil party

When a civil party has not received damages and civil compensation from the convict or the civilly liable person, the civil party may request the Prosecutor to apply physical coercion.

Physical coercion to recover damages and civil compensation may only be applied against the convict.

The civil party must provide evidence that he/she has exhausted all legal means of execution, such as the seizure of movable or immovable property. The Prosecutor may invite the civil party to initiate new procedures to attempt to recover the damages and civil compensation.

If the convict refuses to pay, physical coercion shall be applied under the conditions set forth in Articles 523 (Cases of implementation of physical coercion) to 532 (Remaining liability for debt) of this Code. In the case of competition between fines, procedural costs, and damages and civil compensation, damages and civil compensation shall have priority for payment.

### CONTENT 4
### GRANTING AND REHABILITATION

### CHAPTER 1
### JUDICIAL REHABILITATION

#### Article 534.- Competent court to pronounce judicial rehabilitation

Judicial rehabilitation may be granted or refused by the criminal chamber of the Appeal Court that pronounced the conviction or within whose territorial jurisdiction the convicting court is located.

#### Article 535.- Time limits for the judicial rehabilitation petition

A petition for judicial rehabilitation may be submitted to the criminal chamber for review only after the following periods have elapsed:

- Five (5) years for a felony;

- Three (3) years for a misdemeanor;

- One (1) year for a petty offense.

These periods run from:

- The completion of the sentence;

- The completion of physical coercion in the cases provided for in the second paragraph of Article 536 (Mandatory evidence) of this Code.

#### Article 536.- Mandatory evidence

The convict shall provide evidence proving that he/she has paid the fine, the procedural costs, and the damages and civil compensation. If the civil party has waived the recovery of damages and civil compensation, the convict shall provide confirmation of this waiver, unless the civil party cannot be located.

If the convict has undergone physical coercion, the non-payment of the fine, the procedural costs, or the damages and civil compensation is not an obstacle to rehabilitation if the convict provides evidence of his/her insolvency.

If the petitioner was convicted of fraudulent bankruptcy, he/she shall provide evidence proving that the bankruptcy debts have been paid. If the creditors have waived the payment of these debts, the convict shall provide confirmation of this waiver.

#### Article 537.- Form of the petition for judicial rehabilitation

The convict shall submit a petition for judicial rehabilitation to the territorially competent Prosecutor. The convict shall specify the type of penalty served and state the place(s) where he/she has resided since his/her release.

#### Article 538.- Transmission of the file by the Prosecutor to the Prosecutor General

The Prosecutor shall order a character investigation in every place where the convict has resided since his/her release.

The Prosecutor shall obtain:

- A copy of the convicting decision;

- A certificate of the completion date of the sentence;

- Bulletin No. 1 of the criminal record.

The Prosecutor shall forward the file, along with his/her reasoned opinion, to the Prosecutor General attached to the Appeal Court.

#### Article 539.- Procedure before the Appeal Court

The Prosecutor General shall seize the criminal chamber of the Appeal Court.

The Prosecutor General shall notify the petitioner and his/her lawyer of the hearing date.

The court shall decide in a public hearing after hearing the convict, the lawyer, and the Prosecutor General.

#### Article 540.- Refusal of the petition for judicial rehabilitation

In case the court refuses the petition, the person concerned may not submit a new petition before a period of two (2) years has elapsed.

However, if the first petition was refused for failure to comply with the time periods set forth in Article 535 (Time limits for judicial rehabilitation petition) of this Code, a new petition may be submitted once those periods have elapsed.

### CHAPTER 2
### REHABILITATION BY OPERATION OF LAW

#### Article 541.- Rehabilitation by operation of law

Rehabilitation shall occur by operation of law in the following cases:

- Upon expiration of a five (5) year period starting from the date of completion of the sentence; or at the time of

... statute of limitations for the penalty, in case the person was sentenced to a term of imprisonment not exceeding five (5) years or to a fine.

- Upon expiration of a ten (10) year period starting from the date of completion of the sentence; or upon expiration of the statute of limitations for the penalty, in case the person was sentenced to a term of imprisonment exceeding five (5) years.

### CONTENT 5
### CRIMINAL RECORD

### MAIN CHAPTER
### CRIMINAL RECORD

#### Article 542.- Management of criminal record cards

The criminal record shall be managed by a department of the Ministry of Justice.

For persons born in Cambodia, the criminal record shall record:

- All contradictory convictions, convictions deemed contradictory, and default convictions pronounced by Cambodian criminal courts;

- Convictions pronounced by foreign courts which, in accordance with international agreements, are notified to the Cambodian authorities;

- Acts of pardon and sentence reductions granted by the King of the Kingdom of Cambodia;

- Decisions regarding conditional release;

- Decisions regarding physical coercion;

- Judgments or decisions pronouncing bankruptcy;

- Decisions granting judicial rehabilitation;

- Decisions to expel an alien.

When a convict has served institutional sentences or has undergone physical coercion, the date of completion of the sentence or physical coercion shall be mentioned in the criminal record.

When a convict has paid the fine and the procedural costs, the date of payment shall be entered in the criminal record.

When a conviction has been rehabilitated by operation of law, a mention of this shall be entered in the criminal record.

The following shall be removed from the criminal record:

- Default convictions when they have been nullified following an opposition;

- Convictions nullified by a general amnesty;

- Convictions resulting from an identity error;

- Convictions nullified through a revision procedure.

#### Article 543.- Mentions relating to identity

The criminal record shall also receive cards concerning:

- Persons born abroad;

- Persons whose identities cannot be verified or are suspect.

In the second case, a special mention shall be entered in the criminal record stating that the identity cannot be verified or that the identity is suspect.

#### Article 544.- Information for the criminal record

Court clerks shall send cards corresponding to each decision to be recorded in the criminal record to the criminal record department, under the supervision of the President of the Court.

The prison director shall inform the criminal record department of the date of completion of sentences or physical coercion.

The tax administration shall inform the criminal record department of the payment of fines and procedural costs.

The criminal record department shall be responsible for collecting information regarding acts of pardon and sentence reductions granted by the King and decisions to expel aliens.

#### Article 545.- Information concerning legal entities for the criminal record

The criminal record shall receive cards indicating all types of convictions pronounced by a criminal court against legal entities.

#### Article 546.- Extracts of criminal record

The criminal record department may issue extracts (bulletins) from the criminal record. A bulletin is a statement of all or some of the entries in the criminal record concerning a specific person.

#### Article 547.- Bulletin No. 1

Bulletin No. 1 of the criminal record shall contain all the entries in the criminal record for each individual.

Bulletin No. 1 may only be issued to judicial authorities.

#### Article 548.- Bulletin No. 2

Bulletin No. 2 shall contain all the entries listed in Bulletin No. 1, except for:

- Convictions nullified by judicial rehabilitation or by operation of law;

- Suspended sentences which have not been revoked;

- Judgments or decisions pronouncing bankruptcy in cases of rehabilitation.

Bulletin No. 2 shall be issued to:

- Judicial authorities;

- Military authorities receiving recruitment applications;

- Officials managing the commercial register.

Bulletin No. 2 may also be issued to provincial and municipal governors and state institutions:

- When provincial and municipal governors and state institutions have received applications for public employment;

- When provincial and municipal governors and state institutions have received a bid for a public contract or public authorization;

- When provincial and municipal governors and state institutions have received a request to open an establishment that requires an authorization;

- Within the framework of disciplinary procedures.

#### Article 549.- Bulletin No. 3

Bulletin No. 3 records only unsuspended sentences of imprisonment pronounced against a person for a felony or a misdemeanor.

However, convictions for which rehabilitation has been granted shall not be listed in Bulletin No. 3.

Bulletin No. 3 shall be issued only to the person concerned. In no case may Bulletin No. 3 be issued to a third party.

#### Article 550.- Functioning of the criminal record: Prakas

A Prakas of the Ministry of Justice shall determine all necessary measures for the functioning of the criminal record department. This Prakas shall, in particular, determine the conditions under which cards are created, verified, and transmitted by clerks, as well as the conditions for the creation, verification, and issuance of bulletins.

#### Article 551.- Correction of the criminal record

When a person has been convicted due to an error of identity, the prosecution shall immediately request a correction of the criminal record.

#### Article 552.- Effects of rehabilitation

Rehabilitation clears the criminal penalty. However, the conviction remains listed in the criminal record, with a mention that rehabilitation has been granted.

### CONTENT 6

### MAIN CHAPTER
### COURT COSTS

#### Article 553.- Court costs

Court costs are state resources. The list of court costs, the scale of court costs, and general procedures shall be determined by a Prakas.

#### Article 554.- Procedural costs

Each convict shall pay procedural costs to the state. The amount of procedural costs may vary according to the type of case.

The amount of procedural costs shall be determined by a Prakas.

### CHAPTER 1
### JUDGES

### SECTION 1
### SELF-WITHDRAWAL

#### Article 555.- Valid grounds for withdrawal

Any judge who has valid reasons for not participating in the investigation or trial of a case may request to be replaced. This request shall be submitted to the President of his/her Court. If the reasons are found to be valid, the President shall appoint another judge to replace that judge. The decision of the President of the Court is not appealable.

### SECTION 2
### RECUSAL OF TRIAL JUDGES

#### Article 556.- Grounds for recusal of trial judges

A motion for recusal may only be filed against trial judges.

Trial judges may be recused for the following reasons:

- If the judge or his/her spouse or former spouse is a party to the case;

- If the judge is a blood relative up to the sixth (6th) degree, or a relative by marriage up to the third (3rd) degree, or a former relative by marriage up to the third (3rd) degree of any party;

- If the judge is a guardian of any party;

- If the judge in charge of the case has been or is currently in litigation with any party;

- If the judge has served as a witness or expert in the case;

- If the judge is or has been a representative or assistant to any party in the case;

- If the judge has participated in a decision in the case as an arbitrator, or has participated in the decision of the first instance court that is under appeal, or in the trial of an appeal that was under cassation, or has provided legal advice on the case.

#### Article 557.- Motion for recusal of trial judges

A party wishing to recuse a trial judge must submit the motion as soon as they become aware of the grounds for recusal; otherwise, it shall be inadmissible.

In any case, a motion for recusal cannot be filed after the closing of the oral arguments.

#### Article 558.- Recipient of the recusal motion for trial judges

The party shall submit the motion:

- To the President of the Appeal Court when the motion concerns the President or...

... judge of a first instance court.

- To the President of the Supreme Court when the motion concerns a judge of the Supreme Court, or when it concerns the President or a judge of the Appeal Court.

The motion must clearly specify the grounds for recusal and must be accompanied by clear evidence; otherwise, it shall be inadmissible.

#### Article 559.- Notification of the motion for recusal of trial judges

The motion shall be notified to the judge who is sought to be recused. This judge shall withdraw from the investigation or trial.

In urgent cases, the judge shall be replaced by another judge appointed by the President of his/her Court.

#### Article 560.- Report of the judge concerned by the recusal motion

Within eight (8) days after notification, the judge concerned shall state in a reasoned report whether he/she agrees or objects to the recusal. This report shall be submitted to the President of the Appeal Court or the President of the Supreme Court, as the case may be, to decide on the motion.

If the judge agrees the recusal, he/she shall be replaced immediately by another judge.

If the judge objects to the recusal, a decision shall be made on the merits of the motion.

#### Article 561.- Review of the motion for recusal of trial judges

The motion shall be reviewed without hearing the parties or the judge concerned.

If the recusal is granted, a new judge shall be appointed to replace the judge concerned.

In the contrary case, the petitioner may be fined a sum not exceeding 200,000 (two hundred thousand) Riels, without prejudice to any damages that may be awarded to the judge against whom the motion for recusal was filed.

#### Article 562.- Decision on recusal motions for trial judges

The authorities mentioned in Article 558 (Recipient of the recusal motion for trial judges) of this Code shall decide by an order that is not open to appeal. This order shall be notified by the court clerk to the judge concerned and to the petitioner.

#### Article 563.- Acts performed prior to notification of the motion

The acts performed by a judge who was sought to be recused before the notification of the recusal motion may not be challenged.

#### Article 564.- Motion against multiple judges

When the petitioner wishes to recuse several judges sitting on the same panel, they may submit a single motion. The petitioner may submit an additional motion if new grounds for recusal arise subsequently.

#### Article 565.- Recusal of the President of the Supreme Court

When a motion for recusal is filed against the President of the Supreme Court, the President of the Supreme Court shall decide according to his/her conscience whether or not he/she should withdraw from the trial.

### CHAPTER 2
### EXTRADITION

### SECTION 1
### EXTRADITION REQUESTED BY A FOREIGN STATE

### SUB-SECTION 1
### EXTRADITION

#### Article 566.- Extradition of foreign nationals on the territory

The Kingdom of Cambodia may agree to surrender to a foreign state foreign nationals residing on Cambodian territory who:

- Is the subject of judicial proceedings in that state; or

- Has been sentenced to imprisonment by a court of that state.

#### Article 567.- Treaties and international agreements

The extradition of foreign nationals arrested on Cambodian territory is governed by the provisions of treaties and international agreements ratified by the Kingdom of Cambodia. In the absence of a treaty or international agreement ratified by the Kingdom of Cambodia, the provisions of this Chapter shall apply, unless there are special provisions in other laws.

#### Article 568.- Definitions: Requesting State, Person sought

In this section:

- "Requesting State" means the foreign state requesting the extradition of a foreign national;

- "Person sought" means the foreign national residing on Cambodian territory and who is the subject of an extradition request.

### SUB-SECTION 2
### CONDITIONS FOR EXTRADITION

#### Article 569.- Conditions for extradition relating to the acts

Extradition may only be granted if the act for which the person is sought is an offense under both the law of the requesting state and Cambodian law, even if:

- The act constitutes an offense of a different category;

- The name of the offense, the terminology used, the definitions, or the characterization of the offense are not identical;

- The components of the offense under the law of the requesting state differ from those defined under Cambodian law, provided that the total acts described by the requesting state constitute an offense under the laws in force in Cambodia.

#### Article 570.- Attempt and conspiracy

If the act for which the person is prosecuted under the law of the requesting state is an attempt, extradition may only be granted if that attempt is punishable under Cambodian law.

The provisions of this Article shall also apply to cases of conspiracy.

#### Article 571.- Conditions relating to the sentence of imprisonment

Extradition may only be granted if the act for which the person is prosecuted carries a maximum penalty of at least two (2) years of imprisonment under the law of the requesting state.

However, extradition may also be granted if the person sought was sentenced by a court of the requesting state to at least six (6) months of imprisonment and this conviction has become final, regardless of the duration of the penalty provided by law.

#### Article 572.- Conditions relating to the place where the offense was committed

When the act for which the person is prosecuted was committed on the territory of the requesting state, extradition may be granted even if the person sought is not a national of that state.

When the act for which the person is prosecuted was committed outside the territory of the requesting state, extradition may be granted only if the person sought is a national of that state.

#### Article 573.- Political acts

Extradition will not be granted when the act for which it is requested is of a political nature.

However, acts of violence that endanger life, physical integrity, or individual liberty will not be considered political offenses.

#### Article 574.- Acts committed on the territory and acts already finally adjudicated

Extradition will not be granted when the act for which the person is prosecuted was committed on Cambodian territory and has already been finally adjudicated in Cambodia.

#### Article 575.- Extinction of the criminal prosecution

Extradition will not be granted when the criminal prosecution is time-barred under the law of the Requesting State.

When the act for which the person is prosecuted was committed on Cambodian territory, extradition will not be granted when the criminal prosecution is time-barred under Cambodian law.

#### Article 576.- Multiple extradition requests for the same person

When several foreign states request the extradition of the same person, the following circumstances shall be considered:

- Treaty obligations;

- Gravity of the offenses;

- Time and place where the offenses were committed;

- Date and order of arrival of the requests;

- Nationality, residence, and place of stay of the person sought and of the victims;

- The possibility of re-extradition of the person sought by the Requesting State.

#### Article 577.- Conditions for extradition relating to the request

Extradition may only be granted if the Requesting State undertaken not to prosecute for any offense other than that mentioned in the extradition request, when such an offense was committed prior to the arrest of the person sought.

However, such a prosecution may occur if the Kingdom of Cambodia consents. In this case, the Requesting State shall submit a supplementary request to the Cambodian authorities.

#### Article 578.- Suspension of extradition

When the person sought is being prosecuted in Cambodia, extradition shall be suspended during the prosecution. If the person has been convicted, extradition shall also be suspended until the sentence has been carried out.

However, the Kingdom of Cambodia may agree to temporarily transfer the person sought to the Requesting State for trial. The Requesting State must undertake to return the person to the Cambodian authorities after the trial procedures are completed.

### SUB-SECTION 3
### EXTRADITION PROCEDURES

#### Article 579.- Supporting documents for the extradition request

Every extradition request shall be submitted to the Royal Government of Cambodia through diplomatic channels. The request must be accompanied by supporting documents.

The supporting documents shall include:

- Documents sufficient to establish the identity of the person sought;

- A statement of the acts for which the person is prosecuted;

- The legal provisions applicable to the acts and the penalty incurred;

- A copy of the convicting decision, if any.

All documents must be signed, officially sealed, and enclosed. If the documents are not in Khmer, French, or English, they must be accompanied by a translation into one of these three languages, with an official certification.

#### Article 580.- Transmission of the extradition request

The Minister of Foreign Affairs of Cambodia shall forward the extradition request and supporting documents to the Minister of Justice. The Minister of Justice shall verify the regularity of the request and forward it to the Prosecutor General attached to the Phnom Penh Appeal Court.

#### Article 581.- Request for provisional arrest

The state requesting extradition may request the provisional arrest of the person sought.

In urgent cases, a request for provisional arrest may precede the extradition request mentioned in Article 579 (Supporting documents for the extradition request) of this Code.

A request for provisional arrest aimed at preventing the person sought from fleeing is not subject to any particular formality.

A person who is subject to provisional arrest procedures must be released automatically if the government has not received the documents mentioned in Article 579 (Supporting documents for the extradition request) of this Code within two (2) months from the date of arrest.

#### Article 582.- Arrest and detention order against the person sought

The Prosecutor General attached to the Phnom Penh Appeal Court may issue an order to arrest and detain the person sought.

This order shall mention the following:

- The identity of the person sought;

- The reference to the foreign state's request for provisional arrest;

- The name and title of the official who issued this order.

This order shall be dated and signed by the Prosecutor General and shall be sealed.

The arrest and detention order is enforceable throughout the territory of Cambodia.

#### Article 583.- Presentation of the person sought to the competent Prosecutor or Prosecutor General

In case of the arrest of the person sought, they must be brought as soon as possible before the territorially competent Prosecutor, who shall notify the person of the arrest and detention order and receive their statements.

The person sought shall be transferred to be detained at Phnom Penh Prison. That person may request the Prosecutor General attached to the Phnom Penh Court of Appeal to hear his/her statement.

#### Article 584.- Appeal to the Investigative Chamber

The Prosecutor General shall prepare a case file in order to appeal to the Investigative Chamber of the Phnom Penh Court of Appeal.

#### Article 585.- Procedure Before the Investigative Chamber

The person sought must appear before the Investigative Chamber. This person may be accompanied by a lawyer of his/her choice or a lawyer appointed in accordance with the Law on the Statute of Lawyers.

The debate shall take place in a closed room. In case of necessity, the Investigative Chamber may call for an interpreter.

After hearing the statement of the person sought, the requests of the Prosecutor General, and the defense arguments of the lawyer, the Investigative Chamber shall provide an opinion in the form of a reasoned judgment on the request for extradition.

#### Article 586.- Opinion of the Investigative Chamber

The Investigative Chamber shall provide an unfavorable opinion if it finds that the legal conditions for extradition are not met.

#### Article 587.- Request for Release of the Person Sought

The person sought may request to be released.

The request must be made in writing.

The request shall be submitted to the Investigative Chamber which shall decide after hearing the statement of the person sought, the requests of the Prosecutor General, and the defense arguments of the lawyer.

#### Article 588.- Consent to Extradition

If the person sought consents to be surrendered according to the request of the requesting State after being fully informed of the consequences of the consent to extradition, the Investigative Chamber must record this consent in its judgment.

#### Article 589.- Effect of the Opinion of the Investigative Chamber

Once the matter has been decided, the judgment of the Investigative Chamber shall be immediately notified to the Minister of Justice.

If the Investigative Chamber provides an unfavorable opinion, extradition cannot be carried out by the Royal Government of Cambodia. The person sought shall be immediately released, unless this person is the subject of a detention order in another case.

If the Investigative Chamber gives a favorable opinion, the Minister of Justice may submit the matter to the Royal Government to issue a sub-decree ordering the extradition of the person sought.

When extradition is ordered, the person sought shall be surrendered to the requesting State. The costs of extradition shall be borne by the requesting State. Once the person sought has left Cambodian territory, he/she shall be the responsibility of the requesting State.

If the requesting State has not commenced the repatriation procedure within 30 (thirty) days after the notification of the sub-decree ordering the extradition, the person sought shall be released.

### SECTION 2: EXTRADITION REQUESTED BY THE KINGDOM OF CAMBODIA FROM A FOREIGN STATE

#### Article 590.- Competence of the Investigative Chamber

The Investigative Chamber of the Phnom Penh Court of Appeal is the sole body competent to consider the regularity of the extradition obtained by the Royal Government of Cambodia.

The person who has been extradited has a period of 15 (fifteen) days from the time of arrival in Cambodia to raise the nullity of the extradition.

The request must be made in writing.

#### Article 591.- Procedure Before the Investigative Chamber

The Investigative Chamber shall make a decision after hearing the statement of the person who has been extradited, the requests of the Prosecutor General, and the defense arguments of the lawyer.

#### Article 592.- Non-suspensive Effect of the Request for Nullity

A request for nullity of extradition shall not suspend the prosecution proceedings currently being carried out against the extradited person.

#### Article 593.- Effect of Nullity of Extradition

If the extradition is declared void by a final decision of the Investigative Chamber, the prosecution proceedings currently in progress must be terminated. The extradited person shall be released and shall have the possibility to leave Cambodian territory freely.

However, after a period of 30 (thirty) days from the day of release, the extradited person may be re-arrested on Cambodian territory and may be subject to new prosecution proceedings for the acts that led to the extradition of this person.

#### Article 594.- Consent of the State that Surrendered the Resident to the Kingdom of Cambodia

When the Kingdom of Cambodia has obtained the extradition of a foreign resident and later receives a request for extradition from another foreign State, the Kingdom of Cambodia may consent to this request after obtaining the consent of the State that surrendered this resident to the Royal Government of Cambodia.

However, the above consent is not necessary if the foreign resident already had the possibility to leave Cambodian territory freely within a period of 30 (thirty) days.

The request shall be made through diplomatic channels. The request must contain supporting documents certifying that it is consistent with.

#### Article 596.- Provisions on the Execution of Certain Penalties

For all disputes related to the execution of penalties provided for in the Criminal Code, the court shall receive the complaint from the Prosecutor through an ordinary request. The Prosecution shall notify the relevant parties of the date of the hearing.

## CHAPTER 5: LEGAL ENTITY

#### Article 597.- Competence Regarding Legal Entities

For the prosecution, investigation, and trial of offenses committed by a legal entity, the competent authorities are as follows:

- The Prosecutor, the Investigating Judge, and the Court of First Instance of the place where the offense was committed;

- The Prosecutor, the Investigating Judge, and the Court of First Instance of the place where the legal entity has its head office.

#### Article 598.- Representation of a Legal Entity Before the Court

The prosecution shall be carried out against the legal entity through its legal representative at the time of the prosecution. The legal representative represents the legal entity in performing all procedural acts.

When the legal representative is unavailable, the Prosecutor may request the President of the Court of First Instance to appoint an agent to represent the legal entity.

When criminal prosecution is initiated for the same acts or for related acts against both a legal entity and its legal representative, the legal representative may request the President of the Court of First Instance to appoint an agent to represent the legal entity.

This request may be made through a written request.

#### Article 599.- Identity of the Representative of the Legal Entity

The person in charge of representing the legal entity must present his/her identity to the court that received the complaint. This presentation of identity must also be done in case of a change of the legal representative during the proceedings.

#### Article 600.- Compulsory Measures Applied to a Legal Entity

In the capacity of representative of the legal entity, this representative cannot be subject to any compulsory measures, other than those that can be applied to a witness.

#### Article 601.- Placing a Legal Entity Under Judicial Supervision

The investigating judge may place a legal entity under judicial supervision. The judge may declare the following obligations:

1- Deposition of a security, specifying the amount, the period for payment, and the modalities;

2- Prohibition from carrying out certain professional activities;

3- Placement under the supervision of the court for a period not exceeding 6 (six) months.

The decision to place [the legal entity] under court supervision results in the appointment of a court agent whose mission is specified by the investigating judge.

#### Article 602.- Summons and Notification to a Legal Entity

Summons and all decisions provided for in this Code shall be sent and notified to the legal entity through its legal representative, under the same conditions as provided for a natural person.

## TITLE 2: LOSS OF DOCUMENTS, INTERPRETATION AND RECTIFICATION OF DECISIONS

## CHAPTER 1: LOSS OF DOCUMENTS

#### Article 603.- Provisions Applicable in Case of Loss of Documents

The provisions of this Title shall apply in case of destruction, suppression, or loss of the following documents:

- Original court decisions; or

- Procedural documents.

#### Article 604.- Copies Replacing the Original Documents

If there is a copy of a court decision certified as a true copy of the original by the clerk or by another authority, that copy shall be considered as the original court decision. The person who possesses a certified true copy must surrender that document to the clerk upon the request of the President of the Court. A new certified true copy shall be provided to that person by the clerk.

If there is no certified true copy, a new decision must be made based on copies, minutes of the hearing, and documents contained in the case file.

#### Article 605.- Reconstruction of Procedural Documents

Procedural documents shall be reconstructed based on copies kept by the police or gendarmerie units, the parties, experts, the clerk, or other persons.

## CHAPTER 2: INTERPRETATION AND RECTIFICATION OF DECISIONS

#### Article 606.- Competence of the Court that Pronounced the Decision

Any difficulty in the interpretation of a court decision must be submitted for review to the court that pronounced that decision.

This court also has the competence to rectify purely clerical errors in its decisions. The court shall receive requests from the Prosecution on its own initiative or upon the complaint of the parties. The Prosecution shall notify the relevant parties of the date of the hearing. In all cases, the Prosecution must provide its opinion during the hearing.

#### Article One.-

#### Article 247 (Closing Order), Article 368 (Time Limit for Opposition), Article 372 (Opposition Filed by a Civil Party or Civil Liable Party), and Article 382 (Time Limit for Appeal by the Convicted Person, Civil Party, and Civil Liable Party) of the Code of Criminal Procedure are amended as follows:

**New Article 247.- Closing Order**

The investigating judge closes the investigation by a closing order. This order can be a referral order [to trial] or a non-suit order [dismissal].

If it is found that the act constitutes a felony, misdemeanor, or petty offense, the investigating judge shall decide to refer the charged person to the trial court. The order must describe the acts charged and the legal qualification of the offense.

The investigating judge issues a non-suit order in the following cases:

1- The act committed does not constitute a felony, misdemeanor, or petty offense;

2- The person who committed the act remains unknown;

3- There is insufficient evidence against the charged person;

4- The criminal action is extinguished.

A closing order must always be reasoned. The investigating judge is not bound to follow the final submission of the Prosecutor. The order can be a referral order for some acts and a non-suit order for other acts.

In cases where the investigation file involves multiple facts, the investigating judge may issue a closing order for those facts for which the investigation has been completed and continue the investigation for other facts. The investigating judge may also issue a non-suit order for a charged person for whom the investigation found no involvement in the commission or participation in the offense, and continue the investigation to identify the offenders.

The closing order must be notified within the shortest time to the Prosecutor, the charged person, and the civil party.

In cases where a non-suit order is issued because the identity of the charged person is unknown or in cases where the charged person has died, the investigating judge may request the criminal court by a referral order to decide on the confiscation of instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or properties if there is reasonable suspicion that such instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or properties are related to the offense. In this case, the criminal court shall decide on the confiscation by a judgment in a public hearing with the participation of the representative of the Prosecution and relevant persons, if any. The judgment deciding on the confiscation must be posted at the place where the court issued the judgment and published to the public through the press or audiovisual media.

Any person with a relevant interest or who claims to be the owner of the objects which are the subject of the confiscation has the right to appeal against such decision within 1 (one) month from the day of the pronouncement of the judgment if this person participated in the trial proceedings of the court, and within 2 (two) months from the day of the public publication through the press or audiovisual media if this person did not participate in the court proceedings. The modalities and procedure for this appeal shall follow the provisions of this Code.

**New Article 368.- Time Limit for Opposition**

The opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days, starting from:

- The day of notification of the judgment in absentia, when the judgment has been delivered to the convicted person in person; or

- The day on which the convicted person became certainly aware of the judgment, when the judgment was not delivered to the convicted person in person. The convicted person shall be considered to have certainly become aware of the judgment in case where the procedure for notification of the court decision was correctly implemented in accordance with...

...provisions provided for in Book 7 (Summons for Direct Referral to Trial, Summons and Notification), Title 4 (Notification of Court Decisions) of this Code.

**New Article 372.- Opposition Filed by a Civil Party or Civil Liable Party**

The civil party may also file an opposition against a judgment rendered in his/her absence. This opposition shall be limited to the civil parts of the judgment only.

The opposition must be filed within 15 (fifteen) days, starting from:

- The day of notification of the judgment in absentia, when the judgment has been delivered to the civil party in person; or

- The day on which the civil party became certainly aware of the judgment, when the judgment was not delivered to the civil party in person. The civil party shall be considered to have certainly become aware of the judgment in cases where the procedure for notification of the court decision was correctly implemented in accordance with the provisions provided for in Book 7 (Summons for Direct Referral to Trial, Summons and Notification), Title 4 (Notification of Court Decisions) of this Code.

The opposition must be made in writing at the clerk's office of the court that pronounced the judgment.

The civil party may be represented by a lawyer, a spouse, or a direct relative. The representative must have a power of attorney to file the opposition.

The provisions of this Article shall also apply to persons declared by the court to be civilly liable.

**New Article 382.- Time Limit for Appeal by the Convicted Person, Civil Party, and Civil Liable Party**

The appeal by the convicted person, the civil party, and the civil liable party must be filed within 1 (one) month.

When the judgment is rendered after an adversarial hearing, the time limit for appeal starts from the time of pronouncement of the judgment.

When the judgment is considered as rendered after an adversarial hearing, the time limit for appeal starts from the time the notification of the judgment was received, regardless of the method of notification.

When the judgment is rendered in absentia, the time limit for appeal starts from the time the period for filing an opposition has expired.
