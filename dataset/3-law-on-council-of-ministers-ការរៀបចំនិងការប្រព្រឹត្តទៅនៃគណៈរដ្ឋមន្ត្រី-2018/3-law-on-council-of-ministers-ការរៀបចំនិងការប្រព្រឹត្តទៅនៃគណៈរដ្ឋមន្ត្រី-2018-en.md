# Promulgate

The Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers which was adopted by the National Assembly on May 31, 2018 at its 10th session of the 5th legislature and which was reviewed by the Senate on its form and legal aspects in its entirety on June 11, 2018 at its 1st plenary session of the 4th legislature and which the Constitutional Council declared as constitutional in its decision No. 256/001/2018 CC.D dated June 15, 2018, the entire content of which is as follows:

# Law
# on
# Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

**Article 1 .–**
This law aims to ensure the efficiency of the work of the Council of Ministers, as the Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia, which is the executive organ as specified in the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 2 .–**
This law has the objective to determine the composition, competence and functioning of the Council of Ministers, as the Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 3 .–**
This law has the scope of application on the executive organ.

# Chapter 2
# Competence and Composition of the Council of Ministers

**Article 4 .–**
The Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia is the executive organ, having the competence to determine and implement national policies in accordance with the principles set forth in the Constitution. The Royal Government ensures the implementation of laws and leads the general affairs of the State, except for affairs that are within the competence of the legislative and judicial organs.

**Article 5 .–**
The Royal Government leads the general affairs of the State in accordance with its political program.

**Article 6 .–**
The Royal Government must protect the legality, ensure public order and security.

**Article 7 .–**
The Royal Government manages, leads, commands, and utilizes the public administration at all levels, including the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, National Police, and other armed forces in performing its activities.

**Article 8 .–**
The composition of the Royal Government consists of:

- Prime Minister

- Deputy Prime Ministers

- Senior Ministers

- Ministers.

**Article 9 .–**
The Prime Minister is a member of the National Assembly. Other members of the Royal Government may be chosen from among the members of the National Assembly or outside the National Assembly, but they must be members of a political party represented in the National Assembly. All members of the Royal Government shall be appointed by a Royal Decree after receiving a vote of confidence from the National Assembly.

**Article 10 .–**
The functions of the members of the Royal Government are incompatible with commercial activities, all types of businesses, or holding leadership, management, or board of director positions in companies or enterprises, and holding any other public office.

**Article 11 .–**
Upon the end of the legislature of the National Assembly or upon the change of the Royal Government, the outgoing Royal Government has the duty to lead only the daily affairs until a new Royal Government takes office.

**Article 12 .–**
The Prime Minister and members of the Royal Government in office shall receive functional allowances for performing their duties as determined by a Sub-decree.

# Chapter 3
# The Council of Ministers, Competence of the Prime Minister and Members of the Royal Government

**Article 13 .–**
The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the National Assembly for the general policies and activities of the Royal Government. The Prime Minister is the head of the Royal Government, leading and commanding all work activities of the Royal Government in all sectors.

**Article 14 .–**
The Prime Minister convenes and presides over the meetings of the Council of Ministers. The meetings of the Council of Ministers consist of plenary sessions and research sessions. Every meeting of the Council of Ministers must have minutes.

**Article 15 .–**
 The plenary session is a full meeting of the Council of Ministers to review and decide on all general affairs of the Royal Government.

Plenary sessions must be led by the Prime Minister.

The Prime Minister may invite relevant persons to participate in the meeting of the Council of Ministers as necessary.

The Prime Minister may assign a Deputy Prime Minister to lead a research session.

**Article 16 .–**
The Prime Minister may delegate the right to any member of the Royal Government to convene and preside over inter-ministerial meetings, but such meetings do not have the right to decide on affairs on behalf of the Royal Government.

**Article 17 .–**
The Prime Minister leads negotiations and signs agreements on trade, economic cooperation, culture, science, technology, national defense, national security, and other fields within the competence of the Royal Government. The Prime Minister may delegate the right to negotiate and sign to members of the Royal Government or any dignitary.

The forms and procedures for negotiating and signing these agreements shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

**Article 18 .–**
The Prime Minister signs Sub-decrees, Decisions, Regulations, and Circulars of the Royal Government. All legal norms of general application of the Royal Government shall be published in the Royal Gazette.

**Article 19 .–**
The Prime Minister has the right to request the National Assembly to convene extraordinary sessions. The Prime Minister submits draft laws to the National Assembly for review and adoption.

**Article 20 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to His Majesty the King to terminate the positions of members of the Royal Government by a Royal Decree.

**Article 21 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to the National Assembly to change or replace members of the Royal Government for a vote of confidence. After receiving the vote of confidence, those members shall be appointed by a Royal Decree.

**Article 22 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to His Majesty the King to appoint, change, or terminate the positions of Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State at the Office of the Council of Ministers and at various ministries and institutions by a Royal Decree.

**Article 23 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to His Majesty the King to appoint, change, or terminate the duties of high-ranking civil servants, military personnel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, and National Police officers as follows:

- Governor and Deputy Governors of the National Bank of Cambodia

- Secretary-Generals of Ministries, Director-Generals of Ministries, or equivalent positions

- Governors of the Capital and Provincial Boards of Governors

- Ambassadors or equivalent positions.

- Envoy Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary

- Commander-in-Chief, Deputy Commander-in-Chief and Joint Chief of Staff of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces

- Commissioner-General of the National Police.

The positions and frames of high-ranking civil servants, military personnel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces and National Police officers from the rank of General upward shall be appointed, changed and terminated by a Royal Decree upon the request of the Prime Minister, in accordance with the provisions in force.

**Article 24 .–**
The Prime Minister appoints, changes and terminates by a Sub-decree the high-ranking civil servants, military personnel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces and National Police officers who are not specified in Article 23, except for those mentioned in the second paragraph of Article 46 of this law.

**Article 25 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to grant honors and titles to individuals, dignitaries or philanthropists in accordance with the legal norms in force.

**Article 26 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to grant or may grant medals and decorations to civil servants, military personnel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, National Police officers, individuals, dignitaries or philanthropists in accordance with the legal norms in force.

**Article 27 .–**
The Prime Minister may submit requests to grant the honorary title of Advisor to the Royal Government to any dinitary by a Royal Decree.

**Article 28 .–**
In special cases or force majeure, the Prime Minister has the right to decide on expenditures outside the national budget to address necessary and urgent matters, and shall report for approval from the legislative organ in accordance with the provisions in force.

**Article 29 .–**
In the absence of the Prime Minister, one (1) Deputy Prime Minister shall take over the work as delegated by the Prime Minister.

**Article 30 .–**
The Deputy Prime Ministers are members of the Royal Government who have precedence over Senior Ministers and Ministers.

The Deputy Prime Ministers assist the Prime Minister in his work as delegated by the Prime Minister. For affairs related to the overall leadership of the Royal Government, without the approval of the Prime Minister, the Deputy Prime Minister has no right to decide.

**Article 31 .–**
Members of the Royal Government who lead and manage a ministry are called Ministers.

Members of the Royal Government who do not lead a ministry shall perform duties as determined by the Prime Minister.

**Article 32 .–**
The Prime Minister and all members of the Royal Government have their own Cabinet and have advisors, assistants, and secretaries as determined by the legal norms under the law.

**Article 33 .–**
Each member of the Royal Government is individually responsible to the Prime Minister and the National Assembly for his/her own acts.

The Prime Minister shall supervise the work, activities, acts and responsibilities of the members of the Royal Government, and has the right to remove and replace members of the Royal Government in accordance with the forms and procedures in force.

# Chapter 4
# Office of the Council of Ministers

**Article 34 .–**
The Office of the Council of Ministers is established to serve as the secretariat for the Royal Government to coordinate the daily work of the Council of Ministers under the leadership of the Prime Minister.

The Minister in charge of the Office of the Council of Ministers has the role to manage and organize the work of the Office of the Council of Ministers.

**Article 35 .–**
The Office of the Council of Ministers has a number of Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State as necessary.

The Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State perform duties as delegated by the Minister in charge of the Office of the Council of Ministers.

**Article 36 .–**
The Office of the Council of Ministers has one (1) Permanent Secretary of State, appointed by the Prime Minister upon the request of the Minister in charge of the Office of the Council of Ministers, to assist in the general daily work of the Office of the Council of Ministers.

The roles and duties of the Permanent Secretary of State shall be determined by the legal norms of the Royal Government.

**Article 37 .–**
The Office of the Council of Ministers may have advisors and assistants as necessary.

**Article 38 .–**
The organization and functioning of the Office of the Council of Ministers shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

# Chapter 5
# Establishment of Ministries

**Article 39 .–**
The establishment of a ministry shall be determined by law.

Each ministry shall implement its competence in accordance with the law establishing that ministry.

The organization and functioning of the ministry from the department level upward shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

The organization and functioning of the ministry below the department level shall be determined by a Proclamation (Prakas).

In case of a conflict of competence between two or more ministries, the Council of Ministers shall review and decide.

**Article 40 .–**
The Minister leads the work in the ministry and manages the civil servants and staff who are administrative agents under his/her authority.

**Article 41 .–**
The Minister has the duty to lead and manage the ministry and its subordinate units within the framework of competence determined by law.

**Article 42 .–**
When a Minister is absent or is temporarily unable to lead and manage the ministry, the Permanent Secretary of State as mentioned in Article 44 of this law shall take over the daily work of the ministry as Acting Minister until the presence of the concerned Minister. In case the Permanent Secretary of State cannot fulfill the role of Acting Minister, the Minister may appoint another Secretary of State as Acting Minister.

The Acting Minister is not a member of the Royal Government and shall exercise power as delegated by the Minister.

In case of necessary tasks according to the Constitution and laws requiring the Minister to fulfill the role, the Prime Minister shall appoint any member of the Royal Government to fulfill those tasks on behalf of the absent Minister.

The roles, duties, and powers of the Acting Minister shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

**Article 43 .–**
Each ministry has a number of Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State as necessary.

The Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State perform duties as delegated by the Minister of the concerned ministry.

**Article 44 .–**
Each ministry has one (1) Permanent Secretary of State, appointed by the Prime Minister upon the request of the Minister of the concerned ministry, to assist in the daily general work of the ministry.

The roles and duties of the Permanent Secretary of State shall be determined by the legal norms of the Royal Government.

**Article 45 .–**
Each ministry may have advisors and assistants as necessary.

**Article 46 .–**
The Minister has the right to submit requests for appointment, change and termination of the duties of civil servants under his/her authority in accordance with the forms and procedures in force, from the level of department director and equivalent upward and the rank of Colonel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces and the National Police.

The Minister has the right to appoint, change and terminate the duties of civil servants under his/her authority in accordance with the forms and procedures in force by a Proclamation (Prakas), from the level below department director and equivalent and rank below Colonel of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces and the National Police.

The Royal Government may issue a Sub-decree on the delegation of power to sub-national administrations in managing, organizing, using, appointing, changing and terminating the duties of civil servants of the ministry working within their jurisdiction.

**Article 47 .–**
The Minister has the right to issue Proclamations (Prakas), Decisions, Circulars and Instructions.

A Proclamation, Decision, Circular or Instruction of the ministry shall not contain matters that are not within the competence of that ministry, and shall not conflict with other legal norms of the Royal Government.

# Chapter 6
# Establishment of Institutions, Councils, Authorities, Committees and Various Mechanisms

**Article 48 .–**
The Royal Government may submit requests to establish or may establish institutions, councils, authorities, committees and various mechanisms as necessary, by law or by legal norms under the law.

**Article 49 .–**
Institutions, councils, authorities, committees and various mechanisms established in accordance with Article 48 of this law do not have their own separate framework of civil servants. Civil servants performing work in institutions, councils, authorities, committees and various mechanisms shall be selected from the framework of civil servants of the Office of the Council of Ministers and/or relevant ministries, unless otherwise specified in a separate law.

The head of an institution, council, authority, committee and various mechanism has the right to submit requests for appointment or has the right to appoint and change his/her own civil servants in accordance with the law and relevant legal norms.

Institutions, councils, authorities, committees and various mechanisms established in accordance with Article 48 of this law have the competence as determined in the law and/or relevant legal norms.

**Article 50 .–**
The Prime Minister may assign one (1) member of the Royal Government to lead and manage institutions, councils, authorities, committees and various mechanisms established in accordance with Article 48 of this law.

# Chapter 7
# Minister Attached to the Prime Minister, Secretary of State, Under-Secretary of State, Advisor and Assistant

**Article 51 .–**
The Prime Minister submits requests to His Majesty the King to appoint, change and terminate the positions of Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Advisors and Assistants whose rank is equivalent to Director-General and above.

**Article 52 .–**
Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Advisors to the Royal Government, Advisors and Assistants to the Prime Minister perform duties as delegated by the Prime Minister.

**Article 53 .–**
Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Secretaries of State and Under-Secretaries of State are not members of the Royal Government, nor are they permanent administrative agents.

When the Royal Government's mandate ends, or it resigns or is removed by the National Assembly, the Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Secretaries of State, Under-Secretaries of State, Advisors, Assistants and Secretaries as mentioned in Article 32, Article 37, Article 45 and Article 52 of this law shall also lose their positions.

**Article 54 .–**
Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Secretaries of State, Under-Secretaries of State, Advisors, Assistants and Secretaries in office shall receive functional allowances for performing their duties.

The functional allowances for Ministers Attached to the Prime Minister, Secretaries of State, Under-Secretaries of State, Advisors, Assistants and Secretaries shall be determined by the legal norms under the law.

# Chapter 8
# Transitional Provisions

**Article 55 .–**
All laws and legal norms related to the implementation of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers promulgated by Royal Kram No. 02/NS/94 dated July 20, 1994 and the Law on the Amendment to Article 28 of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0613/012 dated June 20, 2013...

... shall continue to be in force until new laws and legal norms are issued to replace them in accordance with the provisions of this law.

# Chapter 9
# Final Provisions

**Article 56 .–**
This law shall be implemented starting from the 6th legislature of the National Assembly.

**Article 57 .–**
The Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers promulgated by Royal Kram No. 02/NS/94 dated July 20, 1994, the Law on the Amendment to Article 28 of the Law on the Organization and Functioning of the Council of Ministers promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0613/012 dated June 20, 2013 and the Law on the Establishment of the Office of the Council of Ministers promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0196/09 dated January 24, 1996 are hereby repealed and replaced by this law.

**Article 58 .–**
Any provisions that conflict with this law are hereby repealed.

**Article 59 .–**
This law is declared as urgent.
