# Press Regime

### Chapter 1
### Rights and Freedoms of the Press

**Article 1.-**

This law determines the press regime and ensures freedom of the press and freedom of publication in accordance with Articles 31 and 41 of the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 2.-**

The press has the right to maintain the confidentiality of its information sources.

**Article 3.-**

In order to maintain the independence of the press, pre-publication censorship shall be prohibited.

**Article 4.-**

The publication of official information such as statements, meetings, minutes of meetings, or any reports, etc., shall not be punishable if such publication is consistent with the full factual situation or a correct summary of the facts.

Official information which is the subject of this article refers to:

1- Statements, meetings, minutes of meetings, or reports of the National Assembly, including various committees or commissions of the National Assembly, except in cases where the National Assembly holds a secret meeting as provided in Article 88 of the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2- Statements, meetings, minutes of meetings, or reports of the executive branch, including statements by ministers and various officials.

3- All aspects of judicial proceedings, including all procedures, except in the following cases:

- The court prohibits opening to the public

- Provisions of Article 16 of this law concerning the evaluation of individuals in judicial proceedings

- Cases that are still under judicial investigation.

**Article 5.-**

1- General Principles

The press has the right to access information held by the government, except for information that causes:

- Harm to national security

- Harm to relations with other countries

- Violation of the rights of individuals, including civil servant files, medical files, [other] files, and other confidential official documents

- Disclosure of commercial or financial secrets obtained from individuals or entities in accordance with any law, and disclosure of information related to regulations or oversight by financial institutions

- Prejudice to any person's right to a fair trial

- Danger to officials who must enforce the law or fulfill their mission.

2- Requesting Information

Requests for information must be made in writing and must clearly specify the information requested to the various institutions.

The competent official in charge of that institution must respond to the request within a maximum of thirty days (30 days). If the request is rejected in whole or in part, the reasons for the rejection must be clearly stated in writing.

### Chapter 2
### Responsibility of the Press

**Article 6.-**

Journalists have the right to form journalist associations. Such journalist associations must be independent. Each journalist association shall adopt its own statutes that are not contrary to the laws in force, with the consensus of its members through a vote. The leaders of a journalist association who are members of the board of directors must be democratically elected by vote.

**Article 7.-**

Each journalist association shall draft a code of ethics to be implemented within its association, which journalists have the obligation to respect, following these essential first-step principles:

1- Respect the truth and respect the public's right to know the truth.

2- Publish information in good faith, as well as provide accurate and appropriate commentary and criticism based on legal justice.

3- Report only facts known from the original source. Journalists must not suppress important information or falsify various documents.

4- Use fair and honest methods to seek information, photographs, and various documents.

5- Make corrections to any published information that is inaccurate and leads to misunderstanding.

6- Avoid publishing information that incites discrimination based on race, color, gender, language, belief, religion, opinion or political tendency, national origin, social status, wealth, or other status.

7- Respect the right to privacy and the freedom of individual private life.

8- Strictly respect Khmer grammar rules in writing articles.

9- Prohibit the publication of obscene articles or images and images of violence.

10- Prohibit the violation of the right to a fair trial for any individual, as provided in the Constitution of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

11- The following points shall be considered as serious professional violations of a journalist:

- Plagiarism of ideas or works of others and claiming them as one's own.

- Deliberate deception, such as deceitfully writing to mislead readers into unfair suspicion of any individual, group, or party.

- Calumny, defamation, and insult.

- Accepting bribes in any form or extortion of money in exchange for publishing or not publishing any information.

**Article 8.-**

Before starting the distribution of a press outlet, the employer or editor-in-chief must submit a copy of an application (1 copy) to the Ministry of Information to state their identity. The Ministry shall issue a receipt to the press outlet upon receiving the completed application.

In cases where the director of the publication is a member of the National Assembly or an institution with immunity, the press outlet must appoint a co-director of the publication.

An employer or editor-in-chief who fails to comply with this formality shall be punished with a fine from five hundred thousand Riels (500,000 Riels) to one million five hundred thousand Riels (1,500,000 Riels). In case of recidivism, the fine shall be doubled.

**Article 9.-**

The application form to be filled out by the employer or editor-in-chief must include, among others:

- Identity card of the press outlet.

- Name and address of the employer or editor-in-chief.

- Name and address of the printing house.

- Criminal record.

Any change to the above information must be notified to the Ministry of Information five days (5 days) in advance, except in cases of force majeure.

**Article 10.-**

Any person who considers that any article, even if written implicitly, or any cartoon or photograph in any press outlet which is false and causes damage

to their honor and dignity, has the right to demand that press outlet to make a correction or has the right to reply and file a complaint for defamation, calumny, or insult concerning the impact on their honor and dignity. The correction or reply must be published within a maximum of seven days (7 days) or in the next issue after receiving the demand for correction or reply.

In cases involving public figures, any assertion or excessive speculation that the journalist publishes directly or indirectly which is false, with the intent to destroy that public figure, is calumny prohibited by law.

If the court finds that any publication is false based on the complaint of a civil plaintiff, the court may order that press outlet to:

- Publish a correction, or

- Pay compensation for damages, or

- Both publish a correction and pay compensation for damages.

The correction article that the journalist has the obligation to publish must be published on the same page and with the same font size as the article considered to have caused damage to the honor and dignity of the person.

Furthermore, the court may also impose a fine from one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels) to five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels).

Additionally, in cases where there is a judgment on any act provided in the above paragraph, the court may order the posting of its decision at various locations specified by the court, with the accused being responsible for the costs, and the court may order the publication of this judgment in one or more press outlets with the accused being responsible for all costs, limited to a maximum of one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels).

**Article 11.-**

The press shall not publish anything that affects public order by directly inciting one or more persons to commit violence.

The commission of the offense provided above shall be punished with a fine from one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels) to five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels).

The victim of the above-mentioned act has the right to appear as a civil plaintiff before the court.

In all cases, the court must examine the direct link between the inciting article and the violence. An article that is more than three months old (3 months) cannot be used as a basis for prosecution.

**Article 12.-**

The press shall not publish or reproduce information that affects national security and political stability.

The employer, editor-in-chief, or author of the article may be punished with a fine from five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels) to fifteen million Riels (15,000,000 Riels), without prejudice to punishment under criminal law.

The Ministry of Information and the Ministry of Interior have the right to seize the offending article and the issue of the press outlet that published the offending article. The Ministry of Information has the right to suspend the publication for a period not exceeding thirty days (30 days) and send the case to court.

**Article 13.-**

The press shall not publish or reproduce false information that is an insult or contempt for national institutions. Such publication may be punished with a fine from two million Riels (2,000,000 Riels) to ten million Riels (10,000,000 Riels).

**Article 14.-**

The press shall not publish anything that affects the good traditions of national society, such as:

- Indecent or inappropriate words, such as "Ah", "Ming", etc.

- Words that clearly describe sexual acts.

- Cartoons or photographs that show genitals or nudity, except for the purpose of education.

- Images that insult or compare humans to animals.

Violation of this article may be punished with a fine from one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels) to five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels).

**Article 15.-**

Unless authorized by the court, the press shall not publish news, photographs, or drawings that allow readers to identify the name and characteristics of:

- Parties in civil cases related to paternity, marriage, divorce, or child guardianship.

- Juveniles under eighteen years of age (18 years) in civil or criminal disputes.

- Women who are victims of physical assault or rape.

In cases where the person concerned or their guardian agrees in writing, the press may publish such stories, except where such publication affects the court's investigation.

Any person whose rights are violated by the press as mentioned above has the right to file a complaint with the court for damages. Violation of the above provisions shall be punished with a fine from one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels) to five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels).

**Article 16.-**

Commercial advertisements may be considered fraudulent if the publication contains excessive exaggeration about the quality or characteristics of a product or service, leading to consumers' misunderstanding of those qualities or characteristics.

Such commercial advertisements are prohibited, but the press outlet that publishes the fraudulent news is not legally responsible unless the press outlet continues to publish the information after receiving a written prohibition from the court or a competent ministry.

Conversely, the owner of the commercial advertisement is responsible before the law and the court if there is a complaint from any individual or consumer association that has suffered damage from such fraudulent commercial advertisement.

Fraudulent commercial advertising may be punished with a fine from one million Riels (1,000,000 Riels) to five million Riels (5,000,000 Riels).

### Chapter 3
### Foreign Control

**Article 17.-**

Any person, whether a natural person or a legal entity, may not own or possess more than two (2) Khmer-language press outlets in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 18.-**

The total number of Khmer-language press outlets owned by foreigners shall not exceed twenty percent (20%) of the total number of Khmer-language press outlets published in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Khmer-language press outlets owned by foreigners that have already been authorized to publish cannot be closed due to a decrease in the total number of Khmer-language press outlets.

### Chapter 4
### Legal Deposit

**Article 19.-**

The editor-in-chief or publication director must deposit two copies (02 copies) of each issue of their publication at the National Library and six copies (06 copies) at the Ministry of Information. This deposit must be made within fifteen days (15 days) for publications in Phnom Penh and forty-five days (45 days) for locations elsewhere, starting from the date of publication.

Violation of the above provisions shall be sanctioned by the Ministry of Information with a transitional fine from thirty thousand Riels (30,000 Riels) to three hundred thousand Riels (300,000 Riels).

### Chapter 5
### Final Provisions

**Article 20.-**

Acts committed by an employer, editor-in-chief, or author of an article that violate criminal law shall be punished according to criminal law. However, no person shall be arrested or imprisoned for expressing an opinion.

**Article 21.-**

All previous provisions related to the press are hereby repealed.
