# Chapter 1: General Provisions

### Article 1.-

The purpose of this Law is to facilitate the urgent settlement of commercial disputes that occur in the Kingdom of Cambodia in accordance with the voluntary consensus of the disputing parties in order to protect the rights and interests of these disputing parties and to promote economic development.

This Law shall not affect the legal validity of other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia in which some disputes can be reconciled or settled through other proceedings and other disputes cannot be reconciled.

### Article 2.-

Definitions and rules of interpretation for terms used in this Law:

a- “Arbitration” is a dispute settlement using an arbitral tribunal and may be within or outside the framework of a permanent settlement institution.

b- “Arbitral Tribunal” is a single arbitrator or a group of arbitrators.

c- “Court” is an institution or organization of the judicial system of a country.

d- “Arbitration Agreement” is an agreement between disputing parties to submit to arbitration all disputes or certain disputes which have arisen or which may arise between them in respect of a defined legal relationship, whether contractual or not.

e- Except for Article 36, whenever a provision of this Law leaves the disputing parties free to determine a certain issue, such freedom includes the right of the disputing parties to authorize a third party, including an institution, to make that determination.

g- Apart from Article 33 (1) and Article 40 (1) of this Law, where a provision of this Law refers to a claim, it also applies to a counter-claim, and where it refers to a defense, it also applies to a defense to such counter-claim.

h- An arbitration is “international” if:

h.1- the disputing parties to an arbitration agreement have, at the time of the conclusion of that agreement, their places of business in different countries; or

h.2- one of the following places is situated outside the country in which the disputing parties have their places of business:

- the place of arbitration if determined in, or pursuant to, the arbitration agreement;

- any place where a substantial part of the obligations of the commercial relationship is to be performed or the place with which the subject-matter of the dispute is most closely connected.

# Chapter 2: Arbitration Agreement

### Article 7.-

An “arbitration agreement” is an agreement by the disputing parties to submit to arbitration all or certain disputes which have arisen or which may arise between them in respect of a defined legal relationship, whether contractual or not. An arbitration agreement may be in the form of an arbitration clause in a contract or in the form of a separate agreement.

The arbitration agreement shall be in writing. An agreement is in writing if it is contained in a document signed by the disputing parties or in an exchange of letters, telex, telegrams or other means of telecommunication which provide a record of the agreement, or in an exchange of statements of claim and defense in which the existence of an agreement is alleged by one disputing party and not denied by another. The reference in a contract to a document containing an arbitration clause constitutes an arbitration agreement provided that the contract is in writing and the reference is such as to make that clause part of the contract.

Year 6, No. 37, Week 3, May 19, 2006, Royal Gazette, Page 3005

### Article 8.-

A court before which an action is brought in a matter which is the subject of an arbitration agreement shall, if a disputing party so requests, refer the disputing parties to arbitration unless it finds that the agreement is null and void, inoperative or incapable of being performed.

Where an action referred to in paragraph (1) of this Article has been brought, arbitral proceedings may nevertheless be commenced or continued, and the Court shall stay its proceedings in favor of the arbitral tribunal while awaiting the decision of the arbitral tribunal.

### Article 9.-

It is not incompatible with an arbitration agreement for a disputing party to request, before or during arbitral proceedings, from a court an interim measure of protection and for a court to grant such measure.

## Chapter 5

## Jurisdiction of Arbitral Tribunal

### Article 24.-

The jurisdiction of the arbitral tribunal is determined as follows:

1- The arbitral tribunal may rule on its own jurisdiction, including any objections with respect to the existence or validity of the arbitration agreement. For that purpose, an arbitration clause which forms part of a contract shall be treated as an agreement independent of the other terms of the contract. A decision by the arbitral tribunal that the contract is null and void shall not entail ipso jure the invalidity of the arbitration clause.

2- A plea that the arbitral tribunal does not have jurisdiction shall be raised not later than the submission of the statement of defense. A party is not precluded from raising such a plea by the fact that he has appointed, or participated in the appointment of, an arbitrator. A plea that the arbitral tribunal is exceeding the scope of its authority shall be raised as soon as the matter alleged to be beyond the scope of its authority is raised during the arbitral proceedings. The arbitral tribunal may, in either case, admit a later plea if it considers the delay justified.

3- The arbitral tribunal may rule on a plea referred to in paragraph

(2) of this Article, either as a preliminary question or in an award on the merits. If the arbitral tribunal rules as a preliminary question that it has jurisdiction, any party may request, within thirty (30) days after having received notice of that ruling, the Court (Commercial Court or Appeal Court or Supreme Court) as referred to in Article 6 of this Law, to decide the matter, which decision shall be subject to no appeal; while such a request is pending, the arbitral tribunal may continue the arbitral proceedings and make an arbitral award.

### Article 25.-

Unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, the arbitral tribunal may, at the request of a party, order any party to take such interim measure of protection as the arbitral tribunal may consider necessary in respect of the subject-matter of the dispute. The arbitral tribunal may require any party to provide appropriate security in connection with such measure.

## Chapter 6

## Conduct of Arbitral Proceedings

### Article 26.-

The disputing parties shall be treated with equality and each party shall be given a full opportunity of presenting its case, including the right to appoint any individual as its representative.

### Article 27.-

The disputing parties are free to agree on the procedure to be followed by the arbitral tribunal in conducting the proceedings.

Year 6, No. 37, Week 3, May 19, 2006, Royal Gazette, Page 3014

Failing such agreement, the arbitral tribunal may, subject to the provisions of this Law, conduct the arbitration in such manner as it considers appropriate. The power conferred upon the arbitral tribunal includes the power to determine the admissibility, relevance, materiality and weight of any evidence.

### Article 28.-

The disputing parties are free to agree on the place of arbitration. Failing such agreement, the place of arbitration shall be determined by the arbitral tribunal having regard to the circumstances of the case, and the agreement of the disputing parties shall also be required.

Notwithstanding the provisions of paragraph (1) of this Article, the arbitral tribunal may, unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, meet at any place it considers appropriate within the Kingdom of Cambodia for consultation among its members, for hearing witnesses, experts or the disputing parties, or for inspection of goods, other property or documents.

### Article 29.-

Unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, the arbitral proceedings in respect of a particular dispute commence on the date on which a request for that dispute to be referred to arbitration is received by the respondent.

### Article 30.-

The disputing parties are free to agree on the language or languages to be used in the arbitral proceedings. Failing such agreement, the arbitral tribunal shall determine the language or languages to be used in the proceedings.

The arbitral tribunal may order that any documentary evidence shall be accompanied by a translation into the language or languages agreed upon by the disputing parties or determined by the arbitral tribunal.

### Article 31.-

Within the period of time agreed by the disputing parties or determined by the arbitral tribunal, the claimant shall state the facts supporting his claim, the points at issue and the relief or remedy sought, and the respondent shall state his defense in respect of these particulars, unless the disputing parties have otherwise agreed as to the required elements of such statements. The disputing parties may submit with their statements all documents they consider to be relevant or may add a reference to the documents or other evidence they will submit.

Unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, either party may amend or supplement his claim or defense during the course of the arbitral proceedings, unless the arbitral tribunal considers it inappropriate to allow such amendment having regard to the delay in making it.

### Article 32.-

The disputing parties shall be given sufficient advance notice of any hearing and of any meeting of the arbitral tribunal for the purposes of inspection of goods, other property or documents.

All statements, documents or other information supplied to the arbitral tribunal by one party shall be communicated to the other party. Also any expert report or

### Article 33.-

Unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, if, without showing sufficient cause:

1- The claimant fails to communicate his statement of claim in accordance with paragraph (1) of Article 31 of this Law, the arbitral tribunal shall terminate the proceedings;

2- The respondent fails to communicate his statement of defense in accordance with paragraph (1) of Article 31 of this Law, the arbitral tribunal shall continue the proceedings without treating such failure in itself as an admission of the claimant's allegations;

3- Any party fails to appear at a hearing or to produce documentary evidence, the arbitral tribunal may continue the proceedings and make the award on the evidence before it.

### Article 34.-

## Chapter 7

## Making of Arbitral Award and Termination of Proceedings

### Article 36.-

In making the arbitral award, the arbitral tribunal shall:

1- Decide the dispute in accordance with such rules of law as are chosen by the disputing parties as applicable to the substance of the dispute. Any designation of the law or legal system of a given State shall be construed, unless otherwise expressed, as directly referring to the substantive law of that State and not to its conflict of laws rules;

2- Apply the law it considers appropriate in the case where the disputing parties fail to designate the applicable law;

3- Decide ex aequo et bono or as amiable compositeur only if the disputing parties have expressly authorized it to do so;

4- Decide in accordance with the terms of the contract and shall take into account the usages of the trade applicable to the transaction.

Year 6, No. 37, Week 3, May 19, 2006, Royal Gazette, Page 3018

### Article 37.-

In arbitral proceedings with more than one arbitrator, any decision of the arbitral tribunal shall be made by a majority of all its members.

### Article 38.-

Before starting the formal arbitral proceedings, the arbitral tribunal shall discuss with the disputing parties to seek the possibility of a voluntary settlement of the dispute:

1- If the disputing parties agree to a voluntary settlement, the arbitral tribunal shall assist the parties in accordance with the appropriate procedures;

2- If the disputing parties settle the dispute before the start of or during the formal arbitral proceedings, the arbitral tribunal shall terminate the proceedings. If requested by the disputing parties, the arbitral tribunal may record the settlement in the form of an arbitral award;

3- An arbitral award based on a settlement shall be made in accordance with the provisions of Article 39 of this Law and shall state that it is an arbitral award. Such an award has the same status and effect as any other award on the merits of the case.

### Article 39.-

The arbitral award shall be made in writing and shall be signed by the arbitrator or arbitrators. In arbitral proceedings with more than one arbitrator, the signatures of the majority of all members of the arbitral tribunal shall suffice, provided that the reason for any omitted signature is stated.

2- The award shall state the reasons upon which it is based, unless the disputing parties have agreed that no reasons are to be given or

settlement on agreed terms in accordance with Article 38 of this Law.

3- The award shall state the costs of the arbitration to be borne by the disputing parties, including the fees for the arbitrators and any other expenses pre-deposited in accordance with the procedures agreed by the disputing parties or, in the absence of such agreement, as determined by the arbitral tribunal. The award may also state the reasonable costs of legal consultations to be reimbursed to the winning party, if the disputing parties have so agreed or if the arbitral tribunal considers it appropriate.

4- The award shall state its date and the place of arbitration as determined in accordance with paragraph (1) of Article 28 of this Law. The award shall be deemed to have been made at that place.

5- After the award is made, a copy signed by the arbitrators in accordance with paragraph (1) of this Article shall be delivered to each disputing party.

### Article 40.-

The arbitral proceedings are terminated by the final award or by an order of the arbitral tribunal in accordance with paragraph (2) of this Article.

The arbitral tribunal shall issue an order for the termination of the arbitral proceedings when:

1- The claimant withdraws his claim, unless the respondent objects thereto and the arbitral tribunal recognizes a legitimate interest on his part in obtaining a final settlement of the dispute;

2- The disputing parties agree on the termination of the proceedings;

3- The arbitral tribunal finds that the continuation of the proceedings has for any other reason become unnecessary or impossible.

### Article 41.-

The correction and interpretation of the award shall be subject to the following conditions:

b- If so agreed by the disputing parties, a party, with notice to the other party, may request the arbitral tribunal to give an interpretation or a specific clarification of a point or part of the award. If the arbitral tribunal considers the request to be justified, it shall make the correction or give the interpretation or clarification within thirty (30) days of receipt of the request. The interpretation or clarification shall form part of the award.

2- The arbitral tribunal may correct any error of the type referred to in paragraph (1)a of this Article on its own initiative within thirty (30) days of the date of the award.

3- Unless otherwise agreed by the disputing parties, a party, with notice to the other party, may request, within thirty (30) days of receipt of the award, the arbitral tribunal to make an additional award as to claims presented in the arbitral proceedings but omitted from the award. If the arbitral tribunal considers the request to be justified, it shall make the additional award within thirty (30) days.

## Chapter 8: Recourse Against Award, Recognition and Enforcement of Awards

### Section 1: Competent Authority for Recourse, Recognition and Enforcement

### Article 42.-

Any decision on recourse against an award, and on the recognition and enforcement of an award, shall fall within the competence of the Court of Appeal of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Article 43.-

The Supreme Court is the court of final instance having the competence to adjudicate any appeal by a disputing party who is dissatisfied with a decision of the Court of Appeal within fifteen (15) days.

a.1- A party to the arbitration agreement referred to in Article 7 was under some incapacity, or the said agreement is not valid under the law to which the parties have subjected it or, failing any indication thereon, under the law of the Kingdom of Cambodia; or

a.2- The party making the application was not given proper notice of the appointment of an arbitrator or of the arbitral proceedings or was otherwise unable to present his case; or

a.3- The award deals with a dispute not contemplated by or not falling within the terms of the submission to arbitration, or contains decisions on matters beyond the scope of the submission to arbitration, provided that, if the decisions on matters submitted to arbitration can be separated from those not so submitted, only that part of the award which contains decisions on matters not submitted to arbitration may be set aside; or

a.4- The composition of the arbitral tribunal or the arbitral procedure was not in accordance with the agreement of the parties, unless such agreement was in conflict with a provision of this Law from which the parties cannot derogate, or, failing such agreement, was not in accordance with this Law; or

b- The Court of Appeal or the Supreme Court finds that:

b.1- The subject-matter of the dispute is not capable of settlement by arbitration under the law of the Kingdom of Cambodia; or

b.2- The award is in conflict with the public policy of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

3- An application for setting aside may not be made after thirty (30) days have elapsed from the date on which the party making that application had received the award or, if a request had been made under Article 41, from the date on which that request had been disposed of by the arbitral tribunal.

4- The Court of Appeal or the Supreme Court, when asked to set aside an award, may, where appropriate and so requested by a party, suspend the setting aside proceedings for a period of time determined by it in order to give the arbitral tribunal an opportunity to resume the arbitral proceedings or to take such other action as in the arbitral tribunal's opinion will eliminate the grounds for setting aside.

### Section 3: Recognition and Enforcement of Awards

### Article 45.-

An arbitral award, irrespective of the country in which it was made, shall be recognized as binding and, upon application in writing to the Court of Appeal, shall be enforced subject to the provisions of this Section and of Article 46.

The party relying on an award or applying for its enforcement shall supply the duly authenticated original award and the original arbitration agreement referred to in Article 7 or a duly certified copy thereof. If the award or agreement is not made in the Khmer language, the party shall supply a duly certified translation thereof into the Khmer language.
