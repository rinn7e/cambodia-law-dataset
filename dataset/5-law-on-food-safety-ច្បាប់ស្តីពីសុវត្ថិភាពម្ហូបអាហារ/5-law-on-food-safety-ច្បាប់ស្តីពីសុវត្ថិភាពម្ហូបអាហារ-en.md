# Law on Food Safety

## Chapter I
## General Provisions

### Article 1 .-
This law defines the framework and mechanisms for managing and ensuring the safety, quality, hygiene, and legitimacy of food at all stages of the food production chain to provide health protection and food safety for consumers and to ensure honest food trade.

### Article 2 .-
This law has the scope of application for the management of food quality and safety, covering all stages of the food production chain and food businesses in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Article 3 .-
Key terms used in this law are defined in the glossary, which is the annex of this law.

## Chapter II
## Competent Institutions

### Article 4 .-
The Ministry of Commerce has the competence to lead and coordinate the management of food quality and safety in cooperation with ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

## Chapter III
## Food Technical Regulations, Food Requirements, and Inspection Principles

### Article 5 .-
The preparation of food technical regulations shall be based on:

- Cambodian food standards, codes of conduct, and guidelines approved by the National Standards Council

- International food standards, codes of conduct, and guidelines issued by international and regional food safety organizations in case Cambodian food standards, codes of conduct, and guidelines have not yet been defined

- Actual requirements and must respond to human health conditions, industry, commerce, and national economic conditions.

Food technical regulations shall be approved by the National Standards Council at the request of the competent ministries and institutions concerned.

In case the national standards codes of conduct have not yet defined food technical regulations, the Ministry of Commerce shall coordinate with competent ministries and institutions concerned to define food technical regulations.

Defining food requirements is the competence of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, and must comply with the food technical regulations as stated in Article 5 above.

Defining food requirements by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall comply with the following principles:

- (a) Food must be safe, quality, and suitable for human consumption.

- (b) Food must not be imported, produced, processed, or sold in a fraudulent or deceptive manner.

- (c) Consumers shall receive sufficient information about food, such as origin, ingredients, price, nutrition and calories, instruction for use, and storage.

- (d) Food must comply with hygiene, packaging, and labeling requirements.

- (e) Food classified as high risk must be properly registered.

- (f) Storage or food storage location must comply with conditions for maintaining food safety and quality.

Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall publicly disseminate all updated information on food requirements by all means.

### Article 6 .-
Food quality and safety inspection shall be conducted by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety at all stages of the food production chain according to their respective roles and duties:

- (a) Based on food requirements to be determined in accordance with Article 5 above, and when necessary, based on international food standards, codes of conduct, and guidelines as stated in Article 5 of this law.

- (b) Based on risk analysis, on the basis of available scientific evidence and implemented in a manner that is independent, objective, and transparent.

- (c) Proportional to that risk.

- (d) Not more restrictive than necessary for inspection.

- (e) Not creating unnecessary obstacles to trade, industry, and economic processes.

- (f) Not discriminating between domestic or foreign products or producers, or among other countries when conditions are identical or similar.

### Article 7 .-
In response to food safety emergencies, ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall issue additional restrictions on food requirements to address that emergency situation. Such additional restrictions shall have a specific duration and be reviewed within a specified period. When the emergency situation no longer exists, ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall lift the additional restrictions on food requirements according to their competence.

### Article 8 .-
Food business activities must have a permit from competent institutions determined by law and regulations in force. Every issuance of a permit must comply with food requirements.

Any enterprise that has conducted a food business shall be subject to tax procedures and receive incentives and exemptions in accordance with the law and regulations in force.

### Article 10 .-
Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall determine groups of food business types and establish different food requirements based on:

- (a) Type and size of business location.

- (b) Type of business operation.

- (c) Type of food.

The implementation of the above food requirements shall be determined by the decision of the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall maintain a list and update all food business operators after providing/receiving permits from the competent institutions.

### Article 11 .-
Food business operators have the following primary obligations:

- Ensure that food is safe, quality, and suitable for human consumption in accordance with food requirements.

- Resolve food safety issues when they occur in their businesses.

- Immediately notify the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety about issues occurring to food.

- Cooperate with ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety in implementing measures to avoid or reduce risks caused by products of the relevant food business, as well as traceability, food recall, and food withdrawal.

### Article 12 .-
Food business operators must immediately notify the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety about issues occurring to food and must cooperate with ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety in implementing measures to avoid or reduce risks caused by products of the relevant food business, as well as traceability, food recall, and food withdrawal.

### Article 13 .-
A specific food producer, processor, or packager must comply with the provisions of this law, as well as food technical regulations as stated in Article 5 of this law, and food requirements as stated in Article 6 of this law, and regulations determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

Food producers, processors, or packagers shall not:

- (a) Produce, process, or package food that is unsafe.

- (b) Produce, process, or package food by counterfeiting trademarks, or without date of expiration, or with labeling not complying with labeling requirements as stated in the Cambodian Standard on the Labeling of Prepackaged Food.

- (c) Produce, process, or package food without identifying the identity of the food producer.

- (d) Produce, process, or package food in violation of the provisions of this law and other regulations determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

### Article 14 .-
Wholesale and distribution business operators must comply with the provisions of this law, as well as food requirements and regulations determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

In wholesale and distribution business operations, wholesale or distribution business operators shall not:

- (a) Supply, wholesale, or distribute food after its expiration date.

- (b) Store, wholesale, or distribute food in violation of the safety instructions of the producer.

- (c) Supply, wholesale, or distribute food that is unsafe or counterfeits trademarks.

- (d) Supply, wholesale, or distribute food whose producer identity cannot be verified.

- (e) Supply, wholesale, or distribute food knowing that it is unsafe.

- (f) Store, cooperate, or deposit in violation of the provisions of this law and regulations determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

### Article 15 .-
Retail business operators must comply with the provisions of this law, as well as food requirements and regulations determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

In retail business operations, retail business operators shall not:

- (a) Display food for sale after its expiration date.

- (b) Cooperate or store food in unsanitary conditions.

- (c) Improperly label in any form.

- (d) Display food for sale whose producer and/or distributor identity cannot be verified.

- (e) Sell food knowing that it is unsafe or counterfeits trademarks.

### Article 16 .-
Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety have the right to trace and recall food according to types of food business that affect human health:

- (a) Trace backward step-by-step from the person who supplied animals, raw materials, or other ingredients for food production, or substances intended or expected to be incorporated into food.

- (b) Trace forward step-by-step to buyers who are distributors, other than the final consumer.

- (c) Identify lots at the receiving place, food production site, reports of food produced from animals or raw materials, and other ingredients incorporated into food or sent to the production site.

Conditions, formats, and procedures for traceability and food recall shall be determined by sub-decree.

### Article 17 .-
Every food business location must comply with food hygiene conditions determined in food requirements and/or in decisions of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, as well as other regulations related to food hygiene.

### Article 18 .-
All prepackaged food for sale must have labeling as determined in the food requirements and in decisions of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, as well as other regulations related to labeling as stated in the Cambodian Standard on the Labeling of Prepackaged Food.

All such labels must:

- (a) Have legible information.

- (b) Be written in Khmer language, at least for points related to food safety and consumer protection; and when the original label is not in Khmer language, additional Khmer labeling can be used on top instead of relabeling.

- (c) Define detailed information that can be prescribed, including nutritional information.

- (d) Not have any information or image that contradicts the information on the label or any prescribed labeling requirements, or must not mislead or confuse consumers in any manner.

- (e) Be readable or not obscured by outer packaging, if any.

Formats and conditions for labeling shall be determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

### Article 19 .-
All kinds of advertisement, in whatever form, that are deceitful, exaggerated, or present any false claim, or mislead or cause confusion regarding the true nature of food quality and safety are prohibited, such as:

- (a) True nature of the product.

- (b) Identity, type, brand, source, characteristics, appearance, or nutrition.

- (c) Quality, ingredients, quantity, method of processing/production, and production date.

- (d) Expiration date, instructions, and conditions for use.

- (e) Sales methods, availability of products, and price.

- (f) Warranties and other claims that exceed the truth.

The Ministry of Commerce shall cooperate with relevant ministries and institutions to determine food product advertising requirements.

### Article 20 .-
The Ministry of Commerce shall establish a public response system related to food quality and safety issues, food labeling, or complaints related to food businesses, or other food safety issues on the market.

### Article 21 .-
Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall arrange to have reference laboratories, including official laboratories, to conduct analysis in accordance with the purpose of this law.

Conditions, formats, and procedures for reference laboratories and official laboratories shall be determined by sub-decree.

## Chapter IV
## Food Inspection

### Article 22 .-
Food inspection shall be conducted by food inspection officers of the Ministry of Commerce and/or by inspection officers of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, at all stages of the production chain according to their respective competence, in accordance with the law and regulations in force.

To promote food quality and safety, ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall:

- (a) Establish a food inspection system based on risk assessment studies.

- (b) Determine a risk classification system for food businesses and assign specific business types under that classification system.

Formats and procedures for food inspection shall be determined by joint-proclamation between ministers of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

### Article 23 .-
Food inspection officers must obtain the status of judicial police officers to inspect offenses stated in this law in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

Formats and procedures for granting the status of judicial police officers to food inspection officers of the Ministry of Commerce or inspection officers of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety shall be determined by joint-proclamation between the Minister of Justice and the Minister of Commerce or ministers of the relevant ministries/institutions.

### Article 24 .-
Food inspection officers as stated in Article 22 of this law who perform duties within the premises and locations of food businesses must comply with the Code of Criminal Procedure and have the right to request assistance from local authorities at all levels and/or other relevant competent authorities.

### Article 25 .-
Food inspection officers must keep information found during inspection confidential, unless authorized by the head of the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, or required by court to disclose such information.

## Chapter V
## Import of Food

### Article 26 .-
Food import must comply with food requirements stated in this law, as well as relevant regulations in force.

Food import may require the attachment of certificates issued by competent authorities of the exporting country, in accordance with risk management principles complying with laws and regulations in force.

Import procedures for food, which are required for customs clearance, documents, and import conditions, shall be determined by sub-decree.

### Article 27 .-
In case it is necessary for imported food to undergo analysis to verify compliance with food requirements, samples must be taken for analysis by official laboratories as stated in Article 21 of this law.

### Article 28 .-
Imported food that does not comply with this law and relevant regulations in force shall be decided to be dealt with as follows:

- (a) Required to be re-exported.

- (b) Required to be re-processed, including re-labeling and sending for official inspection again.

- (c) Destroyed.

All relevant expenses shall be borne by the importer.

## Chapter VI
## Food Circulated on the Market

### Article 29 .-
Food inspection officers must conduct inspection and traceability on the market for high-risk products and for products that have received information that they present risks, on the basis of information confirming the risk, and must be proportional to the level of risk present in that food.

In case of necessity, food inspection officers must take samples on the market for analysis in laboratories, record the results, and take measures according to further procedures.

### Article 30 .-
Food inspection officers shall decide on measures to deal with food on the market that does not comply with food requirements prescribed in the law and in other regulations related to food as follows:

- (a) Allow correction/rectification.

- (b) Require re-processing.

- (c) Seize and destroy, in case correction or re-processing cannot be done.

All relevant expenses shall be borne by the food owner.

## Chapter VII
## Export of Food

### Article 31 .-
Food export must comply with the requirements of the importing country in accordance with multilateral, regional, and bilateral agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party.

## Chapter VIII
## Service Fees

### Article 32 .-
Service fees under the framework of the implementation of this law shall be determined by joint-proclamation between the minister of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

## Chapter IX
## Penal Provisions

### Article 33 .-
Sanctions under this law include administrative sanctions and criminal sanctions.

Administrative sanctions include:

- Written warning

- Suspension or revocation of permit, certificate, or license

- Closure of business.

Criminal sanctions include:

- Transaction fine (interim fine)

- Judicial fine

- Imprisonment.

The written warning, suspension or revocation of permit, certificate, or license, and closure of business for food business operators are the competence of the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety that issued those permits, and/or competent institutions determined by law and regulations in force.

The transaction fine is the competence of food inspection officers. Payment of the transaction fine shall extinguish the public prosecution.

In case the offender refuses to pay the transaction fine, food inspection officers have the competence to build a case file and send it to court.

Procedures for transaction fines shall be determined by joint-proclamation between the minister of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety and the Minister of Justice.

Payment of transaction fines, management of transaction fine receipts, and the collection of revenues from transaction fines shall be determined by joint-proclamation between the minister of ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 34 .-
A transaction fine of 500,000 (five hundred thousand) Riels to 8,000,000 (eight million) Riels shall be imposed on any food business operator who commits any of the following acts:

- (a) Operates a food business without a permit.

- (b) Prepares, stores, cooperates, or sells food in unsanitary conditions.

- (c) Fails to immediately notify the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety about issues occurring to food.

- (d) Fails to establish and implement a traceability system as determined in this law.

- (e) Produces, processes, or packages food without identifying the identity of the producer and/or food distributor.

- (f) Produces, processes, or packages food without an expiration date.

- (g) Supplies, wholesales, distributes, or displays food for sale after its expiration date.

- (h) Fails to comply with hygiene rules determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety.

- (i) Fails to provide information on the content certifying the quality and safety of food to be advertised.

- (j) Fails to comply with instructions and corrections of food inspection officers.

In case of having already received a transaction fine once, and continuing to commit the offense as stated in paragraph 1 above, a double transaction fine of the first transaction fine shall be imposed.

In case of continuing to commit the same offense as stated in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of Article 34 of this law, food inspection officers shall make a report to the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety to decide on issuing a written warning, suspending or revoking the permit, certificate, or license.

### Article 35 .-
Imprisonment of 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels shall be imposed on any food business operator of factories and small and medium enterprises who supplies, wholesales, distributes, or displays food for sale after its expiration date.

### Article 36 .-
A transaction fine of 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels shall be imposed on any food business operator who fails to comply with food requirements prescribed in Article 6 of this law.

In case of having already received a transaction fine once, and continuing to commit the offense as stated in paragraph 1 above, a double transaction fine of the first transaction fine shall be imposed.

In case of continuing to commit the same offense as stated in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of Article 36 of this law, food inspection officers shall make a report to the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety to decide on issuing a written warning, suspending or revoking the permit, certificate, or license.

### Article 37 .-
Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, a transaction fine of 5,000,000 (five million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels shall be imposed on any food business operator who advertises or causes to advertise food falsely, misleadingly, or confusingly regarding the true nature of food.

In case of having already received a transaction fine once, and continuing to commit the offense as stated in Article 37 of this law, a double transaction fine of the first transaction fine shall be imposed.

In case of continuing to commit the same offense as stated in paragraph 1 and 2 of Article 37 of this law, food inspection officers shall make a report to the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety to decide on issuing a written warning, suspending or revoking the permit, certificate, or license.

### Article 38 .-
Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, a transaction fine of 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels shall be imposed on any food business operator who labels food with false, misleading, or counterfeit characteristics regarding the true nature of food.

In case of having already received a transaction fine once, and continuing to commit the offense as stated in Article 38 of this law, a double transaction fine of the first transaction fine shall be imposed.

In case of continuing to commit the same offense as stated in paragraph 1 and 2 of Article 38 of this law, food inspection officers shall make a report to the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety to decide on issuing a written warning, suspending or revoking the permit, certificate, or license.

### Article 39 .-
Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and a fine of 200,000,000 (two hundred million) Riels to 400,000,000 (four hundred million) Riels shall be imposed on any person who produces, processes, packages, distributes, or sells food knowing that such food contains toxic substances or hazardous substances exceeding the permitted limit, leading to human health damage.

Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, when this act causes serious harm to consumer health and safety, the punishment shall be imprisonment from 5 (five) years to 10 (ten) years.

Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, when this act causes permanent disability or loss of life of the consumer, the punishment shall be imprisonment from 7 (seven) years to 15 (fifteen) years.

### Article 40 .-
Legal entities may be declared criminally responsible under the conditions stated in Article 42 (Criminal Responsibility of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code for offenses stated in Articles 34, 35, 36, 37, 38, and 39 of this law.

Legal entities shall be subject to one or more of the following additional penalties:

- 1- Dissolution in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 170 (Dissolution of Legal Entities and Liquidation of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code.

- 2- Placement under court supervision in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 171 (Placement under Court Supervision of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code.

- 3- Prohibition from conducting one or more activities in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 172 (Prohibition of Activity) of the Criminal Code.

- 4- Exclusion from public procurement in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 173 (Exclusion from Public Procurement) of the Criminal Code.

- 5- Prohibition from making a public appeal for savings in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 174 (Prohibition of Making Public Appeal for Savings) of the Criminal Code.

- 6- Confiscation of objects or funds that are the subject of the offense in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 178 (Confiscation of Ownership, Sale, and Destruction of Confiscated Objects) and Article 179 (Confiscation and Third-Party Rights) of the Criminal Code.

- 7- Confiscation of proceeds and assets generated from the offense in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 178 (Confiscation of Ownership, Sale, and Destruction of Confiscated Objects) and Article 179 (Confiscation and Third-Party Rights) of the Criminal Code.

- 8- Publication of the sentencing decision in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 180 (Publication of Decision) of the Criminal Code.

- 9- Dissemination of the sentencing decision in written press or broadcasting by all audio-visual means in accordance with the procedures determined by Article 181 (Dissemination of Decision by Means of Audiovisual Communication among others) of the Criminal Code.

### Article 41 .-
Food inspection officers who abuse their power in contradiction to the purpose of this law, resulting in harm to the rights or physical integrity, or causing damage to the property of others, shall be responsible for their acts in accordance with the laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Without prejudice to other criminal penalties, food inspection officers shall be subject to administrative sanctions in case they commit any of the following acts:

- (a) Seizing food without any reason other than the provisions stated in this law.

- (b) Disclosing any information obtained during the performance of their duties under this law, unless authorized by the head of the ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety, or required by court to disclose that information, or authorized by separate laws or regulations in force.

## Chapter X
## Transitional Provisions

### Article 42 .-
Other laws and regulations related to the quality, safety, and suitability of food that do not contradict this law shall continue to be in force until new laws and regulations are enacted to replace them in accordance with the provisions of this law.

## Chapter XI
## Final Provisions

### Article 43 .-
Any provisions that contradict this law shall be abrogated.

## Annex of the Law on Food Safety
## Glossary

- 1- **Processing** refers to any activity that alters or changes the original form of a product.

- 2- **Advertising** refers to any display in writing, image, audio-visual means, or other means made to publish, directly or indirectly promote the sale, food service provision, or any substance considered as food.

- 3- **Risk assessment study** refers to scientific health impact assessment caused by biological, chemical, and physical hazards, divided into four stages:
  - 1. Hazard identification
  - 2. Hazard characterization
  - 3. Exposure assessment
  - 4. Risk characterization.

- 4- **Packaging** refers to the activity of packaging food in accordance with requirements to ensure quality and safety of the final food product for consumers.

- 5- **Food chain** refers to all stages of food production, starting from initial production to handling and sale of food, starting from production farm to place of consumption.

- 6- **Suitability of food** refers to food that is natural, hygienic, safe, and free from toxic substances or pathogens.

- 7- **Food business location** refers to buildings, bicycles, or other vehicles, whether permanent or temporary, situated on land and water of the attached place, which are used for production, packaging, transport, handling, service provision, storage, or sale of any type of food.

- 8- **Food business operator** refers to the owner of a food business or a person authorized to operate a food business or a person responsible for a food business, in accordance with the laws and regulations of the Kingdom of Cambodia, such as producers, processors, packagers, wholesalers, distributors, and retailers.

- 9- **Treatment** refers to coloring, decoloring, addition of flour, polishing, coating, mixing, storing, adding substances, soaking, causing reaction with any substance, heating, or sterilization.

- 10- **Traceability** refers to the activity of tracing the circulation of food through specific stages of production, processing, and distribution.

- 11- **Hazardous substance** refers to chemical substances used exceeding the permitted limit of national or international standards, having the potential to cause negative effects on health.

- 12- **Food inspection officer** refers to an officer who has obtained the status of judicial police officer and is appointed in accordance with Article 22 of this law.

- 13- **Food** refers to any product or substance, whether fully processed, semi-processed, or unprocessed, for human consumption, including beverages, chewing gum, and any substances used in the production, preparation, or treatment of food, excluding cosmetics, tobacco products, or products used as medicine.

- 14- **Street food** refers to ready-to-eat food or food prepared immediately for sale on streets, markets, and other public places.

- 15- **Vehicle** refers to all means used for transporting food from one place to another.

- 16- **Sell** refers to activities such as offering for sale, advertising, storing, stocking, displaying, transmitting, depositing, sending, delivering for sale, exchanging, or disposing of to any person for any consideration or otherwise.

- 17- **Food requirements** refers to criteria determined by ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety that must absolutely be met.

- 18- **Certification** refers to the procedure by which official certification bodies and officially recognized bodies provide written assurance or equivalent assurance that food products or any food inspection systems comply with requirements.

- 19- **Certificate** refers to document attached to food issued by competent institutions, such as food safety certificates, health certificates, phytosanitary certificates, certificates of analysis, or other documents complying with food requirements.

- 20- **Substance** refers to solid, liquid, or gaseous matter.

- 21- **Food additive** refers to substances generally not consumed as food and normally not applied as ingredients for food production, whether that additive has nutritional value or not. Those additives are intentionally used in food for technological purposes in the process of production, manufacture, preparation, treatment, packing, packaging, transport, or storage of such food to result or be reasonably expected to result (directly or indirectly) in the food or by-products of that food becoming components of the food or affecting the characteristics of the food. However, food additives do not include contaminants or substances added to food to maintain or promote nutritional quality.

- 22- **Food safety** refers to the assurance in the process of ensuring that food does not cause harm to consumers when it is prepared and consumed in accordance with its intended use.

- 23- **Packaging** refers to objects for putting or wrapping food, in part or in whole, and baskets, canisters, wrapping drums, bottles, sacks, or other containers, whether they have covers or not.

- 24- **Food standard** refers to specific product standards and general standards, including codes of conduct and guidelines. This food standard applies to all food, whether fully processed, semi-processed, or fresh, for distribution to consumers.

- 25- **Unsanitary condition** refers to conditions or arrangements that can cause contamination or lead to harm or danger to the health of consumers.

- 26- **Labeling** refers to labels, brands, trademarks, diagrams, or written descriptions, printing, stencil printing, marking, highlighting, or embossing on or attached to containers or packaging of food.

- 27- **Exporter** refers to a person who is the owner, sender, receiver, agent, or representative who owns or obtains rights by any means to store or oversee food exported from the Kingdom of Cambodia.

- 28- **Importer** refers to any person, including the owner, sender, receiver, agent, or representative who owns or obtains rights by any means to store or oversee food to be imported into the Kingdom of Cambodia.

- 29- **Food business** refers to any business activity, including street food businesses, even on a small scale, that must operate at production sites, workshops, preparation, treatment, packing, packaging, transport, handling, service provision, storage, or sales related to food, whether for profit or not.

- 30- **Food business enterprise** refers to any activity directly related to the preparation, storage, and service of food within a food business location.

- 31- **Technical regulation** refers to a document laying down product characteristics or related processes and production methods, including administrative provisions that can be applied, compliance with which is mandatory. A technical regulation may include or deal exclusively with terminology, symbols, packaging, marking, or labeling requirements as they apply to a product, process, or production method.

- 32- **Food hygiene** refers to all conditions and measures necessary to ensure safety and suitability of food at all stages of the food chain.

- 33- **Official laboratory** refers to a laboratory that tests food products, ingredients, processed samples, and relevant environmental samples for analysis of physical, chemical, and microbiological parameters, and refers to state laboratories managed by ministries or government institutions determined by specific functions and scopes.

- 34- **Reference laboratory** refers to a laboratory that tests food products, ingredients, processed samples, and relevant environmental samples for analysis of physical, chemical, and microbiological parameters, and refers to state or private laboratories with specialized analytical expertise determined in specific scopes and evaluated by accreditation bodies.

- 35- **Ministries and institutions related to food quality and safety** refers to the Ministry of Commerce, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, the Ministry of Industry, Science, Technology and Innovation, the Ministry of Tourism, the Ministry of Health, and the Ministry of Economy and Finance, as well as other relevant competent ministries and institutions determined by laws and regulations in force.
