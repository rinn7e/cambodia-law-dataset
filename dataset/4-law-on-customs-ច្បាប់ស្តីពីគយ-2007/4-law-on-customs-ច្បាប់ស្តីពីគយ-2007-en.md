# Promulgate

The Law on Customs which was adopted by the National Assembly on June 22, 2007, during the 6th session of its 3rd legislature, and which was reviewed and approved by the Senate without any modification on July 10, 2007, during the 3rd plenary session of its 2nd legislature, the full text of which is as follows:

# Law on Customs

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

**Article 1.-**

The objectives of this law are:

- To provide the right to manage, inspect, and collect duties, taxes, and other fees on exported or imported goods;

- To provide the right to regulate the movement, storage, and transit of exported or imported goods;

- To promote the prevention and suppression of offenses and smuggling;

- To participate in implementing the international trade policy of the Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia;

- To promote the implementation of international standards and best practices related to customs control and trade facilitation.

The Customs and Excise Administration is the institution responsible for the management and implementation of the provisions of this law. This institution is under the management of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

**Article 2.-**

The customs territory includes the land, water, airspace, and islands of the Kingdom of Cambodia. The Royal Government has the right to establish various free zones which are not subject to full or partial customs procedures.

**Article 3.-**

This law shall apply:

- Uniformly throughout the customs territory;

- Equitably without any exception for any person.

- Without granting immunity or exemption from the application of this law for exported and imported goods in the name of the State.

**Article 4.-**

Customs operations shall be conducted throughout the customs territory, including customs belts along the land borders and coastal areas, under the conditions specified in this law.

The customs belt consists of a maritime zone and a land zone:

a- The maritime zone extends from the coast outward to a distance of twenty (20) kilometers.

b- The land zone extends:

- From the coast inland to a distance of twenty (20) kilometers, and land to a distance of twenty (20) kilometers from the banks of rivers, streams, and canals that flow into the sea up to the first customs office from the sea, and a radius of twenty (20) kilometers around this office.

- Along the land border inland to a distance of twenty (20) kilometers.

To facilitate the suppression of offenses, the Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas:

a- To extend the depth of the land zone up to sixty (60) kilometers for a specific location and duration.

b- To define a customs belt up to three (3) kilometers around customs offices that are not within the belts specified in paragraphs 1, 2, and point a of paragraph 3 of this Article.

Notwithstanding the provisions of paragraph 1 of this Article, the Royal Government may issue a Sub-decree:

a- To appoint customs officers to perform their duties as specified in this law outside the customs territory, based on technical grounds and in compliance with agreements

international agreements, treaties or conventions to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party.

b- To allow customs officers of other countries to perform their duties as specified in the laws of those countries related to the importation and exportation of goods within the customs territory, based on technical grounds and in compliance with international agreements, treaties or conventions to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party.

Customs formalities may only be completed at customs offices, except as otherwise determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

The Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance determines the establishment, operation, and closure of customs offices.

The working hours of customs offices shall be determined by a decision of the Director of Customs and Excise Administration. This decision shall be posted at each customs office.

**Article 5.-**

All imported or exported goods are subject to the application of the provisions of this law.

All goods entering or leaving the customs territory shall be subject to import or export duties and taxes as specified in the Customs Tariff. The formalities for the organization and implementation of the Customs Tariff shall be detailed in a Sub-decree.

In cases related to national interest, including emergencies, the Royal Government may issue an order to suspend fully or partially duties and taxes on goods for the needs of the people, or to suspend the exportation or importation of certain types of goods.

**Article 6.-**

Preferential customs duties may be granted if specified in commitments, agreements, treaties, or international conventions to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party. Preferential customs duties resulting from obligations under these commitments, agreements, treaties, or international conventions shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

**Article 7.-**

The Royal Government may, by Sub-decree, determine necessary measures including increasing tax rates to protect Cambodian producers when domestic producers suffer from a surge in imports or from subsidies provided by the governments of other countries for their exports to Cambodia, or from the dumping of goods on the Cambodian market.

**Article 8.-**

The Royal Government may, by Sub-decree, determine certain types of goods to be prohibited or restricted for exportation or importation for any of the following purposes:

- National security;

- Public order and relevant standards of decency and morality;

- Protection of health and life of humans, animals, or plants;

- Protection of national heritage of artistic, historical, or archaeological value;

- Conservation of natural resources;

- Compliance with any prevailing laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia;

- Fulfillment of obligations under the United Nations Charter.

To combat offenses and smuggling, the Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining sensitive goods or goods subject to high taxes as special goods for the purposes of this law, and may impose additional controls and restrictions on the transportation, movement, storage, and possession of these goods.

**Article 9.-**

Unless the context otherwise requires, for the purpose of this law:

- "Ad Valorem duty" refers to a duty calculated based on the customs value of the goods.

- Specific duty refers to a duty calculated based on the unit, weight, volume, or quantity of the goods.

- "Bonded warehouse" refers to any building, place, or area that meets the conditions set by the Customs Administration for the purpose of storing, processing, displaying for sale, or other related purposes where goods are temporarily suspended from duties and taxes.

- "Means of transport" refers to any ship, vessel, aircraft, or other means used to transport people or goods.

- Smuggling refers to the exportation or importation of goods outside of customs offices, as well as the violation of legal provisions or regulations related to the exportation or importation, possession, and transportation of goods within the customs territory.

- "Country of origin" refers to the country where goods were produced or processed in accordance with the criteria specified for the purpose of applying the Customs Tariff, consistent with quantitative restrictions or other cases related to trade.

- "Customs or Customs Administration" refers to the Customs and Excise Administration of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, including customs officers responsible for the management, oversight of the implementation of customs laws, the collection of duties, taxes, and other fees, as well as the responsibility for the implementation of other laws and regulations related to the exportation or importation, movement, and storage of goods.

- "Customs Broker" refers to a person who is legally authorized to perform

the business of completing customs clearance formalities directly with the Customs Administration on behalf of other persons.

- "Customs clearance" refers to the completion of necessary customs formalities to obtain authorization for goods to be used domestically, exported, or placed under another customs regime.

- "Customs clearance place" refers to a place under the management and supervision of the Customs Administration.

- "Customs control" refers to procedures determined by the Customs Administration for the transportation, movement, and storage of exported or imported goods.

- "Customs declaration" refers to a statement made for the purpose of exportation or importation in the form and manner specified in this law.

- "Customs examination" refers to the physical inspection of goods or documents by the Customs Administration to verify the type, origin, condition, quantity, and value of goods as declared in the customs declaration.

- "Customs formalities" refers to operations to be carried out by relevant persons and by the Customs Administration for the purpose of complying with this law and other laws that the Customs Administration is responsible for managing and implementing.

- "Customs bonded manufacturing area" refers to a factory under the supervision of customs where imported goods are temporarily exempt from duties and taxes for use in the manufacturing process.

- "Customs office" refers to a customs administrative unit authorized to manage, control customs formalities, taxes, or other locations approved for the purpose of these legal provisions.

- "Customs officer" refers to a person authorized to manage, control, and implement this law.

- "Customs value" or "value" related to imported goods refers to the customs value of those goods determined in accordance with Article 21 of this law.

- "Declarant" refers to a person who makes a declaration to customs in their own name or on behalf of another.

- "Documents" refer to documents in any form, whether or not they are signed or initialed, or provided electronically by relevant persons, and include:

- Any form written on material;

- Information recorded, transmitted, or stored by various means such as recorders, computers, or other equipment and materials produced from this information;

- Labels or other marks or writing attached to any object or part of an object;

- Books, maps, plans, graphs, or drawings;

- Photos, films, tapes, or other equipment containing image data that can be reproduced.

- "Duties and taxes" refers to duties, additional duties, taxes, fees or other charges levied on goods in accordance with any provisions of this law or other laws and regulations implemented and managed by customs.

- "Free zone" refers to an area established by the Royal Government where imported goods are not subject to duties and taxes under specific conditions.

- "Goods" refers to all movable properties.

- "Domestic consumption" refers to the free movement of imported goods within the customs territory after customs clearance.

- "Importer" refers to a person who imports goods by themselves or through another. An importer is also considered to be the receiver of goods, the owner, the future owner, or the person with the right to dispose of the property of those goods before they cease to be subject to customs control.

- "Importation" related to goods refers to the arrival of goods in the customs territory by any means, whether legal or illegal, from a point or place outside the customs territory.

- "Release" refers to the authorization by customs to make goods available to the person concerned after customs formalities have been completed.

- "Security" refers to a financial guarantee or other financial instrument used to ensure the payment of duties, taxes, and other potential fees under the provisions of this law.

- "Special goods" refer to goods determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance under paragraph 2 of Article 8 of this law.

- "Legal routes" refer to land, rail, water, or any other routes determined by customs for the transportation of exported or imported goods under customs management and control.

- "Temporary admission" refers to a customs procedure that allows goods to be imported into the customs territory with full or partial relief from duties and taxes. These goods must be imported for a specific purpose and must be intended for re-exportation within a specified period without having undergone any change, except normal depreciation due to use.

- "Transit" refers to a customs procedure that allows the transportation of goods from one customs office to another under customs control.

- "Transshipment" refers to a customs procedure that allows the transfer of goods from an importing means of transport to an exporting means of transport under customs control within the same customs office, which is both the office of importation and exportation.

- "Direct transport" refers to the international transportation of goods from the country of origin

from the country of origin to the country of importation without passing through any other country except the country of origin and Cambodia, unless such transit is justified by geographical or technical reasons, and provided that the goods have not entered into trade or consumption and have not undergone any operation other than unloading or operation to keep the goods in good condition in the transit country.

- "Person" refers to a natural person or a legal entity.

# Chapter 2
# Importation and Exportation

## Section 1
## Importation

**Article 10.-**

All imported goods shall be reported at a customs office or other location as determined by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas determining the time, manner, requirements, documents, circumstances, and exemptions related to the reporting of imported goods.

**Article 11.-**

All imported goods shall be reported at the nearest customs office along a legal route:

a- In the case of goods held by any person arriving in Cambodia as part of their personal luggage, they must be reported by that person;

b- In the case of goods imported by courier or postal service, they must be reported by the person who exported them to Cambodia.

c- In the case of goods imported other than those in paragraphs a and b of point 1 of this Article, which are loaded on a means of transport including a military means of transport arriving in Cambodia, they must be reported by the person in charge of those means of transport.

d- In the case of goods imported by any other means, they must be reported by the person acting on behalf of the importer.

Every person reporting goods under the provisions of this Article shall:

a- Answer truthfully all questions asked by customs officers regarding their goods;

b- Present the goods in the manner determined by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration for inspection when required by customs officers.

**Article 12.-**

No person shall remove goods from any means of transport upon arrival in Cambodia before the goods have been reported to customs in accordance with this law, except in cases where the safety of the means of transport, the goods, or the persons on board or other third parties is threatened in an emergency condition, as specified in a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

**Article 13.-**

The Customs Administration may authorize goods as specified in Article 10 of this law to be removed from a customs clearance place before the payment of duties, taxes, and other fees after customs formalities have been completed and under customs control for the purpose of:

- Temporary storage;

- Bonded warehouse storage;

- Continued transportation within or through the customs territory to a certain destination along a legal route and within a period determined by customs, including transit, transshipment, or transportation to a port of entry or between customs offices and bonded warehouses.

**Article 14.-**

Goods as specified in Article 10 of this law may be authorized by the Customs Administration to be released after customs formalities have been completed, including the payment of duties, taxes, or other fees and the provision of security for goods for:

- Importation for domestic consumption;

- Temporary importation;

- Use under duty suspension regime.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas determining the conditions for goods that may be authorized for release before the payment of duties, taxes, and other fees.

**Article 15.-**

Imported goods may be authorized by the Customs Administration for release under the temporary admission regime if at the time of importation it can be shown that those goods will be re-exported.

Temporarily imported goods shall remain under customs control until the conditions of the temporary admission regime have been fully met.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining regulations for managing imported goods under these temporary admission regimes, including the determination of time limits, security requirements, inspection, and other restrictions.

## Section 2
## Exportation

**Article 16.-**

All types of exported goods shall be reported at a customs office or other location as determined by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining the time, manner, requirements, documents, circumstances, and certain exemptions related to the reporting, movement, storage, and transportation of goods to be exported.

Every person reporting goods under the provisions of this Article shall:

a- Answer truthfully all questions asked by customs officers regarding their goods;

b- Present the goods in the manner determined by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration for inspection when required by customs officers.

**Article 17.-**

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining the conditions under which the Customs Administration may authorize the temporary exportation of goods from the customs territory for repair, modification, or further processing, as well as the formalities for the duties and taxes to be paid on those goods upon re-importation.

# Chapter 3
# Classification, Origin, and Customs Valuation

**Article 18.-**

The classification, origin, and customs value of imported goods shown in the customs declaration shall be declared in accordance with the following rules:

a- Classification and Origin

- The importer or their agent completing the customs declaration of imported goods shall declare the classification and origin of those goods for the calculation of duties and taxes. The Customs Administration shall verify the classification and origin of imported goods.

- The importer or their agent completing the customs declaration of imported goods shall do so in accordance with the provisions of Articles 51 and 52 of this law.

- The Customs Administration may require the importer or their agent to present evidence in the form of a declaration or a set of necessary documents required by prevailing laws and regulations regarding the accuracy of the declaration, and may refuse to release the goods until such evidence is provided.

- Within three (3) years from the date of registration of the customs declaration, following an audit, investigation, inspection, or re-examination of imported goods, the Customs Administration may re-determine the classification or origin declared by issuing a notice. This notice shall state the reasons for the re-determination of the classification or origin.

- When an audit, investigation, inspection, or re-examination conducted under this Article reveals any irregular activities, a notice may be issued only for the goods under investigation within a period not exceeding ten (10) years from the date of registration of the customs declaration.

- All additional duties and taxes, other fees, and penalties resulting from the issuance of this notice shall be paid to the Customs Administration.

- Any refund of duties, taxes, fees, and penalties overpaid by the importer or their agent resulting from the issuance of a notice shall be refunded by the Customs Administration.

b- Customs Valuation

- The importer or their agent completing the customs declaration shall declare the customs value of imported goods as specified in the provisions of Article 21 of this law for the assessment of duties and taxes. The Customs Administration shall verify the declared customs value of imported goods.

- The importer or their agent completing the customs declaration of imported goods shall do so in accordance with the provisions of Articles 51 and 52 of this law.

- The importer or their agent is responsible for the accurate declaration of the customs value for the payment of duties and taxes, and shall disclose all information, invoices, and other documents to enable the Customs Administration to verify and correctly determine the customs value of imported goods.

- The Customs Administration may require the importer or their agent to present evidence in the form of a declaration or a set of necessary documents required by prevailing laws and regulations regarding the accuracy of the declaration, and may refuse to release the goods until such evidence is provided.

- Within three (3) years from the date of registration of the customs declaration, following an audit, investigation, inspection, or re-examination of imported goods, the Customs Administration may re-determine the declared customs value by issuing a notice. This notice shall state the reasons for the re-determination of the customs value.

- The Customs Administration may adjust the declared customs value if the declared customs value is not in accordance with the provisions of Article 21 of this law or is incorrect for other reasons.

- When an audit, investigation, inspection, or re-examination conducted under this Article reveals any irregular activities, a notice may be issued only for the goods under investigation within

a period not exceeding ten (10) years from the date of registration of the customs declaration.

c- Voluntary declaration of additional duties, taxes, or fees to be paid

- The importer or their agent may voluntarily declare to the Customs Administration additional duties, taxes, or fees that have not been paid on imported goods without being subject to penalties or sanctions, provided that such declaration is made to the Customs Administration within one (1) year from the date of registration of the customs declaration.

d- Exporters and Exported Goods

- The provisions of paragraphs a and c of this Article shall also apply to exported goods.

**Article 19.-**

For the purpose of exportation and importation, all goods shall be classified and duties and taxes shall be calculated in accordance with the Customs Tariff, unless exempted by this law or other prevailing laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas determining regulations related to the classification of goods.

**Article 20.-**

For importation, the collection of duties and taxes shall be implemented in accordance with the origin of the goods.

The origin of natural products is the country where those products were harvested or extracted from the soil.

Industrial products manufactured in a single country without incorporating raw materials from other countries originate from the country where those industrial products were manufactured.

The procedures to be implemented by the Customs Administration to verify the origin of goods produced in a country using products obtained from the harvest or extraction

from the soil or manufactured in another country shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Imported goods shall receive preferential treatment regarding origin only if correct evidence of origin and direct transport from the country of origin to the country of importation are provided. The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining the conditions under which evidence is or is not required.

The exporting country is the country from which the goods were sent directly to the customs territory.

**Article 21.-**

The customs value of imported goods shall be determined in accordance with the following rules:

a- The customs value of imported goods shall be the transaction value, meaning the price actually paid or payable for the goods when sold for export to the Kingdom of Cambodia, in accordance with the provisions of a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance as specified in paragraph g of this Article.

b- If the customs value of imported goods cannot be determined under the provisions of paragraph a of this Article, the customs value shall be the transaction value of identical goods.

c- If the customs value of imported goods cannot be determined under the provisions of paragraphs a and b of this Article, the customs value shall be the transaction value of similar goods.

d- If the customs value of imported goods cannot be determined under the provisions of paragraphs a, b, and c of this Article, the customs value shall be based on the deductive method.

e- If the customs value of imported goods cannot be determined under the provisions of paragraphs a, b, c, and d of this Article, the customs value shall be based on the computed method.

f- The order of application of paragraphs d and e of this Article may be reversed upon the request of the importer.

g- If the customs value of imported goods cannot be determined under the provisions of paragraphs a, b, c, d, and e of this Article, the customs value shall be determined using reasonable means consistent with the principles and provisions of paragraphs a, b, c, d, and e of this Article, based on existing data available in the customs territory under specific conditions.

h- The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas determining all matters related to customs valuation.

**Article 22.-**

The customs value of exported goods shall be the value of the goods at the point of exportation.

This customs value shall be determined by adding the transportation costs and all expenses to be paid for the export operations up to the border, excluding export taxes, internal taxes, and similar charges that the exporter has paid and are shown in the invoice.

**Article 23.-**

The customs value of imported and exported goods shall be declared in Riels.

If the customs value is expressed in a currency other than Cambodian currency, the exchange rate to be applied shall be the rate determined by the National Bank of Cambodia. However, for exchange rates not specified in the exchange rate table of the National Bank of Cambodia, the Customs Administration may determine them.

The exchange rate to be applied shall be the exchange rate used on the date of registration of the customs declaration.

The exchange rate shall be published by the Customs Administration.

**Article 24.-**

The importer or their agent who is dissatisfied with the reasons or accuracy of the re-determination of classification, origin, or customs value provided in the notice as specified in the provisions of paragraphs a and b of Article 18 of this law, may appeal against this decision in writing to the Director of Customs and Excise Administration within thirty (30) days from the date the declarant received the notice of re-determination of classification, origin, or customs value.

In the case where the customs value is the subject of a protest, the goods shall be authorized to be released from customs without payment of duties and taxes, provided that the importer provides sufficient security to ensure the payment of duties and taxes.

The Director of Customs and Excise Administration shall make a decision on the appeal as specified in point 1 of this Article within sixty (60) days after the appeal is received. Otherwise, the appeal shall be deemed to have been accepted, and the security shall be returned.

Any person who objects to the decision of the Director of Customs and Excise Administration as specified in point 1 of this Article may lodge a written protest with the Customs Tariff Committee.

The organization and functioning of the Customs Tariff Committee shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

The exporter or importer has the right to appeal to a competent court against the decision of the Customs Tariff Committee within thirty (30) days from the date of receipt of the notice of such decision.

# Chapter 4
# Exemption, Partial Exemption, and Refund of Duties and Taxes

**Article 25.-**

Goods entering the customs territory for transit or transshipment shall not be subject to import duties and taxes.

**Article 26.-**

Exemption from import duties and taxes shall be granted for the importation of:

a- Goods for foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments for use in their official functions, with certification from the head of the mission and from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation.

b- Goods for the personal use of the official staff of the missions and organizations described in paragraph a of point 1 of this Article. The implementation of this paragraph and paragraph a of point 1 of this Article shall be based on international law principles and reciprocity between the governments concerned.

c- Goods originating in Cambodia or imported goods on which duties and taxes have been paid, which have left the customs territory and are re-imported from abroad without any increase in value.

d- Goods to be exempted from duties and taxes under the provisions of other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

e- Goods as humanitarian gifts and goods for research and scientific purposes, sample goods, and exhibition goods with no commercial value.

coffins containing human remains and other goods, as determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Imported goods subject to exemption from duties and taxes under the provisions of point 1 of this Article shall not be sold, transferred, diverted to other uses, or disposed of without prior authorization from the Customs Administration.

A certain amount of goods with specific value and quantity, determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance, imported by travelers, transport crews, and persons crossing the border, shall be exempted from import duties and taxes.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining regulations related to goods as specified in paragraphs a, b, c, d, and e of point 1 of this Article, including the sale, transfer, diversion, and disposal under the provisions of point 2 of this Article.

**Article 27.-**

Partial exemption from import duties and taxes may be granted for the importation of:

a- Goods and materials specified by other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

b- Seeds and animal breeds for agriculture; goods to be repaired, modified, or tested; goods re-imported in their original state; goods imported by the government for public interest; goods imported under the temporary admission regime; and other goods determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Imported goods under the provisions of this Article shall not be sold, transferred, diverted, or disposed of without prior authorization from the Customs Administration.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining regulations related to the goods specified in point 1 of this Article, including the sale, transfer, diversion, and disposal under the provisions of point 2 of this Article.

**Article 28.-**

The Customs Administration may provide full or partial refund of import duties and taxes already paid for:

a- Overpayment for any reason, including cases caused by administrative error;

b- Imported goods that, before being authorized for release from customs, are found to be short, broken, damaged, of low quality, and are re-exported or destroyed under customs supervision, even after the authorization for release;

c- Overpayment found through a decision of the authority in charge of resolving appeals as specified in Article 24 of this law, or by a competent court.

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas on the refunds mentioned above and refunds for other reasons, including the determination of time limits.

# Chapter 5
# Customs Declaration and Responsibility for Duties and Taxes

**Article 29.-**

All exported or imported goods, whether subject to duties and taxes or exempted, shall be the subject of a customs declaration.

**Article 30.-**

The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas related to:

- The form, content, and validity of customs declarations and documents;

- The filing, verification, and registration of customs declarations;

- The amendment, supplement, and cancellation or replacement of customs declarations and documents;

- The distribution and management of control for customs declarations and documents.

**Article 31.-**

Exported or imported goods shall be declared by the owner of the goods or by a person authorized to carry out customs formalities on behalf of the owner of the goods.

**Article 32.-**

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas granting or canceling authorization for any person to act as a customs broker and determining the locations where such authorization is valid and the conditions or qualifications for such authorization.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may establish a committee to provide advice on such authorization and all matters related to customs brokers.

Authorization as a customs broker shall be granted to individuals. In the case of an application by a legal entity, the authorization shall be granted to that legal entity and the persons authorized as representatives.

The refusal or temporary or permanent cancellation of authorization as a customs broker shall not be subject to any compensation or damages.

Authorized customs brokers may be required to provide security related to their operations in the form and amount determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

their own business.

Persons specified in point 1 of this Article may be authorized to release goods from customs for others. This authorization shall be granted by the Minister of Economy and Finance on a temporary basis and may be revoked for specific transactions related to certain goods.

**Article 34.-**

Import duties and taxes shall be calculated based on the tariff rates in force on the date of registration of the customs declaration.

Import duties and taxes shall be calculated based on ad valorem or specific rates.

**Article 35.-**

The importer or the owner of the goods shall be responsible for import duties and taxes.

In the case where the importer or owner of the goods cannot be found, the customs broker shall be responsible for import duties and taxes.

In the case of temporary customs storage or customs warehouses, the operators of these storage facilities are responsible for duties, taxes, and other fees, without prejudice to other penalties that may apply, until the goods are re-exported, temporarily imported, imported for domestic use, transferred to another storage facility, or destroyed with the authorization of the Customs Administration.

In the case of Articles 26 and 27 of this law, if the conditions for exemption from import duties and taxes are not fully met, the responsibility for import duties and taxes shall be the burden of the person who received the exemption. If such person cannot be found, the burden shall fall upon the person in possession of the goods.

Any person in possession of imported goods at the time the means of transport enters

in the customs territory, including operators of means of transport, is responsible for the import duties and taxes on those goods.

**Article 36.-**

The exporter or owner of the goods shall be responsible for export duties and taxes.

In the case where the exporter or owner of the goods cannot be found, the customs broker shall be responsible for export duties and taxes.

In the case of temporary customs storage and customs warehouses, the operators of these storage facilities shall be responsible for export duties and taxes.

# Chapter 6
# Payment of Import and Export Duties and Taxes, Debt Collection, and Security

**Article 37.-**

Export and import duties and taxes, as well as authorized fees, penalties, interest, or sanctions under this law and other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia, shall be paid at the place, in the manner, and at the time determined in a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

Duties and taxes to be paid for each item in a single customs declaration shall be calculated in Riels by rounding up to the nearest hundred.

A receipt shall be provided for all payments made.

**Article 38.-**

Arrears of debt under the provisions of this law that have not been paid in full or in part by the deadline shall be subject to additional interest at the rate determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

**Article 39.-**

The Customs Administration has priority in claiming goods and property of the debtor for debts owed under the provisions of this law.

**Article 40.-**

Procedures for collection and cancellation of uncollectible debts shall be based on prevailing regulations and laws.

**Article 41.-**

Security required by this law may be used once or multiple times and may take the form of cash, guarantees, or other instruments. The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining regulations related to such security.

**Article 42.-**

The Director of Customs and Excise Administration may release the security provided for guaranteeing the export or re-export of certain goods after receiving evidence confirming that such goods have indeed been exported or re-exported.

# Chapter 7
# Temporary Customs Storage and Customs Warehouses

**Article 43.-**

Temporary customs storage refers to the storage of goods under customs control in authorized locations while awaiting the completion of customs clearance formalities.

The license for operating temporary customs storage locations shall be decided by the Minister of Economy and Finance. This license shall determine the conditions for the owner

ownership and operation, including the location, structures, building designs, and procedures for management and control.

Expenses related to temporary customs storage locations, including maintenance and repair costs, are the burden of the owner or operator.

The owner or operator of a temporary customs storage location shall ensure the provision of suitable and adequate space, free of charge, for customs officers to carry out their duties as specified in this law.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining all cases related to temporary customs storage, including procedures, security, information requirements, storage duration, the type of goods allowed for storage, and penalties for exceeding the storage period.

**Article 44.-**

A customs warehouse is a place where goods are stored for a specific period under customs control, with a clearly defined timeframe.

The storage of goods in a customs warehouse stays the payment of duties and taxes and any restrictions to which those goods are subject.

There are three types of customs warehouses:

a- Public Warehouse, authorized by the Minister of Economy and Finance, which may be operated by a government agency or any person. This warehouse is used to provide services to all persons entitled to store goods.

b- Private Warehouse, authorized by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration, to be used exclusively by a person for storing goods for their own use, including the operation of duty-free shops

c- Special Warehouse, authorized by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration, for storing dangerous goods that may affect the quality of other goods or that require special storage space.

The license for a customs warehouse shall determine the conditions for the owner and operator, including the location, structures, building designs, and procedures for management and control.

Expenses related to customs warehouses, including maintenance and repair costs, are the burden of the owner or operator.

The owner or operator of a customs warehouse shall ensure the provision of suitable and adequate space, free of charge, for customs officers to carry out their duties as specified in this law.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining all cases related to customs warehouses, including procedures, security, information requirements, storage duration, and the type of goods allowed for storage.

**Article 45.-**

The operator of a customs warehouse shall pay duties and taxes on goods stored in the warehouse if the quantity and quality differ from those specified in the customs warehouse declaration, without prejudice to any penalties that may apply. If the goods are subject to an import ban, the warehouse operator shall also pay an amount equal to the value of the goods, without prejudice to any penalties that may apply.

The Director of Customs and Excise Administration may authorize, in addition to re-exportation, the destruction of damaged goods in a customs warehouse under conditions where the remains from such destruction are subject to duties and taxes, or require the payment of duties and taxes according to the state of the goods previously reported to the Customs Administration.

If it is claimed that the loss of goods stored in a customs warehouse

is the result of force majeure or reasons related to the goods themselves, the warehouse operator shall be exempted from paying duties and taxes, or if the goods are subject to a ban, from paying an amount equal to the value of the goods.

**Article 46.-**

Goods may be stored in a customs warehouse for up to two (2) years from the date of registration.

Within the period specified in point 1 of this Article, the goods shall be removed from the warehouse with customs authorization. Otherwise, the Customs Administration shall issue a notice to the warehouse operator requiring the removal of the goods. If this requirement remains ineffective for one (1) month after the notice is issued, the goods shall be deemed abandoned as specified in Article 54 of this law. The Customs Administration may collect duties and taxes owed from the security provided by the warehouse operator, or the goods may be sold at public auction as specified in the provisions of Article 55 of this law.

As an exception, provided that the goods are in good condition, the period specified in point 1 of this Article may be extended up to twelve (12) months by the Customs Administration upon the request of the warehouse operator.

**Article 47.-**

Customs officers may conduct necessary searches and inventory of goods in the warehouse. The operator shall present the goods for such inspection.

**Article 48.-**

The import duties and taxes to be collected shall be those in force on the date of registration of the customs declaration for removing goods from the customs warehouse.

**Article 49.-**

In specific circumstances, the Minister of Economy and Finance may authorize the establishment of manufacturing warehouses under customs control for the purpose of processing and manufacturing goods.

Unless otherwise provided, products entered into manufacturing warehouses are stays the payment of import duties and taxes.

When goods are removed from a manufacturing warehouse for domestic use, the duties and taxes that were stays as specified in point 2 of this Article shall be collected according to the Customs Tariff, and the rates to be applied shall be those in force on the date of registration of the customs declaration when the goods were authorized to enter the warehouse.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining all cases related to manufacturing warehouses.

**Article 50.-**

Petroleum refining or processing operations at or using mineral bitumen to obtain petroleum products shall be placed under the manufacturing warehouse regime under customs control.

Suspension of duties and taxes as specified in point 2 of Article 49 of this law may be granted for the importation of crude oil or mineral bitumen for processing for export.

The conditions for the implementation of the provisions of this Article shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 8
# Documents, Accounting Books, Records, and Other Information Regarding Export and Import

**Article 51.-**

Persons involved in or related to export and import shall maintain documents, accounting books, records, and other information, including accurate and sufficient electronic information, related to such export or import.

Included in point 1 of this Article are importers, exporters, customs brokers, operators of temporary customs storage and customs warehouses, transport operators, and other related parties.

Documents, accounting books, records, and other information as specified in point 1 of this Article shall be kept for ten (10) years at the place of business in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 52.-**

Upon request by the Customs Administration, persons specified in point 1 of Article 51 of this law are obligated to present documents, accounting books, records, and other information related to export and import for customs inspection.

If the person specified in point 1 of this Article is absent, their representative is obligated to provide the documents, accounting books, records, and other information.

In case required by customs officers, the persons specified in the above paragraph of this Article shall:

- Provide documents as required by customs officers.

b- Answer all questions asked by customs officers related to activities occurring under the provisions of this law.

c- Operate all devices to provide necessary information to customs officers upon request, if the information specified in the above paragraph of this Article is stored by electronic means or other devices.

**Article 53.-**

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining special requirements related to documents, accounting books, records, and other information under the provisions of Article 51 of this law.

# Chapter 9
# Abandoned Goods

**Article 54.-**

Abandoned goods are:

a- Goods stored in a temporary customs storage location or in a customs warehouse that exceed the period specified under the provisions of this law.

b- Goods sent by post that the consignee refuses to accept or the consignee cannot be found and the goods cannot be returned to the sender.

c- Goods and means of transport not subject to a ban that are in a customs clearance location and the owner has abandoned them in writing.

If the owner of the goods is known, the Customs Administration shall immediately notify the owner of the abandoned goods that such goods will be sold at public auction in accordance with the provisions of Article 55 of this law, if no claim is made within sixty (60) days from the date of the notice.

which will be determined by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration to notify the owner of the goods. Within sixty (60) days from the date of this notice, the owner shall claim their goods. If this period is exceeded, the goods shall be sold at public auction in accordance with the provisions of Article 55 of this law.

Within the period specified in points 2 and 3 of this Article, abandoned goods may be authorized for release from customs after the payment of duties, taxes, other fees, and penalties, and provided that all restrictive conditions have been met.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining cases where abandoned goods must be destroyed, sold by urgent auction, abandoned, or used for public interest based on the nature or low value of the goods, by giving written notice to the owner or issuing a public notice if the owner cannot be found.

**Article 55.-**

The sale of abandoned goods shall be conducted by public auction.

The Director of Customs and Excise Administration may determine a minimum price for goods to be sold at auction. If the goods cannot be sold at this price, the Director may determine a new minimum price or seek a decision from the Minister of Economy and Finance to abandon or use the goods for public interest.

The sale proceeds shall be used to pay for unpaid duties and taxes, fees, and other charges, and the costs of organizing the auction. The owner of the goods may claim the remaining balance after this settlement within six (6) months of the notice by the Customs Administration, which shall be made within seven (7) days after the auction. # Chapter 10
# Powers and Obligations

**Article 56.-**

Customs officers have the powers as specified in this law within the scope of customs authority. In the case of goods specified under the provisions of point 2 of Article 8 of this law, the powers of customs officers shall cover the entire customs territory.

Regarding exported or imported goods, customs officers have the following powers:

a- Inspect goods, and open or order the opening of any package or container and take samples of goods in appropriate quantities for inspection.

b- Detain, stop, board, enter, and inspect any means of transport and order the transport to be taken to a customs office or other suitable place for inspection.

c- Search private residences or business premises to collect evidence or temporarily detain goods, in accordance with the regulations and laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia, where there is a reasonable suspicion that an offense has been committed.

d- Inspect the identity of any person entering or leaving the customs territory, detain, stop, question, and search such person if customs officers suspect that the person is concealing goods or other items on or in their body that may be evidence of a violation of this law or other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Expenses for the inspections specified in paragraphs a and b of point 2 of this Article shall be the burden of the owner or the person in possession of the goods or means of transport

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining procedures for inspection and control.

**Article 57.-**

Within the time permitted by law, customs officers have the right to enter the locations or places where documents as specified in Article 51 of this law are kept, and have the right to conduct audits or inspections of those documents related to the separate reproduction or the accuracy and admissibility of manual or electronic recording systems in which those documents were created and maintained.

Customs officers may conduct inspections on documents, records, and other information, or property, processes, or other matters that customs officers deem necessary and related to the goal of collecting duties and taxes under the provisions of this law, to fulfill the duties of customs officers or for the purpose of collecting information required by this law.

Customs officers have the right to exit and enter any location, place, building, and other premises and have the right to inspect documents, records, and other information, even if they are under the management or control of a licensee, exporter, importer, or any other person.

Customs officers may extract information or copy any records or documents, seize them with a record of seizure, and keep them in the files presented related to any declaration or as required by this law, without any charge.

Upon request by the person in possession of the documents, customs officers shall provide that person with a certified copy of the documents.

**Article 58.-**

Customs officers who have taken an oath may fulfill the functions of Judicial Police Officers based on their hierarchy and have received qualification from the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal.

Customs officers who have received qualification as Judicial Police Officers shall prepare and sign customs offense reports within their jurisdiction.

When fulfilling a mission to research, prevent, and suppress customs offenses, customs officers shall have a mission order and wear their uniform, badges, and rank insignias, unless otherwise instructed by the Director of Customs and Excise Administration.

The uniform, badges, and rank insignias of customs officers shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

**Article 59.-**

Customs officers have the right to carry and use weapons while fulfilling their duties in the following circumstances:

- For lawful self-defense;

- If it is not possible to detain or stop a means of transport suspected of involvement in smuggling or other offenses, where the driver refuses to stop as ordered;

- For other reasons as specified in the regulations and laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The weapons used by customs and excise officers during operations in the line of duty shall be equipped and managed by the Ministry of Interior.

**Article 60.-**

Customs officers who have taken an oath and have the qualifications of Judicial Police Officers may decide to detain a person for a maximum of forty-eight (48) hours, in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

**Article 61.-**

No person shall obstruct or hinder customs officers in the exercise of their lawful powers as specified in this law or other laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

**Article 62.-**

Drivers of means of transport shall comply with the orders of customs officers in the movement of their means of transport.

Customs officers may use appropriate equipment to stop a means of transport when the driver fails to stop as ordered.

Upon request, competent authorities, police, and military shall immediately assist customs officers in fulfilling their duties as specified in this law.

**Article 63.-**

Customs officers shall not participate in commercial activities in a manner that involves or conflicts with their official duties.

Information obtained by customs officers in the management or implementation of this law may only be used for the purposes specified in this law, except as otherwise authorized by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

**Article 64.-**

Customs officers who commit offenses against this law and other regulations shall be subject to punishment according to the laws in force.

**Article 65.-**

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining conditions for providing rewards and incentives to deserving customs officers and to relevant competent institutions, including the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, the National Police, local authorities at all levels, as well as any individuals who have cooperated and supported the Customs Administration in the implementation of this law.

**Article 66.-**

Customs officers have the right to temporarily detain goods and means of transport involved in violations of the provisions of this law. Those goods and means of transport may be subject to confiscation by a competent court.

Outside the customs territory, temporary detention may be conducted at customs offices, temporary customs storage locations, customs warehouses, and other places under the implementation of the Customs Administration.

Goods specified in point 2 of Article 8 of this law may be temporarily detained at any location within the customs territory.

Temporary detention may also be applied throughout the customs territory in cases of armed hot pursuit or accidental discovery of goods that do not match the declaration or documents held by the possessor.

Customs officers have the right to copy or seize documents and other equipment that are evidence of violations of this law.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining procedures related to the temporary detention of goods, means of transport, documents, and other items, as well as the copying of those documents.

**Article 67.-**

Provided that circumstances permit, goods, means of transport, or items temporarily detained shall be sent to the customs office nearest to the place of detention. If there are multiple customs offices in the same area, the temporarily detained items may be transported to any of them. If they cannot be taken immediately to a customs office, or if there is no customs office in that area, the temporarily detained items may be handed over to the accused person or the occupant of the place of detention or another place for safekeeping.

If the temporarily detained goods are not subject to a ban, and the goods, means of transport, documents, and other detained items are not required for evidence, these detained items may be returned to the owner after a security has been provided as specified under the provisions of Article 41 of this law.

Temporarily detained goods that are not subject to a ban or restriction, and means of transport or other items used for concealing or transporting those goods, shall be returned to the owner after the payment of duties, taxes, other charges, and the correct amount of fines.

**Article 68.-**

Customs officers making a detention of goods shall prepare a detention report without delay.

If the offender is present, it shall be stated in the report that the content has been read to the person, the person has been asked to read it and sign it, and one copy of the report has been immediately provided to that person.

If the offender is absent, a copy of the report shall be posted within twenty-four (24) hours after the report has been prepared at the nearest customs office or at the nearest local authority office if there is no customs office there.

Further details regarding the form, content, and procedures for the preparation and submission of the report to the court, as specified in point 1 of this Article, shall be determined by a joint Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Justice.

**Article 69.-**

In the case where detention is made on graded vessels and ships, if the goods cannot be removed immediately, the person making the detention shall apply customs seals on the hatches, holds, and covers of that vessel.

**Article 70.-**

In the case where detention is made in a residence, goods not subject to a ban

shall not be taken anywhere if the accused person has provided security in accordance with the provisions of Article 41 of this law. If the accused person has not provided security, or if the goods are subject to a ban, those goods shall be transported to the nearest customs office or handed over to the occupant for safekeeping at the place of detention or another place.

**Article 71.-**

A person subject to a penalty or detention of goods, means of transport, or other items may protest in writing to the Director of Customs and Excise Administration within thirty (30) days after receiving notification of the penalty or detention, stating the reasons for the protest and providing security in accordance with the provisions of Article 41 of this law.

The Director of Customs and Excise Administration shall issue a decision on the protest within sixty (60) days from the date the protest is received; otherwise, the protest shall be deemed approved.

The Minister of Economy and Finance may issue a Prakas determining procedures related to this protest process.

# Chapter 11
# Provisions on Punishments

**Article 72.-**

Any person who exports or imports, or attempts to export or import goods in violation of the law or any regulations, shall be deemed to have committed an offense.

Any person who commits a customs offense shall be subject to administrative fines determined by the Customs Administration and judicial penalties determined by a competent court, or either of the two.

**Article 73.-**

Any person who commits a minor violation of the provisions and regulations of this law, including incorrect entry, omission, or failure to enter any information required in a customs declaration, and failure to fulfill requirements and obligations that do not affect duties, taxes, bans, or restrictions, shall be subject to a fine of 100,000 Riels to 500,000 Riels, provided that such inaccuracy is not subject to a more severe penalty in this law.

**Article 74.-**

Any person who violates the provisions and regulations of this law, including violations as specified in Article 73 of this law that affect duties or taxes, and in cases where the goods are subject to a ban or restriction, shall be subject to administrative penalties including a fine of one (1) to three (3) times the avoided duties and taxes and judicial penalties including confiscation of the goods, means of transport, and other items used for concealing the offending goods, or imprisonment from one (1) month to one (1) year, or either of the two penalties.

The following are considered offenses as specified in point 1 of this Article:

a- Failure to report goods to the Customs Administration in accordance with the provisions of Article 10 and Article 16 of this law.

b- Unloading goods from a means of transport before reporting to the Customs Administration as specified in the provisions of Article 12 of this law.

c- Opening, breaking, picking, making open or breaking any package of imported goods that have not yet received authorization from the Customs Administration for release, or causing breakage, damage to seals applied or attached to goods, means of transport, temporary customs storage locations, customs warehouses, or other places required by this law.

d- Removal of goods from a customs clearance location, temporary customs storage location, or customs warehouse without authorization from the Customs Administration.

e- Failure to lodge a customs declaration in accordance with the provisions of Article 29 of this law.

f- Failure to comply with the provisions of Article 30 of this law.

g- Sale, transfer, transformation, or abandonment of imported goods that received exemptions from duties and other taxes as specified in Article 26 and Article 27 of this law without authorization from the Customs Administration.

h- Failure to implement conditions determined by the Customs Administration related to goods under a duty and tax suspension regime in accordance with the provisions of this law.

i- Committing, consenting to, or participating in oral or written declarations that lead to inaccuracy, forgery, or deception contrary to this law.

j- Smuggling or attempting to smuggle goods.

k- Possession, sale, purchase, transfer, or receipt without authorization from the Customs Administration of goods imported in violation of the provisions of this law.

Any person who commits the following offenses shall be subject to a fine of one million (1,000,000) Riels to five million (5,000,000) Riels and imprisonment from one (1) month to six (6) months, or either of the two penalties:

a- Failure to comply with the provisions specified in Article 51, Article 52, and Article 53 of this law.

b- Any person who obstructs or hinders customs officers as specified in the provisions of Article 61 of this law.

c- Drivers of means of transport who do not comply with the orders of customs officers as specified in point 1 of Article 62 of this law.

**Article 75.-**

Any person who violates the provisions and regulations of Article 74 of this law related to restricted or banned goods as specified in point 1 of Article 8 of this law, except for paragraph j of point 1 of Article 74 of this law, shall be subject to administrative penalties including a fine of up to three (3) times the value of the goods and means of transport, and shall be subject to judicial penalties including confiscation of the goods, means of transport, and other items used for concealing the offending goods, or imprisonment from one (1) year to five (5) years, or either of the two penalties.

**Article 76.-**

In cases of recidivism, the offenses as specified in this law shall be subject to double the administrative fines and judicial penalties.

**Article 77.-**

The Customs Administration has the right to settle customs offenses as specified in this law. This right of settlement applies only to monetary fines. In case no settlement is possible, the Customs Administration shall send the case file to a competent court.

Settlement may be made before a judgment is rendered by a competent court.

The conditions for the exercise of the right to settle shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.
