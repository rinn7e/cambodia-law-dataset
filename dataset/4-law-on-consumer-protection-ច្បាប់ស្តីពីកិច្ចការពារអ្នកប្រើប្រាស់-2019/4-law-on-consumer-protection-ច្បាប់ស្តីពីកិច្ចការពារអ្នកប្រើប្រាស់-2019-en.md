### PROMULGATE

The Law on Consumer Protection as adopted by the National Assembly on October 08, 2019 at the 3rd plenary session of the 6th legislature, and which the Senate has entirely reviewed and approved as to its form and legality on October 18, 2019 at its extraordinary session of the 4th legislature, the full text of which is as follows:

# LAW
# ON
# CONSUMER PROTECTION

# CHAPTER 1
# GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 1.- Purpose

This Law aims to ensure consumer protection and to contribute to the promotion of fair competition.

#### Article 2.- Goal

This Law has the goal of defining rules and mechanisms to contribute to creating a business environment in which:

- The rights and interests of consumers are protected;

- Business activities are conducted with fair competition; and

- Consumers and businessmen act together with confidence.

#### Article 3.- Scope

This Law shall apply to all persons who conduct business, whether for profit or non-profit, including the sale of various goods or services or rights in rem over immovable property, to consumers in the Kingdom of Cambodia, unless there is a specific provision stating otherwise.

#### Article 4.- Definitions

1- Acquisition refers to receiving goods and rights in rem over immovable property through gift, sale-purchase, exchange, or other agreements, as well as receiving services through various agreements.

2- Advertising refers to public dissemination or dissemination to any group among the public, regardless of the form, with the purpose of promoting the supply of goods or services or the sale or provision of rights in rem over immovable property.

3- Competent Regulator refers to any ministry or institution that has the function and role of protecting the legal rights and interests of consumers under its responsibility.

4- Business refers to activities in which:

- Goods or services are received or supplied, or rights in rem over immovable property are received or disposed of; or

- Carried out in an organized and clear manner; or

- The person carrying out the activity has the purpose of seeking profit, whether the activity carried out by that person is the primary or secondary activity, or not regular.

5- Consumer refers to a person who receives goods or services:

- Which are normally intended for personal, domestic, or household consumption and with the purpose of:

- Not re-supplying in business; or

- Not used in the production process or manufacturing; or

- Not used goods in business activities, such as for the repair of buildings for use as decorative items on immovable property.

#### 6- Consumer Rights refers to:

- The right to receive information and education to know the material characteristics of goods or services and to be protected from fraud and deception through advertising;

- The right to choose goods or services with competitive prices and quality;

- The right to be heard about concerns and to receive review and resolution from the competent regulator and the Royal Government;

- The right to claim compensation under this Law or other laws.

#### 7- Act refers to doing, refusing, or neglecting to perform any action, including managing or leading business activities.

#### 8- Disclosure of Information refers to showing sufficient and correct information to the public.

#### 9- E-commerce refers to the activities of buying, selling, leasing, or exchanging goods or services, including commercial and civil activities, as well as various activities and operations of the State through electronic systems.

#### 10- Fair Competition refers to competition based on low price, good quality, and good service, based on the principle of long-term economic sustainability and without abusing power or dominant position in the market.

#### 11- Misleading Representation refers to a representation made by a person conducting any business activity that makes a consumer believe something is true, but in reality, it is not true.

#### 12- Person refers to a natural person or legal entity established properly according to the laws in force, whether for profit or non-profit, registered or not registered.

#### 13- Dissemination of Information refers to making known to the general public, including dissemination through the website of the relevant regulator or through other means that the public has the ability to access information freely and without hindrance.

#### 14- Services refers to all rights, benefits, priorities, or all facilities provided or given according to the type of contract, including the following, except non-bank financial services:

a- Contracts related to:

- The performance of work, whether with or without the supply of goods; or

- Providing facilities or satisfaction with facilities, including accommodation, leisure, care of people, animals, or other objects, entertainment (excluding commercial gambling), parking or;

- Providing rights, benefits, or providing priorities for a fee.

- Contracts between financial service providers and their customers.

#### 15- Supply refers to:

- Supplying goods through gifts, sale, exchange, lease, or hire-purchase and

- Providing services.

#### 16- Multiple Supply refers to:

- Multiple supplies of goods through gifts, sale, exchange, lease, or hire-purchase; and

- Providing multiple services.

#### 17- Commercial Description refers to a representation or description of goods, including quantity or size, manufacturing or production methods, ingredients used, production time, fitness for purpose, durability, function, characteristics or accuracy, and physical shape attached to the goods. Commercial description does not include trademarks or trade names.

#### 18- Unfair Acts refers to any acts of a person in business that may mislead or deceive consumers, whether intentional or unintentional:

- Representations such as advertising, sales promotion, and other claims;

- Making consumers confused about the price, value, or quality of goods or services. Persons in business cannot rely on fine print that is difficult to see and misleading disclaimers to escape responsibility;

- Not disclosing to consumers relevant promises, expectations, and information;

- Taking advantage of consumers if the supplier knows that the consumer is not in a position to protect their interests or has no capacity to understand the goods or services, such as characteristics, type, language, power of transaction, or issues related to that transaction; or

- Other acts defined by a proclamation of the Ministry of Commerce.

# CHAPTER 2
# COMPETENT INSTITUTION

#### Article 5.- National Committee for Consumer Protection

The National Committee for Consumer Protection, led by the Minister of Commerce and involving relevant ministries and institutions, is established, with the General Department in charge of consumer protection of the Ministry of Commerce as the Secretariat, to perform various tasks related to consumer protection in accordance with the provisions of this Law.

The organization and functioning of the National Committee for Consumer Protection shall be determined by a sub-decree.

# CHAPTER 3
# CONSUMER ASSOCIATION

#### Article 6.- Establishment of Consumer Association

Consumers in each sector have the right to establish their own association by registering with the Ministry of Interior in accordance with the Law on Associations and Non-Governmental Organizations.

#### Article 7.- Consumer Association

The Consumer Association in any sector must obtain prior permission from the ministry or institution that has the role and function covering its sector.

After establishment and registration with the Ministry of Interior and obtaining permission from the relevant competent regulator, that association must deposit the registration documents and the above permission at the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

#### Article 8.- Role and Duties of Consumer Association

The Consumer Association has the roles and duties as follows:

- Providing independent advice to consumers and coordinating issues related to consumers;

- Acting as a representative before the National Committee for Consumer Protection or before the court on behalf of any consumer or group of consumers whose rights and interests have been violated;

- Representing consumer views and interests in public forums or the media;

- Receiving advice from any competent regulator on provisions regarding information standards provided to consumers issued by that competent regulator;

- Establishing consumer protection working groups by sector;

- Performing other duties assigned by the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

# CHAPTER 4
# UNFAIR ACTS IN BUSINESS

#### Article 9.- Unfair Acts

Persons who conduct business shall not engage in unfair acts.

Unfair acts refer to any act of a person in business that may mislead or deceive consumers, whether the act is intentional or unintentional:

- Representations such as advertising, sales promotion, and other claims;

- Making consumers confused about the price, value, or quality of goods or services. Persons in business cannot rely on fine print that is difficult to see and misleading disclaimers to escape responsibility;

- Not disclosing to consumers relevant promises, expectations, and information;

- Taking advantage of consumers if the supplier knows that the consumer is not in a position to protect their interests or has no capacity to understand the goods or services, such as characteristics, type, language, power of transaction, or issues related to that transaction; or

- Other acts defined by a proclamation of the Ministry of Commerce.

#### Article 10.- Use of Coercive Selling Methods

Persons who conduct business shall not use coercive selling methods.

Coercive selling methods include:

- Use of physical force;

- Harassment;

- Coercion; or

- Use of undue influence.

#### Article 11.- Deceptive Bait Selling

Persons who conduct business shall not:

- Advertise the sale of goods or services at any special price if they know or should know that they are unable to supply those goods or services at the advertised price and in a quantity reasonable to the demand; or

- Have the intention of not supplying goods or services at the advertised price.

The above practices shall not be considered deceptive bait selling if the person has specified to consumers in advance:

- The duration of the sale at the special price; or

- The quantity of goods or services to be sold at the special price.

#### Article 12.- Deceptive Prize Selling

Persons who conduct business shall not advertise that they will provide rewards as free goods or services, or provide gifts or other rewards with the intention of not providing them as advertised.

- Whether those goods or services include or do not include insurance, compensation, or resolution of rights or other conditions;

- Goods which have a certain place of origin.

Other misleading representations shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of Commerce, who is the Chairperson of the National Committee for Consumer Protection, upon the request of a competent regulator.

# CHAPTER 5
# UNFAIR PRACTICES

#### Article 13.- Prohibition of Unfair Selling

Selling that causes consumers to be confused in purchasing goods or services is prohibited.

#### Article 14.- Promises to Provide Gifts and Rewards

Every person shall not promise to provide gifts, rewards, or other free items with the intention of deceiving or lying to consumers regarding:

- The supply of goods or services; or

- The sale or provision of rights in rem over immovable property.

#### Article 15.- Bait Advertising

1- Every person shall not advertise to supply goods or services at a certain price if that person has no intention of supplying or has no reasonable basis to believe that the person can supply at the advertised price.

2- Every person who has advertised goods or services to supply at a certain price must supply those goods or services at that advertised price for a certain duration and in a reasonable quantity.

3- In case of a complaint or protest against any person regarding the failure to provide goods or services to consumers according to the provisions of this Article, that person can defend themselves if they show that:

a- They have provided the supply by appointing another person to supply in their place within a certain duration, in a certain quantity, and at the price as advertised, and which has been agreed to by the consumer;

b- They have provided the supply immediately or through another person appointed for them within a certain duration to consumers for goods or services equivalent in both quantity and price as advertised, and which has been agreed to by the consumer.

#### Article 16.- Unfair Referral Selling

Unfair referral selling refers to enticing or encouraging any purchaser to buy goods or services based on the condition that the purchaser will receive rewards, commissions, or

...other benefits in return for giving names of potential customers or otherwise assisting that person to include or entice other purchasers or consumers to receive commissions or price discounts on goods or creating other events to entice other purchasers to buy goods according to the persuasion of the initial consumer, and those potential customers actually come to buy goods or services according to the persuasion of the initial purchaser or consumer, but those conditions are not fulfilled by the seller.

#### Article 17.- Demanding or Accepting Payment without Intention to Supply Goods or Services according to Purchase Orders

Before supplying goods or services, the supplier shall not demand or accept payment or other consideration in cases where the supplier:

- Has no intention of supplying goods or services according to the purchase order; or

- Intends to supply goods or services that are different from the goods or services according to the purchase order; or

- Does not have reasonable grounds to believe that the supplier can supply the goods or services within a certain duration or within a reasonable duration according to the purchase order.

#### Article 18.- False and Misleading Representations regarding certain Business Activities

1- No person shall make false or misleading representations regarding any profit, risk, or other importance in any business activity that the person has represented that other persons can conduct that business from their residence.

2- No person shall make false or misleading representations on key points regarding any profit, risk, or other importance of any business activity in which the person invites other persons through advertising or other means to participate and that business activity requires:

a- Participation in performing work by other persons; or

b- Investment of money and participation in performing work by persons related to that investment.

#### Article 19.- Coercion by Force and Mental Harassment

No person shall coerce by force, harassment, or mental harassment regarding the supply of goods or the payment for goods or services.

#### Article 20.- Pyramid Selling Schemes

No person shall promote or operate a pyramid selling scheme.

A pyramid selling scheme is a scheme that:

- 1- Provides the supply of goods and/or services to receive rewards or consideration back; and

- 2- Creates an opportunity to sell or buy, or an investment opportunity to many participants individually or through each other which is not the creation of an opportunity to sell or buy actual goods or services; and

- 3- This scheme is unfair to many participants because:

- The rewards or consideration of those participants depends on recruiting more participants;

- New participants cannot find more participants.

#### Article 21.- Selling Goods with False Commercial Description

No person shall sell goods with a false commercial description.

False commercial description refers to a description related to the supply of goods or the promotion of the supply of goods through any means or the use of goods that is considered false.

This Article applies to all goods with a false commercial description by:

- Sewing, branding, printing on, inserting, attaching, or sticking on the goods; or

- Displaying on the cover, label, packaging, envelope, bottle, or tube on the goods.

#### Article 22.- Other Unfair Practices

Other unfair practices shall be determined by a proclamation of the competent regulator in accordance with the scope of roles and duties of that regulator, with the approval of the National Committee for Consumer Protection, to protect the legal rights and interests of consumers.

# CHAPTER 6
# INFORMATION FOR CONSUMERS

#### Article 23.- Information Standards for Consumers

Persons who conduct business in the Kingdom of Cambodia must disclose minimum information to consumers in accordance with information standards to be determined by a proclamation of the competent regulator and may consult with the National Committee for Consumer Protection in case of necessity.

#### Article 24.- Obligation to Comply with Information Standards for Consumers

Persons who conduct business, supply, promise to supply, or advertise related to the supply of goods or services must comply with information standards for consumers.

#### Article 25.- Notification of Information Standards for Consumers

Information standards for consumers shall be disseminated by the relevant regulator and/or the National Committee for Consumer Protection and once disseminated, those information standards shall become part of the information standards under this Law and shall be implemented by the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

#### Article 26.- Organization and Implementation of Information Standards for Consumers

The competent regulator must organize information standards for consumers related to goods or services for business activities within the scope of roles and duties of those regulators to protect the legal rights and interests of consumers.

In case any regulation prepared by a competent regulator conflicts with a regulation prepared by another competent regulator, the National Committee for Consumer Protection shall review and find a way to resolve it. In case it cannot be resolved, the National Committee for Consumer Protection shall submit a request to the Head of the Royal Government for review and decision.

#### Article 27.- Content of Information Standards for Consumers

1- Information standards to be provided to consumers may:

a- Disclose information related to the type, grade, safety, quantity, origin, function, maintenance, ingredients, design, installation, method of use, price, packaging, advertising or supply, date of manufacture and expiration date, information about production or information related to the supply of goods or services; and/or

b- Specify the method in which that information is received; and/or

c- Specify the form and manner in which that information is disseminated related to:

- Supply of goods or services; or

- Re-supply or the possibility of re-supply of goods or services; or

- Promotion through any means of any point related to supply, re-supply, or the possibility of re-supply for goods or services; or

d- Define minimum information standards for e-commerce in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2- All written information must be in the Khmer language, according to the proclamation of the competent regulator.

3- Models for organizing other information standards provided to consumers can be determined by the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

# CHAPTER 7
# PROCEDURES FOR COMPLAINT AND INVESTIGATION

#### Article 28.- Institution Receiving Complaints and Investigating

The National Committee for Consumer Protection has the competence to receive complaints and investigate through:

- 1- Its own initiative; or

- 2- Complaints from any person or any consumer association;

- 3- Complaints from any competent regulator.

In case of necessity, the National Committee for Consumer Protection may request cooperation from any institution or competent regulator to perform the above duties, and that institution or competent regulator must provide cooperation upon the request of the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

#### Article 29.- Appointment of Investigating Officers

Investigating officers must obtain the status of judicial police officers to inspect offenses stated in this Law, in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The formalities and procedures for granting the status to investigating officers shall be determined by an inter-ministerial proclamation between the Minister of Justice and the Minister of Commerce.

#### Article 30.- Inspection Powers

Investigating officers have the following powers:

- 1- Inspect, research, and collect evidence related to violations of this Law.

- 2- Inspect products, goods, and services, sample advertisements, sale of any products, or storage and distribution that requires compliance with the provisions of this Law.

- 3- Take samples of products or other relevant tools in case of suspicion of violation of this Law.

- 4- Question relevant persons to receive answers or order the submission of documents or other objects for consideration.

- 5- Take temporary prohibitive measures against the supply, distribution, or circulation in the market of any type of products, goods, or services where evidence has been found through research of non-compliance with the provisions of this Law, and must report to the National Committee for Consumer Protection to coordinate with relevant competent institutions to cooperate in taking further measures according to the laws in force.

The formalities and procedures for inspection shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of Commerce.

#### Article 31.- Powers of Search

Investigating officers are authorized to access and search any place in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

Investigating officers who must perform duties within an area or location have the right to request assistance from local authorities at all levels or other relevant competent authorities to join in cracking down on offenses stated in this Law and shall comply with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

# CHAPTER 8
# PROCEDURE FOR ISSUING DECISIONS

#### Article 32.- Procedure for Issuing Decisions

After the investigation, the National Committee for Consumer Protection has the power under this Law to:

- 1- Issue a decision; and/or

- 2- Issue administrative sanctions.

In issuing decisions and/or defining administrative sanctions, the National Committee for Consumer Protection may consider various measures on suggestions proposed by relevant competent regulators regarding the approval, correction, or rejection of measures for the proposed suggestions.

#### Article 33.- Procedure for Resolution

The National Committee for Consumer Protection may resolve through negotiation with the person who has violated any provision of this Law.

Every resolution shall not apply to:

- 1- Criminal cases;

- 2- Further violations of other provisions of this Law by that person.

The procedure for resolution shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of Commerce.

#### Article 34.- Decision to Disclose Information or Re-publish

When the National Committee for Consumer Protection finds that any relevant person has violated any provision of this Law, it may issue the following orders:

- 1- Require the person who failed to publish information or published insufficient information to publish that information or any point of that information sufficiently, in the manner specified in the order, at their own expense to the public or any relevant person.

- 2- Require the person who published incorrect or misleading information to make a correction in the manner specified in the order at their own expense.

#### Article 35.- Decision to Bar from Management Positions

The National Committee for Consumer Protection may issue a decision to bar any person from management positions if:

- 1- That person has committed the following offenses at least 2 (two) times or more:

- Unfair acts related to goods and/or services;

- Misleading representation;

- Promises to provide gifts and rewards;

- Bait advertising;

- Unfair referral selling;

- Demanding or accepting payment without intention to supply goods or services according to purchase orders;

- False and misleading representations regarding certain business activities;

- Coercion by force and mental harassment;

- Pyramid selling schemes; and

- Selling goods with false commercial description.

- 2- That person has committed the following offenses at least 2 (two) times within 5 (five) years while that person holds the position of director or manager of a legal entity:

- Failing to disclose information standards for consumers;

- Failing to fulfill the obligation to comply with information standards for consumers;

- Failing to notify about information standards for consumers.

- 3- Persons who are barred by the administration related to consumer protection as stated in points 1 and 2.

#### Article 36.- Publication of Decision to Bar from Management Positions

The National Committee for Consumer Protection shall provide a copy of each barring decision stated in Article 35 of this Law to:

- a- The relevant person or any consumer association.

- b- The competent regulator; and

- c- The commercial registration unit of the Ministry of Commerce.

The National Committee for Consumer Protection shall publish barring decisions stated in Article 35 of this Law on its official website or through other means.

#### Article 37.- Duration of Barring from Management Positions

The barring from holding a position as director or manager of a legal entity in the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be no less than 2 (two) years and shall not exceed 5 (five) years.

# CHAPTER 9
# APPEAL

#### Article 38.- Correction of Decisions of the National Committee for Consumer Protection

Any person related to a decision of the National Committee for Consumer Protection may request the National Committee for Consumer Protection to review, correct, or withdraw the decision within a maximum of 15 (fifteen) days from the date of receiving the notification of the decision.

The request for review must specify the following points:

- Clearly specify that one or more findings of the National Committee for Consumer Protection may be misleading; and

- Show that the decision or administrative sanction of the National Committee for Consumer Protection does not have solid evidence as stated in the minutes of the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

In this case, the National Committee for Consumer Protection shall review and decide within a maximum of 30 (thirty) days.

#### Article 39.- Right to Appeal to Competent Court

Any person who is dissatisfied with a decision of the National Committee for Consumer Protection may appeal to a competent court of the Kingdom of Cambodia within a maximum of 30 (thirty) days from the date of receiving the notification of the final decision.

Formalities and procedures for notification by the National Committee for Consumer Protection shall be determined by a proclamation of the Minister of Commerce.

# CHAPTER 10
# PENALTY PROVISIONS

#### Article 40.- Sanctions and Interim Fines

Sanctions under this Law include written warning, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of commercial registration certificate or license, interim fines, penalties of fines, and imprisonment.

Written warning, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of commercial registration certificate or license is the competence of the National Committee for Consumer Protection.

Interim fines are the competence of investigating officers.

Payment of interim fines shall cause the extinction of the criminal prosecution.

In case the offender refuses to pay the interim fine, the investigating officer may build a file of the offense to be submitted to a competent court.

The procedures for interim fines, payment of fines, management of fine receipts, and management of revenue from fines for offenses as stated in the provisions of this Law shall be determined by an inter-ministerial proclamation between the Minister of Commerce, the Minister of Justice, and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 41.- Unfair Acts related to Goods, Services, or Misleading Representations

A written warning shall be given to any person who commits unfair acts related to goods, services, or misleading representations as stated in Article 9, Article 10, Article 11, and Article 12.

In case of having received a written warning once and continuing to commit the offense under the first paragraph above, the commercial registration certificate or license shall be suspended, revoked, or cancelled.

An interim fine of no more than 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels shall be given for offenses under the first paragraph above related to the quality and origin of goods.

#### Article 42.- Aggravating Circumstances of Unfair Acts related to Goods, Services, or Misleading Representations which Affect Health and Safety

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels for offenses under the first paragraph of Article 41 in case of causing serious impact on the health and safety of consumers.

#### Article 43.- Aggravating Circumstances of Unfair Acts related to Goods, Services, or Misleading Representations causing Permanent Disability or Death

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and a fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels for offenses under the first paragraph of Article 41 which cause permanent disability or loss of life.

#### Article 44.- Offenses related to Unfair Practices

An interim fine of no more than 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels shall be given to any person who commits bait advertising, demanding or accepting payment without intention to supply goods or services according to purchase orders, false or misleading representations regarding certain business activities, or coercion by force and mental harassment.

#### Article 45.- Pyramid Selling Schemes or Sale of Goods with False Commercial Description

An interim fine of no more than 80,000,000 (eighty million) Riels shall be given for committing pyramid selling schemes or sale of goods with false commercial description.

#### Article 46.- Aggravating Circumstances of Pyramid Selling Schemes or Sale of Goods with False Commercial Description which Affect Health and Safety

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years and a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 4,000,000 (four million) Riels for committing pyramid selling schemes or sale of goods with false commercial description and causing serious impact on the health and safety of consumers.

#### Article 47.- Aggravating Circumstances of Pyramid Selling Schemes or Sale of Goods with False Commercial Description causing Disability or Death

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and a fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels for committing pyramid selling schemes or sale of goods with false commercial description which causes permanent disability or loss of life.

#### Article 48.- Offenses on Non-Compliance with Information Standards for Consumers

An interim fine of no more than 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels shall be given to any person who commits non-compliance with information standards for consumers.

#### Article 49.- Violation of Decision Barring from Management Positions

An interim fine of no more than 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels shall be given to any person who violates a decision barring them from management positions.

# CHAPTER 11
# FINAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 50.- Abrogation

Any provisions that contradict this Law shall be abrogated.

#### Article 51.- Immediate Proclamation

This Law shall be proclaimed as urgent.
