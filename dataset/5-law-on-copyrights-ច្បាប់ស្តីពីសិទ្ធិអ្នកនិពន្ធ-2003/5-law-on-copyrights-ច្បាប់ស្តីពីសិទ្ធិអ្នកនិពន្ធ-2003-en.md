# PROMULGATE

The Law on Copyright and Related Rights which was adopted by the National Assembly on January 21, 2003 at its 9th plenary session of the 2nd legislature and which was entirely approved by the Senate as to its form and substance without any amendment on February 13, 2003 at its 8th plenary session of the 1st legislature, the whole text of which is as follows:

# LAW
# ON
# COPYRIGHT AND RELATED RIGHTS

# CHAPTER 1
# GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Article 1..

This law has the objective to ensure the protection of copyright and related rights and the preservation of cultural products which are the works of authors, performers, producers of phonograms and broadcasting organizations in order to ensure a fair and legal exploitation of those cultural products to contribute to the development of the cultural sector.

### Article 2..

For the purposes of this law, the following terms shall have the following meanings:

a- Work: shall mean the product of the literary, scientific, artistic or musical field, resulting from the creative talent, developed by intelligence or emotion.

b- Author: shall mean the person who creates the work.

c- Performance: shall mean the acting of a work on stage such as dancing, playing music, singing or presenting in any other means or methods professional artistic works, traditions, literature, education or science.

d- Recitation: shall mean the expression of voice through speaking, reading and reciting various works.

e- Performer: shall mean an artist, dancer, musician, singer or person who performs.

f- Database: shall mean a set of information, texts, numbers, diagrams created and researched by a computer system.

g- Audiovisual work: shall mean a work created by combining consecutive images in sequence, forming a visible image, whether or not accompanied by sound, and if accompanied by sound, the sound can be heard.

h- Broadcasting: shall mean the transmission of sound, images, documents or various messages by...

...by means of radio, television, cable television or wave network.

i- Communication to the public: shall mean the transmission by wire or without wire outside the family circle of images or sounds or both of any work that can be received by the public at the place of origin or at any other place, regardless of whether they receive those images or sounds at the same time and place or at different times and places of their own choice.

j- Phonogram: shall mean a fixation of the sounds of a performance or of other sounds, or of a representation of sounds, other than in the form of a fixation incorporated in a cinematographic or other audiovisual work.

k- Producer of phonogram: shall mean the person who produces a phonogram as defined in point (j).

l- Computer program: shall mean a set of instructions expressed in words, codes, schemes or in any other form, which is capable, when incorporated in a computer, of causing the computer to perform or achieve a particular task or result by means of computer or electronic methods for information processing.

m- Reproduction: shall mean the making of one or more copies of a work or a phonogram in whole or in part, in any manner or form, including any permanent or temporary storage of the work or phonogram in electronic form.

n- Broadcasting organization: shall mean a radio station, television station, cable television or wave network.

o- Related rights: shall mean the rights of performers, the rights of producers of phonograms and the rights of broadcasting organizations.

### Article 3..

The works which are entitled to protection under this law are the works of authors...

performers, phonograms and broadcasts.

1- Author's works shall refer to:

a- Works created by Cambodian nationals or by persons who have their residence in the Kingdom of Cambodia, including legal entities established under the laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia and having their offices in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

b- Works first published in the Kingdom of Cambodia, or works first published abroad and subsequently published in the Kingdom of Cambodia within 30 (thirty) days.

c- Audiovisual works, the producer of which has his headquarters or his residence in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

d- Architectural works constructed in the Kingdom of Cambodia and other artistic works incorporated in a building or other structure located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

e- Works for which the Kingdom of Cambodia has an obligation to protect under international treaties.

2- Performers' works shall refer to:

a- Works of performers who are Cambodian nationals.

b- Works of performers who are not Cambodian nationals, but whose performance:

- has taken place on the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia; or

- has been fixed in a phonogram protected under this law; or

- has not been fixed in a phonogram but has been transmitted through a broadcast protected under this law.

3- Phonograms shall refer to:

a- Phonograms of producers who are Cambodian nationals.

b- Phonograms which have been first fixed in the...

Kingdom of Cambodia.

c- Phonograms first published in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

4- Broadcasts shall refer to:

a- Broadcasts of broadcasting organizations which have their headquarters in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

b- Broadcasts transmitted from transmitters located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The provisions of this law shall also apply to performers, producers of phonograms and broadcasting organizations who are entitled to protection under international treaties or international agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party.

# CHAPTER 2
# COPYRIGHT

## SECTION 1
## WORKS

### Article 4..

An original work shall be considered as any work created by the author's own intellectual creation.

### Article 5..

A work, whether or not completed or published, shall be considered as created by the unique knowledge from the author's creative talent.

### Article 6..

A joint work is a work created by the collaboration of several natural persons to...

create that work.

A derivative work is a work created based on an original work through translation, adaptation or arrangement.

A collective work is a work created by several natural persons at the initiative of a natural person who publishes it under his direction and name.

### Article 7..

The works which, according to Article 3, are entitled to protection under this law are:

a- Reading books of all types or other documents concerning literature, art, science and education.

b- Sermons, speeches, lectures, defense in court or by writing and other works of the same nature.

c- Dramatic or dramatico-musical works.

d- Choreographic works, whether modern or adapted from traditional or folk works.

e- Circus performances and pantomimes.

f- Musical compositions with or without words.

g- Audiovisual works.

h- Paintings, sculptures, engravings or other works of fine arts or applied arts.

i- Photographic works and works achieved by technical processes similar to photography.

j- Architectural works.

k- Maps, plans, sketches or three-dimensional works relative to geography, topography or other sciences.

l- Computer programs and the preparatory design materials of those programs.

m- Handicraft products, textile designs or other clothing models.

### Article 8..

Derivative works, including translations, adaptations, arrangements, modifications or other alterations of a work or a compilation of data, whether in machine-readable form or in any other form, shall also be protected under this law.

The protection of any work referred to in the above paragraph shall be without prejudice to the protection of the original work incorporated or utilized for the creation of the new work.

### Article 9..

The title of a work, if it has a distinctive character for the original work, shall be protected in the same way as the work itself.

Even if a work is no longer protected by law according to Articles 30 and 31 of this law, no person has the right to use the title of that work for another work in a manner that causes confusion.

### Article 10..

The following shall not be protected under this law:

a- Constitution, laws, royal decrees, sub-decrees and other administrative regulations.

b- Proclamations (Prakas), decisions, certificates, circular instructions and other documents issued by state organizations.

c- Decisions, verdicts or judgments of the courts.

d- Foreign language translations of the documents referred to in points (a), (b) and (c) above in this Article.

e- Ideas, procedures, systems, methods of operation, concepts, principles, discoveries or simple data, even if they are expressed, described, explained or embodied in any work.

## SECTION 2
## AUTHORS

### Article 11..

The status of an author is the status of a person or several persons who own the copyright in the work they have created, and whose work has been published to the public unless there is evidence to the contrary.

### Article 12..

Co-authors are the owners of the copyright in a joint work. Co-authors must exercise their rights by mutual and written agreement. In case of disagreement, they shall file a lawsuit to the court.

### Article 13..

A natural or legal person who is the owner of a collective work and whose name is publicly disclosed at the time of publication of the work, that person shall be the beneficiary of the copyright unless there is evidence to the contrary.

### Article 14..

An author who uses a pseudonym or remains anonymous is the person who receives all rights in the work.

As long as the author has not disclosed his identity and has not provided evidence of his status, the first publisher or the first broadcaster shall represent the author and shall be entitled to exercise and protect the moral and economic rights of the author. The first publisher or broadcaster must have a contract specifying the identity, pseudonym or anonymity of the author.

Paragraph 2 shall not apply if the pseudonym chosen by the author has been published with the author's real name.

### Article 15..

A natural person or several persons who have created an audiovisual work through their creative talent shall be granted the status of authors of the audiovisual work.

Unless there is evidence to the contrary, the co-authors of an audiovisual work are:

a- The director.

b- The author of the screenplay.

c- The author of the adaptation.

d- The author of the spoken text.

e- The author of the musical compositions, with or without words, created specially for that work.

f- The author of the artistic drawings for animated works.

### Article 16..

The author of a work shall have the status of owner of the moral and economic rights. In the case where an author has created a work for the benefit of a natural or legal person who is the employer within the framework of an employment contract and a contract for the use of the work, the economic rights in that work shall be deemed transferred to the employer unless there are provisions to the contrary in those contracts.

### Article 17..

The producer of an audiovisual work who has obtained contributions from the co-authors to create the work by contract, excluding the authors of musical works, shall be the beneficiary of the economic rights of the co-authors unless there are provisions to the contrary in the contract. However, the co-authors shall retain their economic rights in their contributions to the extent that those contributions can be used separately from the audiovisual work.

## SECTION 3
## COPYRIGHT

### Article 18..

The author of a work shall be the beneficiary of the exclusive copyright in that work, which must be recognized by everyone. This right consists of moral and economic rights.

### Article 19..

The moral right of the author is perpetual, inalienable, imprescriptible and unseizable. This right can be transferred to the heirs of the author upon the death of the author or to a third party by means of a will. In case there is no heir, the State represented by the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts shall be in charge of this right.

### Article 20..

The moral right of the author has three components as follows:

a- The author has the exclusive right to decide on the manner and time of...

publication of his work to the public and the conditions for managing that publication.

b- The author has the right to require that his name, status and work be associated with him in relations with the public.

c- The author has the right to object to any distortion, mutilation or modification of the content which would be prejudicial to his honor or reputation.

### Article 21..

Economic rights are the rights to exploit the author's work through reproduction, communication to the public and creation of derivative works.

Except as provided in Articles 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 and 29 of this law, the author has the exclusive right to do or to authorize the following:

a- Translation of his work into a foreign language.

b- Adaptation, arrangement or other transformation of the work.

c- Rental of the original or a copy of an audiovisual work, a phonogram, a computer program, a database or a musical work in the form of a score.

d- Public distribution by sale, rental or any other means of the original or a copy of the work which has not yet been sold or transferred.

e- Importation into the country of copies of the author's work.

f- Reproduction of the work.

g- Public performance of the work.

h- Public display of the work.

i- Broadcasting of the work.

j- Communication of the work to the public by any other means.

The right to rent referred to in point (c) of this Article shall not apply to...

computer programs in the case where the program itself is not the essential object of the rental.

### Article 22..

The economic rights in a computer program and its preparatory design materials created by one or more employees in the scope of their duties or following the instructions of their employer shall belong to the employer unless there are provisions to the contrary in the contract.

Any person who hires another person to create a computer program for him based on a procurement contract, that person shall be the beneficiary of the economic rights.

## SECTION 4
## LIMITATIONS ON COPYRIGHT

### Article 23..

A natural person may import one copy of a work for his personal use without the authorization of the author or the owner of the copyright in that work.

### Article 24..

The reproduction of a single copy of a published work for the private use of the user shall be permitted without the authorization of the author or the owner of the copyright.

The permission referred to in the above paragraph of this Article shall not apply to the reproduction of:

a- Architectural works in the form of buildings or other structures.

b- Reprographic reproduction of an entire book or a substantial part of a book or a musical work in the form of a score.

c- Reproduction of a whole or a substantial part of a database in digital form.

d- Computer program, except for a back-up copy of the program to prevent loss.

e- Any work in a manner that affects the normal exploitation of the work or causes unreasonable prejudice to the legitimate interests of the author or the owner of copyright.

### Article 25..

The author cannot prohibit:

a- Private performance of a work without charging fees, within a family or circle of relatives and friends.

b- The making of a copy of a work by a library for preservation or for research purposes.

c- Use of the work for education without charging fees.

d- Translation of the work from Khmer into an ethnic minority language or from an ethnic minority language into Khmer.

Provided that the author's name and the source of the work are clearly mentioned, the author cannot prohibit the following:

- Analysis and commentary on the work for the purpose of criticism, polemic, teaching, science or information concerning that work.

- Publication of press reviews.

- Publication of the whole or a part of speeches delivered in public in the press or through broadcasting.

- Parody, caricature or pastiche based on the original work.

- Reproduction of artistic works or works of fine arts located in a public place, unless the reproduction is the main subject of the copying.

### Article 26..

The author of a film, video, play or novel concerning the life of a specific person or family must obtain prior authorization from that person or his heirs.

### Article 27..

Temporary reproduction of a work is permitted only if the reproduction is made during the use of the work authorized by the owner of the copyright.

### Article 28..

Notwithstanding Article 21, the brief quotation of a work which has already been lawfully published is permitted to be incorporated into another work without the author's authorization and without payment. The quotation must mention the source and the author's name if the name appears in the source. This quotation shall not exceed the purpose to be achieved.

### Article 29..

Notwithstanding Article 21, the following are permitted:

a- Use of a lawfully published work to illustrate in newspapers or in radio and television broadcasts for the purpose of education without payment.

...payment, but the source and the author's name must be mentioned if they appear in the source.

b- Reproduction of standalone articles, articles published in newspapers or magazines, or short excerpts of any work lawfully published. This reproduction shall be made by technical means without commercial profit for the purpose of teaching or examination in educational institutions. The reproduction of articles or short excerpts does not require payment, but the source and the author's name must be mentioned if they appear in the source.

## SECTION 5
## DURATION OF PROTECTION OF ECONOMIC RIGHTS

### Article 30..

Economic rights shall be protected starting from the date the work was created. The duration of this protection shall be the life of the author and 50 (fifty) years after his death.

For a collaborative work, the duration of protection of economic rights shall be the life of the last surviving author and 50 (fifty) years after his death.

### Article 31..

For a work published anonymously or under a pseudonym, the duration of protection of economic rights shall be 75 (seventy-five) years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was first lawfully published with the authorization of the owner of the copyright.

If the work has not been published for the first time within 50 (fifty) years after its creation, the duration of protection shall be 100 (one hundred) years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was created.

If the identity of the author is revealed before the expiration of the term of protection or becomes undoubtedly known to the public, the provisions of Article 30 shall apply.

The duration of protection for collective works, audiovisual works or posthumous works shall be 75 (seventy-five) years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was first lawfully published.

If the work has not been published within 50 (fifty) years after its creation, the duration of protection shall be 100 (one hundred) years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was created.

## SECTION 6
## TRANSFER OF ECONOMIC RIGHTS

### Article 32..

The transfer of any of the rights referred to in Article 21 of this law shall not be deemed as a transfer of other rights.

If there is a contract for the transfer or licensing of any right referred to in this Article, the effect of the transfer or licensing shall be in accordance with the terms of exploitation as provided in the contract.

### Article 33..

The management of economic rights may be transferred to the heirs of the author upon the author's death or to a third party by means of a will.

In case there is no heir or no will, the State represented by the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts shall be in charge of this right.

## SECTION 7
## EXPLOITATION OF ECONOMIC RIGHTS

### Article 34..

All contracts for the exploitation of economic rights must be made in writing, otherwise they shall be null and void.
Only the author or the owner of copyright has the right to invoke the nullity of the contract.

### Article 35..

The contract for the transfer of the author's exploitation rights must be in writing and must specify separately each right, the extent, purpose, place and duration of the transfer.

### Article 36..

Within the framework of a collective work, each author may exploit his contribution separately unless there are provisions to the contrary, but that author shall not cause prejudice to the exploitation of the collective work.
For a joint work, the exploitation can be made only with the agreement of the co-authors. In case of disagreement, the court shall decide.

### Article 37..

The transfer of the author's exploitation rights can be made in whole or in part. The owner of the copyright shall receive remuneration for this transfer in accordance with the provisions...

...of the transfer contract.

## SECTION 8
## DEPOSIT AND REGISTRATION OF WORKS

### Article 38..

All works shall be protected automatically under this law. The author or the owner of copyright may deposit their works at the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts.

### Article 39..

Registration is voluntary at the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts. The registration must record the real name of the author, the date of first publication of the work and the date of creation of the work, as well as the copyright of the author.

### Article 40..

The Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts shall issue a certificate for the registered work. The applicant for registration shall pay a fee in accordance with a joint proclamation of the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts and the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# CHAPTER 3
# RELATED RIGHTS

## SECTION 1
## RIGHTS OF PERFORMERS

### Article 41..

A performer has the exclusive right to do or to authorize:

a- The broadcasting and communication to the public of his performance...

of his performance, except where the broadcasting or the communication is made from a fixation of the performance which has been authorized by the performer or which is a re-broadcasting authorized by the broadcasting organization which made the first broadcasting.

b- Fixation of his unfixed performance on a phonogram.

c- Reproduction of a fixation of his performance on a phonogram.

d- Public distribution by sale or other transfer of ownership of the performance reproduced on a phonogram that has not yet been authorized for distribution.

e- Rental or lending to the public of the original or a copy of his performance on a phonogram.

Unless otherwise agreed, the performer has the right to:

- Authorize the broadcasting by a broadcasting organization, but other broadcasting organizations do not have the right to broadcast that performance.

- Authorize the broadcasting by a broadcasting organization, but that organization does not have the right to fix that performance.

### Article 42..

Regardless of the transfer of economic rights, the performer shall have the right to require that his name be associated with his live performance or with the fixation of his performance, except where the manner of use of the performance does not require the mention of his name. The performer also has the right to object to any distortion, mutilation or other modification of his performance which would be prejudicial to his reputation.

### Article 43..

Performers cannot prohibit the reproduction and communication to the public of their performance if the performance is incidental or secondary to the main subject of a play, work or audiovisual document.

## SECTION 2
## RIGHTS OF PHONOGRAM PRODUCERS

### Article 44..

A phonogram producer has the exclusive right to fix, reproduce or communicate to the public his phonogram.

### Article 45..

Any reproduction, sale, exchange, rental or communication to the public of a phonogram must be authorized by the phonogram producer.

The phonogram producer has the right to distribute to the public by sale or transfer of ownership of the original or a copy in the case where the phonogram producer has not yet authorized any person to distribute it.

The phonogram producer has the right to import copies of his phonogram for the purpose of communicating it to the public.

## SECTION 3
## RIGHTS OF VIDEO PRODUCERS

### Article 46..

A video producer is a natural or legal person who takes the initiative and has the responsibility for the fixation of images, with or without sound, in order to produce a video.

Any reproduction of a video for communication to the public, sale, exchange or rental must be authorized by the video producer.

The transfer of the video producer's rights as provided in this Article cannot be made separately from the rights of the authors and performers who have collaborated in the production of the video.

## SECTION 4
## RIGHTS OF BROADCASTING ORGANIZATIONS

### Article 47..

A broadcasting organization has the exclusive right to do or to authorize the fixation of its broadcasts, the communication to the public, the re-broadcasting, the reproduction, the distribution or the first rental of copies of its broadcasts.

### Article 48..

The reproduction of the broadcasts of a broadcasting organization for the purpose of sale, rental, exchange, broadcasting or communication to the public in any place must be authorized by the broadcasting organization.

## SECTION 5
## REMUNERATION

### Article 49..

If a phonogram produced for commercial purposes or a reproduction of such phonogram is used for broadcasting or for any other communication to the public or for public performance, the performers and the phonogram producers shall be entitled to a single equitable remuneration to be paid by the user to the collective management organization.

The collective management organization is a legal person whose duties regarding this remuneration are determined by sub-decree.

## SECTION 6
## LIMITATIONS ON RIGHTS

### Article 50..

Notwithstanding Articles 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47 and 48 of this law, the following acts shall be permitted without the authorization of the right holders and without payment:

a- Reporting on current events, provided that the report uses brief excerpts of the performance, the phonogram or the broadcast.

b- Reproduction made solely for the purpose of scientific research.

c- Reproduction for the purpose of education, except for a performance or a phonogram produced solely for educational purposes.

d- Brief excerpts of a performance, a phonogram or a broadcast consistent with fair practice for the purpose of information.

e- Any other use which constitutes an exception to the protection of copyright under this law.

f- The capture of image and sound for re-broadcasting in whole or in part by a broadcasting organization of the news of a festival, meeting or other national events from the original station at the same time.

### Article 51..

Article 41 shall not apply when a performer has authorized the fixation of his performance in an audiovisual fixation.

### Article 52..

Article 41 of this law shall not apply to any broadcasting organization that makes fixations and reproductions by its own means for its own broadcasts for commercial purposes.

All reproductions or copies made under the above paragraph of this Article must be destroyed within 6 (six) months after the reproduction, except for a single copy which may be kept for archival purposes.

## SECTION 7
## DURATION OF PROTECTION

### Article 53..

1- The duration of protection for performers shall be 50 (fifty) years from the end of the calendar year in which the performance was fixed on a phonogram, or in the case where the performance was not fixed on a phonogram, from the end of the calendar year in which the performance took place.

2- The duration of protection for phonogram producers shall be 50 (fifty) years from the end of the calendar year in which the phonogram was published, or in the case where the phonogram was not published, from the end of the calendar year in which the fixation of the phonogram was made.

3- The duration of protection for the broadcasts of broadcasting organizations shall be 50 (fifty) years from the end of the calendar year in which the broadcast took place.

## SECTION 8
## TRANSFER OF RIGHTS

### Article 54..

Articles 32, 33 and 34 of this law shall apply to the rights of performers, phonogram producers and broadcasting organizations.

## SECTION 9
## DEPOSIT

### Article 55..

Articles 38, 39 and 40 of this law shall apply to the rights of performers, phonogram producers and broadcasting organizations.

# CHAPTER 4
# COLLECTIVE MANAGEMENT OF RIGHTS

### Article 56...

Authors and owners of related rights may establish a collective management organization to protect and manage their economic rights.

The establishment of a collective management organization for the rights of authors, performers, phonogram producers or video producers must be recognized by the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts.

A collective management organization for the rights of radio, television or cable television of a broadcasting organization must be recognized by the Ministry of Information.

# CHAPTER 5
# DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND PENAL PROVISIONS

### Article 57..

Any person who has suffered or is likely to suffer an infringement of his copyright or related rights may file a lawsuit to the court to:

a- Prohibit the infringement of his rights, if the infringement is imminent.

b- Order the defendant to stop the infringement if the activity continues. The plaintiff may also demand the defendant to pay damages, compensate for losses, return the objects, equipment or materials involved in the case, and return the profits derived from the illegal act.

### Article 58..

The court has the jurisdiction to seize or destroy the objects, equipment or materials that have been produced, used or put into circulation illegally, or the equipment used for the infringement which is in the possession of the defendant or being kept by him.

### Article 59..

The court has the jurisdiction to take necessary provisional measures to ensure the preservation of evidence produced from the illegal reproduction of any work.

The plaintiff has the obligation to compensate for the damages caused to the defendant in the case where the lawsuit is found by the court to be without merit.

### Article 60..

Within 30 (thirty) days from the date of seizure of equipment or materials, the owner or the third party who is the possessor of the seized equipment or materials may apply to the court to lift the seizure or to limit the effect of that seizure.

### Article 61..

If the seizure of evidence, including equipment or materials, is made without a lawsuit being filed to the court within 30 (thirty) days from the date of seizure, the court has the jurisdiction to lift the seizure upon the request of the owner or the third party who is the possessor.

### Article 62..

In accordance with Articles 64 and 65 of this law, the following acts shall also be considered as offenses:

1- The production or importation for sale or rental of any device or means produced to circumvent any effective technological measure that controls access to a work, phonogram or broadcast, or limits the number of reproductions of those works or their quality.

2- The production or importation for sale or rental of any device or means that allows persons without rights to receive encrypted program-carrying signals transmitted by radio, television or other means of public communication, including satellite.

3- The removal or alteration of any electronic rights management information without the authorization of the owner of the copyright.

4- The distribution or importation for distribution, broadcasting, communication to the public or making available to the public of works or performances...

...or phonogram or broadcast of a broadcasting organization without authorization, and the person knows that the electronic rights management information has been removed or altered.

The term "rights management information" means:

- Information which identifies the author, the work, the performer, the performance of the performer, the producer of the phonogram, the phonogram, the broadcasting organization and the broadcast.

- Information which identifies the owner of any right, or information about the terms and conditions of use of the work and other products covered by this law, and any numbers or codes that represent such information.

### Article 63...

Upon written request of the owner of copyright or related rights, the Custom and Excise Department may suspend the clearance of goods which the owner of the rights claims to be counterfeit. The Custom and Excise Department shall immediately notify the competent court, the competent authorities, the plaintiff and the possessor of the goods of such suspension.

Unless otherwise provided in the customs regulations, the suspension of clearance shall be legally lifted if, within 10 (ten) working days from the date of notification of the suspension, the plaintiff has not provided sufficient evidence to the Custom and Excise Department that:

- An application has been made to the court for provisional measures for the preservation of goods as provided in Article 59 of this law.

- A lawsuit has been filed to the competent court accompanied by a sufficient guarantee to cover the liability of the plaintiff that may eventually arise...

...arise.

The plaintiff shall compensate for the damage caused by the suspension of customs clearance of goods if his lawsuit is found by the court to be without merit.

The provisions regarding border measures in the "Law on Marks, Trade Names and Acts of Unfair Competition" shall also apply in this Article.

### Article 64.

The infringement of copyright as determined in this law through production, reproduction, performance or communication of a work by any means is an offense punishable by law.

The offense of production or reproduction is punishable by a fine of 5,000,000 (five million) Riels to 25,000,000 (twenty-five million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 12 (twelve) months. Double penalties apply in case of recidivism.

Importing or exporting any product produced through illegal reproduction is punishable by a fine of 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 12 (twelve) months. Double penalties apply in case of recidivism.

The offense of performance or communication to the public is punishable by a fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 5,000,000 (five million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 3 (three) months. In case of multiple offenses, the penalty shall be multiplied by the number of offenses. In case of recidivism, double penalties of the previous sentence shall apply.

### Article 65..

The production or reproduction without authorization of performers, phonogram producers, video producers or broadcasting organizations is punishable by a fine...

...of 5,000,000 (five million) Riels to 25,000,000 (twenty-five million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 12 (twelve) months. Double penalties apply in case of recidivism.

Importing or exporting phonograms, performances or video performances without the authorization of performers, phonogram producers, video producers or broadcasting organizations is punishable by a fine of 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 3 (three) months. Double penalties apply in case of recidivism.

Broadcasting without the authorization of performers, phonogram producers, video producers or broadcasting organizations is punishable by a fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels and/or imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 3 (three) months. Double penalties apply in case of recidivism.

### Article 66..

In all cases provided in Articles 64 and 65 of this law, the court has the jurisdiction to order:

- Seizure of all proceeds from the offense and equipment specifically used for the commission of the offense.

- Order that the seized equipment or materials be returned to the owner of the copyright or related rights regardless of the payment of damages.

- Order the destruction of the materials and equipment seized.

# CHAPTER 6
# APPLICATION OF INTERNATIONAL TREATIES

### Article 67..

The provisions of international treaties relating to copyright and...

...and related rights to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party shall be applied together with this law.

In case of conflict with the provisions of this law, the provisions of those international treaties shall prevail.

# CHAPTER 7
# TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

### Article 68..

Upon the entry into force of this law, the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts shall issue a proclamation to immediately stop all activities that conflict with this law.

Six months after the entry into force of this law, the penal provisions of Articles 64 and 65 shall apply to activities that are still in conflict with this law.

# CHAPTER 8
# FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 69..

Any provisions that are contrary to this law shall be null and void.
