### Promulgate

The Law on Drug Control, which was adopted by the National Assembly on November 25, 2011 at its 7th session of the 4th legislature, and which was reviewed and approved entirely without change by the Senate on December 16, 2011 at its 10th plenary session of the 2nd legislature, with the following full text:

# LAW
# ON
# DRUG CONTROL

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

## Article 1._ Purpose of this Law

This law aims to prevent, suppress, and combat drug offenses, control all legal activities related to drugs in the Kingdom of Cambodia, and ensure the implementation of the obligations of the Kingdom of Cambodia as a party to the United Nations conventions and protocols related to drugs.

## Article 2._ Goals of this Law

This law has the following goals:

- To crack down on the illegal cultivation, production, trafficking, circulation, storage, and distribution of drugs.

- To prevent and eliminate the illegal use of drugs.

- To treat, rehabilitate, and reintegrate drug addicts back into society.

- To protect and promote the well-being of the people.

- To contribute to the protection of public order and social security and safety.

## Article 3._ Scope of this Law

This law applies to all activities related to drugs in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

## Article 4._ Definitions

Technical terms in this law are defined as follows:

Drugs refers to narcotic substances, psychotropic substances, and all precursor chemicals defined in the tables annexed to this law.

Drug plants refers to opium poppy, coca bush, cannabis plants, and other plants containing narcotic substances defined by the Royal Government.

Narcotic substances refers to addictive substances, including narcotics and psychotropic substances obtained from production, synthesis, or semi-synthesis, as listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 annexed to this law.

Precursor chemicals refers to chemicals used in the process of producing narcotic substances, as defined in Table 4 annexed to this law.

Production refers to the transformation or separation of narcotic substances from plants, purification, conversion, mixing, or any other activities in order to obtain drugs.

Storage refers to possession, hiding, stockpiling, and other similar activities of drugs.

Transport refers to sending by any means leading to the movement from one place to another, from one person to another, or from one country to another of drugs, including mailing, circulation, import, or export.

Trafficking refers to offering for sale, selling, buying for sale or exchange, and the exchange of drugs.

Legal activities related to drugs refers to research, experimentation, production, transport, storage, trafficking, distribution, supply, use of drugs or cultivation of drug plants, and other activities permitted or licensed by competent authorities and subject to strict control measures in accordance with the provisions of the law.

Control of legal activities related to drugs refers to the authorization by this law to monitor, inspect, research, investigate, or audit all legal activities related to drugs in order to take measures to prevent or oppose the use of these legal activities for other purposes.

Drug addiction refers to the state of a person under the influence or dependency of drugs due to drug use.

Drug addict refers to a person who has used drugs and is in a state of drug addiction.

Net weight refers to the quantity of drugs, excluding mixtures and other substances that do not contain narcotic substances.

Medicines containing narcotic substances refers to types of therapeutic medicines containing narcotic substances listed in Tables 2 and 3, and as defined by the Proclamation of the Minister of Health.

Harm reduction services refers to service programs or activities that benefit in reducing the harmful health consequences for humans, communities, economy, and society as a whole related to drug use.

Controlled delivery refers to the technique of allowing the transport, transit, or mailing of goods suspected of containing drugs, equipment that can be used for trafficking, or illegal money or suspected illegal money into the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia, or through the Kingdom of Cambodia to other countries, with the knowledge and monitoring and control of competent authorities for the purpose of identifying the source and identity of other persons involved, including the leaders of criminal groups in committing offenses provided for in this law.

## Article 5._ Classification of Drugs

Drugs are classified as follows:

Table 1: Plants and substances causing severe harm and having no medical utility.

Table 2: Plants and substances causing severe harm but having medical utility.

Table 3: Plants and substances causing harm but having medical utility.

Table 4: Precursor chemicals classified in the United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances 1988 or in the implementation of this convention against illicit traffic in drugs.

This Table 4 is divided into Table 4 (I) and Table 4 (II).

These 4 tables above are detailed in the annex tables of this law.

## Article 6._ Modification of Classification

The 4 tables mentioned in Article 5 (Classification of Drugs) above can be modified, including the inclusion of new drugs, deletion, or transfer from one table to another, by a Proclamation of the Minister of Health after consulting the National Authority for Combating Drugs.

# Chapter 2
# Institutions for Combating Drugs

## Section 1
## National Authority for Combating Drugs

## Article 7._ National Authority for Combating Drugs

The National Authority for Combating Drugs is the institution implementing the policies of the Royal Government in combating drugs.

The National Authority for Combating Drugs has a General Secretariat as its staff.

The General Secretariat of the National Authority for Combating Drugs is the central hub for coordinating drug combat activities within the framework of implementing all decisions of the National Authority for Combating Drugs.

The establishment, organization, and functioning of this National Authority shall be determined by Royal Decree.

## Section 2
## Anti-Drug Department

## Article 8._ Roles of the Anti-Drug Department

The Anti-Drug Department of the General Commissariat of National Police of the Ministry of Interior collects all information that can facilitate the investigation and suppression of illegal drug offenses and coordinates all operations within the country and internationally to suppress drug offenses.

## Section 3
## Institutions for Controlling Legal Activities Related to Drugs

## Article 9._ Establishment of the Commission for Controlling Legal Activities Related to Drugs

In case of necessity, an Inter-Ministerial Commission may be established as an institution to control all legal activities related to drugs in accordance with the provisions of this law.

The establishment, organization, and functioning of the Commission for Controlling Legal Activities Related to Drugs shall be determined by sub-decree upon the request of the Chairman of the National Authority for Combating Drugs.

# Chapter 3
# Legal Activities Related to Drugs

## Section 1
## General Provisions

## Article 10._ Legal Activities Related to Drugs

Cultivation of drug plants, production, transport, storage, trafficking, distribution, supply, use of drugs, and other activities related to drugs as listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 are strictly prohibited in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia, except for those permitted or licensed directly by competent authorities and subject to strict control measures in accordance with the provisions of the law.

## Article 11._ Conditions for Issuing Licenses or Permits Related to Drugs

Licenses or permits for performing any activities related to drugs as stated in Article 10 (Legal Activities Related to Drugs) above can only be issued if those activities are limited to research, experimentation, science, or for medical or industrial purposes only.

For the licenses, they must have a validity period not exceeding 2 (two) years. These licenses can be renewed. The license must clearly state the types of activities related to drugs that are permitted in accordance with this law. The license must clearly specify the location for performing the activities.

The issuance of licenses or permits requires verification of the background and professional necessity of the applicant. A license shall not be issued to any person who has been convicted of an offense related to drugs or a money laundering offense related to drug activities.

The forms, procedures, and conditions for granting, renewing, revoking, suspending, and types of licenses or permits shall be determined by the competent authorities in issuing these licenses in accordance with the provisions of this law.

## Article 12._ Authorization for Research Using Drugs

For the purpose of medical, scientific, or forensic research, the Minister of Health may authorize a person from any organization to cultivate, produce, buy, use, or store drugs as listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 of this law in a quantity strictly limited to the actual necessary amount.

The authorized person must record in a registry the quantity of drugs they cultivate, produce, buy, use, and destroy, as well as the dates of operations and the names of suppliers. This registry must be kept for at least 10 (ten) years.

Every year, the authorized person must report to the Minister of Health the quantity used or destroyed and the quantity in stock.

## Article 13._ Conditions for Authorization to Distribute, Supply, and Sell Medicines Containing Narcotic Substances

The purchase of medicines containing narcotic substances as listed in Tables 2 and 3 of this law for professional supply purposes can only be done with private individuals or state enterprises that have a license issued by the competent ministry.

Natural and legal persons who can purchase and possess medicines containing narcotic substances as listed in Tables 2 and 3 for use in their professional needs without having to apply for a license or permit include:

1- Licensed pharmacists practiced as agents or employees of a natural or legal person who has a license for the legal distribution of medicines containing narcotic substances listed in Tables 2 and 3.

2- Licensed pharmacists working in hospitals or private or public health centers that have a license to distribute medicines containing narcotic substances while performing their duties in the normal course of commercial operations as agents or employees of those hospitals or health centers.

3- Licensed pharmacists in charge of central public or private management who have a license to distribute medicines containing narcotic substances.

4- Hospitals or health centers that do not have a pharmacist in charge in cases of emergency and unforeseen events, provided that any qualified doctor of that institution who has a license to practice and for distributing medicines containing narcotic substances has agreed to be responsible for the management of those stocks.

Doctors, dentists, veterinarians, midwives, and nurses who have a license to practice may purchase and possess quantities of medicines containing narcotic substances according to the necessary needs as listed in the list defined by the Ministry of Health.

The provision of drugs listed in Tables 2 and 3 to humans and animals can only be done if the use is in the form of medicines according to a prescription from any of the following professionals:

1- Doctors who have a license to practice and a license to distribute medicines containing narcotic substances for treating patients in their professional practice.

2- Dentists who have a license to practice and a license to distribute medicines containing narcotic substances for any type of dental treatment.

3- Veterinarians who have a license to practice and a license to distribute medicines containing narcotic substances for treating animals.

4- Nurses or midwives who have a license to practice for treatments related to their professional duties and within the limits set by the Ministry of Health.

In cases of urgent necessity and under the conditions they define, the Minister of Health may authorize licensed pharmacists or other licensed distributors or retailers supplying across the entire national territory or a part of it, to provide without a prescription a small quantity of one or more types of medicines containing narcotic substances as listed in Table 3.

The Minister of Health may define other rules related to the implementation of this article, especially rules related to writing and filling out prescriptions for medicines containing narcotic substances as listed in Tables 2 and 3.

## Section 2
## Competent Institutions for Issuing Licenses or Permits Related to Drugs

## Article 14._ Licenses or Permits for Legal Activities Related to Drugs in Tables 1, 2, and 3

The Ministry of Health is granted the right to issue licenses or permits and to control all legal activities related to drugs as listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 annexed to this law.

If there is a necessity to use drugs as listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 above for industrial production purposes, the Ministry of Health may also grant licenses or permits. In this case, the applicant must clearly and sufficiently demonstrate that the use of these drugs is necessary for the industrial production process and

The drugs permitted for use, mixing, or creation of any industrial products shall not be easily extractable from these industrial products. The applicant must attach a certificate from the Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy to the license application.

## Article 15._ Licenses or Permits for Legal Activities Related to Drugs in Table 4

The Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy is granted the right to issue licenses or permits and to control all legal activities related to drugs as listed in Table 4 annexed to this law for industrial production purposes only.

Apart from industrial production purposes, the issuance of licenses or permits and the control of all legal activities related to drugs as listed in Table 4 is the responsibility of the Ministry of Health.

## Section 3
## Control of Legal Activities Related to Drugs

## Article 16._ Control Measures for Mixed Products

For products mixed with one or more types of drugs, whether in solid or liquid mixture form, they shall be subject to the same management and control measures as the drugs that are ingredients, in accordance with the provisions of this law.

## Article 17._ Prohibition of Commercial Advertising

The following are prohibited:

- Commercial advertising of plants, substances, and ingredients or medicines as listed in Tables 2 and 3 of this law.

- Providing samples or drug treatments to individuals, providing plant samples, substance samples, or ingredient samples as listed in Tables 2 and 3 of this law.

The Minister of Health shall issue additional instructions related to the advertising of medicines containing substances as listed in Table 4.

## Article 18._ Control of Transit or Stopover of Drugs

The transit and stopover of drugs as listed in Tables 2 and 3 on the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia are prohibited, whether or not the drugs are unloaded or not unloaded from the means of transport, except for the purchase, transport, and storage for producing medicines or finished products for treating humans or animals which must be under authorization or license and control by the Ministry of Health.

## Article 19._ Provisions for Precursor Chemicals in Table 4

The production, export, import, possession, sale, purchase, or distribution of precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4 must comply with the following provisions:

1- Authorization for import or export must be denied if the goods are intended to be delivered for the illegal production of drugs.

2- Imported or exported goods that are substances listed in Table 4 annexed to this law must be clearly labeled according to existing regulations.

3- Any person who becomes aware of any economic, industrial, or professional information or commercial processes of precursor chemicals listed in Table 4 due to their professional requirements shall not disclose it to a third party.

4- Producers, importers, exporters, wholesalers, and retailers of precursor chemicals listed in Table 4 must notify the police or competent officers of purchase orders or business transactions that appear suspicious, especially due to the quantity of substances being purchased or ordered multiple times, the frequency of orders or purchases, or the means used for payment or transport.

5- If there is a clue showing that any precursor chemical listed in Table 4 is to be used for the illegal production of drugs, the substance shall be seized immediately pending the results of the court investigation.

## Article 20._ Information to be Reported

The Minister of Industry, Mines and Energy shall provide reports to the National Authority for Combating Drugs and copy to the Ministry of Health the following information:

1- Issuance, renewal, revocation, and suspension of licenses under their competence.

2- Issuance, renewal, revocation, and suspension of permits, especially permits related to export and import with specifications of precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

3- Actual quantity of imported and exported precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

4- Estimated annual quantity planned for import and export of precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

5- Actual quantity of planned export and import of precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

6- Information received related to precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

The Minister of Health shall provide reports to the National Authority for Combating Drugs the information about the import, export, and stock of precursor chemicals as listed in Table 4.

## Article 21._ Determination of the Quantity of Drug Requirements

The Minister of Health shall determine once a year the maximum quantity of drugs actually required and produced by state and private enterprises according to actual needs and shall report to the National Authority for Combating Drugs once a year the predetermined annual quantity of drugs and the quantity used, as well as the remaining stock.

The Minister of Industry, Mines and Energy shall determine once a year the maximum quantity of precursor chemicals according to actual needs and shall report to the National Authority for Combating Drugs and copy to the Minister of Health once a year the predetermined annual quantity of precursor chemicals and the quantity used, as well as the remaining stock.

## Article 22._ Obligation to Register

Producers, importers, exporters, wholesalers, and retailers who have received a license or permit to perform legal activities related to drugs from the Minister of Health or from

The Minister of Industry, Mines and Energy must record in a registry with numbered pages and initials of the competent authority all purchases, sales, uses, and other operations affecting the quantity of substances listed in Tables 2, 3, and 4.

Entries in this registry must be made at the time of operation without gaps, erasures, or overwriting.

Entries in this registry must state the date of the operation, the name and quantity of products received or sold, and the name, address, and occupation of the buyer or seller.

This registry must be kept for 10 (ten) years starting from the date of the last entry according to the conditions set by the Commission for Controlling Legal Activities Related to Drugs.

The Minister of Health and the Minister of Industry, Mines and Energy shall issue additional instructions on the preparation of this registry.

## Article 23._ Inventory and Balancing

State enterprises, private enterprises, medical and scientific institutions, and other persons performing activities or holding an operating license and permit in accordance with the provisions of this law must conduct at the beginning of each year an inventory of drugs listed in Tables 1, 2, and 3 in their possession and compare it with the previous stock balance which must be calculated together with the quantity held in the latest inventory.

License holders, pharmacists, and all persons who have received a license or permit for distributing medicines containing narcotic substances through wholesalers, pharmacies, or sub-pharmacies must conduct an inventory and balancing as stated in the first paragraph of this article.

Any discrepancy noted in the results of the balance comparison or between the balance results and the inventory, the license holder, pharmacist, or recipient of the license or permit for distributing medicines containing narcotic substances must immediately report to the Ministry of Health, which shall acknowledge receipt of this notification.

## Article 24._ Packaging and Labeling

The distribution of drugs listed in Tables 2 and 3 is possible only if those drugs are packed in packages or boxes that bear their name, and in the case of drug products listed in Table 2, the package or box must be sealed, stamped, and packed with a safety tie.

The outer packaging, as stated in the first paragraph above, shall not contain any information other than the name and address of the sender and the recipient. These packages must be sealed and stamped with the sender's seal.

Labels on products containing any medicines with narcotic substances offered for sale must indicate the name of the drug ingredients together with the percentage and weight of those substances.

Labels attached to packages for retail or distribution as stated in the first paragraph above must indicate the instructions for use, as well as necessary precautions and warnings for the safety of the user.

In case of necessity, additional conditions regarding packaging and labeling shall be determined by Proclamation of the Minister of Health.

## Article 25._ Control and Inspection

All individuals, private enterprises, state enterprises, medical institutions, pharmaceutical institutions, and scientific institutions performing any activities or operations that have a license or permit in accordance with the provisions of this law shall be under the control and monitoring of the Minister of Health, who shall assign pharmaceutical inspectors to conduct regular inspections at least once a year and extraordinary inspections as necessary on the institutions, storage sites, and records. The inspectors of the Ministry of Health may request necessary assistance to conduct inspections.

The first aid kits kept on public transport means performing international transport shall also be under the above control and monitoring.

All individuals, companies, factories, or enterprises performing any activities or operations that have a license or permit according to the first paragraph of Article 15 (Licenses or Permits for

Legal Activities Related to Drugs in Table 4) of this law shall be under the control and monitoring of the Minister of Industry, Mines and Energy, who shall assign inspectors to conduct regular inspections at least once a year and extraordinary inspections as necessary on the institutions, storage sites, and records. The inspectors of the Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy may request necessary assistance to conduct inspections.

The forms and procedures of control and inspection shall be determined by a Proclamation of the respective ministry.

## Article 26._ Control and Monitoring at Packaging Ports and Free Zones

The control and monitoring measures provided for in this law shall also be implemented at packaging ports and in free zones.

# Chapter 4
# Penal Provisions

## Section 1
## General Provisions

## Article 27._ Application of the General Provisions of the Penal Code

The provisions of Book 1 (General Provisions) of the Penal Code shall be applied, except where otherwise specifically provided for in this law.

## Article 28._ Sentencing

The provisions of Article 97 (Sentencing) of the Penal Code shall not be applied to the offenses provided for in this law. Sentencing shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of this law.

The principal penalties for all offenses provided for in this law cannot be replaced by alternative penalties or additional penalties under conditions provided for in the Penal Code.

## Article 29._ Definition of Attempt

An attempt to commit a felony or a misdemeanor is punishable when the following conditions are met:

- The perpetrator has started to execute the offense, meaning the perpetrator has committed an act intended directly to commit the offense, and

- The perpetrator has not voluntarily stopped their act but has been hindered or interrupted by the influence of circumstances beyond their will.

Preparatory acts that do not have a direct intention to commit an offense are not considered starting to execute.

An attempt to commit a felony or a misdemeanor shall be punished the same as a completed felony or misdemeanor.

## Article 30._ Order to Recieve Treatment

The court shall decide to order treatment as provided for in Chapter 6 (Treatment and Rehabilitation Measures) of this law for any person who is convicted of any offense provided for in this law if that person is in a state of drug addiction.

## Article 31._ Exemption from Punishment

Any person who has joined a criminal group to commit any offense provided for in this law shall be exempted from punishment as provided for in Article 54 (Participation in a Criminal Group) of this law if that person reports the existence of this criminal group to the competent authorities before the offense occurs with sufficient time to take measures against that offense and identified other participants.

## Article 32._ Mitigation of Punishment

Apart from the cases provided for in the above article, perpetrators, co-perpetrators, accomplices, or instigators of an offense provided for in Chapter 4 (Penal Provisions) of this law who have reported the identities of other participants before being charged or who have helped

facilitate the arrest of other persons after being charged, the punishment shall be reduced under the following conditions:

1- The maximum imprisonment penalty shall be reduced by half.

2- If the maximum penalty is life imprisonment, the maximum shall be reduced to 20 (twenty) years of imprisonment.

3- The minimum imprisonment penalty shall be reduced by half if this minimum exceeds 1 (one) day.

4- The minimum and maximum fine penalties shall be reduced by half.

In case of a felony charge, if the provisions of this article have the effect of reducing the maximum imprisonment penalty to a level equal to or less than 5 (five) years, the charged offense remains a felony.

## Article 33._ Application of Mitigating Circumstances

The provisions of Article 93 (Definition of Mitigating Circumstances) to Article 95 (Life Imprisonment and Mitigating Circumstances) of the Penal Code shall not be applied to felonies provided for in this law.

For misdemeanors provided for in this law, the court may decide to grant the accused mitigating circumstances under the conditions provided for in the Penal Code. In this case, the minimum imprisonment penalty for that offense shall be reduced by half if the minimum imprisonment penalty exceeds 1 (one) day.

## Article 34._ Additional Penalties for Natural Persons

In derogation to Article 54 (Conditions for Sentencing of Additional Penalties) of the Penal Code, for offenses provided for in this law, the court may pronounce one or more additional penalties as stated in Section 2 (Additional Penalties), Chapter 1 (Types of Penalties), Title 3 (Penalties), Book 1 (General Provisions) of the Penal Code.

## Article 35._ Criminal Responsibility of Legal Persons

Legal persons, with the exception of the state, may be declared criminally responsible for offenses provided for in this law under the conditions that these offenses have been committed by their organs or representatives for the benefit of those legal persons.

The criminal responsibility of legal persons does not exclude the criminal responsibility of natural persons for the same acts.

## Article 36._ Principal Penalty Applied to Legal Persons

In cases where the court declares a legal person criminally responsible, the court shall pronounce a fine as the principal penalty, which maximum amount is equal to 5 (five) times the maximum fine for natural persons provided for in this law.

## Article 37._ Additional Penalties Applied to Legal Persons

In derogation to Article 169 (Conditions for Decision on Additional Penalties) of the Penal Code, the court may pronounce one or more additional penalties as stated in Section 2 (Additional Penalties), Chapter 7 (Penalties Applicable to Legal Entities), Title 3 (Penalties), Book 1 (General Provisions) of the Penal Code.

## Section 2
## Offenses Related to Drugs in Tables 1 and 2

## Article 38._ Illegal Cultivation of Narcotic Plants

Any person who intentionally violates the provisions of this law and regulations related to the cultivation of narcotic plants listed in Tables 1 and 2:

1- Shall be punished by imprisonment from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years. In addition to the imprisonment penalty, the offender may be subject to a fine from 1,000,000 (one million) Rials to 4,000,000 (four million) Rials.

2- Shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials if the offense is committed for the purpose of distribution, production, or trade.

If the offense is committed for personal use in small quantities and committed within the framework of customary acquisition, the prosecutor may release the perpetrator from the charges. If this case reaches the court, the court may decide on the merits of the case not to punish or only to reprimand that person. Customary acquisition is considered an acquisition that does not cause addiction and the recipient is a resident who acquired it as their ancestors used to do.

## Article 39._ Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances

The extraction or isolation of narcotic substances from plants, purification, transformation, mixing, or any other activities carried out illegally to obtain drugs is the act of illegal production of narcotic substances.

The illegal production of narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years. In addition to the imprisonment penalty, the offender may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 5 (five) years to 10 (ten) years and shall be punished by a fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Rials if the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- The offense is committed multiple times.

2- The offense is committed within the framework of a function or on the occasion of performing a function.

3- If the production of heroin, opium resin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 400 (four hundred) grams to less than 800 (eight hundred) grams.

4- If the production of cannabis oil has a net quantity from 200 (two hundred) grams to less than 400 (four hundred) grams.

5- If the production of morphine, heroin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 4 (four) grams to less than 25 (twenty-five) grams.

6- If the production of amphetamines, methamphetamines, or ecstasy has a net quantity from 15 (fifteen) grams to less than 80 (eighty) grams.

7- If the production of other narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 has a net quantity from 15 (fifteen) grams to less than 80 (eighty) grams.

8- If the production of two or more types of narcotic substances, and the total net quantity of those substances is equal to the net quantity of narcotic substances specified in any point from point 3 to point 7 of this paragraph.

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 10 (ten) years to 20 (twenty) years and shall be punished by a fine from 20,000,000 (twenty million) Rials to 40,000,000 (forty million) Rials if the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- It has the nature of a profession.

2- If the production of heroin, opium resin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 800 (eight hundred) grams to less than 1,600 (one thousand six hundred) grams.

3- If the production of cannabis oil has a net quantity from 400 (four hundred) grams to less than 800 (eight hundred) grams.

4- If the production of morphine, heroin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 25 (twenty-five) grams to less than 80 (eighty) grams.

5- If the production of amphetamines, methamphetamines, or ecstasy has a net quantity from 80 (eighty) grams to less than 240 (two hundred forty) grams.

6- If the production of other narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 has a net quantity from 80 (eighty) grams to less than 240 (two hundred forty) grams.

7- If the production of two or more types of narcotic substances, and the total net quantity of those substances is equal to the net quantity of narcotic substances specified in any point from point 2 to point 6 in the 4th paragraph above.

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 20 (twenty) years to 30 (thirty) years or life imprisonment and shall be punished by a fine from 40,000,000 (forty million) Rials to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Rials if the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- If the production of heroin, opium resin, or cocaine has a net quantity of 1,600 (one thousand six hundred) grams or more.

2- If the production of cannabis oil has a net quantity of 800 (eight hundred) grams or more.

3- If the production of morphine, heroin, or cocaine has a net quantity of 80 (eighty) grams or more.

4- If the production of amphetamines, methamphetamines, or ecstasy has a net quantity of 240 (two hundred forty) grams or more.

5- If the production of other narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 has a net quantity of 240 (two hundred forty) grams or more.

6- If the production of two or more types of narcotic substances, and the total net quantity of those substances is equal to the net quantity of narcotic substances specified in any point from point 1 to point 5 of this paragraph.

## Article 40._ Illegal Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Narcotic Substances

Possession, hiding, stocking, and other similar activities carried out illegally with narcotic substances is the act of illegal possession of narcotic substances.

Sending in any form that results in displacement from one place to another, from one person to another, or from one country to another illegally with narcotic substances, including through sending, trafficking, importing, or exporting, is the act of illegal transport of narcotic substances.

Providing for sale, selling, buying for resale, exchanging, or trading illegally with narcotic substances is the act of illegal trafficking of narcotic substances.

Illegal possession, transport, or trafficking of narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years. In addition to the imprisonment penalty...

the offender may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

Shall be punished by imprisonment from 5 (five) years to 10 (ten) years and shall be punished by a fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Rials if the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- The offense is committed multiple times.

2- The offense is committed within the framework of a function or on the occasion of performing a function.

3- If opium resin, cannabis resin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 400 (four hundred) grams to less than 800 (eight hundred) grams.

4- If cannabis oil has a net quantity from 200 (two hundred) grams to less than 400 (four hundred) grams.

5- If morphine, heroin, or cocaine has a net quantity from 4 (four) grams to less than 25 (twenty-five) grams.

6- If amphetamine, methamphetamine, or ecstasy has a net quantity from 15 (fifteen) grams to less than 80 (eighty) grams.

7- If dried cannabis or coca leaf has a quantity from 8 (eight) kilograms to less than 20 (twenty) kilograms.

8- If fresh opium flower has a quantity from 8 (eight) kilograms to less than 40 (forty) kilograms.

9- If dried opium pod has a quantity from 40 (forty) kilograms to less than 160 (one hundred sixty) kilograms.

10- If other narcotic substances listed in Tables 1 and 2 have a net quantity from 15 (fifteen) grams to less than 80 (eighty) grams.

11- If two or more types of narcotic substances, and the total net quantity of those substances is equal to the net quantity of narcotic substances specified in any point from point 3 to point 10 of this paragraph.

## Section 7
## Other Offenses

## Article 58._ Receiving Proceeds of an Offense

Receiving proceeds of an offense is the act of receiving, hiding, keeping, or transferring any object knowing that the object is derived from a felony or misdemeanor under this law.

The following acts shall also be considered as receiving proceeds of an offense:

1- Acting as an intermediary to transfer any object, knowing that the object is derived from a felony or misdemeanor under this law.

2- Acts of receiving benefits from the proceeds of a felony or misdemeanor under this law with clear knowledge of the circumstances.

Receiving proceeds of an offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

Receiving proceeds of an offense shall be punished by imprisonment from 5 (five) years to 10 (ten) years and shall be subject to an additional fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Rials if the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- The offense is committed multiple times.

2- By using the facilities provided by professional activities.

## Article 59._ Participation in an Organized Criminal Group

An organized criminal group is a group of 3 (three) or more persons established with a systematic organization, whether formal or informal, in order to commit offenses under this law.

Participation in an organized criminal group shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

## Article 60._ Sentencing of Leaders of Organized Criminal Groups

In case an organized criminal group commits an offense as stated from Article 38 (Illegal Cultivation of Narcotic Plants) to Article 42 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), from Article 47 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances) to Article 50 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 56 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Equipment or Materials for the Purpose of Illegal Production or Consumption of Narcotic Substances), and Article 71 (Money Laundering) of this law, the leader of this group shall be punished as follows:

1- If the maximum imprisonment penalty for that offense is less than or equal to 10 (ten) years, the maximum imprisonment penalty and fine shall be doubled.

2- If the maximum imprisonment penalty for that offense is more than 10 (ten) years, the maximum imprisonment penalty is life imprisonment. In addition to the imprisonment penalty, the offender shall be subject to an additional fine from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Rials to 500,000,000 (five hundred million) Rials.

2- Causing serious harm.

## Article 64._ Violation of Secrecy of Monitoring Measures

Any person who knows, through their professional practice, about any measure provided in the first paragraph of Article 85 (Monitoring) of this law and intentionally informs the person subjected to those measures, shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

Any person who provides competent authorities with documents or letters specified in point 4 of the first paragraph of Article 85 (Monitoring) of this law, which they know to be forged, altered, or falsified, but fails to inform those authorities, shall be punished the same as provided in the first paragraph above.

## Article 65._ Obstruction of Inspection

Any person who obstructs pharmaceutical inspectors from performing their duties by any means shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 6 (six) months and may be subject to an additional fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Rials to 1,000,000 (one million) Rials.

## Article 66._ Violation of Provisional Measures to Guarantee Confiscation

Acts of destroying, hiding, or displacing objects or property that have been frozen or seized shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

## Article 67._ Non-compliance with Confiscation

Acts of destroying, hiding, or displacing confiscated objects or property shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Rials to 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials.

## Article 68._ Evasion of Mandatory Treatment Obligations

Any person who evades mandatory treatment measures as provided in Section 4 (Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation), Chapter 6 (Treatment and Rehabilitation Measures) of this law, shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and may be subject to an additional fine from 100,000 (one hundred thousand) Rials to 2,000,000 (two million) Rials.

## Section 8
## Provisions on Money Laundering Related to Drug Offenses

## Article 69._ General Provisions

The provisions of the Law on Anti-Money Laundering and Combating the Financing of Terrorism shall also apply to anti-money laundering measures derived from offenses as stated in this law.

## Article 70._ Definition of Money Laundering

The conversion, transfer, acquisition, possession, or use of property, the concealment or disguise of the true nature, source, location, disposition, movement, ownership, or rights with respect to property, knowing that such property is the proceeds of an offense stated from Article 39 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances) to Article 42 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), from Article 47 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances) to Article 50 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 54 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Precursor Chemicals), and Article 56 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Equipment or Materials for the Purpose of Illegal Production or Consumption of Narcotic Substances) of this law, for the purpose of concealing

or disguising the illegal origin of this property or assisting any person who has been involved in an offense so that the person evades the legal consequences of their actions is the act of money laundering.

The act of assisting in capital certification operations for concealing or for the direct or indirect conversion of a crime as referred to in the first paragraph above shall also be considered as an act of money laundering.

## Article 71._ Money Laundering Offense

Money laundering shall be punished by imprisonment from 2 (two) years to 5 (five) years and may be subject to an additional fine from 4,000,000 (four million) Rials up to the value of the funds or the value of the property that was the object of the money laundering.

In case the offense that is the source of the property or funds as the object of money laundering is punished with an imprisonment penalty heavier than the imprisonment penalty stated in the first paragraph above, money laundering shall be punished with the same imprisonment penalty as the offense that the perpetrator knew about, and in case the offense is associated with several aggravating circumstances, the perpetrator shall be punished based only on the aggravating circumstances they were aware of.

## Article 72._ Aggravating Circumstances

Money laundering shall be punished by imprisonment from 5 (five) years to 10 (ten) years and shall be subject to an additional fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Rials up to the value of the funds or the value of the property that was the object of money laundering in case the offense is committed under any of the following circumstances:

1- The offense is committed multiple times.

2- By using the facilities provided by professional activities.


# Chapter 5
# Provisions on Criminal Procedure

## Section 1
## General Provisions

## Article 73._ Application of Provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure

The provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure shall apply, except in cases specifically provided for in this chapter.

## Article 74._ Participation in Procedures

In the process of investigation and trial, the court shall summon and hear the testimony of police officers or judicial police agents who participated in the investigation and inquiry, in their capacity as witnesses.

The National Authority for Combating Drugs (NACD) or its legal representative may submit a request to the prosecutor to file an appeal against the decision of the investigating judge or the court.

The NACD or its legal representative may also submit a request to the General Prosecutor attached to the Court of Appeal or the Minister of Justice to exercise their power as provided in the Code of Criminal Procedure to issue orders for an appeal against the decision of the investigating judge or the court.

## Section 2
## Inquiry and Investigation Measures

## Article 75._ Search at Borders

Judicial police officers who have the authority to examine evidence of offenses as provided in this law are authorized to conduct searches at the borders on persons, vehicles, materials, etc., if there are signs leading to suspicion that those persons, vehicles, or materials contain narcotic substances.

The search of a person's body shall be conducted by a person of the same sex, in accordance with the regulations in force.

## Article 76._ Body Search Using Medical Search Techniques

When there are clues that lead to the assumption that a person within the border area is carrying drugs hidden in their body, the judicial police officer who has authority may place that person under the observation of a physician or a medical technician, etc., using medical search techniques, after receiving written consent from the person concerned.

In case the person concerned does not consent, the judicial police officer shall seek authorization from the prosecutor by any means. In this case, the judicial police officer must first receive authorization, even if it is verbal authorization. The prosecutor shall appoint a physician or medical technician to conduct an immediate examination. The results of the examination, including the physician's remarks on the person concerned and the examination process, shall be recorded in a report to be sent to the prosecutor.

## Article 77._ Inspection of Postal Services

Judicial police officers who have the authority to examine evidence of offenses as provided in this law are authorized to monitor at all times, day and night, postal services in order to search for illegal shipments of drugs.

When there are signs leading to suspicion of such shipments, the prosecutor or investigating judge in charge of the case may order postal officials to open the shipment, even if the order is verbal.

## Article 78._ Controlled Delivery

The transit through the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia of drugs, currencies, or instruments associated with currencies as stated in this law, which are sent illegally or suspected of being illegal, and which the authorities become aware of and monitor to examine evidence of offenses stated from Section 2 (Offenses Related to Narcotic Substances in Table 1 and Table 2) to Section 5 (Offenses Related to Equipment and Materials) of Chapter 4 (Criminal Provisions) of this law, may be allowed to pass in order to identify persons involved in the offense for prosecution.

Incitement for illegal sale of drugs conducted by competent public officials to examine evidence of an offense, whether directly or through any person acting under the instruction of those public officials, is also authorized for the same objective as mentioned above.

Incitement for illegal purchase of drugs conducted by competent officials to examine evidence of offenses as provided in this law is prohibited. Conversely, it shall be prosecuted as an offense of incitement as provided in Article 57 (Incitement to Commit an Offense) of this law, and the investigation shall be nullified, whether the official acts directly or through any person.

The forms, procedures, and detailed conditions for controlled delivery and incitement for illegal sale of drugs as specified in the first and second paragraphs above shall be determined in a Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

## Article 79._ Controlled Delivery and Incitement for Sale of Drugs

In case of necessity for drug offense investigations, competent officials appointed by the Minister of Justice or by the delegated official of the Ministry of Justice may decide to authorize the use of controlled delivery techniques or the use of incitement for illegal sale of drugs. Controlled delivery and incitement for the sale of drugs may be conducted by competent officials directly or through any person acting under the order of those competent officials.

The decision to use controlled delivery or incitement for illegal sale of drugs shall be made on a case-by-case basis and, if it is international in nature, based on agreements with other interested countries.

The decision to use controlled delivery shall be immediately notified to the competent authorities of the place of destination or place of entry into the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia. Furthermore, it shall be immediately notified to the competent authorities of the place of delivery or place of issuance.

The decision to authorize incitement for sale shall be immediately notified to the competent court of the place of sale.

The forms, procedures, and conditions for the application of controlled delivery and incitement for sale of drugs techniques as mentioned above shall be determined in a Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

## Article 80._ Coordination and Supervision of Controlled Delivery Operations

The authority appointed by a Prakas of the Minister of Justice or by the delegated official of the Ministry of Justice shall direct and supervise operations within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia and order interventions when the official deems it appropriate.

In case there are agreements with other interested countries or, where applicable, under financial agreements, those authorities or officials may decide to suspend the seizure of illegal evidence to allow it to pass intact, or to allow it to pass after having seized currencies, instruments associated with currencies, or drugs and replacing them with other products.

## Article 81._ Sealing and Preservation of Evidence

In addition to the provisions of Article 92 (Sealing of Exhibits), Article 113 (Search), and Article 160 (Sealing of Exhibits), Article 181 (Search and Seizure Conducted by Judicial Police Officers) of the Code of Criminal Procedure, exhibits suspected of being drugs shall be kept in packages, boxes, or cases and sealed.

The sealed packages, boxes, or cases shall be made in such a way as to prevent any theft or removal of the exhibits from them.

Each sealed package, box, or case shall be numbered and have written on its cover or on the seal information about the nature, source, and weight of the substance suspected of being a drug, as well as the number of packages bundled together.

In addition to the data to be recorded in the report as provided in the Code of Criminal Procedure, this report shall also include the following data:

1- Date and place of discovery.

2- Nature and weight of the substance suspected of being a drug.

3- Weighing method used.

4- Confirmation of testing and the results of those tests, if any.


Reports and inscriptions on each package, box, or case shall be signed by the person who participated in the packaging.

The storage of packages, boxes, or cases in any place, the change of storage place, or any modification of the original state of the packages, boxes, or cases shall be recorded in a report.

## Article 82._ Sampling of Substances Suspected of Being Drugs for Forensic Analysis

The competent judge shall immediately take at least 3 (three) samples or an amount as necessary in the presence of the person occupying the place or the person involved in the offense, or in the presence of 2 (two) witnesses if there is no occupant or person involved, to serve as evidence and to determine the purity of the substance suspected of being a drug that has been seized.

Each sample shall be placed in a sealed package. The nature and weight of the substance inside shall be noted on the package and the seal.

After sampling, the original package shall be resealed, and a report shall be made specifying the number of samples taken, the nature and weight of the substance suspected of being a drug in each package, and the changes made to the original labels and seals.

Reports and all inscriptions on each sample and the resealing shall be signed by the persons who participated in the sampling operation.

## Article 83._ Forensic Analysis Using Scientific Techniques

Forensic analysis using scientific techniques by experts recognized by the Ministry of Justice to determine the composition and activity level of the drug samples shall be conducted as soon as possible after sampling to avoid damage or degradation of the physical and chemical substances. The expert shall specify in their report the number of samples provided, the nature and weight of the drug in each sample, the number of samples used for forensic analysis, and, if any, the number of samples recovered after the forensic analysis and any changes to those samples.


## Article 84._ Handing Over or Destruction of Seized Drugs

After seizure and after taking sufficient samples for procedural purposes, those drugs shall be immediately confiscated and disposed of as follows:

1- Medicines containing narcotic substances that can be used shall be handed over to the Ministry of Health.

2- Drugs that can be used in pharmaceutical industry or other sectors shall be handed over to the Ministry of Health or the Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy, according to the respective use of those drugs.

3- Drugs that have no use shall be destroyed. The complete destruction of drugs shall be done as quickly as possible and by the most appropriate means in the presence of a commission established by a Prakas of the Minister of Justice.

The confiscation for handing over or destruction is the competence of:

1- The prosecutor, during the investigation phase.

2- The investigating jurisdiction, during the investigating phase.

3- The trial jurisdiction, during the trial phase.

When the investigation is concluded by a final decision of non-prosecution or when the trial jurisdiction has issued a final decision and has not decided on the confiscation for handing over or destruction, the prosecutor or general prosecutor has the competence to decide on a case-by-case basis.

## Article 85._ Monitoring

The prosecutor or the investigating judge in charge of a case involving an offense under the provisions of this law may authorize the competent authorities to:

- Monitor bank accounts or other accounts treated as bank accounts, or

- Require the inspection and order the provision or copying of authentic or private documents, the freezing of all bank, financial, and commercial records, or


- Order the interception and recording of all communications made by telecommunication means, such as facsimile or internet messages, etc., or

- Access and inspect computer systems.

If there are sufficient signs to suggest that those accounts, telecommunication means, or computer systems are being used by persons suspected of having committed or planning to commit an offense, or when those letters or documents are related or could be related to any offense provided in this law.

The measures above shall not be considered a violation of professional secrecy.

The order for interception and recording of all communications by telecommunication means may only be authorized for a period of 2 (two) months. The extension of these measures and the period may only be authorized by the investigating judge in charge of the case.

## Article 86._ Bank Secrecy

Bank professional secrecy is not a justification for refusing to provide evidence related to offenses provided in this law.

## Article 87._ Closing of Establishments and Temporary Prohibition of Business Activities

In case of prosecution for any offense stated in Article 39 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances), Article 40 (Illegal Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Narcotic Substances), Article 42 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances) to Article 44 (Facilitating Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 47 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances), Article 48 (Illegal Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Narcotic Substances), Article 50 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances) to Article 52 (Facilitating Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 54 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Precursor Chemicals), and Article 56 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Equipment or Materials for the Purpose of Illegal Production or Consumption of Narcotic Substances) of this law.


The investigating judge may prohibit activities in an establishment where an offense was committed on the occasion of such activities, or prohibit business activities in an establishment open to the public or used by the public if the owner or the person conducting business in that establishment participated in the commission of the offense mentioned above.

The temporary closure of an establishment or prohibition of business activities may be ordered for a maximum period of 6 (six) months.

This decision shall be implemented immediately, notwithstanding any appeal.

## Section 3
## Provisional Measures to Guarantee Confiscation

## Article 88._ Urgent Measures to Guarantee Confiscation

In case of investigation, prosecution, or inquiry into any offense provided in this law, measures to seize or freeze property belonging to the suspect or the accused, which are the proceeds of the offense or suspected of being used or intended for use in the commission of the offense, shall comply with the provisions of this section.

## Article 89._ Seizure of Property

The prosecutor shall seize property which are the proceeds of the offense or suspected of being used or intended for use in the commission of the offense and shall immediately apply to the investigating judge or the court, depending on the stage of prosecution, to freeze that property.

## Article 90._ Freezing of Property

The investigating judge or the court may, ex officio or upon request of the prosecutor, order the freezing of property if there are reasonable grounds to suspect that the property is the proceeds of an offense or suspected of being used or intended for use in the commission of the offense.

## Article 91._ Bail Bond

When property which is the proceeds of an offense or suspected on reasonable grounds of being the proceeds of an offense, or suspected of being used or intended for use in 


the commission of an offense is mixed with other property, the value of the property seized according to Article 89 (Seizure of Property) or frozen according to Article 90 (Freezing of Property) above shall not exceed the value of the property which are the proceeds of the offense or suspected of being used or intended for use in the commission of the offense.

## Article 92._ Burden of Proof for Persons Opposing the Decision

A person who requests the release of property that has been decided to be seized according to Article 89 (Seizure of Property) or frozen according to Article 90 (Freezing of Property) of this law shall bear the burden of proving the legality of that seized or frozen property to the judge in charge of the case.

Any person who objects to the decision of the investigating judge to freeze property according to Article 90 (Freezing of Property) of this law may appeal that decision to the investigating chamber.

## Section 4
## Confiscation Measures

## Article 93._ Mandatory Confiscation

Drugs that have been seized and not yet destroyed or handed over to the competent authorities shall be mandatorily confiscated, even if the offense is not prosecuted, the charge is dismissed, or the accused is acquitted or punished.

The provisions on the restitution of seized objects as provided in the Code of Criminal Procedure shall apply, according to the stage of the proceedings.

## Article 94._ Confiscation of Objects, etc.

In case of conviction for any offense provided in this law, the court shall decide to:

1- Confiscate instruments, materials, or other objects used during the commission of the offense or intended for use in the commission of the offense.

2- Confiscate objects or funds which were the object of the offense.


3- Confiscate proceeds or property derived from the offense.

Except for the cases provided in the first paragraph above, if there is evidence proving the guilt of the accused who died during the trial phase of the proceedings that the person committed any offense stated from Article 39 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances) to Article 42 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), from Article 47 (Illegal Production of Narcotic Substances) to Article 50 (Illegal Provision for Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 54 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Precursor Chemicals), Article 56 (Illegal Production, Possession, Transport, or Traffic of Equipment or Materials for the Purpose of Illegal Production or Consumption of Narcotic Substances), Article 71 (Money Laundering Offense), and Article 72 (Aggravating Circumstances) of this law, the court shall confiscate the assets left by the accused, whether already distributed or not, within a period of 5 (five) years after the date the accused died, if it is clear or reasonably suspected that those assets are related to the offense.

However, confiscation cannot be declared if it affects a third party.

## Article 95._ Burden of Proof for Persons Opposing the Confiscation Decision

A third party who has interests related to the decision according to Article 94 (Confiscation of Objects, etc.) above has the right to file an accessory claim (incident) according to the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure against this confiscation decision, and shall bear the burden of proving the legality of the property, etc., that was confiscated.

## Article 96._ Confiscation of Property, etc.

In case prosecution cannot be conducted due to the death of the offender, the prosecutor may apply to the criminal court to confiscate instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or property if there is reasonable suspicion that those instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or property are related to the offense.

In case of dismissal of the charge due to the death of the accused or the identity of the person is unknown, the investigating judge may apply to the criminal court to confiscate


instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or property if there is reasonable suspicion that those instruments, materials, funds, proceeds, or property are related to the offense.

However, confiscation cannot be decided if it affects a third party. A third party who has an interest or who claims to be the owner of the object of confiscation has the right to participate in all stages of the court proceedings and has the right to file an accessory claim (incident) against the decision in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

## Article 97._ Sale of Confiscated Objects or Property by the State

The Ministry of Economy and Finance shall manage the confiscated objects or property. In case the confiscated objects or property are sold, they shall comply with the procedures for selling state property.

The proceeds from the sale according to the first paragraph above, along with other confiscated property that is cash, shall be paid into a national fund to be used for:

- Paying debts and all legal expenses arising from the confiscation of property, including expenses for confiscation, transport, storage, and maintenance, and

- Paying compensation to victims, if any, or

- Paying into the fund of the National Authority for Combating Drugs (NACD).

The NACD shall manage the funds to be provided to state organizations or private associations that provide services in combating drug use or drug offenses.

The conditions for organizing and functioning of the above fund shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

## Article 98._ Claim Against Final Decisions Related to Confiscation of Property, etc.

A third party who has interests or who claims to be the owner of the object of confiscation has the right to file a claim in the civil court to recover the property, etc., that was confiscated by a final decision of the criminal court, in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure.


The right to claim as stated in the first paragraph shall be extinguished by prescription if a period of 3 (three) years has elapsed after the third party or the legal representative of that person became aware of the situation.

Notwithstanding the provisions of the second paragraph above, if a period of 10 (ten) years has elapsed from the date the decision of the criminal court became final, the right to claim shall be extinguished.

## Article 99._ Nullity of Acts Aimed at Obstructing Freezing or Confiscation

The court shall nullify or refuse all actions, whether for consideration or without consideration, which were done for the purpose of diverting property from freezing or confiscation measures.

A person who receives property due to the effect of an action without consideration shall lose the right due to the nullification or refusal of those actions. However, a person who receives property in good faith shall only have the obligation to return up to the level of the benefit they still receive.

The nullification or refusal in the first paragraph above cannot be done if the person who received the property due to the effect of that action acted in good faith and for consideration.

# Chapter 6
# Treatment and Rehabilitation Measures

## Section 1
## General Provisions

## Article 100._ State Obligations to Provide Treatment and Rehabilitation Services

The State shall use all mechanisms to support the provision of appropriate services for medical treatment and rehabilitation for every drug addict without discrimination.

The State shall also ensure the availability of harm reduction services resulting from drug abuse, health services, and national policies that help reduce health, social, and economic harms to individuals, communities, and society caused by drugs.


All expenses for the treatment and rehabilitation of drug addicts in public treatment and rehabilitation centers shall be the responsibility of the State. Public treatment and rehabilitation centers may receive contributions from various sources to support the functioning of the centers.

The State shall support and encourage private centers and communities to participate in providing medical treatment and rehabilitation services for drug addicts. Private centers and communities shall have appropriate expertise and be recognized by the Ministry of Health for drug addict treatment and by the Ministry of Social Affairs, Veterans and Youth Rehabilitation for drug addict rehabilitation. This recognition shall be reported to the National Authority for Combating Drugs (NACD). In all cases, receiving drug addicts for treatment and rehabilitation in private or community centers shall comply with the conditions provided in this law.

## Article 101._ Principles of Treatment and Rehabilitation

As a principle, treatment and rehabilitation may only be conducted with the consent of the drug addict. However, in special cases and for the benefit of the drug addict and for the general interest, treatment and rehabilitation may be mandatory, in accordance with the conditions and procedures provided in this law.

In case the drug addict is a minor or a person with limited legal capacity, consent from their legal representative is required. Other provisions related to minors and persons with limited legal capacity shall be applied for treatment and rehabilitation.

Experts responsible for drug treatment and rehabilitation shall not be subject to any prosecution in the exercise of their professional functions in accordance with medical expertise and the laws in force.

## Article 102._ Rules for Managing Treatment and Rehabilitation

Treatment and rehabilitation of drug addicts shall include appropriate medical and social services based on science, as follows:


1- Treatment and rehabilitation shall be managed by health care experts and rehabilitation experts.

2- Treatment and rehabilitation methods shall be clear regarding the needs based on the symptoms of each individual and shall be decided by health care experts and rehabilitation experts after consultation and with the consent of that individual.

3- The treatment and rehabilitation shall comply with national and international standards and be executed in the best way for the treatment of drug addiction with effectiveness and humanity.

4- The treatment and rehabilitation shall be managed solely in the interest of the drug addict so that they can regain full legal capacity.

5- Treatment and rehabilitation measures for drug addiction in public treatment and rehabilitation centers of the State shall last from 6 (six) months to 2 (two) years.

Detailed procedures for treatment methods and management of treatment and rehabilitation measures for drug addiction shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

In all cases, no individual under treatment and rehabilitation for drug addiction shall be the object of medical or scientific experimentation.

## Article 103._ Confidentiality of Treatment and Rehabilitation

The confidentiality of all information related to the treatment and rehabilitation of drug addicts shall be guaranteed. Records of identity, diagnosis, medical evolution, or results of treatment and rehabilitation of drug addicts shall be kept confidential and cannot be publicly disclosed or shared with other persons or agencies, except as required for the public interest or with the consent of the person concerned.


## Section 2
## Voluntary Treatment and Rehabilitation

## Article 104._ Voluntary Treatment and Rehabilitation

Any person in a state of drug addiction may seek medical treatment and rehabilitation services at centers established for the purpose of treatment and rehabilitation. Before deciding to undergo treatment and rehabilitation, the drug addict has the right to receive essential information from the center regarding treatment and rehabilitation, including:

- Nature and duration of treatment and rehabilitation.

- Methods of treatment and rehabilitation and the difficulties involved.

- Expected benefits of treatment and rehabilitation.

- Possible side effects of treatment and rehabilitation.

- Other alternatives to treatment and rehabilitation, if any.

- Consequences of Refusal of treatment and rehabilitation.

Before providing treatment and rehabilitation, the center shall ensure that the drug addict has truly given voluntary consent to the treatment and rehabilitation after having received essential information about the treatment and rehabilitation. The consent of the drug addict to undergo treatment and rehabilitation shall be made in writing.

The drug addict has the right to participate in the decision to undergo treatment and rehabilitation methods, including the time of entering and leaving treatment and rehabilitation. The drug addict may stop receiving voluntary treatment at any time.


## Section 3
## Conditions for Prosecution and Execution of Penalty for Drug Addicts

## Article 105._ Conditions for Prosecution of Drug Addicts

Before deciding to prosecute a person who has committed an offense as provided in Article 45 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) and Article 53 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) of this law, the prosecutor may instruct that person to undergo treatment and rehabilitation at a public or private center if the person is in a state of drug addiction verified by medical certificate from a specialized physician.

If the person does not consent to undergo treatment and rehabilitation, the prosecutor shall exercise their power regarding the options for prosecution in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure.

However, if the person consents to undergo treatment and rehabilitation, the prosecutor shall record that consent in writing and immediately notify the center of this decision. In this case, the prosecution shall be suspended. The prosecution process shall be resumed if the person evades undergoing treatment and rehabilitation until completion as determined by the center.

If the person completes the treatment and rehabilitation measures until the end, the prosecutor may drop the case.

## Article 106._ Conditions for Execution of Penalty for Drug Addicts

If a convict is in a state of drug addiction, verified by medical certificate from a specialized physician, and voluntarily requests to undergo treatment and rehabilitation measures in accordance with Chapter 6 (Treatment and Rehabilitation Measures) of this law after the court has issued a final judgment for offenses as provided in Article 45 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) and Article 53 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) of this law, in this case, the execution of the penalty shall be suspended.


If the convict completes the treatment and rehabilitation measures until the end, the convict shall be considered as having served the sentence. Conversely, if the convict does not complete the treatment and rehabilitation measures until the end, the penalty that was already pronounced shall be executed immediately.

## Section 4
## Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation

## Article 107._ General Conditions for Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation

No person shall be forced to undergo treatment and rehabilitation for drug addiction, except in cases where the person is in a state of severe drug addiction and there is an actual threat of immediate and serious harm to the drug addict themselves or to others. In this case, treatment and rehabilitation may be mandatory if this treatment and rehabilitation is a necessary measure to protect the general interest and for the benefit of the drug addict.

Mandatory treatment and rehabilitation may also be conducted if the drug addict is in a state of lack of capacity to express will to undergo voluntary treatment and rehabilitation.

## Article 108._ Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation Ordered by the Prosecutor

The prosecutor may receive an application directly from the parents, legal guardian, or relatives of the drug addict, or through other competent officials. In this case, the drug addict may be sent immediately by competent officials or by the drug addict's family to the prosecutor for competent action according to the procedure.

Before deciding, the prosecutor shall request a medical examination by a physician and may interrogate the person in the presence of a lawyer, if the person has a lawyer. The physician assigned to perform the medical examination shall provide the results as soon as possible.


The prosecutor may order the person to be detained at the detention place of the judicial police unit while waiting for the results of the medical examination.

If the results of the medical examination confirm that the person is in a state of drug addiction but does not meet all the conditions as provided in Article 107 (General Conditions for Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation) above, the prosecutor shall follow the procedure as provided in Article 105 (Conditions for Prosecution of Drug Addicts) of this law, in case the person has committed an offense as provided in Article 45 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) and Article 53 (Illegal Consumption of Narcotic Substances) of this law.

If the results of the medical examination confirm that the person is in a state of severe drug addiction or there is other evidence confirming a clear threat of immediate and serious harm to themselves or others, the prosecutor may decide to order mandatory treatment and rehabilitation.

The person who is ordered to undergo treatment and rehabilitation has the right to appeal against the order of mandatory treatment and rehabilitation issued by the prosecutor to the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal. This protest shall be made by a simple complaint to the relevant prosecution office. Subsequently, the case file shall be immediately sent to the Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal by the prosecution representative attached to the Court of First Instance. The Prosecutor General attached to the Court of Appeal shall decide within a maximum of 72 (seventy-two) hours from the date of receiving the case file.

## Article 109._ Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation during Investigation and Trial Phases

Mandatory treatment and rehabilitation may be ordered during the investigation phase by the investigating judge and during the trial phase by the trial judge, if all the conditions as provided in Article 107 (General Conditions for Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation) and in the second and fifth paragraphs of Article 108 (Mandatory Treatment and Rehabilitation Ordered by the Prosecutor) of this law are met.

The decision of the investigating judge or the trial judge may be appealed in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.


## Article 110._ Specific Grounds for Determining Penalty

In addition to the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure, for a person who has undergone mandatory treatment and rehabilitation until the end, the court shall take this into consideration when determining the penalty.

# Chapter 7
# Extradition and Mutual Legal Assistance

## Section 1
## Extradition

## Article 111._ Extradition of Foreign Nationals in the Territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia

The Kingdom of Cambodia may agree to surrender to a foreign state a foreign national residing in the territory of Cambodia who:

- Is the object of judicial prosecution in that foreign state for an offense provided in this law; or

- Has been sentenced to imprisonment by a court of that foreign state for an offense provided in this law.

## Article 112._ Provisions Governing Extradition for Drug-Related Offenses

The extradition of foreign nationals in the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia is governed by the provisions of international treaties and conventions related to extradition recognized by the Kingdom of Cambodia. In the absence of an international treaty or convention recognized by the Kingdom of Cambodia, the conditions and procedures for extradition requested by a foreign state or by the Kingdom of Cambodia to a foreign state shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.


## Article 113._ Transit

Transit through the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia by any person who is the subject of extradition proceedings may be authorized provided that the extradition is not related to a political offense. The request for transit shall be made through diplomatic channels. The request must be accompanied by appropriate supporting evidence documents.

## Section 2
## Mutual Legal Assistance

## Article 114._ Requests from Foreign States to the Kingdom of Cambodia for Assistance in Investigation or Judicial Proceedings in the Foreign State

This article shall apply if the Royal Government of Cambodia receives a request from any foreign state for assistance related to:

1- Investigation of an offense under the law of the foreign state which corresponds to an offense under the provisions of this law.

2- Judicial proceedings for such an offense.

3- Investigation related to property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense.

4- Proceedings for freezing or confiscating property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense in the requesting state.

5- Freezing or confiscation of property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

Through the court or other competent authorities, the Royal Government of Cambodia may provide assistance in any of the following ways:

1- Preparing records of evidence in writing or by oral interrogation of persons.


2- Facilitating the appearance of persons, including persons in custody, with their consent, to provide evidence or to assist in investigations.

3- Providing judicial documents.

4- Executing searches and seizures.

5- Assisting in tracing property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense.

6- Investigating financial transactions.

7- Obtaining information or evidence that could assist in recovering property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense for the purpose of confiscation.

8- Freezing property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense, to prevent the sale, transfer, or disposal of the property pending a decision on confiscation.

9- Confiscating property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense, including documents.

10- Executing final decisions of foreign states for the confiscation of property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense.

11- Conducting technical or scientific examinations of evidence and crime scenes.

12- Providing information and evidence to the foreign state.

13- Providing original documents or certified copies of relevant documents or records, including bank, financial, commercial, or business documents, etc., to the foreign state.

Information and evidence that may be provided to the foreign state according to second paragraph, point 12 above, includes all legally obtained information, including information obtained from the interception and recording of all communications by telecommunication means or by use of other technological equipment.


To implement a request for assistance from a foreign state, the procedures and processes in the Kingdom of Cambodia for investigation, prosecution, or inquiry into offenses under the provisions of the law of the Kingdom of Cambodia may be used to provide assistance to the foreign state.

## Article 115._ Assistance May Be Provided Based on Conditions

The Royal Government of Cambodia may provide assistance to a foreign state according to this section, based on the condition requiring the requesting state to provide a written undertaking that it will provide assistance in similar matters if the Kingdom of Cambodia makes a similar request.

## Article 116._ Mandatory Grounds for Refusing a Request for Assistance

The Royal Government of Cambodia shall not agree to a request for assistance related to offenses under the provisions of this section if it is found that:

1- The request is made for the purpose of punishing any person on account of that person's race, religion, nationality, origin, ethnicity, or political opinion.

2- That person's situation may be prejudiced on account of that person's race, religion, nationality, origin, ethnicity, or political opinion.

3- The request relates to the punishment of that person for an offense, and:

- That person has been finally acquitted or pardoned by a court or competent authority in the foreign state, or

- That person has already served the sentence under the laws of that state for that offense or for another offense which is the same act as that offense.

## Article 117._ Rules on Bank Secrecy

A request for assistance under the provisions of this section shall not be refused solely on the grounds of bank secrecy.


## Article 118._ Requests to Provide Evidence in a Court of a Foreign State

This article shall apply if:

1- A hearing relates to:

- An offense under the laws of a foreign state which corresponds to an offense under the provisions of this law, or

- The freezing or confiscation of property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of an offense, has commenced in the foreign state; and

2- The foreign state requests the presence at the hearing for the purpose of the trial of any person who is in the Kingdom of Cambodia; and

3- There are reasonable grounds to believe that the person is capable of providing evidence relevant to the trial; and

4- The person has consented to provide evidence in the foreign state.

The foreign state must provide the following undertakings:

1- That the person shall not:

- Be detained or punished for any offense against the laws of the foreign state alleged to have been committed or committed before the person's departure from the Kingdom of Cambodia; or

- Be subject to any civil suit for any act or omission by the person alleged to have occurred or occurred before the person's departure from the Kingdom of Cambodia, if the person would not have been subject to that civil suit had the person not been in the foreign state; or

- Be required to provide evidence in any other trial in the foreign state other than the trial related to the request. Except where the person has departed from the foreign state or has had the opportunity to depart from the foreign state and remains in the foreign state for a purpose other than providing evidence in the trial related to the request.


2- Any evidence provided by the person in the trial related to the request shall not be admissible or valid for the prosecution of an offender against the laws of the foreign state, unless it is for an offense of perjury in providing that evidence.

3- The person shall be returned to the Kingdom of Cambodia in accordance with the agreement reached by the Royal Government of Cambodia.

4- If the person is in provisional detention in the Kingdom of Cambodia, and the Royal Government of Cambodia requests the foreign state to make arrangements to keep the person in custody while in the foreign state:

- That state shall make arrangements for this purpose; and

- The person shall not be released on bail in the foreign state, unless the Royal Government of Cambodia notifies the appropriate authorities of the foreign state that the person is entitled to be released under the laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia; and

- If the person is released in the foreign state as provided in the second part of point 4 above, the foreign state shall bear the expenses for the stay and other expenses of the person until the proceedings related to the request are completed.

## Article 119._ Requests by the Kingdom of Cambodia to a Foreign State for Assistance in Investigation or Judicial Proceedings

The Royal Government of Cambodia may request a foreign state to provide assistance related to:

1- Investigation of an offense under the provisions of this law.

2- Judicial proceedings for an offense under the provisions of this law.

3- Investigation related to property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense.

4- Proceedings to freeze or confiscate property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

5- Freezing or confiscation of property used or intended to be used in the commission of an offense, which is the object or proceeds of the offense in the requested state.


A request may be made for assistance in a foreign state in any case provided for in the second paragraph of Article 114 (Requests from Foreign States to the Kingdom of Cambodia for Assistance in Investigation or Judicial Proceedings in the Foreign State) of this law.

Evidence legally obtained in a foreign state may be used as evidence in proceedings in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

If a person who is being detained in a foreign state is required to provide evidence in the Kingdom of Cambodia, and that foreign state requests the Royal Government of Cambodia to make arrangements to keep that person in custody while the person is in the Kingdom of Cambodia, the Royal Government of Cambodia:

1- Shall make arrangements for this purpose; or

2- Shall not release that person on bail in the Kingdom of Cambodia, except where the foreign state notifies the appropriate authorities of the Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia that the person is entitled to be released under the laws of that foreign state; and

3- If the person is released in the Kingdom of Cambodia as provided in point 2 above, the Royal Government of Cambodia shall bear the expenses for the stay and other expenses of that person until the proceedings related to the request are completed.

Any person who is required to provide evidence in the Kingdom of Cambodia by a foreign state must be returned to that state without the need for extradition proceedings, in accordance with the agreement reached with that foreign state.

## Article 120._ International Cooperation

The provisions of this section do not limit or obstruct the providing or receiving of information or international cooperation related to any offense provided in this law or a corresponding offense under the laws of a foreign state, by whatever procedure.


# Chapter 8
# Transitional Provisions

## Article 121._ Transitional Provisions on the National Authority for Combating Drugs

The National Authority for Combating Drugs, established by Royal Decree No. NS/RKT/0509/608 dated May 30, 2009, shall continue to be the institution for implementing the Royal Government's policy in the fight against drugs until there are new provisions to replace it.

## Article 122._ Transitional Provisions on Licenses and Authorizations

Licenses and authorizations granted in accordance with previous provisions for legal activities related to drugs shall continue to be valid until such licenses and authorizations expire.

The granting, renewal, or revocation of licenses and authorizations after the entry into force of this law shall comply with the provisions of this law.

## Article 123._ Transitional Provisions on Procedure

The provisions on procedure provided in this law shall apply immediately to all procedural acts after the entry into force of this law.

The application of the provisions of this law shall not affect the validity of procedural acts performed in accordance with the provisions of the old law.

## Article 124._ Transitional Provisions on Offenses

Criminal offenses committed during the period when the Law on the Amendment of the Law on Drug Control, promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0505/014 dated May 09, 2005, was in effect shall continue to be governed by the old law and in accordance with the provisions of Part 1 (General Provisions) of the Criminal Code promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/1109/022 dated November 30, 2009.


# Chapter 9
# Final Provisions

## Article 125._ Abrogation of Provisions and Exceptions

The Law on Drug Control promulgated by Royal Kram No. NS/RKM/0197/01 dated January 24, 1997, and the Law on the Amendment of the Law on Drug Control promulgated by Royal Kram dated May 09, 2005, shall cease to have effect from the date this law is implemented, except for cases as provided in Article 124 (Transitional Provisions on Offenses) of this law.

Sub-decrees and regulations related to the Law on Drug Control shall continue to be in force until they are replaced by new ones, except for any provision that is contrary to the spirit of this law.
