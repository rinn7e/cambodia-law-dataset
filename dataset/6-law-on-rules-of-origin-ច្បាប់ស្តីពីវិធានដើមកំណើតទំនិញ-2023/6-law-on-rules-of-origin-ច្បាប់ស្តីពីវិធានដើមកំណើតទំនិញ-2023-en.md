## PROMULGATES

The Law on Rules of Origin, which was adopted by the National Assembly on June 12, 2023, during the 9th session of the 6th legislature, and fully reviewed on its form and legal aspects by the Senate on June 21, 2023, during the 10th plenary session of the 4th legislature, the full text of which is as follows:

# LAW

## ON

# RULES OF ORIGIN

# Chapter 1
## General Provisions

### Article 1 .- Purpose

This Law defines the principles and rules of origin of exported and imported goods in order to promote and facilitate trade that benefits from trade preferences and the implementation of all preferential trade systems, as well as to participate in preventing the falsification of the origin of goods.

### Article 2 .- Scope

This Law has the scope of application on the rules of origin of goods under the preferential trade system and the rules of origin of goods outside the preferential trade system, and shall apply to:

1- Producers and exporters/importers who require proof of origin of goods and product origin marking.

2- Authorities issuing certificates of origin for exported goods.

3- Authorities receiving proof of origin for imported goods.

4- Legal entities or institutions involved in trade activities that perform operations related to the origin of goods.

### Article 3 .- Definitions

Key terms used in this Law shall be defined as follows:

1- Rules of Origin refers to the rules of origin of goods or origin of goods rules, which include rules of origin under the preferential trade system and rules of origin outside the preferential trade system.

2- Rules of Origin Under Preferential Trade System refers to rules determining the origin of goods under free trade agreements between two or more countries, in accordance with the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade 1994 of the World Trade Organization, or other preferential trade agreements.

3- Rules of Origin Outside Preferential Trade System refers to rules determining the origin of exported and imported goods for the application of national treatment principles, most-favored-nation treatment, anti-dumping duties, countervailing duties, safeguard measures, restrictions in determining the origin of goods, quantitative restrictions, government procurement, and the collection of trade statistics.

4- Authority receiving proof of origin of goods refers to the Customs Administration which has been authorized to receive proof of origin of imported goods.

5- Authority issuing certificate of origin of goods refers to the Ministry of Commerce, which has the General Directorate of Trade Services or other entities authorized by the Ministry of Commerce to issue certificates of origin for exported goods and manage exporters who have

6- Certificate of Origin refers to a specific form issued by the authority issuing certificates of origin of goods and certifying that the goods covered by that certificate originate in a specific country.

7- Declaration of Origin refers to a declaration made by the exporter or by the importer certifying the origin of the goods in accordance with national laws and regulations, or under free trade agreements or other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party, or under a unilateral preferential system.

8- Certificate of Origin under Preferential Trade System refers to a certificate of origin used for implementation with the purpose of obtaining tariff preferences under free trade agreements or other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party, or under a unilateral preferential system.

9- Certificate of Origin Outside Preferential Trade System refers to a certificate of origin used for implementation for purposes other than preferential tariff treatment.

10- Most-Favored-Nation Treatment refers to the principle of non-discrimination between trade partners of a country.

11- Proof of Origin refers to a document or declaration in paper or electronic form that serves as evidence certifying that the relevant goods meet the origin criteria under the defined rules of origin. Proof of origin includes certificates of origin and declarations of origin.

12- Transit Country refers to one or more countries through which goods are transported in transit, other than the country of origin and the final importing country.

# Chapter 2
## Rules of Origin Under Preferential Trade System

### Article 4 .- Rules of Origin Under Preferential Trade System within the Framework of Free Trade Agreements or Other Trade Agreements

The determination of the origin of goods to obtain preferential tariff treatment under free trade agreements or other trade agreements shall be implemented in accordance with the rules of origin under free trade agreements or other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party.

### Article 5 .- Rules of Origin Under Unilateral Preferential Trade System

The determination of the origin of goods to obtain preferential tariff treatment within the framework of granting unilateral preferences shall be implemented in accordance with the rules of origin under the preferential system determined by the importing country.

### Chapter 3
### Rules of Origin for Non-Preferential Trade

#### Article 6 .- Determination of Origin of Goods

The determination of the origin of goods shall be based on the following criteria:

- Goods wholly obtained or produced in a country; or

- Substantial transformation.

Goods wholly obtained or produced in a country shall be considered as originating in that country. Goods to be considered as wholly obtained or produced in a country include:

a- Mineral products, including petroleum, extracted from its soil, waters, or seabed.

b- Plant and plant products harvested and gathered in that country.

c- Live animals born and raised in that country.

d- Goods obtained from live animals raised in that country.

e- Goods obtained by hunting or fishing in that country.

f- Goods of sea-fishing and other marine products taken from the sea by vessels registered in that country and flying its flag.

g- Goods obtained or produced on board factory ships of that country solely from products referred to in point f above.

h- Goods extracted from the seabed or subsoil outside its territorial waters, provided that the country has sole rights to exploit such seabed or subsoil.

i- Waste and scrap derived from manufacturing or processing operations, and used articles collected in that country, fit only for the recovery of raw materials.

j- Goods produced in that country solely from products referred to in points a to i above.

In the event that multiple countries are involved in the production of goods, the determination of origin shall be based on the substantial transformation criterion. The determination of origin using the substantial transformation criterion shall be carried out in accordance with the change in tariff classification or the ad-valorem percentage rule.

The detailed technical specifications of substantial transformation shall be determined by a joint Prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Commerce.

#### Article 7 .- Minimal Operations and Processes

The following operations, when carried out on non-originating materials to produce a product, shall be considered as insufficient operations or processes to confer origin on that product:

1- Operations necessary to ensure the preservation of goods during transport or storage.

2- Operations to improve packaging or market quality of goods, or to prepare goods for transport, such as breaking up bulk, grouping and classifying packages, and repacking, etc.

3- Simple assembly.

4- Mixing of products of different origins, provided that the characteristics of the resulting product are not significantly different from those of the mixed products.

5- Other operations or processes shall be determined by a joint inter-ministerial Prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Commerce, in accordance with the latest updates of free trade agreements and other trade agreements, or international standards.

#### Article 8 .- Direct Consignment

Goods can retain their originating status as provided in Article 6 of this Law, provided that they satisfy the following conditions:

1- The goods are transported directly from the originating country to the importing country.

2- The goods are transported through one or more transit countries, provided that they do not undergo any further modification or processing in those transit countries, other than logistical operations such as unloading, reloading, storing, or any other operations necessary to preserve and transport them to the importing country.

### Chapter 4
### Proof of Origin and Origin Markings of Products

#### Article 9 .- Proof of Origin

A certificate of origin in paper or electronic form, and an origin declaration, shall be considered as proof of origin. Proof of origin under preferential trade schemes shall be implemented in accordance with the origin requirements under free trade agreements or other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party. Proof of origin under preferential trade schemes in the context of unilateral tariff preferences shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions on rules of origin of the importing country.

A Certificate of Origin of goods outside the preferential trade system must be accompanied by the minimum information and in the format of the Certificate of Origin of goods outside the preferential trade system for exported goods, which shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Commerce.

### Article 10.- Conditions and Procedures for Providing Product Origin Identification Information under the Rules of Origin

The conditions and procedures for providing product origin identification information shall be determined by an inter-ministerial Prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Commerce, in compliance with the Rules of Origin of goods under the preferential trade system of the importing country or under free trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party, and the criteria for origin of goods as provided in Article 6 of this Law.

### Article 11.- Authenticity of the Certificate of Origin

A Certificate of Origin under the preferential trade system and a Certificate of Origin under the non-preferential trade system in paper form with authenticity must bear the signature and seal of approval from the competent authority and the seal of the authority issuing the Certificate of Origin, or other authentication of authenticity as determined by the authority or competent institution issuing the certificate of the exporting country.

In the event that those Certificates of Origin are issued in electronic format by the authority issuing the Certificate of Origin or the competent institution issuing the certificate of the exporting country, they must comply with the provisions of free trade agreements and other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia and the importing country are parties, or unilateral preferential trade systems, or laws and regulations in force.

Procedures for determining the authenticity of the Certificate of Origin in electronic format issued by the competent authority of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Commerce.

### Article 12.- Record-Keeping Requirements

Any person who is a producer or exporter must keep the supporting documents of origin of goods at the place of business in the Kingdom of Cambodia for a period of 3 (three) years for inspection and verification by competent officers of the Ministry of Commerce.

Any person who is a producer, exporter, or importer must keep the supporting documents of origin of goods at the place of business in the Kingdom of Cambodia in accordance with the Law on Customs for inspection and verification by the customs administration.

Relevant documents as specified in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of this Article may be kept in any storage device from which data can be retrieved immediately, including in electronic format or in paper format, in accordance with the laws and regulations in force.

### Article 13.- Falsification of Origin of Goods

No person shall intentionally commit or attempt to commit any act of falsification of the origin of goods to obtain preferential tariff treatment, or to evade anti-dumping duties or countervailing duties applied in the importing country.

The following acts of falsification of the origin of goods are prohibited:

1- Falsifying the proof of origin of goods; or

2- Falsifying information on the proof of origin of goods.

### Article 14.- Non-compliance in Application for Certification of Origin of Goods

No person shall intentionally commit any act of non-compliance in the application for certification of the origin of goods.

The following acts of non-compliance in the application for certification of origin of goods are prohibited:

1- Declaring incorrect origin of goods.

2- Declaring incorrect raw materials for producing goods.

3- Providing incorrect price/value information on the import invoice of raw materials.

4- Providing incorrect price/value information on the export invoice of goods.

5- Incorrect classification of goods.

6- Providing incorrect information on the criteria of origin of goods.

7- Providing incorrect productivity reports.

8- Providing incorrect information on production means.

9- Providing incorrect cost information.

### Article 15.- Exemption from Proof of Origin of Goods

Exemption from the proof of origin of goods under the preferential trade system shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of free trade agreements or other trade agreements to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party, or the provisions under unilateral preferential trade systems.

The proof of origin of goods under the non-preferential trade system shall be exempted for:

1- Exported goods, except where required by the importing country or upon request of the exporter.

2- Imported goods, except where necessary for use in the implementation of trade measures, or measures for the purpose of protecting public welfare and safety by the Kingdom of Cambodia.

# Chapter 5
## Competent Authorities

### Section 1
#### Authorities Issuing Certificates of Origin

**Article 16.- Authority Issuing Certificate of Origin**

The Ministry of Commerce, which has the General Department of Trade Services or other entities authorized by the Ministry of Commerce, is the authority issuing Certificates of Origin in both paper and electronic formats. The Ministry of Commerce is the institution that manages and qualifies approved exporters for self-certification of the origin of goods.

The procedures for applying for and issuing Certificates of Origin shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Commerce.

**Article 17.- Retention of Documents by the Authority Issuing Certificate of Origin**

The authority issuing Certificates of Origin shall retain documents, records, and information relevant to the Certificates of Origin that have obtained origin, in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

The relevant documents as specified in the first paragraph of this Article may be retained in any storage device or medium from which data can be retrieved immediately, including electronic or paper formats, in compliance with applicable laws and regulations.

**Article 18.- Verification**

The Ministry of Commerce has the competence to conduct retroactive verifications on Certificates of Origin or Declarations of Origin for goods exported from Cambodia, as necessary or upon request by the competent authorities of the importing country that have doubts regarding the authenticity of the documents or the accuracy of the information related to the origin of the goods in question, or any part of the information in the documents of those exported goods.

Upon request of the Ministry of Commerce, the exporter shall provide information or documents related to the origin of goods to the Ministry of Commerce in order to conduct retroactive verification as determined.

### Section 2
#### Authorities Receiving Proof of Origin

**Article 19.- Authority Receiving Proof of Origin**

The Customs Administration is the authority receiving proofs of origin under both preferential and non-preferential trade regimes, provided by the importer, producer, or authorized representative. The procedures for receiving proofs of origin for imported goods shall comply with the Law on Customs and relevant regulations in force.

**Article 20.- Verification**

The Customs Administration has the competence to conduct retroactive verifications on proofs of origin presented at the time of importation regarding the authenticity of the documents or the accuracy of the information related to the origin of the goods. The Customs Administration has the right to require the importer to provide information or documents related to the origin of goods in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

The Customs Administration may request cooperation from the authority or institution issuing proofs of origin of the country of origin or the exporting country to conduct retroactive verification.

# Chapter 6
## Competent Officers and Investigation Procedures

**Article 21.- Origin Investigation for Exported Goods**

The Ministry of Commerce has the competence to conduct investigations on the origin of exported goods.

Investigations on the origin of exported goods shall be conducted only in cases involving fraud of origin of goods to obtain trade preferences in the importing country or other benefits, or to evade anti-dumping measures, countervailing measures, and safeguard measures.

Investigations on the origin of exported goods shall be conducted by visiting and inspecting production lines, documents, accounting books, records, and other information related to the origin of goods. The procedures for origin investigation of exported goods shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Commerce.

**Article 22.- Origin Investigation Officers for Exported Goods**

Investigation officers shall be selected from the General Department of Trade Services by a Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Commerce to examine offenses under this Law.

**Article 23.- Qualification Procedure**

Investigation officers shall be qualified as judicial police to examine offenses set forth in this Law, in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The formalities and procedures for granting judicial police qualification to origin investigation officers for exported goods shall be determined by an Inter-Ministerial Prakas between the Minister of the Ministry of Justice and the Minister of the Ministry of Commerce.

**Article 24.- Powers of Investigation Officers**

Investigation officers qualified as judicial police have the power to inspect, research, investigate, summon and question, search, seize evidence, and collect data in order to prevent and suppress offenses under this Law, by implementing the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

Investigating officers have the right to request assistance from competent authorities of all levels and the armed forces or other relevant competent authorities to participate in cracking down on offenses stipulated in this Law.

### Article 25 .- Hearing of Witness Testimony

An investigating officer may hear the testimony of any witness if the investigating officer deems that the testimony of the witness may be useful for the investigation, in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

In the event that any witness refuses to appear without a valid reason, affecting the process of investigation and search for offenses, the investigating officer may request the competent prosecutor to order that witness to appear to provide testimony before the investigating officer.

### Article 26 .- Origin Investigation for Imported Goods

Origin investigation for imported goods in cases involving the falsification of the origin of goods in order to obtain preferential tariff treatment or other benefits, or to evade anti-dumping measures, countervailing measures, and safeguard measures, shall be the competence of customs officers who have obtained the qualification as judicial police officers, in accordance with this Law, the Law on Customs, and regulations in force.

### Article 27 .- Joint Investigation on Origin of Goods

In case of necessity, the Ministry of Commerce or the Customs Administration may request cooperation from relevant institutions to conduct a joint investigation.

## Chapter 7

## Penal Provisions

### Article 28 .- Sanctions

Sanctions in this Law include administrative sanctions and criminal sanctions.

Administrative sanctions include:

- Written warning

- Temporary suspension or cessation of business operations

- Suspension, revocation, or cancellation of permits or licenses.

Written warning, temporary suspension or cessation of business operations, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of permits or licenses shall be the competence of the Ministry of Commerce.

Offenses subject to administrative sanctions shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Commerce.

Any person who is dissatisfied with the decision on the imposition of administrative sanctions may file an appeal to the Minister of Commerce within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the decision on the imposition of such sanctions.

The Minister of Commerce shall make a decision on the appeal within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the appeal.

In the event that the person remains dissatisfied with the decision of the Minister of Commerce, that person may file a complaint to the court within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the notification on the decision on such appeal.

Types of administrative sanctions, competence, and procedures of appeal against the decision on the imposition of administrative sanctions related to the origin of imported goods shall be implemented in accordance with the Law on Customs and regulations in force.

Criminal penalties include:

- Transactional fine

- Fine

- Imprisonment.

Other offenses subject to transactional fine under this Law shall be determined by sub-decree.

The decision to impose a transactional fine is the competence of investigating officers and/or customs officers who have obtained the qualification as judicial police.

The payment of a transactional fine shall extinguish the criminal action.

The payment of a transactional fine does not exempt the offender from the obligation to implement administrative measures and other administrative sanctions imposed under this Law.

In the event that the offender refuses to pay the transactional fine, the investigating officers and/or customs officers who have obtained the qualification as judicial police shall build a case file to be forwarded to the competent court.

The formalities and procedures for the transactional fine for offenses related to the origin of exported goods shall be determined by an inter-ministerial joint declaration (Prakas) between the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Justice. For the formalities and procedures of the transactional fine for offenses related to the origin of imported goods, they shall be implemented in accordance with the Law on Customs and regulations in force.

The payment of transactional fines, the management of transactional fine payment receipts, and the allocation of revenues obtained from transactional fines for offenses related to the origin of exported goods as prescribed in accordance with the provisions of this Law shall be determined by an inter-ministerial joint declaration (Prakas) between the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 29.- Failure to keep documents

Any person who fails to keep the reference documentation of rules of origin of goods as prescribed in paragraph 1 of Article 12 of this Law shall receive a written warning. In case of failure to comply with the written warning, a transactional fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels shall be imposed.

#### Article 30.- Commission of Non-compliant Acts in Applying for Proof of Origin of Goods

Any person who commits a non-compliant act in applying for verification of origin of goods, as provided for in Article 14 of this Law, shall receive a written warning.

In case of having already received a written warning as provided for in the first paragraph above once, and this person continues to commit non-compliant acts in applying for verification of origin of goods, his/her business operations shall be temporarily suspended.

In case the business operations have been temporarily suspended as provided for in the second paragraph above, and this person still continues to commit non-compliant acts in applying for verification of origin of goods, he/she shall be subject to a transactional fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels.

The transactional fine shall be doubled in case the person who has already received a transactional fine once continues to commit non-compliant acts in applying for verification of origin of goods.

Any person who has received a doubled transactional fine as provided for in the fourth paragraph of this Article and continues to commit non-compliant acts in applying for verification of origin of goods shall be subject to imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine of 1,000,000 (one million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels.

#### Article 31.- Commission of Falsification of Origin of Goods

Any person who commits an act in violation of Article 13 of this Law shall be subject to imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and a fine of 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels to 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels.

#### Article 32.- Liability of Legal Entities

Legal entities may be declared criminally responsible, as provided for in Article 42 (Criminal Responsibility of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code, for the offenses set forth in Article 30 and Article 31 of this Law.

Legal entities committing offenses as provided for in Article 30 and Article 31 of this Law shall be subject to a fine from 2,000,000 (two million) Riels to 40,000,000 (forty million) Riels, as well as one or more additional penalties as provided for in Article 168 (Additional Penalties Applicable to Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code.

#### Article 33.- Application of Other Criminal Laws

The implementation of the provisions of Chapter 7 of this Law shall not preclude the application of other criminal laws in cases where the act defined as an offense under this Law is also an offense provided for under other criminal laws.

## Chapter 8

## Transitional Provisions

#### Article 34.- Continuation of Validity of Certificates

Certificates of origin issued prior to the entry into force of this Law shall continue to remain valid.

## Chapter 9

## Final Provisions

#### Article 35.- Abrogation

Any provisions contrary to this Law shall be abrogated.
