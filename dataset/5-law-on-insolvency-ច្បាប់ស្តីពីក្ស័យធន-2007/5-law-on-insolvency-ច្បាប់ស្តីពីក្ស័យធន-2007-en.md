# Law on Insolvency

## Chapter 1
## General Provisions

### Article 1 .-
This Law has the objective to determine the rules, conditions, procedures, and proceedings of insolvency in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Article 2 .-
This Law has the purpose to provide collective, orderly, and fair distribution or payment according to the claims of creditors by using the debtor's assets to distribute or pay, and to provide opportunities to restructure the debtor's business when the interested parties deem it appropriate.

### Article 3 .-
This Law has the scope of application to merchants and legal entities that have assets in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Article 4 .-
Technical terms used in this Law shall have the following meanings:

1- Insolvency refers to the state where a merchant or a legal entity is unable to pay their debts and is declared as such by the court.

2- Claim refers to the right to receive payment from the debtor or payment from the debtor's assets arising before the opening of insolvency proceedings, regardless of whether such claim is conditional, unmatured, or urgent, unless the law explicitly states that it is an administrative claim.

3- Capital Company refers to a limited liability company, including a private limited company and a public limited company, established under the Law on Commercial Enterprises.

៤- Court costs and expenses of continuing proceedings refer to court fees, costs and remuneration, and expenses of the temporary administrator.

៥- Court refers to the court of the Kingdom of Cambodia that has jurisdiction over insolvency cases under this Law.

៦- Partnership refers to a general partnership or a limited partnership established under the Law on Commercial Enterprises.

៧- Related person refers to:

    a- A shareholder, director, partner, or manager of the debtor, in the case where the debtor is not a natural person;

    b- A spouse of the debtor, or a spouse of a shareholder, director, partner, or manager of the debtor, in the case where the debtor is not a natural person;

    c- An ancestor or descendant of the debtor, or of a person described in point a and point b above;

    d- A spouse of a person described in point c above;

    e- A biological child of the spouse of the debtor, or of any person described in points a to d above;

    f- A person described in points a to e of this Article who holds more than 5% (five percent) of shares, or is a director, or is a manager;

    g- A partnership in which a person described in points a to e of this Article is a partner or manager;

    h- A legal entity established under foreign law in which a person described in points a to e of this Article holds more than 5% (five percent) of the capital, or is a director, manager, or partner.

    i- A person living in the debtor's household, in the case where the debtor is a natural person;

    j- A natural person or legal entity who, due to professional reasons or commercial relations with the debtor, has access to confidential information regarding the business or finances of the debtor;

    k- A creditor who has a claim against the debtor representing more than 5% (five percent) of the total claims of all creditors against the debtor.

8- Creditor refers to a natural person or a legal entity that has the right to claim compensation from the debtor or from the debtor's assets.

9- Secured Creditor refers to a creditor that has rights over assets that the debtor has placed to secure an obligation. This creditor has priority to receive compensation before other creditors.

10- Debtor refers to a natural person or a legal entity that is insolvent.

11- Set-off refers to a situation where, if a creditor accidentally becomes a debtor of the debtor, both parties may set off the obligations between themselves by deducting the mutual debts.

12- Creditors' Committee refers to a committee appointed by the creditors from among the creditors to perform duties representing the interests of all creditors.

### Article 5 .-
1- An appeal or an objection does not have the effect of suspending the execution of the court's decision on the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings.

2- In the case where this Law does not contain specific provisions, the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure shall apply.

## Chapter 2
## Opening of Insolvency Proceedings, Subject Matter, Grounds, and Complaints

### Article 6 .-
1- An interested party may file a complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings under this Law against debtors who are merchants and legal entities as follows:

    a- Partnerships or legal entities established under the laws of the Kingdom of Cambodia;

    b- Natural persons having domicile and assets in the Kingdom of Cambodia;

    c- Partnerships or legal entities established under foreign laws that have assets in the Kingdom of Cambodia;

    d- Natural persons having domicile outside the Kingdom of Cambodia who have assets in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2- Insolvency proceedings opened under this Law against persons or partnerships specified in points 1.c and 1.d of this Article shall apply only to the assets of such persons or partnerships located in the Kingdom of Cambodia or having their main establishment located in the Kingdom of Cambodia. The following assets shall be deemed to be located in the Kingdom of Cambodia:

    a- Tangible assets located within the territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia;

    b- Property and rights of ownership or other rights that are required to be registered under the laws or regulations of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

3- Opening of insolvency proceedings cannot be opened under this Law against a debtor or any institution that is a person under the scope of the Law on Banking and Financial Institutions and

Institutions, the Law on Insurance, and the Law on the Issuance and Trading of Public Securities, unless there are provisions specified in those laws.

### Article 7 .-
1- An insolvency case initiated by a debtor or by one or more creditors on the grounds that the debtor has failed to perform one or more valid and due obligations with a total amount exceeding 5,000,000 (five million) Riels, is a ground for opening insolvency proceedings against that debtor, except for obligations that lead to claims as specified in Article 36, points 3 to 9 of this Law.

2- The Minister of the Ministry of Justice may decide to modify the minimum obligation amount that constitutes a ground for opening insolvency proceedings as specified in point 1 above.

### Article 8 .-
1- A complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings may be filed by the debtor, one or more creditors, a director of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office.

2- A complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings must be made in writing and signed by the complainant. This petition must state the following information:

    a- Name and address of the debtor;

    b- Name and address of the complainant;

    c- Grounds under Article 7 of this Law, which form part of the complaint;

    d- A statement of the circumstances leading to the grounds of the complaint;

    e- Supporting evidence of the complaint, including any attached documents that can support the complaint;

    f- A list of all creditors of the debtor clearly specifying the names, addresses of the creditors, and the amounts claimed, in the case where the complaint is filed by the debtor.

3- The examination of the complaint, the payment of court fees, and the security for the complaint shall comply with the provisions of the Code of Civil Procedure.

### Article 9 .-

1- In the event that the debtor is no longer able to meet one or more payment obligations as specified in Article 7, which are due and valid, within 30 (thirty) days from the due date, the debtor must file a complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings against themselves.

2- In the event that the debtor is a sole proprietorship, the debtor shall have the obligation to ensure that the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings is filed in accordance with point 1 above. In the event that the debtor is a partnership or a capital company, depending on each case, every director, partner, or manager shall have the obligation to ensure that the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings is filed in accordance with point 1 above.

3- Any person who fails to perform the obligations specified in point 2 above, whether intentionally or negligently, shall be personally liable to one or more creditors of the debtor for damages arising directly from such failure. If the obligations specified in point 2 above fall upon multiple persons, those who acted intentionally or negligently shall be jointly and severally liable for damages arising directly from the failure to perform that obligation. If these insolvency proceedings are filed against the debtor, these damages shall be included in the assets, and the provisional administrator shall take measures on behalf of all creditors to claim these damages.

### Article 10 .-

The court shall send the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings of a creditor, a director of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office to the debtor within no later than 5 (five) working days from the date of receipt of that complaint by the court.

### Article 11 .-

1- From the time of filing the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings until the court issues a decision to open the insolvency proceedings, the court shall issue a decision to appoint a provisional administrator, upon the written request of the debtor, a creditor, a director of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office.

2- The provisional administrator shall take all necessary or appropriate measures to protect the interests of the creditors. In particular, if deemed necessary, the provisional administrator may file a request to the court to arrest assets or suspend all secured or unsecured claims of creditors filed against the debtor or assets within the debtor's estate.

3- All decisions of the court made under points 1 and 2 of this Article shall be notified to the parties concerned and published to the public in a daily newspaper.

## Chapter 3
## Decision on the Complaint Requesting the Opening of Insolvency Proceedings

### Article 12 .-

1- The court shall hold a hearing on the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings filed by the debtor as soon as possible after receiving the complaint, and in any event, the hearing must be held within no later than 15 (fifteen) days from the date of receipt of the complaint.

2- The court shall hold a hearing on the complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings filed by a creditor, a director of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office within no later than 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt of the complaint.

3- The debtor, creditors, directors of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office have the right to participate in the hearing.

### Article 13 .-

1- After holding a hearing on the complaint, if the court deems that the complaint is based on appropriate grounds, the court shall issue a written decision to:

    a- Open insolvency proceedings against the debtor;

    b- Appoint a provisional administrator in accordance with Article 11 of this Law;

    c- Schedule the date for opening the first creditors' meeting, which shall be from 30 (thirty) days but not exceed 60 (sixty) days after opening the insolvency proceedings;

    d- Specify the deadline for submitting proof of claims, which shall be 7 (seven) days before the date of the first creditors' meeting.

2- In scheduling the date for the first creditors' meeting, the court shall examine whether the debtor has submitted a proposal for a compromise plan which the debtor claims has or lacks the support of the creditors.

3- After holding a hearing on the complaint, if the court finds that there are no appropriate grounds supporting the complaint, or if the complaint is filed by the debtor or a creditor in collusion with the debtor as a scheme or with dishonest intent, the court shall decide to dismiss the complaint.

4- The court shall issue a decision ordering the opening of insolvency proceedings or dismissing the complaint within no later than 14 (fourteen) days after the conclusion of the hearing on the complaint.

5- The debtor or creditors may appeal against the decision of the court within 7 (seven) days from the date of receipt of the notification of that decision.

### Article 14 .-

1- At the time of issuing the decision ordering the opening of insolvency proceedings or at any time after the issuance of this decision, the court shall order the liquidation of the business

of the debtor, if the court deems that the debtor's business is previously incapable of being rehabilitated and no compromise plan can be agreed upon.

2- The court may issue a decision ordering the liquidation of the business under point 1 or upon the request of the debtor, the provisional administrator, a creditor, a director of a capital company, or the prosecutor's office.

### Article 15 .-

An insolvency case shall be deemed open on the date the court issues the decision ordering the opening of insolvency proceedings.

### Article 16 .-

1- An insolvency case shall not be opened and the complaint shall be dismissed if the assets of the debtor are insufficient to cover the costs of the proceedings.

This provision shall not apply if any person concerned provides sufficient advance payment to guarantee the costs of conducting the proceedings.

2- A person who has provided an advance payment under point 1, paragraph 2 may demand the return of that money from a physical person who has failed to perform their obligation in the proceedings to open insolvency proceedings against the debtor when there is a complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings. The claim for the return of that money may be made within 5 (five) years starting from the time the money was provided to that physical person. In the case of a dispute regarding the claim that the physical person did not perform their obligation, that physical person must provide evidence that they did not fail to perform the obligation.

### Article 17 .-

1- When dismissing a complaint requesting the opening of insolvency proceedings because the complaint lacks appropriate grounds, the court shall issue a decision ordering the complainant to pay the debtor or creditors all legal costs incurred by the parties in participating in this complaint.

2- If the court finds that the complaint was filed through a scheme or with dishonest intent, or with the intention to falsely accuse the debtor or creditors, the court shall decide to order the complainant to be liable for damages caused by this complaint.

### Article 18 .-

1- The decision ordering the opening of insolvency proceedings shall be published to the public in a daily newspaper and shall be notified to the registry office and the information filing office managing security rights to also be informed.

2- Within no later than 7 (seven) days after the opening of insolvency proceedings, the provisional administrator shall issue a notification, including publication in at least two daily newspapers in the Kingdom of Cambodia, announcing the following information:

    a- Stating that the insolvency case has been opened against the debtor;

    b- The deadline for submitting written proof of claims and the address to which the written proof of claims must be sent;

    c- Announcing the time and place of opening the first creditors' meeting.

3- Within no later than 7 (seven) days after the provisional administrator has received the debtor's declaration made in accordance with Article 28 of this Law, the provisional administrator shall send a written notification to all creditors whose addresses are in that declaration, as specified in points 2-a to 2-c of this Article.

## Chapter 4
## Effects of the Opening of Insolvency Proceedings

### Section 1
### General Effects

### Article 19 .-

1- After the court decides to order the opening of insolvency proceedings and until the insolvency proceedings are closed, the court shall suspend any lawsuit, proceeding, or other claim in any form filed by or on behalf of creditors against the debtor or the assets of the debtor.

2- In the event that the provisional administrator deems that the debtor's assets still have value, the provisional administrator may permit in writing a creditor to take possession of and sell the collateral secured by a mortgage by applying the law in force, or permit the creditor to use the collateral by other means, requiring that the creditor receive payment for the debt from such use.

3- In the event that insolvency proceedings are opened against a debtor that is a general partnership, the suspension under point 1 of this Article shall also apply to all claims, lawsuits, or proceedings to enforce the debts of the debtor as well as the individual assets of the partners.

In the event that insolvency proceedings are opened against a debtor that is a limited partnership, the suspension under point 1 of this Article shall also apply to all claims, lawsuits, or proceedings to enforce the debts of the debtor as well as the individual assets of the general partners.

Only the provisional administrator may demand that the partners be liable for the debts of the debtor during the course of the insolvency proceedings.

4- The suspension of claims under point 1 of this Article shall not apply to claims for administrative expenses related to wages, fees and expenses of the provisional administrator, and other claims for administrative expenses incurred by the provisional administrator.

### Article 20 .-

1- The opening of insolvency proceedings shall determine the estate of the debtor.

2- The estate of the debtor shall include all assets, rights, and claims of every kind in which the debtor has a proprietary interest or other interest that the debtor has the right to receive on or after the date of opening the insolvency proceedings.

3- In the event that the debtor is a physical person, the following assets shall not be included in the debtor's estate:

    a- The place constituting the principal residence of the debtor, having a market value not exceeding 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels;

    b- Assets, rights, or claims that the Code of Civil Procedure exempts from execution against the debtor.

### Article 21 .-

The estate of the debtor shall be under the management and representative rights of the provisional administrator.

### Article 22 .-

The debtor must cooperate with the provisional administrator to the best of their ability and must provide the provisional administrator with all necessary information relating to their business.

### Article 23 .-

1- In order to perform their duties and other affairs, the provisional administrator shall have the following powers:

    a- To represent the debtor and manage the debtor's business in the insolvency proceedings;

    b- To receive the debtor's mail by post and electronic systems addressed to the debtor, except for letters and communications of a purely private nature;

    c- To continue the debtor's business, but only to the extent necessary to achieve the objectives of the insolvency proceedings;

    d- To receive the debtor's assets, including the debtor's books of account and documents;

    e- To collect and include in the debtor's estate the money owed from the sale of the debtor's shares or the transfer of capital contributions that the partners have not yet paid to the debtor;

    f- To use and sell the debtor's assets in the course of business, including the sale of assets not subject to security rights and pledging;

    g- To act as the representative of the debtor who is a party to resolve cases in court;

    h- To prepare a list of claims;

    i- To permit creditors to sell assets that are subject to their security rights in accordance with the law in force, and use the sale price of those assets to pay the claims of those creditors;

    j- To keep separate assets that cause difficulties or complications to the debtor's estate.

    k- To request the court to review any matter of a person that relates to the debtor's business;

    l- To terminate, cancel, or declare void any business transactions, contracts, agreements, or property transfer documents to which the debtor is a party, in accordance with this Law or other laws;

    m- To convene and organize creditors' meetings as specified in this Law;

    n- To prepare and submit proposals for a compromise plan, if necessary;

    o- To hire agents and professionals to serve as assistants in their duties;

    p- To take any other actions necessary to perform their duties in accordance with the objectives of the insolvency proceedings.

2- The Minister of the Ministry of Justice may issue a declaration (prakas) to supplement provisions on the powers of the provisional administrator in point 1 of this Article.

### Article 24 .-

1- From the date of appointment until the opening of the first creditors' meeting, the provisional administrator shall:

    a- Continue the debtor's business, if it is viable and still has economic benefits;

    b- Study and analyze the financial and economic status of the debtor and the possibility of the debtor's rehabilitation, and prepare a report as specified in Article 52 of this Law.

2- In performing their duties, the provisional administrator shall use the experience, skills, and reasonable care as best as possible.

### Article 25 .-

1- The provisional administrator must obtain the consent of the creditors through a creditors' meeting before taking the following actions:

    a- Selling real property of the debtor through a private contract;

    b- Selling or transferring any asset of the debtor to a related person, by any means;

    c- Undertaking any transaction that may affect the approval or implementation of a compromise plan.

2- Any transaction executed in violation of points 1-a and 1-b of this Article shall be deemed a breach of duty by the provisional administrator and shall render the provisional administrator personally liable for damages to the debtor's estate or to any party in this case.

### Article 26 .-

1- Within no later than 30 (thirty) days after the opening of insolvency proceedings, the provisional administrator shall determine the value of the collateral held by a creditor that the debtor pledged in good faith. The valuation by the provisional administrator shall be deemed as the correct determination of the value of the secured portion of the creditor's claim, unless there is an objection by the debtor or the creditor.

2- A creditor's claim that exceeds the value determined under point 1 of this Article, or is not fully paid after using the sale price of the collateral for repayment, shall have the right to receive payment from the insolvency case in the same manner as an unsecured claim. This provision shall not apply if the collateral was provided by the debtor to secure the debt of a third party who is not the debtor.

3- Before the expiry of the 30 (thirty) day period specified in point 1 of this Article, the valuation shall not be carried out if:

a- The court has ordered the termination of the liquidation of the debtor's business;

or

b- The provisional administrator has sold or permitted the creditor to sell the secured assets in accordance with the applicable law, or permitted the exercise of rights over the secured assets and used the proceeds from such sale to settle the creditor's claims.

### Article 27 .-

1- The provisional administrator shall pay to the creditor who has a security right over the debtor's assets all interest accrued, calculated at the contractual interest rate of the secured asset. If the security right over the secured asset was provided to secure the debt of any person other than the debtor, the provisional administrator shall only pay the interest arising from the security placement, if that person has no capacity to settle the debt owed to the creditor.

2- In case there is no interest rate specified in the contract, the interest rate shall be calculated based on the market interest rate.

3- The interest rate under point 1 of this Article which is payable monthly shall only apply upon the expiry of a period of 30 (thirty) days after the opening of the insolvency proceedings, and shall continue to be paid during the period in which the provisional administrator has not yet sold the assets. The proceeds from the sale shall be used to settle the creditor's claims.

### Article 28 .-

1- The court may decide that a debtor who is a sole proprietor and the debtor's family may or may not receive a portion of the distribution of the debtor's assets

2- In case there is a decision of the court under point 1 above, the provisional administrator, with the approval of the creditors' committee, may deduct the maintenance cost

the debtor's assets to a debtor who is a sole proprietor and to the family of the debtor to the extent necessary to support their livelihood.

### Article 29 .-

1- At the latest within 15 (fifteen) days after the opening of the insolvency proceedings, the debtor must submit to the court and to the provisional administrator a written declaration detailing:

a- The debtor's assets; and

b- All of the debtor's creditors, specifying the names of the creditors, their addresses, and detailed statements of the debts owed to each creditor.

2- The debtor must agree to allow inspections of his assets and business affairs upon request by the provisional administrator or the court.

## Section 2
## Safeguarding, Protecting, and Strengthening the Debtor's Assets

### Article 30 .-

1- Upon the opening of insolvency proceedings, if a contract between the debtor and a contract party has not yet been fully performed by the debtor or by the contract party, the provisional administrator may decide to continue the performance of the contract in accordance with the provisions of this Article.

2- The provisional administrator who decides to continue the performance of the contract must declare this decision in writing to the contract party within 30 (thirty) days calculated from the opening of the insolvency proceedings.

In the absence of a written declaration issued by the provisional administrator, the contract shall be deemed rejected, and the contract party may file a complaint seeking compensation for damages as a creditor in the insolvency proceedings. Upon receipt of a written application for an extension of time

in writing from the provisional administrator, the court may extend the period during which the provisional

administrator must declare the decision to continue or not to continue the contract. Any such extension shall not exceed 15 (fifteen) days.

3- The contract party may also submit a written request to the provisional administrator at any time, asking the provisional administrator to perform this contract.

In the absence of a written declaration from the provisional administrator within 7 (seven) days calculated from the date the contract party submitted the request, the contract

shall be deemed terminated, and the contract party may file a complaint seeking compensation for damages as a creditor in the insolvency proceedings. Upon receipt of a written request for extension of time

from the provisional administrator, the court may extend the period during which the provisional administrator must declare whether he will perform or reject the contract. Any such extension shall not

exceed 15 (fifteen) days.

4- In case the contract is a contract for the purchase and sale of securities, rights, or other commodities

that have similar characteristics on the market, or the securities market is a part

of a netting framework agreement that includes multiple contracts, the decision

of the provisional administrator to continue the contract must be made for the benefit

of all contracts under that framework agreement.

The provisional administrator may not

decide to continue only some of the contracts contained in this framework agreement.

5- For the decision of the provisional administrator to continue the performance of a contract that

requires regular payments to the contract party, this decision shall not have

the effect of limiting the payments that remain unpaid before the opening of the insolvency proceedings.

All claims arising from the contract that the provisional administrator

has decided to continue shall be deemed administrative obligations arising

out by the provisional administrator in the course of the insolvency proceedings and shall be in accordance

with the priority ranking specified in Article 57 of this Law.

### Article 31 .-

1- Essential services shall not be terminated, modified, or cut off from the provisional administrator

or the debtor solely on the grounds of the opening of the insolvency proceedings against

the debtor or the grounds that the debtor remains in arrears for essential services consumed

before the opening of these insolvency proceedings.

2- Without prejudice to the rights of the provisional administrator to decide to continue the performance of

the contract in accordance with Article 30 of this Law, the provider of essential services

may refuse to continue the contract to provide services to the debtor if the provisional administrator

fails to provide adequate security for payment within 30 (thirty) days calculated

from the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings in the form of a deposit or a guarantee for services

to be provided under the contract continuation after the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings.

### Article 32 .-

Upon the complaint of the provisional administrator and after hearing the other party,

the court may decide to declare null and void certain transactions and may

declare appropriate measures in accordance with Article 33 of this Law if those specific transactions

are as follows:

1- Transactions made by the debtor for the purpose of hindering the creditors

by concealing his assets so that the creditors cannot find them;

2- Transactions executed within 3 (three) years prior to the opening of the insolvency

proceedings, for which the debtor did not receive a fair value from that transaction, except

for ordinary transactions made for the benefit of the spouse of the debtor, or collateral relatives

of the spouse, or ancestors, or descendants.

3- Transactions executed within 1 (one) year prior to the opening of the insolvency proceedings, in which the obligation of the debtor is greater than the value of the obligation of the other party.

4- Transactions executed within 1 (one) year prior to the opening of the insolvency proceedings, in which the debtor settled a debt that was not yet due, or provided new security or additional quality on the debt, where the other party to the transaction is a related person.

5- Transactions executed within 6 (six) months prior to the opening of the insolvency proceedings, in which the debtor settled a debt that was not yet due, or provided new security or additional quality on the debt, where the other party to the transaction is not a related person.

6- Transactions executed within 1 (one) year prior to the opening of the insolvency proceedings, in which the debtor settled a debt that was not yet due, or provided new security, or granted security rights for debts of a type described in Article 36, point 5 of this Law.

### Article 33 .-

1- When a transaction or transfer is declared by the court under Article 32 of this Law, all cash, assets transferred, or proceeds from the sale of the transferred assets shall be recovered and included in the debtor's assets.

2- A person who is sued for recovery of money or assets due to the nullity of the transaction has the right to receive only the claim for the value that he had provided for that transaction. The recovery shall be deducted from the debtor's assets to the extent that the transaction assets are still among the debtor's assets. This person shall also have the right to file a complaint seeking compensation as a creditor in the insolvency proceedings.

### Article 34 .-

1- On the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings, by operation of law or by virtue of a contract, if the creditor has the right to set-off any claim against the obligation he owes to the debtor, the opening of the insolvency proceedings shall not affect this right of set-off.

2- In case there is an objection from the debtor, the set-off by the creditor shall be deemed null and void if:

a- The creditor became a debtor of the estate after the opening of the insolvency proceedings;

b- The creditor acquired the claim from another creditor after the opening of the insolvency proceedings;

c- The creditor obtained the right to set-off through a commercial transaction which is subject to avoidance or nullity under this Law or other laws;

d- The claim of the creditor is of a type specified in Article 36 of this Law.

## Section 3
## Creditors' Rights

### Article 35 .-

1- The debtor's assets shall be used to settle claims against the debtor and administrative claims. Administrative claims shall include fees and other expenses of the provisional administrator in the course of the proceedings.

2- Claims that are not yet due at the time of the opening of the liquidation proceedings shall be deemed due on the date of the opening of the liquidation proceedings.

3- Claims subject to a resolutory condition shall be treated as unconditional claims, as long as that condition has not yet been fulfilled.

### Article 36 .-

The following claims shall not be admissible in insolvency proceedings:

1- Interest accrued on claims after the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings;

2- Expenses incurred by a creditor for participating in the insolvency proceedings;

3- Fines and penalties arising from criminal offenses, administrative fines, and penalties arising from administrative offenses that require the debtor to pay money;

4- Claims requesting the performance of an obligation that has no fair value;

5- Claims for payment of money made by any person who directly or indirectly holds more than 10 (ten percent) of the debtor's capital, which was lent or resulted in that debt in bad faith, or who has provided a guarantee or security for the debtor. If the debtor is a partnership, the claims in this paragraph shall only apply in cases where all partners of the debtor are capital companies;

6- Claims that a co-debtor or guarantor can assert against the debtor if they have settled the claim of the creditor;

7- Claims for which proof of claim was submitted after the deadline for submitting such proof;

8- Subordinated claims in the insolvency proceedings which were agreed upon between the creditor and the debtor;

9- Interest on claims of the types mentioned above.

### Article 37 .-

Non-cash claims shall be settled at an estimated value in Riels on the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings. Claims for debt denominated in foreign currency or other exchange documents shall be converted into Riels at the exchange rate set by the National Bank of Cambodia on the date of opening of the insolvency proceedings.

### Article 38 .-

1- The creditor must submit all proof of claims to the provisional administrator in writing by the specified deadline.

2- Each proof of claim must contain the following information:

a- Legal basis and grounds for the claim

b- Time when the claim arose

c- Amount of the claim

d- Any priority rights that may be attached to the claim under Article 57 of this Law

e- Nature and ranking of the security interest specified in the assets of the debtor, and a detailed description of the secured assets

f- All documents and other evidence, if any.

3- The provisional administrator must assist the creditor in completing the legal forms.

### Article 39 .-

1- The provisional administrator must prepare a list of claims, specifying details of all claims submitted by the creditors in accordance with Article 38 of this Law. The list of claims must be submitted to the court not later than 5 (five) days before

the opening date of the creditors' meeting. The list of claims must be made available for inspection by any person free of charge.

2- The list of claims must contain a summary in a simple format, in accordance with Article 38, point 2 (a) to point 2 (e). In addition, in case the secured property is subject to a claim, the valuation of the secured portion of the claim shall be determined in accordance with Article 26 of this Law.

3- The list of claims shall be verified at the opening of the creditors' meeting in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 6 of this Law.

## Chapter 5
## Settlement Plan

## Section 1
## Preparation of the Plan

### Article 40 .-

Any proposed settlement plan must be submitted to the court not later than 7 (seven) days before the date of opening of the creditors' meeting. The proposed settlement plan submitted to the court must be made available for inspection by the public free of charge.

### Article 41 .-

1- The settlement plan must state the implementation period and may state the methods for resolving the debtor's insolvency status, including:

a- The cancellation or reduction of the amount of any claim, such as swapping it for shares or assets in the debtor's business;

b- The setting of a schedule for payments of the claims;

c- The continuation of the debtor's business or any part of this business by the debtor or another person;

d- The sale or liquidation of the debtor's assets, whether secured or unsecured, or subject to a third-party right of retention, or the distribution of part or all of the debtor's assets to persons having rights to those assets.

2- The settlement plan must provide for the payment in full of all costs of the proceedings and other administrative claims, and claims made by the provisional administrator in the course of the insolvency proceedings.

### Article 42 .-

Claims that are not recognized shall not be included in the settlement plan, except for claims made in accordance with Article 39, point 3 of this Law.

### Article 43 .-

Expenses incurred in the preparation of a proposed settlement plan by the debtor or the provisional administrator shall be treated as administrative expenses to be paid out of the debtor's assets. Expenses incurred from other proposed plans shall be treated as expenses of the party who proposed them.

## Section 2
## Approval of the Settlement Plan

### Article 44 .-

The settlement plan must be submitted for review and approval by the creditors during the opening of the creditors' meeting or another meeting called for this purpose.

### Article 45 .-

1- In approving the settlement plan, the creditors shall be divided into groups, and each group must vote in accordance with the amount of their claims, according to the groups described below:

a- Secured creditors with claims;

b- Creditors whose claims are under Article 57, point 1-c of this Law;

c- Creditors whose claims are under Article 57, point 1-d of this Law.

2- A compromise plan must be approved by:

a- All classes of creditors, through a vote of approval by creditors of each class, representing not less than 3/4 (three-quarters) of the claims of the creditors present at the voting meeting; or

b- At least one class of creditors, through a vote of approval by creditors of that class, representing not less than 3/4 (three-quarters) of the claims of the creditors present at the voting meeting.

### Article 46 .-
Voting rights of creditors in voting on the compromise plan shall comply with Article 75 of this Law and shall be subject to the following conditions:

1- Creditors whose claims are not impaired or affected by the compromise plan shall not have voting rights; and

2- Secured creditors shall have voting rights only:

a- In cases where the total amount of claims exceeds:

- the value of the secured property determined under Article 26 of this Law, or

- the value of the proceeds from the sale of the secured property, or

b- In cases where the creditor waives their rights to the property placed

### Article 47 .-

1- Within a period of not more than 7 (seven) days after all creditors have agreed on the compromise plan under the conditions of Article 45 of this Law, the provisional administrator shall file an application for court approval of the compromise plan.

2- The court shall issue a decision to approve the compromise plan if the following conditions are met:

a- The voting was conducted in accordance with the conditions of this Law;

b- Under the compromise plan, all creditors of every class shall be treated equally within the same class, unless a creditor has agreed in writing to a lower priority than other creditors;

c- Under the compromise plan, every claiming creditor shall receive payment for their claim at a level not less than the level that this creditor would receive from the distribution during liquidation in accordance with Article 78 of this Law;

d- Under this compromise plan, no creditor may receive an amount exceeding the total amount of their claim;

e- Under this compromise plan, no payment related to dividend income or capital contributions shall be made to shareholders or co-owners of the debtor until the distribution of money has been made to the entitled persons according to the class of creditors whose claims are in the compromise plan first;

f- Under this compromise plan, no payment for maintenance allowance shall be made that exceeds the maintenance allowance determined by the court for a debtor who is a physical person or a co-owner of a debtor that is a partnership, until the distribution of money has been made to the entitled persons according to the class of creditors whose claims are in the compromise plan.

3- If the compromise plan does not meet the conditions specified in point 2 of this Article, the court shall decide to open liquidation proceedings against the assets of the debtor.

4- The decision of the court under this Article shall be made not later than 7 (seven) days after the provisional administrator files an application for court approval of the compromise plan.

### Article 48 .-

1- The court's approval of the compromise plan shall have the effect of closing the insolvency case and shall commence the determined period of implementation of the compromise plan, but the implementation of this plan shall not exceed the maximum period of 2 (two) years after the court approves the compromise plan.

2- The compromise plan approved by the court shall bind all creditors and extinguish all claims that cannot be recognized, except for the claims specified in Article 36, point 3 of this Law.

### Article 49 .-

1- During the period of implementation of the compromise plan, all business documents issued by the debtor or on behalf of the debtor containing the debtor's name must contain a statement from the administrator in order to permit the implementation of the compromise plan on the debtor.

2- Upon approval of the compromise plan or at a later date, for the purpose of ensuring the implementation of the compromise plan, the court may order the provisional administrator to continue managing, under conditions that the court deems appropriate, the activities of the debtor or the activities of the manager of the debtor. In doing so, in addition to this, the court shall also determine the amount of expenses for

the provisional administrator, which shall also be paid by the debtor.

### Article 50 .-

1- In cases where the compromise plan is not implemented within the determined period, the court shall decide to open insolvency proceedings against the debtor.

2- An application to open insolvency proceedings under point 1 above may be made by the debtor, a creditor, or the prosecutor's office, or the provisional administrator, as specified in Article 49, point 2 of this Law.

3- Insolvency proceedings opened under point 1 above shall commence immediately along with the liquidation of the debtor's assets. The effects of the compromise plan relating to claims, secured rights, and other rights of creditors shall remain preserved in this liquidation.

## Chapter 6
## Convening Creditors' Meetings

### Article 51 .-

With the assistance of the provisional administrator, the judge shall convene and preside over the first creditors' meeting.

### Article 52 .-

Upon convening the creditors' meeting, the provisional administrator shall report on the business status of the debtor and the reasons leading to such business status. The provisional administrator must indicate whether there is feasibility or not in continuing all or part of the debtor's business, whether there is feasibility or not to request approval of the compromise plan and the implementation of the compromise plan, as well as specify the consequences that will arise for payments to the creditors.

### Article 53 .-

At the creditors' meeting, the creditors shall decide on a number of important issues as follows:

1- Decide on the plan to continue the insolvency proceedings based on the report prepared by the provisional administrator for this meeting.

2- Decide on the compromise plan proposed by the debtor.

3- Review and verify claims.

4- Decide on other issues relating to the insolvency case.

### Article 54 .-

1- After considering the report of the provisional administrator, if the compromise plan is agreed to by the creditors, the creditors must vote to decide on this compromise plan.

2- In cases where there is no proposal for a compromise plan by the debtor, or the proposed compromise plan is not agreed to by the creditors, the creditors shall decide to either:

a- Adjourn the creditors' meeting, but not for more than 60 (sixty) days, to allow the debtor, the provisional administrator, or one or more creditors holding a total amount of claims of not less than 1/5 (one-fifth) of all claims to submit a proposal for a compromise plan, or

b- Open liquidation proceedings against the assets of the debtor.

3- If the creditors' meeting is adjourned in accordance with point 2 a above, at the reconvened meeting, the creditors shall consider and vote only on the compromise plan(s) proposed. In cases where the creditors

do not approve the compromise plan at this reconvened meeting, the liquidation of the debtor's assets shall commence.

### Article 55 .-

1- The provisional administrator, the debtor, or any creditor may object to the validity, the amount, the determination of secured rights, or other priorities of a claim in the claims registry. A claim shall be recognized for distribution in the insolvency proceedings if there is no objection to that claim by the provisional administrator or by any creditor at the creditors' meeting.

2- In cases where the provisional administrator or a creditor objects to the validity, the amount, the determination of secured rights, or other priorities of this claim, the creditor must seek a judgment deciding on this claim through the court. The court must hold a hearing on this case as soon as possible.

3- The claims registry shall be prepared by the provisional administrator and filed with the court after making necessary modifications in accordance with the effects of the final decision specified in point 2 above.

## Chapter 7
## Liquidation and Settlement of Claims

### Article 56 .-

1- At the commencement of liquidation, the provisional administrator shall convert the non-cash assets of the debtor into cash as quickly as possible to meet the requirements of settling the claims of creditors in the insolvency case.

2- In managing the assets of the debtor, the provisional administrator must use all appropriate commercial means that lead to obtaining the highest cash yield for the debtor.

3- All non-cash assets of the debtor sold by the provisional administrator in liquidation must be free of any encumbrance or third-party rights.

### Article 57 .-

1- Cash proceeds from the liquidation of the debtor's assets shall be used to pay claims in the following order of priority:

a- Salaries and wages of staff and employees, remuneration of the provisional administrator, administrative expenses, and court costs;

b- Secured claims, up to the maximum value of the secured portion of the claim as determined under Article 26 of this Law, or the net proceeds from the sale of that secured property;

c- State taxes for which no notice of claim has been filed;

d- All recognized unsecured claims.

2- For secured claims specified in point 1-b, any portion of these claims exceeding the limit specified in these points shall have the right to receive payment in the same manner as general unsecured claims under point 1-d.

3- Any remaining proceeds from the distribution after paying all claims specified in point 1 shall be returned to the debtor.

### Article 58 .-

1- Except for the debtor's assets that cannot be sold, the provisional administrator must implement the liquidation of the debtor's assets and distribute the proceeds of the sale within a period not exceeding 6 (six) months after the commencement of liquidation.

Upon written application of the provisional administrator, the court may extend the period for an additional 6 (six) months for the liquidation of the debtor's assets and the distribution of proceeds of the sale.

2- Funds may be distributed to creditors in the insolvency proceedings immediately when there is sufficient money in the debtor's estate.

## Chapter 8
## Closing of Insolvency Case After Liquidation

### Article 59 .-

The provisional administrator must submit to the court a written report on their activities. This report shall specify the final accounts of distribution and claims that have not yet been settled. The provisional administrator must submit this report not later than 30 (thirty) days after completely liquidating all sellable parts of the debtor's assets.

### Article 60 .-

The court must convene a final creditors' meeting within a period of 14 (fourteen) days after it has received the report from the provisional administrator in accordance with Article 59 of this Law. This creditors' meeting must approve the final accounts of distribution and claims that have not yet been settled and decide on the disposition of parts of the debtor's assets that cannot be sold.

### Article 61 .-

1- The court shall issue a decision to close the insolvency case immediately after the final creditors' meeting.

2- The decision of the court to close the insolvency case shall be published in the Royal Gazette and in daily newspapers of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

### Article 62 .-

Upon the closing of the insolvency case after liquidation, the office of the provisional administrator and the office of the creditors' committee shall be closed. The debtor has the right to liquidate any remaining assets.

### Article 63 .-

In cases where the debtor is a capital company, the company shall be deemed dissolved when the court issues a decision to close the insolvency case during liquidation, unless the closing of this insolvency case arises from the payment of all claims in accordance with Article 57 of this Law.

### Article 64 .-

1- In cases where the insolvency case is opened against a debtor who is a physical person or a partnership in the Kingdom of Cambodia, and there is a closing of the insolvency case during liquidation, the debtor may file an application with the court to be discharged from all claims that have been recognized but not yet paid in the insolvency proceedings, unless the debtor has received assets or funds that were not completely distributed under Article 57, point 3 of this Law. Notwithstanding the decision under point 1, unrecognized claims shall be deemed void upon the closing of the insolvency case, except for the claims specified in Article 36, point 3 of this Law.

2- All creditors and the prosecutor's office have the right to participate in the court hearing to object to the application under point 1.

3- The court shall refuse to discharge the debtor from unpaid claims if:

a- The debtor has been convicted of a criminal offense related to fraud or breach of trust in connection with the insolvency of the debtor's business;

b- Within the last 3 (three) years prior to the opening of insolvency proceedings, the debtor intentionally or negligently made false or insufficient written statements regarding their financial condition in order to obtain credit or payments from public funds, or to avoid payments to public entities;

c- Within the last 10 (ten) years prior to the opening of insolvency proceedings, the debtor was already granted a discharge of unpaid claims in a previous insolvency proceeding;

d- Within the last 12 (twelve) months prior to the opening of insolvency proceedings, the debtor failed to make full payments to creditors due to creating inappropriate debts resulting in damage to assets, or delaying the opening of insolvency proceedings, or if the creditors had no intention to improve the debtor's economic conditions, whether intentionally or unintentionally;

e- The debtor failed to fulfill their obligations under Article 9 of this Law, whether intentionally or unintentionally;

f- During the opening of insolvency proceedings, the debtor breached the obligation to provide information and the obligation to cooperate in the insolvency proceedings, whether intentionally or unintentionally;

g- The debtor provided false or insufficient information under Article 29 of this Law, whether intentionally or unintentionally.

4- A decision made under this Article shall not affect the ability of a creditor to file a petition requesting to reopen insolvency proceedings against the debtor under Chapter 9.

## Chapter 9
## Reopening of Closed Insolvency Proceedings After Liquidation

### Article 95 .-
Every creditor who has a claim on the list of claims and has not received payment in full may file a written petition to the court requesting to reopen the insolvency proceedings within 1 (one) year from the closure of the insolvency proceedings.

### Article 96 .-
1- The insolvency proceedings shall be reopened by a court decision when:

a- The amount specified for the claim was discharged from the claims at the time of the closure of the insolvency proceedings.

b- There are assets that can be used to pay the creditors' claims, and the court finds that the administration of these assets was not considered during the insolvency proceedings.

2- Insolvency proceedings shall not be reopened when the cash or value of the assets specified in point 1 is insufficient to pay the expenses of reopening the proceedings.

### Article 97 .-
A decision to reopen insolvency proceedings shall reinstate the rights of the provisional administrator, the administrator, or the creditors' committee.

2- Reopened insolvency proceedings shall be conducted in the same manner as liquidation, and the provisions relating to this Law shall apply in the same manner as in normal insolvency proceedings.

## Chapter 10
## Provisional Administrator and Creditors

### Section 1
### Provisional Administrator

### Article 68 .-
1- Only a natural person may be appointed by the court as a provisional administrator.

2- A provisional administrator shall not:

a- Be a person who has been convicted by the court of an offense related to fraud or breach of trust;

b- Be a debtor, a creditor, or a related person; or

c- Be a spouse of the creditor or the debtor, or a relative of either the debtor or any creditor.

3- A Prakas of the Minister of Justice may require that:

a- The provisional administrator has additional qualifications than those specified in paragraph 2;

b- All provisional administrators must obtain a license from the Ministry of Justice in accordance with the conditions determined in the Prakas of the Ministry of Justice.

### Article 69 .-
1- The court shall declare the removal of the provisional administrator from the insolvency proceedings in any of the following cases:

a- Upon the written resignation of the provisional administrator;

b- The provisional administrator loses the qualifications as specified in Article

68 of this Law;

c- Upon the request of creditors representing at least 51% (fifty-one percent) of the total value of claims;

d- There is evidence proving that the provisional administrator has failed to perform their duties or has performed their duties in a manner that harms the interest of the creditors in general.

2- The court may declare the removal of the provisional administrator at any time by its own decision in accordance with paragraph 1 above.

3- The court shall appoint a new provisional administrator to replace the provisional administrator who has been removed.

### Article 70 .-
1- A provisional administrator shall be deemed an officer of the court and shall have a duty of loyalty to the court only.

2- The provisional administrator must provide information related to the management of the debtor's assets or the status of the insolvency proceedings as quickly as possible upon the written request of one or more creditors.

### Article 71 .-
1- A provisional administrator must perform their duties with care and attention, in a reasonable manner under similar circumstances as a businessman.

2- The provisional administrator shall be liable to all parties to the insolvency proceedings for damages resulting from the failure to perform their functions with care and due diligence.

### Article 72 .-
1- A provisional administrator shall receive remuneration and compensation for all reasonable expenses incurred in the performance of their duties. The calculation of this remuneration

shall consider a number of factors such as the time spent by the provisional administrator in performing their work, the total value of the debtor's assets, and the scope and complexity of the insolvency proceedings.

2- The Minister of Justice may issue a Prakas to regulate the rate of remuneration for the provisional administrator upon the proposal of the creditors.

### Section 2
### Creditors

### Article 73 .-
1- A creditors' meeting may be convened by the provisional administrator at any time if the provisional administrator deems that such meeting is beneficial to the administration of the insolvency proceedings.

2- Any creditor may make a written request to the provisional administrator to convene a creditors' meeting. The written request must specify the issues proposed for the meeting.

3- The creditors' meeting must be convened by the provisional administrator within 15 (fifteen) days after receiving the written request from the creditor who requested the meeting.

4- Every creditors' meeting shall be chaired by the provisional administrator or the judge.

5- This Article shall not apply to the initial meeting of creditors.

### Article 74 .-
1- The provisional administrator must notify all creditors of the convening of the creditors' meeting by sending a written notice to those creditors and publishing it in at least 2 (two) major daily newspapers in the Kingdom of Cambodia, announcing the following information:

a- The time of the meeting;

b- The place where the meeting is to be held;

c- Various matters proposed for discussion in the meeting.

2- All creditors must be notified of the convening of the creditors' meeting at least 7 (seven) days before the meeting is opened.

3- Non-compliance with the requirements of this Article shall result in the nullity of the decisions made in that creditors' meeting.

4- This Article shall not apply to the initial meeting of creditors.

### Article 75 .-
1- All decisions in every creditors' meeting shall be made by a majority vote in value of the total claims of the creditors present at the meeting and who have the right to vote in this meeting.

2- Only creditors whose claims are not objected to by the provisional administrator or by other creditors shall have the right to vote in the creditors' meeting. A creditor whose claim is objected to may vote only with the permission of the provisional administrator or other creditors who have the right to vote in the meeting. In the event that those parties cannot reach an agreement on the voting rights of the creditor in the creditors' meeting, the provisional administrator must send a written complaint to the court to request a decision on this dispute, and the creditors' meeting shall be suspended until a date which is 7 (seven) days from the date the court issues its decision on this dispute.

3- If the court is satisfied that, notwithstanding the provisions of point 2 above, a creditor whose claim is unrecognized shall also have no right to vote.

4- Creditors may attend all meetings and may vote in person or by proxy.

### Article 76 .-
A creditors' meeting may consider and decide on issues related to the insolvency proceedings, including:

1- Receiving and considering the report on the status of the insolvency proceedings from the provisional administrator;

2- Preparing a settlement plan, including plans proposed by the provisional administrator;

3- Approving the settlement plan;

4- Deciding to petition the court to terminate the business operations of the debtor and commence the liquidation of the debtor's assets;

5- Terminating and replacing the provisional administrator;

6- Proposing to the Minister of Justice regarding the remuneration of the provisional administrator;

7- Appointing the creditors' committee;

8- Receiving recommendations from the creditors' committee.

### Article 77 .-
Every decision made in the creditors' meeting shall be binding on all creditors, including those who are absent from the meeting.

### Article 78 .-
1- In every creditors' meeting, the creditors may elect a creditors' committee consisting of at least 3 (three) members and not more than 9 (nine)

elected from among representatives of secured creditors and unsecured creditors.

2- All expenses incurred by the creditors in the establishment and operation of the creditors' committee shall be deemed as claims incurred by the provisional administrator in the course of the insolvency proceedings and shall be paid in accordance with Article 57 of this Law.

### Article 79 .-
1- The creditors' committee shall perform its duties representing the interests of all creditors.

2- The creditors' committee must perform its obligations in accordance with the decisions of the creditors who represent a majority in value of the total claims against the debtor. Upon the request of the provisional administrator, the creditors' committee may assist the provisional administrator in managing the debtor's assets.

3- Upon request from the creditors' committee, the provisional administrator must allow the creditors' committee to inspect the accounting books and accounts of the debtor.

## Chapter 11
## Service of Documents

### Article 80 .-
1- The service of documents related to the insolvency proceedings to a partnership or legal entity shall be made by delivering the documents to the registered business office of the partnership or legal entity.

2- The service of documents related to the insolvency proceedings to a natural person residing in the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be made by delivering the documents to the residence or place of work of that person in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

3- The service of documents related to the insolvency proceedings to a legal entity established under foreign law or a natural person residing outside the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be made by publishing a notice in at least any newspaper in the Kingdom of Cambodia and in another newspaper in the country where the legal entity was established or where the natural person resides.

4- Upon the request of an interested party, the court may order the service of documents related to the insolvency proceedings in a manner other than those specified in points 1 to 3 of this Article, if the court finds that such other manner is more appropriate and reliable.

## Chapter 12
## Penal Provisions

### Article 81 .-
Every offense in insolvency proceedings shall be punished in accordance with the provisions of the criminal law in force.

## Chapter 13
## Transitional Provisions

### Article 82 .-
1- During the period when the Kingdom of Cambodia does not yet have a commercial court, the ordinary courts shall have jurisdiction over all insolvency cases specified in this Law.

2- When the commercial court is established in the Kingdom of Cambodia, the ordinary courts shall transfer jurisdiction over all insolvency cases to the commercial court.

## Chapter 14
## Final Provisions

### Article 83 .-
Any provisions contrary to this Law shall be abrogated.

### Article 84 .-
This Law shall be declared urgent.
