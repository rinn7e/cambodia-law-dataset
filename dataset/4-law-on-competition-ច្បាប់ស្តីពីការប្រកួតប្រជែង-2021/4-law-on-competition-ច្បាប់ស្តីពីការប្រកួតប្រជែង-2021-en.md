#### PROMULGATE

The Law on Competition, which was adopted by the National Assembly on September 09, 2021, at the 6th session of its 6th legislature, and which was reviewed and entirely approved by the Senate on September 20, 2021, at the 7th plenary session of its 4th legislature, the full text of which is as follows:

# Law on Competition

# Chapter 1
# General Provisions

### Article 1 .-

This Law defines activities that restrict, limit, or impede competition, and establishes and determines the competence of the Cambodia Competition Commission in order to encourage fair and honest business relationships, increase economic efficiency, stimulate the creation of new businesses, and help consumers obtain high-quality, low-cost, abundant goods and services with many choices.

### Article 2 .-

This Law applies to persons performing business activities or performing activities supporting businesses related to significantly restricting, limiting, or impeding competition in the markets of the Kingdom of Cambodia, regardless of whether those activities originate inside or outside the country.

### Article 3 .-

Key terms used in this Law are defined as follows:

1- Competition refers to the activities of persons conducting business in the market to compete for more customers to use their goods or services, gain more market share, and gain dominant power in the market.

2- Agreement refers to all forms of contracts, agreements, or arrangements between persons, whether made in writing, orally, implicitly, or including coordination by persons with direct or indirect relationships, where the objective or effect of such relationships is:

a- To influence the behavior of one or more persons in the market; or

b- To exhibit acts or activities that any person has already decided or intends to implement in the market.

3- Business Combination refers to:

- The acquisition or purchase of control rights or voting rights through the purchase of shares or assets by any person from another person; or

- The merger between two or more persons to become joint owners of an existing legal entity or a new legal entity.

4- Conflict of Interest refers to financial interests, family interests, or personal interests that may influence the acts of any person in the performance of their official duties.

5- General Department refers to the General Department in charge of competition affairs.

6- Dominant Market Position refers to a situation where any person has power in the market that allows that person to impede other competitors.

7- Essential Facilities refers to infrastructure or resources that cannot be reproduced and without which competitors cannot reasonably supply goods or services to customers.

8- Horizontal Agreement refers to an agreement between persons who operate or may operate at the same level of the production and distribution chain.

9- Vertical Agreement refers to an agreement between persons who operate or may operate at different levels of the production and distribution chain.

10- Market refers to a market of goods or services that are in competition.

11- Person refers to a natural person or legal entity established or organized correctly under the laws in force, whether for profit or non-profit, registered or unregistered.

12- Reporting Person refers to any person who voluntarily provides information about activities that may violate the Law on Competition to the CCC, whether those activities have already occurred, are occurring, or are about to occur.

13- Competitor refers to a person currently competing in any market or another person who has the potential to join the competition in that market.

14- Significantly Restricting, Limiting or Impeding Competition refers to activities that have a size or degree of impact on competition for goods or services as determined by the CCC, based on the results of economic analysis or other factors.

15- Business refers to activities where:

a- Goods or services are received or supplied, or rights to use real estate are received or disposed of; or

b- They are carried out in a systematic and clear manner; or

c- The person performing those activities has a profit motive, whether the activities performed by that person are primary, secondary, or non-regular.

16- Competent Regulator refers to any ministry or institution with functions and roles related to competition affairs under its responsibility.

# Chapter 2
# Competent Authorities

### Article 4 .-

The Cambodia Competition Commission, abbreviated as "CCC", is established and led by the Minister of Commerce, with the participation of relevant ministries and institutions, and with the General Department in charge of competition affairs as its Secretariat, to perform various tasks related to competition affairs in accordance with this Law.

The organization and functioning of the CCC shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Article 5 .-

The CCC shall be led by 1 (one) Chairperson who is the Minister of Commerce, and shall have a number of Vice-Chairpersons and members from relevant ministries and institutions as necessary.

The CCC shall have other independent individual members with a 5 (five) year mandate and composition as follows:

- 1 former judge;

- 2 persons with knowledge and experience in law;

- 2 persons with knowledge and experience in economics.

The procedures for selecting the former judge, persons with knowledge and experience in law, and persons with knowledge and experience in economics shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Commerce.

The actual composition of the CCC shall be determined by decision of the Royal Government.

### Article 6 .-

The CCC has the following roles and duties:

1- Develop policies and strategic plans related to competition;

2- Provide comments on draft legal instruments related to competition to the Royal Government;

3- Request the Royal Government to make adjustments or amendments to national and international legal instruments that affect competition;

4- Issue decisions, issue measures to suspend or temporarily set aside, and issue monetary fines;

5- Establish forms and procedures for calculating fines;

6- Establish rules regarding conflict of interest of CCC members;

7- Prepare conditions and procedures for business combinations;

8- Prepare conditions and procedures for granting authorization for any agreement or activity related to competition;

9- Prepare conditions and procedures for granting collective authorization for any agreement or activity related to competition;

10- Prepare conditions and procedures for the leniency policy on monetary fines;

11- Cooperate with national, foreign, and international competent institutions or regulators related to competition;

12- Receive complaints;

13- Issue other regulations and rules within the competence of the CCC related to competition.

# Chapter 3
# Restricting, Limiting or Impeding Competition

# Section 1
# Agreements Restricting, Limiting or Impeding Competition

### Article 7 .-

Creating and implementing horizontal agreements that directly or indirectly affect competition as follows is prohibited:

1- Agreements to fix price, control price, or maintain price for goods or services;

2- Agreements to restrict, limit, or cap:

- The quantity of goods or services available for sale;

- The types of goods or services available for sale;

- The development of new goods or services;

3- Agreements to allocate geographic territories between competitors;

4- Agreements to allocate customers between competitors;

5- Any bid-rigging intended to favor only one bidder to obtain that bid contract for private business interests.

### Article 8 .-

Vertical agreements that require the buyer to resell goods or services purchased at any minimum price directly or indirectly, or require the buyer to accept any conditions set by that person, are prohibited.

Creating and implementing vertical agreements that have or have the object or effect of significantly restricting, limiting, or impeding competition in the market as follows is prohibited:

1- Requiring the buyer to resell goods or services purchased only in a specific geographic location;

2- Requiring the buyer to resell goods or services purchased only to specific customers or specific types of customers;

3- Requiring the buyer to accept all or almost all conditions for the purchase of specific goods or services only from that seller;

4- Impeding the seller from selling goods or services to other buyers;

5- Requiring the buyer to purchase tied-in goods or services that are not related, in addition to the goods or services purchased.

# Section 2
# Abuse of Dominant Market Position

### Article 9 .-

Abusive activities by a person with a dominant market position are prohibited if those activities are performed by any person with a dominant market position and have the object or effect of significantly restricting, limiting, or impeding competition in any market as follows:

1- Requiring or inducing a supplier or customer not to do business with any competitor;

2- Refusing to supply goods or services to any competitor;

3- Selling goods or services on condition that the buyer must purchase other separate goods or services that are unrelated to the object of the contract;

4- Selling goods or services below the cost of production;

5- Refusing to allow any competitor to use essential facilities.

### Article 10 .-

A person with a dominant market position may perform activities as defined in Article 9 of this Law, which shall not be considered abusive activities if the CCC finds that the person has fulfilled both of the following conditions:

1- There is a reasonable justification for performing those activities for legitimate business interests;

2- Those activities do not significantly restrict, limit, or impede competition in the market.

# Section 3
# Business Combinations

### Article 11 .-

Any business combination that has or may have the object or effect of significantly restricting, limiting, or impeding competition in the market is prohibited.

Business combinations subject to review, monitoring, and evaluation of the impact on competition as stated in the above paragraph shall be carried out by the CCC.

The conditions and procedures for business combinations shall be determined by Sub-decree.

# Section 4
# Exemptions for Horizontal and Vertical Agreements, Abuse of Dominant Market Position, and Business Combinations

### Article 12 .-

1- Provide significant direct technological, social, or economic benefits;

2- Those benefits would not occur without those prohibited agreements or activities;

3- Those benefits outweigh the negative impacts stemming from the significant restriction, limitation, or impediment of competition;

4- Do not eliminate competition in any significant part of the goods or services.

### Article 13 .-

Any person who considers that any agreement or activity which may violate the law as stated in Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law may request authorization from the CCC before enter into such agreement or performing such activity. The CCC may grant authorization if the agreement or activity does not violate Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law.

If any agreement or activity violates Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law and the person intends to request an exemption as stated in Article 12 of this Law, they must apply for an exemption to the CCC, attaching evidence that can confirm the fulfillment of all conditions as defined in Article 12 of this Law. The CCC shall grant authorization only if the agreement or activity has fulfilled the conditions as stated in Article 12 of this Law. When granting authorization, the CCC shall specify the validity of such authorization.

The conditions and procedures for this authorization request shall be determined by the CCC.

The application for exemption authorization is subject to an administration fee, as determined by a Joint Prakas between the Minister of Economy and Finance and the Minister of Commerce.

### Article 14 .-

If the CCC considers that a type of agreement or activity on specific goods or services has fulfilled the conditions as stated in Article 12 of this Law, the CCC may grant collective authorization for those types of agreements or activities. When granting authorization, the CCC may specify the validity of such authorization.

The conditions and procedures for collective authorization for types of agreements or activities shall be determined by the CCC.

# Section 5
# Leniency Policy

### Article 15 .-

A person participating in or providing assistance to an unlawful horizontal agreement may receive leniency as determined by the CCC regarding the exemption of monetary fines in the case where this person has provided essential evidence related to the unlawful horizontal agreement. The leniency regarding the exemption of monetary fines shall continue to be provided to that person regardless of any appeal against the CCC's decision.

The conditions and procedures for determining the leniency policy shall be determined by the CCC.

# Chapter 4
# Complaint and Investigation Procedures

### Article 16 .-

The CCC is competent to receive complaints through:

1- The CCC's own initiative; or

2- Complaints from any competent regulator; or

3- Complaints from other persons outside of competent regulators.

### Article 17 .-

Investigation officers shall be selected from the General Department in charge of competition affairs through a Prakas of the Minister of Commerce to review, research, collect data, and interview relevant persons to provide information, documents, or other objects for the evaluation of market competition activities and to search for evidence under this Law, as well as to implement measures to temporarily suspend agreements or activities that significantly restrict, limit, or impede market competition as issued by the CCC.

In case of necessity, the CCC may request cooperation from competent institutions or regulators at the request of the investigation officer, and those institutions or regulators shall provide cooperation upon such request.

### Article 18 .-

Investigation officers shall be qualified as judicial police officers to examine offenses as stated in this Law in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

The forms and procedures for granting qualification to investigation officers shall be determined by a Joint Prakas between the Minister of Justice and the Minister of Commerce.

### Article 19 .-

Investigation officers have the right to search, seize evidence, summon relevant persons, and perform other procedural acts in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

Investigation officers have the right to request assistance from sub-national administrations and armed forces or other relevant competent authorities to participate in the suppression of offenses as stated in this Law.

Investigation officers may not conduct searches before 6:00 (six) AM and after 18:00 (eighteen) PM, unless there is a request for cooperation from a competition institution of any country, in which case the investigation officer must report to the prosecutor for advice.

### Article 20 .-

Investigation officers may interview any witness if the investigation officer considers that the testimony of the witness is useful for the investigation.

In the case where any witness refuses to appear without justification, thereby affecting the investigation process for offenses, the investigation officer may request the competent prosecutor to order such witness to appear and provide testimony before the investigation officer.

### Article 21 .-

A person reporting in good faith shall receive:

1- Confidentiality of identity as stated in Article 22 of this Law;

2- Security protection in case of necessity;

3- Protection from civil or criminal liability or disciplinary sanctions if such liability arises from providing or disclosing confidential information to the CCC.

The above benefits are provided to the person reporting in good faith even if such reporting does not lead to a finding of a violation of this Law.

In the case where the reporting person discloses confidential information or reports false information in bad faith, such reporting person shall not receive protection, unless otherwise decided by the CCC.

### Article 22 .-

Information of a confidential nature includes confidential information related to the business activities of a person, confidential information related to the identity of the reporting person, information about the identity of the person under investigation, information collected from the investigation, and other information determined by the CCC.

Confidential information related to the business activities of a person refers to any information whose disclosure may cause serious damage to that person's business. Confidential information related to the business activities of a person includes:

- Related technical or financial information of that person;

- Cost evaluation;

- Confidentiality of production processes;

- Sources of raw material supply;

- Quantity of production and sales;

- Market share;

- Customer lists and distributor lists;

- Marketing plans;

- Information related to cost, selling price, and sales strategy.

Confidential information related to the identity of the reporting person includes:

- Name, position, address, place of birth, or relevant social media profiles of the reporting person.

- Name, address, place of birth, or relevant social media profiles of the spouse, parents, children, or siblings of the reporting person;

- Images or voices of the reporting person;

- Images of the spouse, parents, children, or siblings of the reporting person.

Any act resulting in the disclosure of information of a confidential nature is prohibited, except as provided in Article 23 of this Law.

### Article 23 .-

During the investigation, investigation officers may disclose information of a confidential nature only in the following cases:

1- Necessary for the performance of their duties in accordance with the provisions of this Law;

2- Authorized by other laws;

3- Facilitating Royal Government agencies or foreign government agencies in the performance of their roles and duties, subject to authorization from the Chairperson of the CCC;

4- Necessary for the exercise of the right to present evidence for self-defense.

### Article 24 .-

During the investigation phase, if the investigation officer considers it necessary to issue interim measures, the investigation officer shall request the CCC to issue interim measures requiring any person who is the subject of the investigation as follows in order to:

1- Suspend the effect and/or terminate activities of any agreement related to the violation of Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law;

2- Terminate any act related to the violation of Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law;

3- Perform any act or refrain from performing any act.

### Article 25 .-

The CCC is competent to decide on negotiated settlements proposed by investigation officers, and such decisions shall be published publicly. Negotiated settlements shall be conducted with transparency, accountability, and integrity.

The conditions and procedures for such settlements shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Commerce.

# Chapter 5
# Interim Measures and Decisions of the CCC

### Article 26 .-

The CCC is competent to:

a- Issue interim measures and/or decisions;

b- Issue administrative sanctions and/or;

c- Impose monetary fines.

### Article 27 .-

The CCC may issue interim measures as follows:

1- At the request of an investigation officer with reasonable grounds to believe that Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law have been or may be violated, and the CCC considers it necessary to issue interim measures as stated in Article 24 of this Law to resolve urgent issues with the aim of:

a- Preventing any activities that are serious and irreparable to the economy or to any person; or

b- Protecting public interests in emergency situations.

2- The interim measures of the CCC as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article may require any person to:

a- Suspend the effect of any agreement or activity suspected of violating Article 7, Article 8, Article 9, and Article 11 of this Law;

b- Perform any act or refrain from performing any act, but not involving any payment.

3- The CCC may withdraw interim measures as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article at any time for reasonable and acceptable grounds.

4- All interim measures as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article shall expire:

a- When the interim measures are withdrawn under paragraph 3 of this Article; or

b- When the CCC issues a decision on the case; or

c- Within 1 (one) year starting from the date of issuing the interim measures, and new interim measures may be issued for reasonable grounds.

### Article 28 .-

After receiving a proposal from an investigation officer upon completion of the investigation, the CCC may issue a decision to any person regarding the violation of this Law based on the following conditions:

a- The decision shall be made only for the relevant person, and those decisions shall be published publicly.

b- The decision shall specify:

1- The time period to fulfill the conditions of the decision;

2- All tasks that the decision requires to be suspended or determined.

The decision shall specify the following points:

a- Arguments of the CCC showing that any Article of this Law has been violated;

b- Name or designation and address of the parties and/or their legal representatives;

c- Confirmation of the date for payment of the monetary fine and details related to the payment of the monetary fine as stated in Article 35 of this Law;

d- Confirmation of obligations for other measures as stated in Article 37 of this Law;

e- Grounds on which the CCC decided to issue the decision.

The CCC may issue a decision requiring further measures that the CCC considers necessary to rectify the implementation of an unlawful competitive activity.

### Article 29 .-

The forms and procedures for issuing interim measures and/or decisions shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of Commerce.

### Article 30 .-

Any person dissatisfied with the interim measures and/or decision of the CCC may appeal to the CCC to review and re-decide on these interim measures and/or decisions within 15 (fifteen) days counting from the date of receiving the notice of the interim measures and/or decision. The CCC may decide not to consider those appeals for justified reasons.

In the case where the CCC refuses the application for review of the interim measures and/or decision of the CCC, that person may appeal to the competent court within 30 (thirty) days counting from the date of receiving the notice of such refusal.

### Article 31 .-

The interim measures and/or decision of the CCC may be subject to appeal to the competent court within 30 (thirty) days after receiving the notice of the interim measures and/or decision.

### Article 32 .-

The interim measures shall remain in effect regardless of any appeal.

The decision shall have the effect of implementation and shall no longer be subject to appeal in the following cases:

a- The period for appeal has expired; or

b- The final competent court has rejected the appeal.

### Article 33 .-

In the case where the person violating the law fails to implement the interim measures and/or decision, the CCC shall request assistance from the competent court authorities to implement the interim measures and/or decision of the CCC.

# Chapter 6
# Penal Provisions

### Article 34 .-

Sanctions in this Law include written warnings, suspension, revocation, or cancellation of commercial registration certificates, licenses, or business-service permits, imposition of monetary fines, criminal fines, and imprisonment.

Written warnings and the imposition of monetary fines are within the competence of the CCC.

The CCC shall request the relevant ministries or institutions to take measures to suspend, revoke, or cancel commercial registration certificates, licenses, or business-service permits.

In the case of refusal to pay the monetary fine, the CCC shall file a lawsuit to claim the monetary fine in the competent court.

All monetary fines as stated in this Law shall be paid into the national budget in accordance with the public financial rules in force.

The procedures for imposing monetary fines as stated in the provisions of this Law shall be determined by a Joint Prakas of the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Justice.

The procedures for paying monetary fines, management of payment receipts, and the allocation of income from the imposition of fines as stated in the provisions of this Law shall be determined by a Joint Prakas of the Minister of Commerce and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Article 35 .-

Any person violating Article 7, Article 8, and Article 11 of this Law shall receive a written warning and a monetary fine of 3% to 10% by the CCC of the total turnover achieved by the person during the period of violation, not exceeding 3 (three) years.

In the case where a written warning and a monetary fine have already been imposed and the violation as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article continues, the commercial registration certificate, license, or business-service permit shall be suspended, revoked, or canceled.

### Article 36 .-

The provisions of the Chapter on Penalties (General Provisions) of Title 1 (General Provisions) and the Chapter on Penalties (Compulsory Payment of Fines) of Title 8 of the Code of Criminal Procedure shall apply in cases where the decision to impose a monetary fine is implemented.

### Article 37 .-

The CCC may issue additional measures against the restriction, limitation, or impediment of competition as follows:

1- Prohibit the person violating this Law from continuing any further unlawful activities;

2- Require the person violating this Law to sell shares or some parts of their business to other persons at market value to ensure competition in the market;

3- Require the person violating this Law to share their licenses or intellectual property related to the violation for the purpose of restoring, maintaining, or protecting a competitive market;

4- Require the person violating this Law who has caused damage to other persons to pay compensation to those persons equal to the amount of damage caused by the violation;

5- Require the person violating this Law to return profits derived from the violation to victims who can be clearly identified as victims of the unlawful business activity;

6- Require the return of profits derived from the violation to social organizations representing the interests of victims of the violation in cases where victims cannot be clearly identified or where individual amounts of damage cannot be specifically allocated to each victim;

7- Require the person violating this Law to provide reports confirming the implementation of the CCC's decision;

8- Require the person violating this Law to pay fees for experts appointed to provide advice to the CCC;

9- Take any other necessary measures to restore competition.

### Article 38 .-

Imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 2 (two) years and a criminal fine from 5,000,000 (five million) Riel to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riel shall be imposed on any natural person participating in an unlawful horizontal agreement as stated in Article 7 of this Law.

A criminal fine from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riel to 2,000,000,000 (two billion) Riel shall be imposed on any legal entity participating in an unlawful horizontal agreement as stated in Article 7 of this Law.

### Article 39 .-

The implementation of the provisions of Chapter 6 (Penal Provisions) of this Law does not prevent the application of other criminal laws in cases where the violation of the provisions of this Law constitutes an offense prescribed in other criminal laws.

# Chapter 7
# Final Provisions

### Article 40 .-

Any provisions that contradict this Law shall be abrogated.

### Article 41 .-

This Law shall be declared as urgent.
