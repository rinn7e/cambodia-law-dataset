## Chapter 1

### General Provisions

#### Article 1.-

This Law defines the framework for management, harvesting, use, development and conservation of forests in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

The objective of this Law is to ensure the sustainable management of forests for social, economic and environmental benefits, including the conservation of biodiversity and cultural heritage.

#### Article 2.-

This Law has scope of application to all forests, whether natural or planted.

The State guarantees customary user rights of forest products and by-products for local communities under the provisions of this Law or other relevant laws.

#### Article 3.-

Forest management shall be under the general jurisdiction of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The management of flooded forests shall be governed by a separate law.

The State grants the management rights of protected areas to the Ministry of Environment under the provisions of the Law on Environmental Protection and Natural Resource Management dated December 24, 1996, the Royal Decree on the Establishment and Designation of Protected Areas dated November 01, 1993, and other legal instruments.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries has the right to cooperate in enforcing the law on all forestry offenses occurring in protected areas under the coordination of the Ministry of Environment, in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 14 of this Law. This activity shall not affect the management jurisdiction of the Ministry of Environment, as set forth in the Law on Environmental Protection and Natural Resource Management.

#### Article 4.-

In the implementation of this Law, any decision by the Royal Government that may have a potential serious impact on the general public, local community livelihoods, and forest resources of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be subject to public participation.

Key activities related to land and forest systems that may cause adverse impacts on society and the environment shall require Environmental and Social Impact Assessments (ESIA), based on this Cambodian forest management technical framework in accordance with the Law on Environmental Protection and Natural Resource Management. The Environmental and Social Impact Assessment documents shall be made available for public comment.

For any final decision on key activities related to the forest environment system, the Royal Government shall consider the final recommendations of the Environmental and Social Impact Assessment. The Royal Government may make public notification of any final decision under this Article.

#### Article 5 -

Key terminology used and their meanings for this Law are defined in the Annex of this Law.

## Chapter 2

### Forestry Administration

#### Article 6 -

The Forestry Administration is a government authority under the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, responsible for managing forests and forest resources in accordance with the national forestry sector policy and this Law.

The Forestry Administration has a single, unified management and organizational structure nationwide as a vertical line divided into the central level, inspectorates, cantonments, divisions, and triages of the Forestry Administration.

The organization and functioning of the Forestry Administration shall be determined by a Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The Forestry Administration shall perform its duties in accordance with the principle of transparency, ensuring the right of the public to participate in decision-making on the management, sustainable use, and conservation of forests.

#### Article 7.-

The Forestry Administration shall perform the following duties:

1- Ensure the management of permanent forest estates in a sustainable manner by regulating all forestry activities.

2- Study and collect data on forests related to scientific, economic, social, and environmental factors to determine sustainable harvest levels.

3- Assess boundaries, classify and demarcate forest boundaries to establish permanent forest estate land-use maps, in coordination with the Ministry of Land Management, Urban Planning and Construction, local authorities, and local communities.

4- Prepare and implement national forestry management plans at each level of the Forestry Administration.

5- Increase reforestation on degraded forest land and deforested land.

6- Promote the development of community forestry agreements and programs by providing financial and technical assistance to communities as appropriate.

7- Develop and implement research, protection, and conservation programs for forest and wildlife resources.

8- Take appropriate measures to investigate, prevent, and suppress all forest destruction, forest fires, and forest clearing, ensuring effective law enforcement.

9- Increase public education through programs demonstrating the importance of managing, protecting, and conserving forest resources, as well as taking measures to restore natural ecosystems and conserve national forests.

10- Enhance international cooperation aimed at strengthening the capacity for protecting and developing forest resources.

11- Ensure timely and comprehensive assessment of all forestry activities that have adverse social and environmental impacts before approving those activities.

Forestry Administration officials shall have the following rights and duties:

1- Inspect harvesting activities, forest products and by-products within the permanent forest reserves, and the state's timber exports.

2- Inspect the transport of forest products, by-products, and wildlife/hunted products by any means of transportation.

3- Inspect storage sites of forest products and by-products, selling locations of forest products and by-products, or wildlife/hunted products.

4- Inspect wood processing industries, sawmills, wood processing handicrafts/workshops, and all types of kilns using forest products and by-products as raw materials or wood energy sources.

5- Inspect licenses, permits, and other documents required to be inspected under this law.

### Chapter 3
### Sustainability of Forest Management

#### Article 8.-

Sustainable forest management shall be implemented in accordance with the National Forest Policy and this Law.

To assist in the effective research of the National Forest Policy, the Royal Government shall establish a National Committee on Forest Policy, chaired by the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and with members from ministries related to the forestry sector. Based on the recommendations of the National Committee on Forest Policy, the Royal Government shall determine the National Forest Policy.

The organization and functioning of the National Committee on Forest Policy shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

The National Forest Policy may be revised by the Royal Government based on the recommendations of the National Committee on Forest Policy, in

accordance with the specific conditions of the forestry sector situation, based on principles that bring about solutions to maintain the sustainability of forest management.

#### Article 9.-

The Forestry Administration shall prepare a National Forest Management Plan in accordance with the National Forest Policy, with broad participation from all relevant authorities and local communities, in compliance with Article 4 of this Law.

The National Forest Management Plan shall be approved by the Royal Government for a long term in order to define priorities and forest management activities. The National Forest Management Plan shall be reviewed and revised every 5 years to reflect changing conditions and circumstances. The National Committee on Forest Policy shall provide recommendations to the Royal Government on the reviewed National Forest Management Plan.

The National Forest Management Plan shall include the following components:

1- Review physical, environmental, and social factors related to forest resources at all levels of the Forestry Administration.

2- Estimate the yield of forest products and by-products at all levels.

3- Determine forest land areas, areas for reforestation, and community forests.

4- Review programs and detailed activity schedules for forestry work and implement developments.

5- Develop intervention programs for stakeholders in the management and use of public and private forests.

6- Develop programs for forestry industry development and markets for forest products and by-products.

7- Develop research programs on the development of forestry technology and science.

8- Enhance and expand forestry knowledge by increasing the participation of

local communities in the management, use, and protection of forests.

9- Develop human resources and necessary material means to achieve activities planned in the program.

10- Strengthen forest management institutions at all levels, improve and strengthen law enforcement.

11- Strengthen regional and international economic cooperation and technical assistance in the protection and development of forest resources.

12- Support all programs that promote the implementation of the National Forest Policy.

Based on the recommendations of the National Committee on Forest Policy, the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall submit an annual report to the Royal Government on the implementation of the objectives of the National Forest Management Plan. This report shall be made available to the public upon request.

### Chapter 4
### Permanent Forest Estate

#### Article 10.-

The Permanent Forest Estate consists of:

1- Permanent Forest Reserves.

2- Private Forests.

The Permanent Forest Reserves consist of three categories:

1- Production Forest shall be maintained for the sustainable production of forest products and by-products, in which protection functions are considered secondary priorities. Production Forest includes:

- Forest Concessions

- Production Forest other than Forest Concessions

- Forest to be reforested

- Land areas reserved for reforestation or forest plantations

- Land areas reserved for forest regeneration

- Degraded forest land

- Community forests under agreement.

2- Protection Forest shall be maintained mainly for the protection of ecosystems and natural resources. Protection Forest includes:

- Forest reserved for special ecosystems

- Forest for research and study

- Watershed regulation forest

- Slope protection forest

- Recreation forest

- Botanical gardens

- Religious forest.

This protection forest may allow the traditional harvesting of forest products and by-products with low environmental impact by local communities.

3- Conversion Forest for other development purposes is idle land covered with secondary forest vegetation that has not yet been put under the use of any sector, and must be classified within the Permanent Forest Reserves until there is a decision by the Royal Government to use and develop it for other purposes.

Private Forest shall be maintained by its owner, who has the right to manage, harvest, use, and sell their own forest products.

#### Article 11.-

The Permanent Forest Estate shall be managed with the objective of maximizing social, economic, environmental, and cultural benefits for the Kingdom of Cambodia and its citizens, in accordance with the principles of sustainable forest management.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall classify, register, and demarcate all forest boundaries within the Permanent Forest Estate. In the implementation of these activities,

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall coordinate with local communities, relevant authorities, and the Ministry of Land Management, Urban Planning and Construction to assist in the process of registering land ownership of indigenous communities and preparing national land-use maps.

All forests classified in the Permanent Forest Reserves shall be organized and managed in accordance with the objectives of the National Forest Management Plan.

Procedures for establishment, classification, and registration of the Permanent Forest Estate shall be determined by a Sub-decree.

#### Article 12.-

The Royal Government of the Kingdom of Cambodia has the right to declassify any forest class from the Permanent Forest Reserves. Such decision shall be in the public interest and consistent with the National Forest Policy, the National Forest Management Plan, and technical, social, and economic data provided by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries. In accordance with the provisions of this law, any declassification of Permanent Forest Reserves for non-forest purposes shall consider the following priorities:

1- Forest land for conversion to other development purposes.

2- Other Permanent Forest Reserves where current demand is higher than previously determined.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may request the Royal Government to determine other idle forest land to replace the Permanent Forest Reserves that have been declassified for protection and reforestation, in order to maintain the permanent forest estate.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may request the Royal Government to decide on changing the classification of any forest within the Permanent Forest Reserves based on new study data and the function of that forest land.

All decisions to declassify forest classifications as stated in paragraph 1 and the

The reclassification of forest as stated in paragraph 3 of this Article shall be determined by Sub-decree.

### Chapter 5

### Forest Concession Management

### Article 13.-

The Royal Government of Cambodia may grant vacant production forest areas as forest concessions through public bidding, at the request of the Minister of Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, after consultation with relevant ministries, local authorities, and local communities.

The bidding documents and public bidding procedures for granting forest concessions shall be determined by a Sub-decree on Forest Concession Management.

### Article 14.-

The Royal Government of Cambodia has the authority to enter into forest concession agreements with investors or any legal entities, provided that this concession grant is consistent with the National Forest Policy, National Forest Management Plan, and all regulations of this law.

Public bidding must be conducted as a fundamental basis before the Royal Government signs a forest concession agreement, in accordance with the provisions of this law and the Sub-decree on Forest Concession Management.

The forest concession bidding committee shall provide opportunity and consideration to all qualified bidders, and shall notify about the public bidding in a timely manner, especially to local citizens in the area designated as a forest concession.

The candidacy of the bidding company must attach all bidding documents with a letter of declaration

statement of the company, which details the good performance on the forest concession agreement implemented in the past, either within the country or abroad, with supporting evidence.

### Article 15.-

A forest concessionaire has the right to manage and harvest forest products and by-products within their forest concession, ensuring that these operations do not affect:

1- Customary user rights practiced on the property land of indigenous community, registered by the State in accordance with the Land Law.

2- Rights to enter, exit and customary user rights practiced by community located inside or near the forest concession.

### Article 16.-

The minimum content of a forest concession agreement shall include:

1- The date of granting the forest concession and the duration of the grant.

2- The location of the forest concession, which indicates the identification of indigenous community property land, community forest areas, special management areas, and forest areas that can be harvested.

3- A written statement that the forest concessionaire must respect and not interfere with the harvesting of forest products and by-products in special management areas, indigenous community property lands, and community forests.

4- The type and quantity of forest products and by-products permitted to be harvested, and the methodology for calculating the harvestable quantity.

5- Description of the obligations of the forest concessionaire in constructing and implementing the forest concession management plan, coupe, and harvesting blocks.

6- Other conditions determined by the Royal Government of Cambodia or the Forestry Administration related to the forest concession.

### Article 17.-

A forest concession agreement shall not be determined for a period exceeding thirty (30) years.

A forest concession agreement may be terminated when the Royal Government finds that the forest concessionaire has seriously violated the conditions required to be terminated, as stated in the Sub-decree on Forest Concession Management.

The forest concessionaire has the right to request an explanation of the reasons for the decision to cancel the forest concession agreement prior to its expiration by the Royal Government. Resolution of disputes must be in accordance with the conditions stated in the forest concession agreement and applicable laws.

Based on the evaluation of the implementation of the forest concessionaire by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, the Royal Government may grant an extension of the agreement, and this extension period shall not exceed the duration specified in the initial forest concession agreement.

### Article 18.-

The preparation of forest concession management plans at all levels shall be the responsibility of the forest concessionaire, in accordance with the guidelines specified in the forest concession management planning manual, the Cambodian Forestry Code of Practice, and all clauses specified in the forest concession agreement.

The forest concessionaire must prepare forest concession management plans according to the following levels:

1- Long-term management plan for the entire forest concession.

2- Annual harvest plan of forest products and by-products for each coupe level.

3- Harvesting block management plan for each annual harvest.

The forest concession management plan and its submission shall be determined by the following rules:

1- The forest concession management plan shall be approved by the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries at the request of the Head of the Forestry Administration. This plan

shall be reviewed every five (5) years.

2- Annual harvesting plans at the coupe level and harvesting block management plans shall be approved by the Head of the Forestry Administration.

### Article 19.-

All forest concessionaires must incorporate into their forest concession management plans an environmental and social impact assessment, in accordance with the provisions of this law and other regulations on forest concession management.

The environmental and social impact assessment on the forest concession, which is part of the forest concession management plan, shall be prepared by the forest concessionaire and reviewed and evaluated by the Ministry of Environment, before being approved by the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall ensure public participation and comment on the forest concession management plan and the environmental and social impact assessment, before deciding to issue permits for harvesting forest products and by-products.

### Chapter 6

### Management of Production Forest outside Forest Concessions and Protection Forests

### Article 20.-

Production forests outside forest concessions shall be managed to meet domestic demand for forest products and by-products as a priority. These forest products and by-products may be exported when the supply exceeds domestic demand, upon approval of the Royal Government at the request of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

### Article 21.-

The Head of the Forestry Administration Division has the duty to prepare and submit the management plan for production forests outside forest concessions within their jurisdiction to the Head of the Forestry Administration for review and approval. The process of preparing the management plan for production forests outside forest concessions shall

proceed in accordance with the forest concession management planning model, as stated in Article 18, paragraph 2 of this Law.

Any physical person, legal entity, or community may apply for bidding to obtain annual harvesting rights for forest products and by-products within production forests outside forest concessions under the public bidding procedures.

The procedures for obtaining annual harvesting rights of forest products and by-products within production forests outside forest concessions shall be determined by a declaration (Pracas) of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

### Article 22.-

Any part of the Permanent Forest Reserve that is a special habitat area or has scientific, cultural, or tourism value, or is a biodiversity, water, and soil conservation area, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall submit a proposal to the Royal Government to designate it as a protection forest.

Any creation or dissolution of a protection forest must be reported to the National Assembly and Senate.

### Article 23.-

The Forestry Administration shall prepare the management plan for protection forests of the Permanent Forest Reserve, which is approved by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The enforcement of forestry laws and all operational activities in organizing the management and protection within the protection forests of the Permanent Forest Reserve shall be the authority of the Forestry Administration at all levels.

### Chapter 7

### Concerning Permits and Authority

### Article 24.-

All forest products and by-products located in and originating from the Permanent Forest Reserve shall be considered state property, except for the rights over forest products and by-products that are

has been granted to physical persons or legal entities in accordance with the provisions of this law.

Any physical person, legal entity, or community wishing to harvest forest products and by-products for commercial purposes must have a forest harvesting permit issued by the Forestry Administration.

The harvesting of forest products and by-products by local community members in an amount equal to or below the estimated customary use requirements, as stated in Chapter 9 of this Law, shall not require a permit.

### Article 25.-

All activities on the Permanent Forest Estate and forest products and by-products in the Kingdom of Cambodia require major permits, including:

1- Permit determining the annual allowable quota for harvesting forest products and by-products.

2- Permit for harvesting forest products and by-products.

3- Waybill for transporting forest products and by-products.

4- Permit to transport forest products and by-products.

5- Permit to use forest.

6- Declaration on the establishment of wood industry, sawmill, and processing workshop of forest products and by-products.

7- Permit to enter the forest for coupe preparation.

8- Permit to establish a depot to sell and distribute forest products and by-products.

9- Permit to establish all types of kilns using forest products and by-products as raw materials.

10- Permit determining the allowable quota for exporting forest products and by-products.

11- Permit for exporting and importing forest products and by-products.

12- Other permits in accordance with the provisions of this Law.

The permits as stated above shall be granted equal rights to:

1- A forest concessionaire who has received approval on the harvesting plan of

annual forest by-products, as required by the forest concession agreement.

2- Legal entity that has received approval on harvesting within production forest outside forest concession.

3- Community that has received the right to harvest within community forest exceeding the customary use limit under community forestry regulations.

4- Individual or customer who transports forest products and by-products from the harvesting forest area towards the designated destination.

5- Physical person, legal entity, community, or general customer who may receive rights in accordance with the provisions of this law.

Permit under this article shall be specified by:

1- Name of the permit holder.

2- Validity period of the permit.

3- Specific location and boundaries of the forest harvesting and by-products area.

4- Quantity of forest products and by-products allowed, calculated in units defined by the Forestry Administration.

5- Source and destination of the transport of forest products and by-products.

6- Other points, depending on the type of permit determined by the Forestry Administration.

Permits under this article may be extended for continued use, based on the evaluation report from the competent Forestry Administration.

Written templates for each permit shall be officially determined by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

### Article 26.-

The responsibility for issuing permits is as follows:

The Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries has the authority to decide on:

1- Permit determining the allowable quota for harvesting forest products and by-products for forest concessions and production forests outside forest concessions.

2- Permitted quota for export and import of forest products and by-products in accordance with the decision of the Royal Government.

3- Forest use permit as provided in Article 27 of this Law.

4- Proclamation on the establishment of wood industry bases, sawmills, and large and medium-scale forest products and by-products processing handicrafts.

The Director of the Forestry Administration has the authority to grant:

1- Permit for harvesting forest products and by-products for forest concessions and production forests outside forest concessions.

2- Transport permit for forest products and by-products from forest concessions and production forests outside forest concessions.

3- Permit to transport forest products and by-products for export and import.

4- Permit to enter the forest to conduct forest preparation before issuing the annual forest products and by-products harvesting permit.

5- Permit or visa on the license to export and import forest products and by-products.

The Chief of the Forestry Administration Cantonment has the authority to grant:

1- Permit determining the allowable quota for harvesting forest products and by-products for local communities.

2- Permit to establish handicraft shops for distributing forest products and by-products, and small-scale forest products and by-products processing handicrafts.

3- Permit to establish all types of kilns using forest products and by-products as raw materials for industrial or handicraft purposes.

4- Transport permit for forest products and by-products from community forests.

The Chief of the Forestry Administration Division has the authority to grant:

1- Permit for harvesting forest products and by-products in quantities exceeding customary use rights for local communities in community forests.

2- Any transport permits for forest products and by-products originating from the jurisdiction of that Forestry Administration Division.

### Article 27.-

The Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries has the authority to issue forest use permits to physical persons and legal entities for conducting activities in the Permanent Forest Reserve as follows:

1- Technical and scientific research.

2- Agricultural education and training.

3- Use of water resources for irrigation systems or agricultural purposes, with approval from the Ministry of Water Resources and Meteorology.

4- Establishment of botanical gardens or experimental stations.

5- Establishment of tree nurseries to be transferred.

6- Establishment of areas for recreation, tourism, or documentary filming.

Forest use permits under this article shall not be used for harvesting forest products and by-products from the area designated for the above activities, except for harvesting for biological research purposes to improve forest quality.

## Chapter VIII
## Prohibitions of Harvesting Forest Products and By-products and Forest Protection

### Article 28.-

Any granting or allocating of the Permanent Forest Reserve for harvesting forest products and by-products in accordance with the provisions of this law may only occur if that Permanent Forest Reserve is a production forest type and has harvesting potential, where management and control must be implemented in accordance with the technical framework for implementing forest management in Cambodia.

No person has the authority to grant permits for harvesting forest products and by-products in all types of protection forests within the Permanent Forest Reserve.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may request the Royal Government to change a protection forest area into a production forest area when the Forestry Administration has provided new research data for any protection forest that may have sufficient potential for harvesting; otherwise, the harvesting of forest products and by-products is prohibited.

### Article 29.-

Except with the permission of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries for reasons such as unfavorable weather conditions, timber cleared from authorized road construction, or other accidental conditions proposed by the Forestry Administration, the harvesting of the following forest products and by-products in the Permanent Forest Reserve is prohibited:

1- Timber species classified in grades that have not reached the minimum allowable diameter for felling.

2- Rare timber species.

3- Timber species that local people tap for resin traditionally.

4- Valuable resin-producing trees.

Forest products and by-products prohibited as provided in the first paragraph of this Article shall be determined by a proclamation of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

### Article 30.-

The processing of forest products and by-products, or the establishment and operation of wood industry bases, sawmills, wood processing factories, forest products and by-products processing handicrafts, and all types of kilns within the Permanent Forest Reserve, is prohibited.

All forest products and by-products processing bases and all types of kilns must be located at least five (5) kilometers away from the Permanent Forest Reserve.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may exempt the prohibition set forth in the second paragraph of this Article in cases where the Forestry Administration's study shows that

The benefit of this exemption shall not have social or environmental impact, or have low impact.

### Article 31.-

Clearing forest land for the purpose of constructing public roads in the Permanent Forest Reserve is prohibited, except with the decision of the Royal Government at the request of the Ministry of Public Works and Transport, with consultation with the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

Clearing forest land for the purpose of constructing forest roads in the Permanent Forest Reserve is prohibited, except with the decision of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries at the request of the Director of the Forestry Administration.

Any public road or forest road construction project in the Permanent Forest Reserve must involve consultation with local authorities and local communities, and an environmental and social impact assessment in accordance with Article 4 of this Law.

Establishing new settlements along public roads or forest roads in the Permanent Forest Reserve is prohibited, except with the permission of the Royal Government.

### Article 32.-

Forests shall be protected against all activities causing damage resulting from over-harvesting, encroaching clearing of forest land, pollution of the forest environment, forest fires, shifting cultivation, diseases, insect pests, and the import of plant and animal species without authorization.

Activities causing damage to forests that are prohibited include:

1- Displacing, removing, or destroying boundary posts or marks for designating forest boundaries.

2- Girdling, poisoning, cutting/destroying, felling trees, or cutting down tree tops without technical necessity.

3- Exercising rights to harvest forest products and by-products other than the rights that have been recognized or permitted.

4- Using any means to release or move livestock in areas with new tree seedlings regeneration after harvesting or forest fires, or in forest areas that are being planted or newly planted.

5- Establishing other processing bases or other forest by-products that bring pollution or destruction to the forest environment.

### Article 33.-

All activities of clearing forest land in the Permanent Forest Reserve are prohibited, except for cases provided in Article 31, Article 35, and Article 37 of this Law.

### Article 34.-

The export and import of forest plant seeds or forest plant species must undergo research and evaluation by the Forestry Administration, and must have permission from the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The import of all types of forest plant seeds must have a phytosanitary visa certified by the scientific authority of the exporting country.

### Article 35.-

In addition to other laws related to quarrying stone, soil, sand, minerals, and other natural resources to be implemented in the Permanent Forest Reserve, there must be a prior study and evaluation by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and permission must be obtained from the Royal Government, which must be implemented in accordance with Article 4 of this Law.

This permission must also limit protective measures and restoration of quarry sites for stone, soil, sand, minerals, and other natural resources, for which the right holder must bear responsibility in order to:

1- Avoid causing or worsening erosion, landslides, or causing damage to plant growth, or causing damage to hydrological systems and water quality.

2- Restore that site to its original condition after completing quarrying of stone, soil, sand, minerals, and other natural resources within the time frame that

the permits as determined.

#### Article 36.-

It is prohibited to cause forest fires within the Permanent Forest Estate. The use of fire may be authorized for the Forestry Administration for silvicultural or forest sanitation purposes.

Guidelines to define forest fire control areas, forest fire prevention, and the establishment of forest fire fighting committees as measures for implementation in all forest areas, shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

Citizens, armed forces and local authorities of all levels shall have the obligation and responsibility to conserve and protect forests, prevent and fight forest fires.

#### Article 37.-

Local communities practicing traditional shifting cultivation may practice it on collective land of indigenous ethnic minorities already registered with the State.

The local Forestry Administration division shall grant permission, management and monitoring of this activity, which is part of the community forest management plan. Shifting cultivation practice is prohibited in undisturbed natural forests within the Permanent Forest Reserve.

Forestland prepared and reserved for shifting cultivation shall be determined by Sub-decree.

#### Article 38.-

Splitting, sawing and installing timber processing machinery within the Permanent Forest Reserve are prohibited.

The use of machinery as a means for forest harvesting within the Permanent Forest Reserve is prohibited, unless a permit is issued by the Forestry Administration.

#### Article 39.-

No physical or legal entity has the right to grant permission, either directly or indirectly, to cut trees, clear forestland or perform any activities

to harvest forest products, by-products or occupy land within the Permanent Forest Reserve, contrary to the provisions of this Law.

### Chapter 9
### Traditional Use Rights, Community Forest and Private Forest Management

#### Article 40.-

For local communities living within or near the Permanent Forest Reserve, the State shall recognize and guarantee the traditional use rights of these local communities for the purpose of custom, belief, religion and living as provided in this Article.

Traditional use rights to forest products and by-products by local communities shall not require a harvesting permit. Traditional use rights under this Article include:

1- Gathering dead wood, picking wild fruits, harvesting beehives, tapping resin, and searching for all other forest by-products.

2- Using timber to build houses, animal barns, fences and to make agricultural tools.

3- Harvesting grass or grazing livestock in the forest.

4- Using other forest products and by-products in accordance with traditional family use.

5- The right to barter or sell forest by-products without a permit, provided that this sale does not threaten the forest sustainability. Any customer or third party who buys these forest by-products from the local communities for commercial purposes in accordance with the provisions of this Law may transport them to their destination by obtaining a transportation permit after paying royalties and premiums on those forest by-products.

Local communities may not transfer traditional use rights to third parties.

whether there is a mutual agreement or under a lease. Traditional use rights shall:

1- Be in accordance with natural balance, ensure the sustainability of forest resources and respect the rights of others.

2- Be in accordance with permissions and prohibitions determined by the provisions of this Law.

#### Article 41.-

The Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries has the right to allocate any part of the Permanent Forest Reserve to communities living inside or near forest areas under the form of community forest.

#### Article 42.-

The Forestry Administration Division, through a consultation process with other relevant authorities, has the duty to study and prepare the Permanent Forest Reserve to establish community forests by defining clear boundaries and appropriate size based on the capacity of forest resources and the traditional use needs of the community.

The Chief of Forestry Administration Division has the right to decide and sign community forest agreements with any local community living within or near the Permanent Forest Reserve. This agreement shall be valid for a period of not more than fifteen (15) years, but can be extended based on monitoring and evaluation reports of the Forestry Administration.

#### Article 43.-

Community forests shall be managed in an economic manner and by ensuring sustainability by local communities, in accordance with the community forest management plan, community forest rules and guidelines on community forest. The Forestry Administration shall provide technical assistance upon request of local communities and shall monitor the implementation of the community forest management plan.

The community forest management plan shall be prepared by the local community and approved by the Forestry Administration Division. This plan shall be submitted to the central Forestry Administration and shall be reviewed regularly every five (5) years or earlier if necessary.

Whether community forests are planted or natural, management must be implemented in accordance with the community forest management plan and under the technical support and monitoring of the Forestry Administration.

Rules for the establishment, management and use of community forests shall be determined by Sub-decree on Community Forest Management.

Guidelines on community forests shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

#### Article 44.-

Local communities operating under a community forest agreement have the right to harvest forest products and by-products within the forest area in accordance with the community forest management plan which defines clear boundaries as provided in the community forest agreement.

Local communities cannot use community forest under the form of concession, or sell, transfer or mortgage that forest to third parties.

#### Article 45.-

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall recognize forests related to religious beliefs of local communities living within or near those forests, and preserve them as protection forests for religious, cultural or conservation purposes. Religious belief trees may be specially marked and are not allowed to be felled, and shall be determined in the community forest management plan.

#### Article 46.-

Any person who has planted trees on their own private land or on leased state land where use rights have been granted, has the right to care for, develop, use, sell and distribute their own products.

Rules on private forest shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries to encourage individuals in planting and caring for forests.

#### Article 47.-

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may provide individuals and communities who implement forest management or tree planting achieving high quantity and quality with incentive systems including:

1- Awarding certificates of appreciation, letters of commendation and medals.

2- Providing direct development assistance under international cooperation.

3- Extending the duration of community forest agreements.

4- Publicizing their outstanding achievements.

### Chapter 10
### Wildlife Conservation

#### Article 48.-

All species of wildlife in the Kingdom of Cambodia are state property and are components of forest resources, which include: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, insects and other invertebrates, as well as their eggs and offspring. All these wildlife species are under the management, research and conservation of the Forestry Administration, excluding fish and aquatic animals.

Wildlife specimens are dead wildlife, whether whole bodies, internal organs, external organs, bone fragments or processed products, and shall be under the management authority of the Forestry Administration.

All wildlife species shall be divided into three categories:

1- Endangered species.

2- Rare species.

3- Common species.

Criteria for classifying each category and the list of species in endangered and rare categories which may vary by region, shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries upon the proposal of the Forestry Administration, in consultation with the Ministry of Environment.

#### Article 49.-

It is strictly prohibited to perform any hunting activities, harassment, abuse or destruction of all wildlife species by:

1- Using any harmful means.

2- Hunting in closed seasons.

3- Hunting in protected areas and special public areas.

With the agreement of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, the Forestry Administration has the right to issue permits for wildlife species in endangered and rare categories for the following purposes:

1- Educational study or scientific research.

2- Support of breeding programs.

3- Exchange of wildlife species in accordance with international cooperation agreements.

All activities below concerning wildlife species in endangered and rare categories are prohibited:

1- Harassing or destroying the above wildlife species and their habitats.

2- Hunting, trapping, snaring, poisoning.

3- Owning, possessing or keeping as a zoo or family pet.

4- Transporting.

5- Commercializing.

6- Exporting or importing.

Rules on activities for all types of wildlife shall be determined by a joint declaration of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Ministry of Environment.

Article 50.-

All of the following activities for common wildlife species shall be prohibited except with a permit issued by the Forestry Administration:

1- Stocking, raising in a zoo or as family property.

2- Transport and trade in quantities exceeding the amount necessary for traditional use.

Export and import of wildlife in the common species group must have a permit issued by the Forestry Administration with the agreement of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

Article 51.-

The Forestry Administration shall collect wildlife conservation fees and wildlife royalties as follows:

1- Wildlife conservation fees shall be paid into the National Forestry Development Fund.

2- Wildlife royalties shall be paid into the national budget.

Wildlife conservation fees and wildlife royalties shall be determined by a joint declaration between the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

#### Chapter 11
#### Royalties and Premiums on Forest Products and By-products

Article 52.-

Except for the cases stated in Article 53 of this Law, any physical or legal person who harvests forest products and by-products for commercial purposes from permanent forest reserves must pay royalties and premiums to the national budget through the Forestry Administration.

The Royal Government shall determine royalties and premiums based on a joint proposal of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

The State shall not require the payment of royalties and premiums for harvesting forest products and by-products from private forests.

The model forest concession agreement shall include a schedule of royalties and premiums on forest products and by-products.

Article 53.-

The Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries may reduce or waive royalties and premiums on forest products and by-products harvested from state forests for scientific purposes or to promote economic incentive for the maximum utilization of the products.

The State shall not collect royalties and premiums from local communities for forest products and by-products harvested by local communities for traditional user rights or harvested from community forests under a community forest agreement.

Article 54.-

All forest products and by-products must be assessed by Forestry Administration officers for volume and quality at the site in the annual harvesting coupe before being transported out of the forest.

The volume and quality of the assessed forest products and by-products must be recorded in Logbook C with the approval of the Head of the Forestry Administration.

Article 55.-

All forest products and by-products recorded in Logbook C must pay royalties and premiums.

Rules on payment and receipt of revenue from royalties on forest products and by-products shall be determined by a joint declaration between the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Article 56.-

The permit holder for harvesting forest products and by-products cannot transfer or sell their rights to the forest products and by-products to any other person, unless they have paid the royalties and premiums on those forest products and by-products.

The Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, after consultation with the Ministry of Economy and Finance, may decide to delay the payment of royalties and premiums on forest products and by-products for the permit holder who has been recognized by the Forestry Administration as having implemented sustainable forest management. This delay shall be based on the rules and guidelines in accordance with Article 55, Paragraph 2 of this Law.

Article 57.-

If the permit holder fails to pay royalties and premiums according to the schedule and set timeframe, the Forestry Administration has the right to confiscate those forest products and by-products unconditionally and seize those forest products and by-products as State property until there is a legal resolution to the dispute.

Article 58.-

Prior to the issuance of a permit for harvesting forest products and by-products, the permit applicant must pay a deposit to guarantee the payment of royalties and premiums to the national budget through the Forestry Administration.

The deposit amount shall be determined in the forest concession agreement or in the annual harvesting quota permit for forest products and by-products.

Harvesting of forest products and by-products from community forests under a community forest agreement shall not require any deposit.

#### Chapter 12
#### Reforestation and National Forestry Development Fund

Article 59.-

Citizens, the armed forces, and local authorities of all levels have the duty to participate in tree planting and reforestation.

The participation of citizens in tree planting and reforestation shall be encouraged by the Royal Government, through community movements and other civil society organizations.

The Forestry Administration shall take appropriate measures in forest development through dissemination, education, and raising awareness among individuals, communities, and the public to plant trees and protect forests by providing necessary technical assistance.

Article 60.-

In order to promote public education on the importance and benefits of forests, a traditional national tree planting day program, called "Arbor Day" (Rukkha Divey), shall be celebrated on July 9 of every year under the high presidency of the King of the Kingdom of Cambodia or his royal representative.

Newlywed couples of both sexes are encouraged to plant two trees under the coordination and provision of seedlings by competent Forestry Administration officers.

The Royal Government shall designate gardens/parks for national and international high-ranking leaders to plant trees as a memorial.

Article 61.-

Tree planting on state forest land may be carried out by the Forestry Administration itself, forest communities, or through the participation of citizens by granting user rights to state forest land.

Tree planting on private land may be carried out by the rightholders in accordance with private forest rules, as stated in Article 46 of this Law.

Rules on granting user rights to state forest land for tree planting shall be determined by sub-decree. These rules and regulations shall take into consideration the potential, environmental impact, and social impact of the tree species selected for planting.

Article 62.-

The Royal Government shall establish a fund called the National Forestry Development Fund, managed, allocated, and responsible by the National Forestry Development Committee with the Minister of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance as co-chairmen.

The organization and functioning of the National Forestry Development Committee shall be determined by sub-decree.

Article 63.-

The National Forestry Development Fund shall have sources of revenue from:

1- Various contributions from the Royal Government.

2- Premiums on forest products and by-products.

3- Wildlife conservation fees.

4- Assistance from various international organizations.

5- Donations from philanthropists and national and international non-governmental organizations.

6- Various service fees from the forestry sector.

All the above revenues shall be paid into the account of the National Forestry Development Fund.

Article 64.-

The National Forestry Development Fund cannot be used for the organization and functioning of the Forestry Administration.

This fund shall be used only for activities of:

1- Reforestation.

2- Silviculture and forest rehabilitation.

3- Forest and biodiversity protection and conservation.

4- Technical and scientific forestry and wildlife research.

5- Extension of forestry and wildlife sectors.

6- Development of forestry and wildlife sectors.

7- Development of community forestry.

8- Human resource training for forestry and wildlife sectors.


## Chapter 13

### Measures for Managing Forestry Activities


Article 65.-

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall establish the Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia in order to:

1- Mark legal timber prior to allowing transport from log landings in the forest.

2- Mark timber that is evidence of forestry offenses.

The Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia shall be circular, bearing the abbreviation R.C. and a serial number below those letters to identify each marking hammer.

Prior to putting this marking hammer into use, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall make a registration report of the imprint of this marking hammer and deposit it as a sample at the Ministry of Interior and provincial and municipal courts.


Article 66.-

The Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia must be clearly stamped without fail by Forestry Administration officials on all logs as follows:

1- Legal logs to be transported from the log landing in the forest to the destination must have four (4) or more stamps of the Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia at each end, the butt and top of the log.

2- Illegal logs seized by the Forestry Administration must have three (3) stamps, namely at the butt, middle, and top of the log, by making three (3) stamps together to form a triangle at each location.

For trees allowed to be felled within the Permanent Forest Reserve, there must be one (1) stamp of the Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia as a mark.

The possession and use of the Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia may be carried out only by Forestry Administration officials who have taken an oath. Rules on the management and use of such marking hammer shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.


Article 67.-

Every forest concessionaire or owner of a forest plantation may have a private marking hammer to stamp their own forest products, but the imprint of this private marking hammer must have a shape, size, and identification letters different from the imprint of the Forestry Administration marking hammer of Cambodia.

Prior to putting it into use, the forest concessionaire or owner of a forest plantation must confirm the identification mark and register the imprint of that marking hammer to request permission from the Head of the Forestry Administration and deposit it in advance at the concerned Forestry Administration office.


Article 68.-

Notwithstanding any permit in accordance with the provisions of this Law, the felling of trees, harvesting or transport of forest products and by-products within the Permanent Forest Reserve shall be prohibited from 7:00 PM to 5:00 AM.


Article 69.-

All types of forest products and by-products in circulation throughout the country must have a transport permit issued by the Forestry Administration.

All types of forest products and by-products after being processed must be transported out of the forest to be stocked at a permanent location within a period not exceeding one (1) month, and must be accompanied by a transport permit issued by the Forestry Administration.

All types of forest products and by-products in stock, wherever they are, must also be accompanied by a transport permit or stock permit issued by the Forestry Administration.

Transport of forest products, by-products or stocks contrary to the specifications in the permit, or without a transport or stock permit, shall be considered a violation of law.


Article 70.-

Prior to opening annual forest harvesting operations, the forest concessionaire or forest plantation owner must bring all types of machinery, vehicles, and mechanical equipment used for harvesting forest products and by-products to request permission for registration and tagging at the Forestry Administration.

All types of machinery, vehicles, and mechanical equipment serving the harvesting of forest products and by-products without registration and tagging by the Forestry Administration and operating within the Permanent Forest Reserve shall be prohibited.

All types of machinery, vehicles, and mechanical equipment that the legal forest concessionaire or forest plantation owner intends to import for harvesting and transporting forest products and by-products must undergo suitability assessment by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The use of all types of mechanical tools serving the harvesting of forest products and by-products must have permission from the Head of the Forestry Administration.


Article 71.-

The Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries shall determine by Prakas the number of wood industry bases, sawmills, and processing handicrafts for forest products and by-products to ensure the sustainable supply of raw materials as forest products and by-products.

Raw materials for wood industry bases, sawmills, and processing handicrafts for forest products and by-products must be sourced from forest concessions, forest plantations, salvage timber, or other legal sources in accordance with the provisions of this Law.


Article 72.-

The export of all types of forest products and by-products shall be decided based on annual quotas set by the Royal Government, upon request of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

The types of forest products and by-products allowed to be exported or imported shall be determined by Sub-decree.

The requirement for export and import licenses for forest products and by-products shall be determined by the Royal Government.


Article 73.-

Export licenses for forest products and by-products shall be issued by the Ministry of Commerce and must be visaed in advance by the Head of the Forestry Administration. The extension of the validity of such licenses shall be granted by the Ministry of Commerce.

All exported forest products and by-products packed in containers must be inspected in advance by Forestry Administration officials and sealed with lead seals prior to allowing transport to the port and customs clearance areas. The sample of lead seals on containers loading forest products and by-products for export shall be determined by Prakas of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

Export and import of forest products and by-products shall be subject to export-import duties and other taxes to be paid into the national budget.


Article 74.-

Driftwood, stranded or sunken timber that bears no mark and has no owner shall be confiscated as state property and sold by public auction.

In the event that any person claims that the timber as stated in paragraph 1 belongs to them, they must show concrete evidence such as a harvesting permit for forest products and by-products, a transport permit, or a stock permit, within thirty (30) days from the date of public announcement by the Forestry Administration.

Proceeds from the sale as stated in paragraph 1 of Article 74 of this Law shall be paid into the national budget.

In the event of loss of driftwood, stranded or sunken timber, as well as the loss of other forest products and by-products that the competent Forestry Administration has handed over for safekeeping, the individual or authority accepting safekeeping shall be held responsible before the law.

Article 75.-

Any person who harvests, transports, processes, stocks, sells, or exports or imports forest products and by-products shall be responsible for presenting the original legal documentation of such forest products and by-products to the competent Forestry Administration officials for inspection.


## Chapter 14

### Procedures for Resolving Forestry Offenses


Article 76.-

A forestry offense is a criminal offense with special characteristics as defined in this Law. The competent Forestry Administration has the status of judicial police officers to investigate forestry offenses and compile case files for the court.

Investigating, preventing, and suppressing forestry offenses is the competence of the Forestry Administration at all levels within their respective jurisdictions.

Operations of Forestry Administration officials who have received judicial police status must be carried out in accordance with the Law on Criminal Procedure.


Article 77.-

When performing missions to investigate, prevent, and suppress forestry offenses, Forestry Administration officials must wear uniforms, badges, rank insignia, and carry mission orders.

Forestry Administration officials taking an oath can perform the functions of judicial police officers only after they have received accreditation from the Prosecutor General of the Court of Appeal.

Forestry Administration officials who have received judicial police status must compile and sign reports on forestry offenses within their respective jurisdictions.

Uniforms, badges, and rank insignia of Forestry Administration officials shall be determined by Sub-decree.

Article 78.-

Local authorities at all levels, the armed forces, customs and excise authorities, seaport and river port authorities, and other relevant authorities shall facilitate and assist in providing cooperative forces to investigate, prevent and suppress forestry offenses, or temporarily keep evidence of forestry offenses, upon request by competent Forestry Administration officials.

In the event that the authorities encounter a flagrant forestry offense, those authorities shall:

1- Immediately inform the competent Forestry Administration officials or the nearest Forestry Administration office.

2- Temporarily detain the offender and seize evidence of the offense, then immediately hand them over to Forestry Administration officials who are qualified as judicial police, in order to proceed according to legal procedures.

3- Not directly collect fines or confiscate evidence of the offense.

Article 79.-

In order to perform their duties, Forestry Administration officials who are qualified as judicial police shall have the following rights:

1- Ask any individual to answer questions and provide information related to the commission of forestry offenses.

2- Enter to inspect or search any location, including airports, seaports, river ports, dry ports, border checkpoints, bus terminals, and railway stations in the event of a flagrant offense, in cooperation with relevant competent authorities.

3- Temporarily revoke all types of licenses or permits defined under this law, if the licensee or permit holder commits an offense against this law.

In the event of a flagrant offense, Forestry Administration officials who are qualified as judicial police have the right to enter and search markets, buildings, or residences in accordance with criminal procedures. The search must be conducted in the presence of the suspect and two (2) witnesses who are neighbors or the homeowner.

Article 80.-

Forestry Administration officials qualified as judicial police, in the event of a flagrant offense, shall temporarily seize:

1- All forest products, by-products, or hunted products that violate the provisions of this law.

2- Livestock encountered while damaging tree crops.

3- Machinery, vehicles, equipment, as well as livestock that have been used as a means to commit forestry and wildlife offenses.

Forestry Administration officials qualified as judicial police shall detain a person committing a forestry offense for up to 48 hours, in order to prepare a case file to be forwarded to court.

Article 81.-

The preparation of a case file for a forestry offense shall be implemented in accordance with the law on criminal procedure.

The format of the forestry offense report shall be determined by a joint declaration of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and the Ministry of Justice.

Article 82.-

Evidence of forestry offenses shall be defined as follows:

1- Forest products, by-products, or hunted products that are physical evidence of the offense.

2- Equipment, materials, and means of transport used in the commission of the offense.

Evidence as stated in paragraph 1 above shall be temporarily seized until the case of the offense is finally resolved by payment of a transactional fine or by a court judgment.

During the transport of illegal forest products, by-products, or illegal hunted products, the driver of a transport vehicle without its owner present shall be temporarily detained for the benefit of the investigation to identify the offender and accomplices.

Fresh hunted products, live animals, and wildlife specimens that have been seized and are perishable, the Forestry Administration has the right to release, destroy, or keep for public benefit with a proper written report.

Article 83.-

Forestry Administration officials qualified as judicial police shall wear uniforms, insignia, and ranks when participating in court hearings.

Article 84.-

At every court hearing, the Forestry Administration officials qualified as judicial police involved in the case file shall have the right to clarify the results of the investigation of that forestry offense to the court.

Article 85.-

The statute of limitations for forestry offenses is:

1- Class I forestry offenses shall have a statute of limitations of fifteen (15) years, calculated from the time of commission of the offense.

2- Class II forestry offenses shall have a statute of limitations of five (5) years, calculated from the time of commission of the offense.

3- Class III forestry offenses shall have a statute of limitations of one (1) year, calculated from the time of commission of the offense.

The application of the statute of limitations for forestry offenses shall be in accordance with the law on criminal procedure.

Article 86.-

Any individual who commits a forestry offense as defined under this law shall be held responsible for the actions they have committed.

Any individual who is an agent of a state or private entity who uses the means of the entity to commit a forestry offense, that individual or the head of the entity, shall be punished according to the provisions of this law.

Forestry offenses committed by individuals or groups under the organization of any entity, the head of that entity shall be punished according to the provisions of this law.

Article 87.-

Any foreign physical or legal person who commits a forestry offense shall be subject to the provisions of this law.

Article 88.-

Competent Forestry Administration officials shall have the power to temporarily suspend activities in the entire or part of a forest concession area, in order to proceed with legal procedures, in the event that the forest concessionaire violates the provisions of this law or violates the forest concession agreement, until the resolution of the dispute is completed.

Article 89.-

Any individual who is dissatisfied with any decision made by the Forestry Administration in accordance with this law has the right to file a protest within a maximum period of thirty (30) days, calculated from the date of receipt of the decision, to the Head of the Forestry Administration.

The Head of the Forestry Administration shall decide on the protest within a maximum period of thirty (30) days. After receiving the final decision on the review

by the Head of the Forestry Administration, in the event that it is unacceptable, that person may file a protest to the court.

Protests under this Article shall not affect the power and process of law enforcement of the competent Forestry Administration officials under this law.

#### Chapter 15
#### Forestry Offenses and Penal Provisions

Article 90.-

Penalties for forestry offenses include: imprisonment, confiscation of evidence, fines imposed by court, transactional fines, repair of damages, warning, revocation or cancellation of agreements or permits.

Transactional fines for forestry offenses, repair of damages, and warnings are within the competence of the Forestry Administration. In the event that the offender refuses to pay the transactional fine or repair the damages, the Forestry Administration may forward the offense case file to court.

Civil servants who commit forestry offenses shall receive additional administrative penalties in addition to the penalties defined under this law.

Article 91.-

The Forestry Administration has the right to decide on transactional fines for forestry offenses as stated in Article 96 of this law. Transactional fines for forestry offenses can only be made in the event that the offender confesses to the fault and agrees to pay the fine in accordance with the penal provisions of this law, within a period not exceeding fifteen (15) days, calculated from the date of the decision.

The format and right to decide on transactional fines shall be determined by a declaration of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

In determining the amount of the transactional fine, the following factors shall be considered:

1- Real economic benefits derived from the offense.

2- Environmental damage.

3- Frequency of the offense.

4- The amount of fine that serves as a deterrent.

5- Intentional commission of the offense.

#### Article 92.-

Transactional fines, fines imposed by court decisions, or proceeds from the sale of evidence shall be paid into the national budget. The Royal Government may decide to reward and encourage citizens and officials who have participated in suppressing any specific forestry offense.

#### Article 93.-

Any physical or legal person who violates the provisions of this Law shall be subject to the following penalties:

1- For first-class forestry offenses, shall be punished by imprisonment of five (5) to ten (10) years, and all evidence shall be confiscated as state property.

2- For second-class forestry offenses, shall be punished by imprisonment of one (1) to five (5) years and/or a fine from ten million (10,000,000) Riels to one hundred million (100,000,000) Riels. All evidence shall be confiscated as state property.

3- For third-class forestry offenses, shall be punished by imprisonment of one (1) month to one (1) year or a fine from one million (1,000,000) Riels to ten million (10,000,000) Riels. All evidence may be confiscated as state property.

4- Warning, repair of damage, transactional fine, retraction or cancellation of agreement or permit.

The basic market value of forest products and by-products shall be determined by a declaration (Prokas) of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries to implement tax units throughout the country.

#### Article 94.-

Any person who commits a forestry offense causing impact or damage to the forest environment shall pay for the cost of restoration or repair the damage to its original condition.

#### Article 95.-

The Forestry Administration may issue a warning letter and require repair of damages for the following offending activities within the permanent forest reserve:

1- Using various means to release or tether animals in permanent forest reserves, plantations, or state nurseries, causing destruction to trees or saplings therein.

2- Causing injury or damage to trees or other plants under maintenance or which have been planted.

3- Stealing or damaging fences, posts, or signs in nurseries or within permanent forest reserves.

#### Article 96.-

Shall be subject to a transactional fine by the Forestry Administration in the amount of two (2) to three (3) times the actual market value of the evidence, for any person who commits any of the following offenses:

1- Cutting trees without the authorized mark allowing them to be felled.

2- Transporting forest products or by-products without a permit.

3- Stockpiling forest products or by-products without a permit.

4- Transporting forest products or by-products to the wrong destination or exceeding the amount specified in the transport permit.

5- Using an expired transport permit for forest products or by-products.

6- Having specific types of forest products or by-products different from those specified in the transport permit.

7- Stockpiling forest products or by-products exceeding the amount specified in the permit.

8- Exporting forest products, by-products, seeds, and wild plant species exceeding the amount specified in the license.

9- Felling, sawing, squaring, and trimming round wood within the permanent forest reserve, or using other means to harvest forest products without a permit or mark.

10- Harvesting forest products or by-products outside authorized hours.

11- Transporting forest products or by-products felled or harvested in violation of the provisions of this Law.

12- Processing forest products or by-products that were felled or harvested in violation of the provisions of this Law.

13- Selling or distributing all types of forest products or by-products derived from felling, keeping, transporting, or processing in violation of the provisions of this Law.

14- All types of forest products or by-products obtained from harvesting, transport, or processing in violation of the provisions of this Law.

15- Exporting forest products or by-products with a license that has not been visaed by the Head of the Forestry Administration.

16- Breeding or raising endangered species of wildlife.

17- Owning, processing specimens, stockpiling, transporting, or importing live wildlife and specimens of rare species.

18- Breeding or raising rare species of wildlife.

19- Transporting, trading, stockpiling, processing specimens, or importing live wildlife and specimens of common species without a permit.

20- Hunting in public areas.

Any person who commits an offense as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article and does not reform within a period of one (1) month shall be subject to a transactional fine by the Forestry Administration in the amount of two (2) to four (4) times the actual market value of the evidence.

The actual evidence of forestry offenses shall be confiscated as state property in accordance with the provisions of this Law. Other evidence, excluding forest products and by-products, may be returned to the owner only in cases of offenses committed against Article 96 of this Law.

#### Article 97.-

Shall be punished under first-class forestry offenses with imprisonment of five (5) to ten (10) years, confiscation of all evidence as state property, revocation of agreements, licenses, permits, and destruction of fake evidence, for any person who commits any of the following offenses:

1- Forging a stamp/seal, using a forged stamp/seal, or destroying stamp marks of the Forestry Administration stamped on round logs.

2- Impersonating by wearing uniforms, wearing insignia, or wearing ranks of Forestry Administration officials.

3- Forging public documents related to the forestry sector or wildlife.

4- Destroying, concealing, selling, or stealing forestry evidence.

5- Destroying, altering, removing, or damaging the boundary markers of the forest domain.

6- Clearing forest, clearing forest land for agriculture, or enclosing and occupying forest land.

7- Intentionally causing forest fires.

8- Girdling, poisoning, destroying, felling trees, or uprooting and harvesting tree stumps.

9- Establishing a facility to re-process sawn wood or other cottage industry facilities for forest by-products that lead to the destruction of forests and the forest environment.

10- Hunting, killing, trading, or exporting animal species in the endangered category.

Any person who repeatedly commits a first-class forestry offense shall be punished with double the penalties for first-class forestry offenses of this Law.

#### Article 98.-

Shall be punished under second-class forestry offenses with imprisonment of one (1) to five (5) years and a fine of ten million (10,000,000) Riels to one hundred million (100,000,000) Riels, or either of these two penalties, and confiscation of all evidence as state property, for any person who commits any of the following offenses:

1- Failing to comply with the technical regulations defined in the Cambodian Code of Practice for Forest Harvesting.

2- Failing to comply with the management plan and the annual harvesting plan for forest products and by-products.

3- Using a forest use permit to harvest forest products or by-products.

4- Using machinery or vehicles to harvest forest products or by-products without a permit or authorized label/mark.

5- Quarrying or excavating stones, soil, sand, or fertilizer located within the permanent forest reserve.

6- Using forest land exceeding the authorized size or without a permit to construct public roads, forest roads, or set up construction bases or dwellings of any kind along roadsides.

7- Establishing a large or medium-sized wood industry facility, sawmill, or cottage industry facility for forest products and by-products without a declaration (Prokas).

8- Establishing all types of kilns that use forest products or by-products as raw materials without a permit.

9- Hunting during the closed season or in protected areas.

10- Hunting, killing, trading, or exporting animal species in the rare category.

11- Hunting wildlife using means that cause danger to animal biology.

12- Possessing, processing wildlife specimens, transporting or importing live animal species and specimens within the endangered group.

Shall be punished under class II forestry offense, subject to imprisonment from one (1) year to five (5) years and a fine of ten million (10,000,000) Riels to one hundred million (100,000,000) Riels, with confiscation of all evidence as state property, for any individual who commits any of the following offenses:

1- Collecting forest products and by-products without a permit.

2- Collecting forest products and by-products outside the forest area specified in the permit, or outside the geographic area specified in the annual forest product and by-product collection plan.

3- Transferring or selling any type of license or permit without authorization.

4- Felling trees in state forest plantations.

5- Collecting forest products and by-products of classified timber species that have not reached the minimum allowable size for felling, rare timber species, timber species from which local people traditionally tap resin, and valuable resin-producing timber species.

6- Importing all types of forest plant seeds without a certificate from the scientific authority of the exporting country and without a permit from the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

7- Exporting forest products and by-products without a license.

Any person who repeatedly commits class II forestry offenses shall be punished under class I forestry offenses of this Law.

#### Article 99. -

Shall be punished under class III forestry offense, subject to imprisonment from one (1) month to one (1) year or a fine from one million (1,000,000) Riels to ten million (10,000,000) Riels, with all evidence subject to confiscation as state property

for any individual who commits any of the following offenses:

1- Using all types of chainsaws for harvesting forest products and by-products without a permit from the Head of the Forestry Administration.

2- Importing all types of machinery, vehicles, and chainsaws for harvesting forest products and by-products without an assessment of suitability from the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

3- Exporting forest products and by-products without transit documents from the Forestry Administration.

4- Harassing, injuring, collecting eggs or offspring of wildlife species within the endangered or rare groups, or destroying their habitat.

5- Establishing depots, wholesaling, retailing forest products and by-products, and small-scale forest product and by-product processing handicraft workshops without a permit or in violation of regulations.

6- Establishing wood industry bases, sawmills, forest product and by-product processing handicraft workshops or any types of equipment that use or do not use forest products and by-products as raw materials or as energy sources outside the location determined by the Forestry Administration.

Any person who repeatedly commits class III forestry offenses shall be punished under class II forestry offenses of this Law.

#### Article 100. -

Any activities carried out by local authorities, police officers, officers of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, or other authority officials, in order to intervene, directly or indirectly grant permission to assist forestry operations, and carry out any activities contrary to the provisions of this Law, or threaten Forestry Administration officials, or obstruct Forestry Administration officials in performing their duties and operations, shall be considered as offenses subject to imprisonment from one (1) year to five (5) years and a fine of ten million (10,000,000) Riels to one hundred million (100,000,000) Riels.

#### Article 101. -

The following activities committed by Forestry Administration officials shall be considered as offenses subject to imprisonment from one (1) year to five (5) years and a fine from ten million (10,000,000) Riels to one hundred million (100,000,000) Riels:

1- Granting any permission contrary to the provisions of this Law.

2- Participating directly, in whole or in part, in any forestry business activities contrary to the provisions of this Law.

3- Tolerating or forgiving any forestry offenses.

4- Engaging in any forestry business, whether as full owner, shareholder, employee, or guarantor for others, during their term of office or within one (1) year after leaving office for any reason.

5- Failing to report or file a complaint on a class I forestry offense that occurred within their jurisdiction in a timely manner.

6- Deliberately neglecting duties or providing false written report information which causes the occurrence of class I forestry offenses.

### Chapter 16
### Execution of Court Judgments

#### Article 102. -

The execution of final court judgments or verdicts for forestry offenses is the duty of the competent Forestry Administration, except for imprisonment sentences.

#### Article 103. -

A copy of all extracts of court judgments or verdicts for forestry offenses shall be sent to the competent Forestry Administration.

#### Article 104. -

After a court judgment or verdict becomes final, the confiscated evidence shall be managed in accordance with the procedures by the Forestry Administration.

### Chapter 17
### Transitional Provisions

#### Article 105. -

All forest concessions, whether existing prior to the entry into force of this Law or granted subsequently through public bidding as provided in Article 13 of this Law, shall enter into forest concession management agreements based on a single new model.

#### Article 106. -

Wherever the Forestry Administration has evidence confirming that the possession of any forest land was obtained through illegal clearing of forest land before the entry into force of this Law, the Forestry Administration has the right to issue a notification on behalf of the Royal Government as the owner of the forest land, to evict and reclaim ownership of the forest land unconditionally.

#### Article 107. -

Upon the entry into force of this Law, all forest product and by-product processing factories and handicraft workshops established and operating under the proclamation of the Ministry of Industry, Mines and Energy shall be transferred under the management of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries.

Those forest product and by-product processing handicraft workshops shall be allowed to continue their activities until the expiration of the proclamation.

As for forest product and by-product processing factories, they must complete the procedures to request the continuation of their production and business in accordance with the procedures of this Law, within six (6) months from the date this Law enters into force.

### Chapter 18
### Final Provisions

#### Article 108. -

Any provisions contrary to this Law shall be considered null and void.

#### Article 109. -

This Law shall be declared as urgent.
