# LAW

# ON

# TAXATION

## Chapter 1
## General Provisions

#### Article 1.- Objectives

This law determines the rules, procedures, and conditions, and consolidates tax provisions for the purpose of managing tax collection for the benefit of the national budget.

#### Article 2.- Scope

This law has the scope of application on the management of collecting all types of taxes stipulated in this law in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

## Chapter 2
## Tax on Income

### Section 1
### General Provisions

#### Article 3.- Subject to Tax

Tax on income is an obligation of a taxpayer who has taxable income received during the tax year.

#### Article 4.- Scope of Tax

Tax on income applies to resident taxpayers for Cambodian source income and foreign source income, and to non-resident taxpayers for Cambodian source income.

#### Article 5.- Definitions

Key terms used in this chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Resident Taxpayer refers to:

a.- A physical person who has a residence or a principal place of abode in the Kingdom of Cambodia, or who is present in the Kingdom of Cambodia for more than 182 (one hundred eighty-two) days in any 12 (twelve) month period ending in the current tax year.

b.- A legal person or partnership which is organized or managed in the Kingdom of Cambodia, or which has its principal place of business in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2.- Non-resident Taxpayer refers to any person who is not a resident taxpayer as defined above in this Article and receives Cambodian source income.

3.- Legal Person refers to an enterprise or organization which carries on business, whether or not it has been officially recognized by the competent institutions of the Royal Government. The term Legal Person also includes state institutions, religious organizations, charitable organizations, non-profit organizations, associations, political parties, and permanent establishments of a non-resident person located in the Kingdom of Cambodia. For the sole purpose of implementing the provisions on tax on income, the term Legal Person does not include partnerships or sole proprietorships.

4.- Permanent Establishment refers to a fixed place of business in the Kingdom of Cambodia, a branch of a foreign company, or a resident agent in the Kingdom of Cambodia, through which a non-resident person carries on their business. The term Permanent Establishment also includes other networks or forms of contact through which a non-resident person performs economic activities in the Kingdom of Cambodia. Any person may be deemed a permanent establishment of a non-resident person in the Kingdom of Cambodia if that person has and regularly exercises the authority to conclude contracts on behalf of the non-resident person or plays a principal role in coordinating leading to the conclusion of contracts. A permanent establishment is deemed a resident legal person only for Cambodian source income.

5.- Partnership refers to an enterprise established by a contract between two or more persons who combine their assets, knowledge, or activities to carry on business or trade for the purpose of making profit. In this definition, Partnership includes general partnerships and limited partnerships. Conditions to obtain legal person status or personality of a partnership as stipulated in the Law on Commercial Enterprises and other laws in force shall not be applied for the purpose of collecting tax on income. Partnership does not include capital companies, permanent establishments, or sole proprietorships.

6.- Sole Proprietorship refers to an enterprise established by only one physical person where the assets of the owner are the capital of the sole proprietorship. In this definition, husband, wife, and dependent children are deemed as one physical person.

7.- Business refers to economic activities as stipulated in paragraph 9 of Article 190 of this law of a person who produces and sells goods, supplies services, constructs, leases or sells property, or other activities, including activities of institutions or organizations as stipulated in paragraph 3 of this Article.

8.- Dividend refers to a distribution in cash or property that a legal person pays to its partners or shareholders according to their participation in the capital of the legal person, except for the return of capital from a total liquidation of the company. Any portion deemed as a dividend or not must be based on the above conditions regardless of whether the legal person has or does not have accumulated income in the tax year or previous years.

9.- Partner refers to a person who has an interest in the capital of a legal person. For the purpose of tax on income, any legal person, even if not a capital company, is deemed as a capital company, and a person who has an interest in the capital of the legal person or a person who may receive income in the capacity of a participant in that legal person is deemed as a partner of that legal person.

10.- Related Person refers to:

a.- Any member of the taxpayer's family.

b.- An enterprise which directly or indirectly controls the taxpayer, or an enterprise which is directly or indirectly controlled by the taxpayer, or an enterprise and the taxpayer are under common control, or the relationship between a permanent establishment and a non-resident taxpayer. "Control" means ownership of 20 (twenty) percent or more of the value or voting rights of the interest in the capital of the enterprise. To determine the size of the control of a taxpayer who is a physical person, all ownership interests in the capital of that person and the direct or indirect ownership of the spouse of the taxpayer shall be included. Notwithstanding the above conditions, the tax administration may judge the direct or indirect control based on the actual facts of each case to determine whether or not control exists.

11.- Qualified Investment Project refers to an investment project that receives a registration certificate from the Council for the Development of Cambodia or the Provincial/Municipal Investment Sub-Committee.

12.- Business Group refers to joint business activities between two or more resident taxpayers, or a resident taxpayer and a non-resident taxpayer, or two or more non-resident taxpayers, without forming a separate legal person.

13.- Bank refers to any entity that normally carries out any of the following operations:

a.- Providing all types of credit to the public for a fee, including financial lease credit.

b.- Receiving non-earmarked deposits from the public.

c.- Handling and providing customers with payment means in scripts or currency.

14.- Gross Income refers to income derived from all types of business operations, including ancillary income and other income before any expense deductions.

15.- Net Income refers to the result of subtracting allowed deductions from gross income as authorized by this law.

#### Article 6.- Tax Regime and Accounting Rules

The tax regime and accounting rules are determined as follows:

1.- The tax regime of the Kingdom of Cambodia is a self-assessment regime that requires taxpayers to calculate tax and file tax returns by themselves. Self-assessment taxpayers are divided into 3 (three) types: small taxpayers, medium taxpayers, and large taxpayers.

2.- Accounting rules for taxpayers as stipulated in paragraph 1 of this Article are determined as follows:

a.- Small taxpayers shall use simple accounting rules.

b.- Medium and large taxpayers shall use accounting rules according to the Cambodian International Financial Reporting Standards. Income shall be included in the year it accrues, whether or not the income has been paid. Any deduction of an expense is allowed only if the event specifying the taxpayer's liability has occurred, the result of economic activity related to the expense has occurred, and the amount of the taxpayer's liability can be clearly shown.

3.- Tax regime and accounting rules shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Section 2
### Taxable Income and Tax Year

#### Article 7.- Taxable Income

Taxable Income is determined as follows:

1.- Taxable income is the net income derived from all results of all types of business operations, including capital gains realized during business operations or at the time of business cessation, interest, rental fees, royalties, and other compensations, as well as income from financial assets or investment assets, including real estate.

2.- For a legal person, taxable income is the result derived from adjusting the accounting result during the tax year in accordance with the provisions stipulated in the Articles of this Chapter.

3.- For a physical person, taxable income is the result derived from taking the total amount of income during the tax year minus expenses and various allowances determined by sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

4.- Rules and procedures for determining taxable income shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 8.- Tax Year

The tax year is determined as follows:

1.- For legal persons:

a.- Tax on income is calculated based on the accounting result achieved during each calendar year.

b.- For a new enterprise starting business at any time during the calendar year, the accounting result shall be calculated from the day of starting business until December 31 of that calendar year.

c.- If there are multiple accounting results in any calendar year, all accounting results shall be combined as the basis for calculating tax on income.

d.- An enterprise controlled by a non-resident legal person may request to apply a tax year other than the calendar year.

e.- For an enterprise that ceases business activities, the final accounting result shall be calculated:

- from January 1 until the day of final cessation of activities for an enterprise using the calendar year as the tax year.

- from the beginning of the current tax year until the day of final cessation of activities for an enterprise that requested to apply a tax year other than the calendar year.

2.- For a physical person, tax on income is calculated from the total income achieved during each calendar year.

#### Article 9.- Exempt Income

Exempt income is determined as follows:

1.- Unless otherwise provided, tax on income shall not apply to:

a.- Income of the Royal Government or income of Royal Government institutions.

b.- Income of:

- Any organization organized and operated exclusively for religious, charitable, scientific, literary, or educational purposes, and no part of whose assets or income is used for private benefit.

- Any association in the case where the income of that association is not used for the private benefit of partners or physical persons.

c.- Dividends received from a resident enterprise in the case where tax has already been paid according to Article 20, Article 23, and Article 26 of this law.

2.- In the case where income as stipulated in paragraph (b) of point 1 of this Article is used for the private benefit of partners or physical persons, such income shall be determined as taxable income again.

3.- In the case where an organization or association as stipulated in paragraph (b) of point 1 of this Article has commercial, industrial, or other business activities that are not related to its objectives or basic functions of tax exemption, the income derived from such unrelated activities shall be subject to tax according to Article 20 of this law.

4.- Rules and procedures for requesting tax exemption and the loss of tax-exempt status shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 10.- Determination of Income of Partnerships and Business Groups

1.- For partnerships and business groups, in determining their income for any tax year, each member shall account separately for their own share in the items of income, gain, loss, deduction, credit, and charitable contributions for that year. For this purpose, each item shall retain its original character and shall be deemed to have been distributed during the tax year, whether or not an actual distribution was made. Losses to be carried forward shall be determined after the items have been distributed.

2.- Rules and procedures for determining the income of each member of a partnership and business group shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Section 3
### Deductions

#### Article 11.- Allowed Deductions

Allowed deductions are determined as follows:

1.- Unless otherwise provided in Articles 12 to 18 of this law, allowed deductions include business expenses that the taxpayer has paid or incurred during the tax year for carrying on business.

2.- Rent, interest, insurance, payments, or various fees paid to a person responsible for or a director of an enterprise, a partner or a member of a partnership, a family member of the taxpayer, or any other related person, in the case where there is evidence confirming that the payment was performed for actual services and is at a reasonable level.

3.- Money paid for making some other assets, permanent improvements, or various improvements therein, including interest and taxes paid during the construction or acquisition period. This amount shall be recorded in the relevant asset account and deducted according to depreciation as stipulated in other Articles of this law.

4.- Various expenses made to a resident related person who is not under the self-assessment regime shall not be allowed for deduction before actual payment.

5.- Domestic banks are allowed to choose allowance rules for bad debts for self-assessment to determine taxable income.

6.- Deduction of expenses of a Qualified Investment Project received according to the Law on Investment of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

7.- Rules and procedures for various deductions shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 12.- Interest Expenses

1.- Deduction of interest expenses that the taxpayer has paid or incurred during the tax year for carrying on business is allowed, but not exceeding the sum of the taxpayer's interest income and 50 (fifty) percent of the taxpayer's net income other than interest in the tax year.

2.- Net income other than interest is gross income other than interest income minus all allowed deductions except for interest expenses.

3.- Rules and procedures for interest expense carry-forward shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 13.- Depreciation of Tangible Assets

1.- Conditions for normal depreciation of tangible assets are as follows:

a.- In determining taxable income, deductions for depreciation are granted to the owner of the tangible asset or the lessee in the case where the lessee is responsible for the risks of loss or damage to the asset, in accordance with the conditions determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

b.- Depreciable tangible assets are tangible assets used in business that lose value through use or obsolescence. Land is not a depreciable asset.

c.- All tangible assets are divided into classes and depreciation methods as follows:

Class 1: Structures, including buildings and their components, roads, railways, transport vessels, shall be depreciated using the straight-line method.

Class 2: Computers, electronic information systems, electronic equipment, and data storage equipment shall be depreciated using the declining balance method.

Class 3: Automobiles, trucks, furniture, and office equipment shall be depreciated using the declining balance method.

Class 4: Other tangible assets shall be depreciated using the declining balance method.

d.- Deductions for depreciation in this point shall be calculated based on the asset's value after deducting the special depreciation amount in paragraph 2 of this Article.

2.- Conditions for special depreciation of tangible assets are as follows:

a.- Special depreciation is an amount that can be deducted in the first year of acquisition or later in the first year of putting into use new tangible assets by a Qualified Investment Project as stipulated in point (b) of this paragraph. This deductible amount is equal to 40 (forty) percent of the capital value of new tangible assets or used tangible assets put into use in production and processing which will be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance. Special depreciation shall reduce the capital value of the tangible asset available for normal depreciation according to paragraph 1 of this Article.

b.- Special depreciation of tangible assets will be taken to deduct in determining taxable income of a Qualified Investment Project for the tax year if the investor does not choose to use the right to receive a tax exemption period as stipulated in paragraph 3 of Article 20 of this law.

c.- Tangible assets that received special depreciation deduction must be put into use for at least 4 (four) tax years after the tax year in which the special depreciation was deducted. If the tangible asset ceases to be used before this period, the Qualified Investment Project must record back into taxable income an amount equal to the special depreciation deducted minus an amount equal to 2 (two) percent of the special depreciation deducted for each month or portion of a month that the asset was put into use. This amount shall not affect the accounting calculation of the tangible asset for determining taxable capital gains according to the Articles of this law.

3.- Rules and procedures for depreciation of tangible assets shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 14.- Depreciation of Intangible Assets

1.- A taxpayer shall depreciate intangible assets, including patents, copyrights and related rights, industrial designs, utility model certificates, trademarks or trade names, business rights with a limited lifespan, and other long-term assets, according to the lifespan of each asset. If the lifespan of the intangible asset cannot be determined, the straight-line method over 10 (ten) years shall be applied.

2.- Rules and procedures for depreciation of intangible assets shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 15.- Agricultural and Natural Resource Depreciation

Agricultural and natural resource depreciation are determined as follows:

1.- Long-term agriculture (biological assets) shall use the straight-line depreciation method:

a.- Agricultural crops: expenses for development, direct costs related to planting, including interest expenses related to development and planting, and infrastructure costs serving the planting, shall be accumulated in the biological asset account.

b.- Animal husbandry: expenses for development, direct costs related to animal husbandry, including interest expenses related to development and animal husbandry, and infrastructure costs serving the animal husbandry, shall be accumulated in the biological asset account.

2.- Natural resource depreciation is determined as follows:

a.- Expenses for exploration and development, including interest related to those expenses, shall be accumulated in the asset account.

b.- Depreciation amount of natural resources other than land and petroleum allowed for deduction in any tax year shall be calculated by taking the balance of the natural resource account multiplied by the ratio of the quantity produced from the natural resource in the year and the estimated total quantity that can be extracted from that resource. Procedures for determining the total estimated quantity shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 16.- Charitable Contributions

1.- Deduction of charitable contributions donated to organizations as stipulated in Article 9 of this law is allowed, but not exceeding 5 (five) percent of taxable income before the deduction of such charitable contributions.

2.- Criteria for charitable contributions shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 17.- Loss Carry-Forward

In the case of a loss in any tax year, that loss shall be treated as a burden for the following tax year and shall be deducted from income achieved in that following tax year. If that income is not sufficient to fully deduct the loss, the remaining portion of the loss shall continue to be carried forward to subsequent tax years up to the 5th (fifth) tax year. Whenever losses occur in more than 1 (one) year, this Article shall apply to those losses in the order in which the losses occurred first.

#### Article 18.- Allocation of Income and Deductions among Related Persons

1.- In the case of two or more enterprises, whether or not organized or established in or outside the Kingdom of Cambodia, which are under common ownership, the tax administration may, if necessary, allocate gross income, deductions, or other benefits between the related persons to prevent tax avoidance or tax evasion or to clearly reflect the income between such related persons.

2.- For the purpose of this Article:

a.- Two or more enterprises are under common ownership if any person owns 20 (twenty) percent or more of the value or interest in the capital of each enterprise.

b.- Allocation of income to a permanent establishment of a non-resident taxpayer in the Kingdom of Cambodia shall also include income from the supply of goods or services similar or identical to the business activities of the permanent establishment that the non-resident taxpayer supplied in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

3.- Rules and procedures for the allocation of income and deductions among taxpayers shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 19.- Non-Allowed Deductions

For the purpose of the provisions on tax on income, non-allowed deductions are as follows:

1.- Expenses on activities generally considered as entertainment, amusement, recreation, or use of any means related to these activities.

2.- Personal or living expenses, except for fringe benefits in cash or in-kind for which tax has been withheld according to the provisions on tax on salary.

3.- Tax determined by the provisions on tax on income and withholding tax determined by the provisions on tax on salary.

4.- Losses on the sale or exchange of property, whether direct or indirect, between related persons.

5.- Various expenses except for expenses that occurred and for which the taxpayer can clearly prove the amount and business purpose.

### Section 4
### Tax Rates and Tax to be Paid

#### Article 20.- Determination of Tax to be Paid

1.- Annual tax rates on income are determined as follows:

a.- 20 (twenty) percent for taxable income achieved by a legal person.

b.- 30 (thirty) percent for taxable income achieved under a petroleum agreement or achieved from business operations on natural resources, including timber, minerals, gold, or various precious stones.

c.- 0 (zero) percent for taxable income of a Qualified Investment Project during the tax exemption period in accordance with the Law on Investment of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

4.- For taxable income achieved by a physical person, a sole proprietorship, and the share allocated to each member of a partnership not deemed a legal person, tax on income shall be paid according to progressive rates by layer which shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 21.- Tax on Income for Insurance Enterprises

Tax on income for insurance enterprises is determined as follows:

1.- For insurance or reinsurance activities on property or various risks in the Kingdom of Cambodia, tax on income is determined to be equal to 5 (five) percent of gross insurance premiums received during the tax year.

2.- For life insurance or reinsurance activities that have a saving character and other activities other than insurance or reinsurance on property or various risks as stipulated in paragraph 1 of this Article, tax on income is determined according to Article 20 of this law.

3.- Rules and procedures for payment of tax on income for insurance enterprises shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 22.- Tax on Income for Foreign Company Branches

Tax on income for foreign company branches is determined as follows:

1.- Income achieved from Cambodian sources shall be subject to tax on income according to Article 20 of this law.

2.- If in the current tax year, any branch of a foreign company transfers Cambodian source income abroad, that income:

a.- Shall not be subject to withholding tax on dividend distribution according to Article 23 of this law if the income transferred is income after the payment of tax on income according to point 1 or point 2 of Article 20 of this law.

b.- Shall be subject to withholding tax on dividend distribution according to Article 23 of this law if the income transferred is income that has not yet been subject to tax on income according to point 1 or point 2 of Article 20 of this law, and shall be subject to withholding tax according to Article 26 of this law on income as stipulated in point 10 of Article 33 of this law.

3.- Rules and procedures for collecting tax on income for foreign company branches shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 23.- Prepayment on Dividend Distribution

Prepayment on dividend distribution is determined as follows:

1.- Unless a Qualified Investment Project is during the tax exemption period, if any enterprise distributes dividends from income before the payment of annual tax on income to resident or foreign partners, that enterprise shall pay an amount of tax equal to the product of the dividend amount grossed up by the tax rate on income multiplied by the annual tax rate on income as stipulated in Article 20 of this law.

2.- Tax paid according to paragraph 1 of this Article is allowed as a tax credit to deduct from the annual tax on income of the tax year in which the distribution was made. If the tax credit exceeds the annual tax on income, the excess amount is allowed to be carried forward to deduct from the tax on income of subsequent tax years.

3.- Dividends for which tax has been paid according to paragraph 1 of this Article, when distributed further, shall not be subject to prepayment on dividend distribution as stipulated in this Article again.

4.- Prepayment on dividend distribution as stipulated in this Article does not apply to income from insurance or reinsurance premiums on property or various risks in the Kingdom of Cambodia as stipulated in paragraph 1 of Article 21 of this law.

5.- Rules and procedures for collecting prepayment on dividend distribution shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Section 5
### Minimum Tax

#### Article 24.- Minimum Tax

1.- Minimum tax is determined at the rate of 1 (one) percent of annual turnover inclusive of various taxes except for Value Added Tax, and shall be paid at the time of paying the annual tax on income. Minimum tax can be reduced by the annual tax on income actually paid according to the rules stipulated in Articles 37, 38, and 39 of this law. Minimum tax is determined for enterprises that do not maintain proper accounting records. A Qualified Investment Project shall receive an exemption from minimum tax provided that it has an independent audit report.

2.- Rules and procedures for criteria of improper maintenance of accounting records and the payment of minimum tax shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Section 6
### Withholding Tax and Advance Payment of Tax on Income

#### Article 25.- General Withholding Tax

General withholding tax is determined as follows:

1.- A resident taxpayer carrying on business who makes payments in cash or in-kind to a resident person shall withhold and pay tax according to the following rates on the amount paid before withholding:

a.- Rate of 15 (fifteen) percent for:

- Income that a person not under the self-assessment regime receives from performing various services, including management or consultancy or similar services.

- Royalties from intangible assets and interest in mineral resources, and interest that a resident taxpayer carrying on business other than a domestic bank pays to a resident person.

b.- Rate of 10 (ten) percent for income from the lease of movable or immovable property.

c.- Rate of 6 (six) percent for interest that a domestic bank pays to a resident person who has a fixed-term deposit account.

d.- Rate of 4 (four) percent for interest that a domestic bank pays to a resident person who has a non-fixed term deposit account.

2.- Withholding in this Article shall not apply to interest paid to a domestic bank and payments for exempt income as stipulated in Article 9 of this law.

#### Article 26.- Withholding Tax on Payments to Non-Resident Taxpayers

A resident taxpayer carrying on business including a permanent establishment of a non-resident person who makes payments of Cambodian source income as stipulated in Article 33 of this law to a non-resident taxpayer shall withhold and pay tax at the rate of 14 (fourteen) percent of the amount to be paid. This withholding tax shall not apply to reinsurance premiums on property or various risks in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 27.- Withholding Tax as Final Tax

Withholding tax on payments to a resident physical person not under the self-assessment regime according to Article 25 of this law and withholding tax on payments to a non-resident taxpayer according to Article 26 of this law shall be determined as the final tax for the recipient of the payment or distribution as specified in the Articles of this law.

#### Article 28.- Advance Payment of Tax on Income

An enterprise subject to tax on income under the self-assessment regime, including a Qualified Investment Project after the tax exemption period, has the obligation to pay a monthly advance payment of tax on income at the rate of 1 (one) percent of turnover inclusive of all taxes except for Value Added Tax achieved in previous months. The advance payment of tax on income shall be deducted from the tax on income at the time of annual payment.

### Section 7
### Obligations of Taxpayers

#### Article 29.- Obligations of Medium and Large Taxpayers

Medium and large taxpayers have the following obligations:

1.- Implement accounting record obligations as stipulated in paragraph 2 of Article 6 of this law and file the annual tax return on income to the tax administration according to the form determined by the tax administration within 3 (three) months after the end of each tax year, attached with the balance sheet, profit and loss account, and additional information.

2.- For enterprises that have a loss, they must also file a tax return in the same form and within the same period.

#### Article 30.- Obligations of Small Taxpayers

Small taxpayers have the following obligations:

1.- File the annual tax return and pay tax on income to the tax administration every year within 3 (three) months after the end of each calendar year according to the form determined by the tax administration.

2.- In the case where they do not maintain records of income and expenses according to the determination of the tax administration, the amount of taxable income shall be determined by the tax administration after inspecting and calculating according to the profit rate based on the type of business, as determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 31.- Obligations of Withholding Agents

1.- A person or a recipient of payment who withholds tax according to Article 25 and Article 26 of this law shall file a tax return and pay the withheld tax to the tax administration according to the form determined by the tax administration at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the payment was made.

2.- An enterprise shall file a tax return for prepayment on dividend distribution as stipulated in Article 23 of this law and pay the tax to the tax administration according to the form determined by the tax administration at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the distribution was made.

#### Article 32.- Obligation to Pay Advance Payment of Tax on Income

A taxpayer has the obligation to file a tax return and pay the advance payment of tax on income to the tax administration every month according to the form determined by the tax administration at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the business operations occurred.

### Section 8
### Source of Income

#### Article 33.- Cambodian Source Income

Without limiting only to the following income, Cambodian source income includes:

1.- Interest paid by a resident enterprise, a resident partnership, or Royal Government institutions of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2.- Dividends distributed by a resident enterprise.

3.- Income from services performed in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

4.- Income from management and technical services paid by a resident person.

5.- Income derived from movable or immovable property in the case where that property is located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

6.- Royalties derived from the use or the right to use intangible assets paid by a resident taxpayer or a non-resident taxpayer through a permanent establishment in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

7.- Gains from the sale of movable or immovable property, including intangible assets, located in the Kingdom of Cambodia, or gains from the transfer of any interest in movable or immovable property, including intangible assets, located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

8.- Insurance or reinsurance premiums for risks in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

9.- Gains from the sale of movable property which is part of the business assets of a permanent establishment of a non-resident taxpayer in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

10.- Income from business activities of a non-resident taxpayer through a permanent establishment in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 34.- Foreign Source Income

All income that is not Cambodian source income as stipulated in Article 33 of this law shall be determined as foreign source income.

#### Article 35.- Determination of Source of Income

In the case where there is not sufficient information to determine the source of income or if the situation is unclear and cannot clearly record income from any source, the tax administration shall determine the source of that income.

### Section 9
### Calculation of Annual Tax to be Paid

#### Article 36.- Foreign Tax Credit

1.- A resident taxpayer who has received foreign source income and paid tax according to foreign tax laws shall receive a tax credit to deduct from the tax on income to be paid in the Kingdom of Cambodia, provided that there is documentation confirming the tax payment abroad.

2.- To calculate the tax to be paid in the Kingdom of Cambodia before deducting this tax credit, the total amount of income received from Cambodian sources and foreign sources shall be used.

3.- Tax credit shall be determined separately for taxes paid by a resident in each foreign country. However, the tax credit allowed for deduction for the tax year is the lower of:

a.- The actual tax paid in any foreign country.

b.- The amount derived from taking the total tax on income from all sources for the same period calculated according to the tax rates in Article 20 of this law multiplied by the ratio of the income received in that foreign country and the total income from all sources.

4.- Foreign tax credit can be granted only if the resident taxpayer has completed the forms and provided various documents determined by the tax administration, especially the certification of the tax payer abroad by that foreign tax administration.

5.- In the case where the tax credit exceeds the tax liability, the excess amount can be carried forward up to the 5th (fifth) year counting from the year following the year in which the tax credit occurred. If there are tax credits from multiple years, those tax credits must be used in the order in which the years occurred first.

#### Article 37.- Calculation of Tax Liability on Income

Calculation of tax liability on income is determined as follows:

1.- Calculate total tax liability according to Article 20 of this law.

2.- Subtract tax credit paid according to Article 23 and foreign tax credit according to Article 36 of this law, but not exceeding the tax liability in point 1 of this Article.

#### Article 38.- Calculation of Tax to be Paid and Tax Credits for the Tax Year

Calculation of tax for the tax year is determined as follows:

1.- In the case where the result of the calculation according to Article 37 of this law is greater than the sum of tax withheld on behalf of the taxpayer by withholding agents according to Article 25 of this law and the advance payment of tax on income that the taxpayer paid annually according to Article 28 of this law, the taxpayer must pay the remaining amount to the tax administration.

2.- If the result of the calculation according to Article 37 of this law is less than the sum of the tax withheld on behalf of the taxpayer by withholding agents according to Article 25 of this law and the advance payment of tax on income that the taxpayer paid for the tax year according to Article 28 of this law, after calculating the minimum tax liability, the taxpayer may request a refund of the excess or carry it forward to be used as an advance payment for the following year.

3.- Before making the tax payment according to paragraph 1 of this Article or requesting a refund or carrying forward to use as an advance payment for the following year according to paragraph 2 of this Article, the taxpayer must first determine the minimum tax liability according to the procedures stipulated in Article 39 of this law.

#### Article 39.- Calculation of Minimum Tax, Tax to be Paid, or Credits for the Tax Year

Calculation of minimum tax, tax to be paid, or credits for the tax year is determined as follows:

1.- A taxpayer shall pay minimum tax at the time of making the tax payment on income. The minimum tax to be paid can be reduced by the tax liability on income in the same tax year according to Article 20 of this law.

2.- In the case where the tax liability on income is greater than the minimum tax liability:

a.- The taxpayer shall pay the tax to be paid according to Article 37 of this law at the time of filing the annual tax return on income.

b.- If the amount of money withheld according to Article 25 and Article 28 of this law is greater than the minimum tax liability, the taxpayer may request it as a tax credit.

c.- In the case as in point 2 of this Article, the taxpayer is not subject to minimum tax.

3.- If the tax liability on income is less than the minimum tax liability:

a.- The taxpayer's tax credit according to point 2 of Article 38 of this law shall be reduced by the amount mentioned above.

b.- The tax credit reduced in implementation of paragraph (a) of this point shall be determined as the payment of minimum tax for the tax year.

## Chapter 3
## Tax on Salary

### Section 1
### General Provisions

#### Article 40.- Scope of Tax

Tax on salary is a monthly tax determined on salary received within the framework of an employment contract.

#### Article 41.- Scope of Tax

1.- A resident physical person in the Kingdom of Cambodia is subject to tax on salary for Cambodian source salary and foreign source salary.

2.- A non-resident physical person is subject to tax on salary for Cambodian source salary.

#### Article 42.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Resident, when used for an employee, taxpayer, or physical person, means having a residence or a principal place of stay in the Kingdom of Cambodia or being present in the Kingdom of Cambodia for more than 182 (one hundred and eighty-two) days in any 12 (twelve) month period ending in the current tax year.

2.- Non-Resident means any physical person who is not a resident according to paragraph 1 of this Article and receives Cambodian source salary.

3.- Unless otherwise provided, the use of the terms "employee," "taxpayer," and "physical person" refers to both residents and non-residents as stipulated in this Article.

4.- Employer includes state institutions, resident legal persons, resident partnerships, permanent establishments in the Kingdom of Cambodia, organizations, associations, political parties, or resident physical persons carrying on business.

5.- Employee refers to a physical person who receives salary from their employment activity, including those responsible for or directors of an enterprise, public officials, and citizens receiving a public mandate through election, except for members of the National Assembly and the Senate.

6.- Cambodian Source Salary refers to salary received within the framework of performing employment activities in the Kingdom of Cambodia. Separately, for salary received by a non-resident in providing technical assistance, it shall be determined as having a source in the country where the payer of the salary resides.

7.- Foreign:

a.- When used with a physical person, means non-resident.

b.- When used in determining the source of income, means outside the Kingdom of Cambodia.

8.- Salary refers to regular salary, wages, remunerations, bonuses, premiums, overtime pay, incentives, and fringe benefits paid to an employee or paid for the direct or indirect benefit of an employee for performing employment activities.

### Section 2
### Tax Exempt Salary

#### Article 43.- Salary of Diplomatic Officials and Other Foreign Officials

Tax exemption on salary of diplomatic officials and other foreign officials is determined as follows:

1.- Tax on salary is exempt for:

a.- Salary that officials and employees of a diplomatic or consular mission of any foreign government holding a diplomatic or official passport of that country received within the framework of official duties in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

b.- Salary that foreign representatives, officials, and employees of international organizations and of technical cooperation agencies of other governments received within the framework of official duties in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2.- Tax exemption in this Article shall be based on the principle of reciprocity between the respective governments.

#### Article 44.- Tax Exempt Income of Employees

Tax exempt income of employees is determined as follows:

1.- Real compensation for professional expenses spent by an employee at the order and for the benefit of the employer and which meets the following 3 (three) conditions:

- Spent for the direct and absolute benefit of the enterprise.

- Does not show excessive or extremely complicated character.

- Shown as evidence by detailed invoices and already paid, and made in the name of the recipient of compensation for the actual expense.

2.- Various bonuses in the case of termination of employment contracts to the extent specified in the Labor Law and types of bonuses that receive tax exemption shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

3.- Additional remunerations for establishing pension funds and social welfare including social character, in cases specified in the Labor Law and the Law on Social Security Regime, shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

4.- Free supply of uniforms or professional equipment.

5.- Per diem for travel mission expenses. This allowance shall not overlap with the real compensation for expenses as stipulated in this Article.

### Section 3
### Tax Base and Tax Rates

#### Article 45.- Monthly Tax Base

Unless fringe benefits which are subject to tax according to Article 48 of this law, the monthly tax base of a resident is the taxable salary by allowing deduction of:

1.- Withheld money which is an obligation in compliance with the Labor Law and the Law on Social Security Regime.

2.- Various payments which are tax exempt as stipulated in Article 44 of this law.

#### Article 46.- Monthly Taxable Salary

Monthly taxable salary is determined as follows:

1.- Monthly taxable salary of a resident employee includes:

a.- Salary received from Cambodian sources.

b.- Salary received from foreign sources.

c.- Advances, loans, or prepayments that the employer has paid to the employee. Advances, loans, or prepayments shall be added to the taxable salary of the month in which those amounts were paid and shall be reduced from the salary in the month in which the employee pays back.

2.- Based on the production of evidence of family situation, any resident employee who has dependent children and/or a spouse who acts only as a housewife at the time tax is due shall receive a reduction of the tax base according to an amount to be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

3.- For a non-resident taxpayer, taxable salary is the Cambodian source salary which is subject to tax according to all provisions of this Chapter.

#### Article 47.- Monthly Tax Rates on Salary

For a resident employee, tax to be paid is determined according to progressive rates by layer of 0 (zero) percent, 5 (five) percent, 10 (ten) percent, 15 (fifteen) percent, and 20 (twenty) percent on the monthly taxable salary and shall be withheld by the employer. The determination of monthly taxable salary layers shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 48.- Determination of Tax on Salary for Fringe Benefits

1.- For fringe benefits every month, the employer shall withhold and pay tax at the rate of 20 (twenty) percent of the total value of fringe benefits provided to all employees. The value of fringe benefits is the market value inclusive of all taxes.

2.- Rules and procedures for tax exemption on salary for fringe benefits shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 49.- Determination of Tax on Salary of Non-Resident Taxpayers

Unless fringe benefits which are subject to tax according to Article 48 of this law, for a non-resident taxpayer, tax shall be withheld by the payer at the rate of 20 (twenty) percent on every payment of taxable salary as stipulated in point 3 of Article 46 of this law. This withholding tax is the final tax on salary for the non-resident recipient of the salary.

#### Article 50.- Foreign Tax Credit

A resident taxpayer who has received foreign source salary and paid tax according to foreign tax laws shall receive a tax credit to deduct from the tax on salary to be paid in the Kingdom of Cambodia, provided that there is documentation confirming the tax payment abroad. The calculation of tax to be paid and the tax credit is determined as follows:

1.- To calculate the tax to be paid in the Kingdom of Cambodia before deducting this tax credit, the total amount of salary received from Cambodian sources and foreign sources shall be used.

2.- Tax credit shall be determined separately for taxes paid by a resident in each foreign country. However, the tax credit granted for tax on salary paid abroad is the lower of:

a.- The actual tax paid in any foreign country.

b.- The amount derived from taking the total tax on salary from all sources for the same period calculated according to the progressive rates in Article 47 of this law multiplied by the ratio of the salary received in that foreign country and the total salary from all sources.

3.- The refund of foreign tax credit can be made only if the resident taxpayer has completed the forms and provided various documents determined by the tax administration, especially the certification of the employer and the tax administration of the place of employment abroad.

### Section 4
### Obligations of Employers and Employees

#### Article 51.- Obligation to Withhold Tax

Obligation to withhold tax is determined as follows:

1.- This Chapter is the obligation of any physical person who receives salary, including foreign physical persons, unless there is a contrary provision stipulated in an international agreement.

2.- Tax on salary shall be collected through monthly withholding by the employer at the time of salary payment.

3.- If the employer is abroad, the representative appointed by the employer in the Kingdom of Cambodia is responsible for withholding tax on salary before paying the salary to the employee and shall file and pay those taxes to the tax administration.

4.- The employer or the resident representative in the Kingdom of Cambodia of a foreign employer and the employee are jointly responsible for the payment of tax on salary in the Kingdom of Cambodia, without limiting whether the salary is paid in the Kingdom of Cambodia or abroad. In the case where tax on salary is not withheld, the employer shall be responsible according to this law.

#### Article 52.- Tax Withholding, Maintenance of Records, and Reporting

An employer who pays taxable salary shall fulfill the following obligations:

1.- Withhold tax before paying the salary.

2.- Report to the tax administration and the employee about the status of the withheld tax.

3.- Maintain and keep records.

#### Article 53.- Obligation to File Returns and Pay Tax

1.- Tax on salary is due and payable at the time of salary payment.

2.- Withholding agents have the obligation to file tax returns and pay tax on salary every month at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the salary occurred.

#### Article 54.- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of tax collection on salary shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

## Chapter 4
## Value Added Tax

### Section 1
### General Provisions

#### Article 55.- Scope of Tax

Value Added Tax shall apply to the taxable supply of goods or services.

#### Article 56.- Scope of Tax

Value Added Tax applies to all taxable supplies of goods or services in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 57.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Goods refers to tangible property other than land and money.

2.- Services refers to the provision of anything of value other than goods, land, and money.

3.- Supply of Goods refers to the transfer of the right to use or the right to dispose as the owner of goods, whether with or without consideration. The supply of ancillary services related to the supply of goods shall be treated as a supply of goods.

4.- Supply of Services refers to a supply that is not a supply of goods, land, or money, made whether for consideration or without consideration. The supply of ancillary goods related to the supply of services shall be treated as a supply of services.

5.- Person refers to a person or group of persons carrying on business and any other person related to that person.

6.- Related, with respect to any person, means:

a.- Any person who owns 20 (twenty) percent or more of the value or voting rights in the capital of the person being considered.

b.- Having common control or having a common director with the person.

c.- Being a family member, husband or spouse, or a family member of the person's spouse.

d.- Having purchased 30 (thirty) percent or more of the total output of the person during 3 (three) consecutive months.

7.- Tax in this Chapter refers to Value Added Tax.

### Section 2
### General Principles

#### Article 58.- Taxable Person

Taxable Person refers to a person under the self-assessment regime who makes taxable supplies as stipulated in Article 59 of this law. For the purpose of this Article, a non-resident person who supplies digital goods or digital services into the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be treated as a taxable person.

#### Article 59.- Taxable Supply

Unless otherwise provided in this Chapter, "Taxable Supply" refers to:

1.- Supply of goods or services by a taxable person in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2.- Appropriation of goods or services for personal use by a taxable person.

3.- Provision as a gift or supply below cost of goods or services by a taxable person.

4.- Supply of digital goods or digital services into the Kingdom of Cambodia as stipulated in Article 58 of this law.

5.- Import of goods into the customs territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 60.- Taxable Value

Taxable value is determined as follows:

1.- Taxable value of a supply is the price of goods or services that the seller takes from the buyer. Taxable value includes transport costs and other charges to be paid to the seller for the supply, including all types of taxes, except for Value Added Tax.

2.- In the case where payment for a taxable supply is any consideration other than money for the direct or indirect benefit of the seller, that consideration shall be included in the taxable value at market price.

3.- Taxable value of imported goods is the customs value including customs duties and various taxes at the time of import.

4.- If the taxable value of the supplied goods or services does not represent the true price, the tax administration may determine a value for the goods or services, and that value shall be treated as the correct value until there is other evidence that the tax administration agrees with.

5.- Taxable value of used goods that a taxable person purchased from a user for regular sale or for sale on behalf of the user is the amount equal to the difference between the selling price and the purchase price, or the commission received from the sale of those goods.

#### Article 61.- Time of Supply

Time of supply is determined as follows:

1.- Value Added Tax is due and payable at the time of supply.

2.- Time of supply of goods or services is the time when the seller must issue an invoice or the time when the seller has issued an invoice if that invoice was issued before the time when the seller must issue the invoice.

3.- Value Added Tax invoices must be issued at the latest 7 (seven) days after the delivery of goods or completion of services, or after payment in the case where payment is made before delivery of goods or completion of services. If the delivery of goods does not have an invoice attached, a shipment document correctly recorded in the delivery journal must be attached.

4.- For the supply of goods or services made continuously or having multiple payments, the time of supply shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

5.- For the import of goods, the time of supply is the time when the importer files the declaration to the customs administration in accordance with the law and regulations in force.

#### Article 62.- Place of Supply

Place of supply is determined as follows:

1.- Supply of goods is treated as being made in the Kingdom of Cambodia if the goods are delivered or if there is a transfer of the right to use or right to dispose in the Kingdom of Cambodia. In the case where the supply includes transport, the supply is treated as being made in the Kingdom of Cambodia if the goods are in the Kingdom of Cambodia at the time the transport started.

2.- Supply of services is treated as being made in the Kingdom of Cambodia if the services are performed in the Kingdom of Cambodia, except for:

a.- Supply of services related to immovable property shall be determined as being made at the place where the immovable property is located.

b.- Supply of services related to transport shall be determined as being made at the place where the transport takes place.

3.- Goods are treated as being imported into the Kingdom of Cambodia if the goods are imported into the customs territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 63.- Non-Taxable Supply

Non-taxable supply includes the following:

1.- Educational services.

2.- Public postal services.

3.- Services of hospitals and clinics, medical and dental services, and the sale of ancillary medical and dental goods related to performing these services.

4.- Passenger transport services by public transport systems which are wholly state-owned.

5.- Insurance services.

6.- Supply of electricity and clean water.

7.- Unprocessed agricultural products produced locally.

8.- Solid and liquid waste collection services.

9.- Basic financial services to be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

10.- Import of personal belongings exempt from customs duties within a value limit to be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

11.- Non-profit activities for public benefit recognized by the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 64.- Non-Taxable Supply for Diplomatic Missions and International Organizations

Non-taxable supply for diplomatic missions and international organizations is determined as follows:

1.- Import or local purchase of goods or services by foreign diplomatic and consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments for use in their official functions shall be determined as non-taxable supply. Non-taxable supply is granted in the case where there is a certification from the head of the mission to the tax administration that those goods were imported or purchased only for the purpose of the above use.

2.- Import or local purchase of goods or services for the personal use of the official staff of foreign diplomatic and consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments as stipulated in point 1 of this Article shall be determined as non-taxable supply only for items listed in the list of items determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

3.- Non-taxable supply in this Article shall be based on the principle of reciprocity between the respective governments.

### Section 3
### Tax Rates and Tax Determination

#### Article 65.- Tax Rates

Tax rates are determined as follows:

1.- Value Added Tax shall be determined at the rate of 10 (ten) percent on the taxable value of every taxable supply in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2.- Value Added Tax shall be determined at the rate of 0 (zero) percent on the taxable value of every taxable supply for goods exported from the Kingdom of Cambodia or for taxable services performed outside the Kingdom of Cambodia as stipulated in Article 62 of this law.

3.- The tax administration may use various documents to certify that an actual export has occurred, including, among others, the export declaration of the customs administration, the import document of the importing country, the used letter of credit, and the payment received by a local bank.

#### Article 66.- Determination of Tax Amount

The tax amount is determined as follows:

1.- Tax determined in Article 65 of this law shall be treated as a liability to the state at the time of supply.

2.- The tax amount to be paid to the state is equal to the total output tax according to the rates in Article 65 of this law minus the total allowed input tax credit in the same month.

#### Article 67.- Immovable Property Ceased to be Used in Business

In the case where any business asset that has received tax credit as stipulated in Article 70 of this law ceases to be used in the business of the taxable person, that asset shall be treated as being sold and subject to tax at market price at the time use ceased.

#### Article 68.- Input Tax Credit and Non-Taxable Supplies

Input tax credit and non-taxable supplies are determined as follows:

1.- Tax that a taxable person has paid on goods or services for use in the business supplied by another taxable person, or tax paid by a taxable person as an importer on imported goods or services for use in their business, shall become input tax credit to be deducted from the output tax. "Input" means goods or services purchased, and "Output" means goods or services sold.

2.- In the case where purchased goods or services are used partly for taxable supplies and partly for non-taxable supplies, input tax credit is allowed only for the portion used for taxable supplies.

3.- For non-taxable supplies of goods or services to foreign diplomatic and consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments as stipulated in Article 64 of this law, the supplier is allowed input tax credit regardless of the conditions stipulated in point 2 of this Article.

#### Article 69.- Necessary Documents for Requesting Input Tax Credit

Requesting input tax credit must be accompanied by:

1.- Value Added Tax invoice made in accordance with Article 78 of this law.

2.- Import customs declaration made in accordance with the Law on Customs and tax payment receipts or other payment receipts of the customs administration.

#### Article 70.- Input Tax Not Allowed to be Used as Credit

Input tax not allowed to be used as credit includes tax that a taxable person has paid on expenses such as entertainment, amusement or recreation, purchase of automobiles, or purchase of certain petroleum products.

### Section 4
### Tax Payment

#### Article 71.- Obligation to File Returns and Pay Tax

A taxable person has the obligation to file tax returns and pay tax every month at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the supply occurred.

#### Article 72.- Treatment of Excess Credit Amount

If input tax that a taxable person has paid according to Article 65 of this law is greater than the output tax collected by that person for any month:

1.- The excess amount shall be used as tax credit against tax arrears from past months.

2.- Any remaining excess shall be treated as input tax credit according to Article 68 of this law for the following month.

#### Article 73.- Refund of Tax to Exporters

The tax administration may refund:

1.- Monthly excess input tax credit upon request of a taxable person with primary export activity, if that person has provided clear evidence of export and has correctly complied with the obligations of record keeping and other documents.

2.- Tax that foreign tourists have paid on the purchase of goods from a taxable person for use outside the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 74.- Refund of Tax in Case of Excess Tax Credit for 3 (three) Months or More

If a taxable person has excess input tax credit for 3 (three) consecutive months or more, that person may file a request for a tax refund at the end of the 3rd (third) month or any following month. To receive a tax refund for any month, the request must be submitted within 20 (twenty) days after the end of those months.

#### Article 75.- Application of Value Added Tax to Qualified Investment Projects

The supply of local production inputs for the implementation of Qualified Investment Projects as stipulated in the relevant provisions of the Law on Investment of the Kingdom of Cambodia shall be subject to Value Added Tax at the rate of 0 (zero) percent.

#### Article 76.- Responsibility for Collection and Payment of Tax

Responsibility for collection and payment of tax is determined as follows:

1.- A taxable person or importer has the obligation to pay tax according to Article 65 of this law on every taxable supply performed by the taxable person or importer.

2.- Special conditions specifying the responsibility of the buyer for the tax in cases where the seller does not have a permanent establishment in the Kingdom of Cambodia, or in cases where there are other obstacles to collecting the tax from the supplier, shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

3.- Any person who makes supplies of goods or services on behalf of the owner and not in the capacity of an employee of any person and who has control over the supply shall be treated as a taxable person for that supply.

### Section 5
### Administrative Provisions

#### Article 77.- Registration

Registration is determined as follows:

1.- A taxable person as stipulated in Article 58 of this law shall register for Value Added Tax within 15 (fifteen) days after the date that person becomes a taxable person.

2.- For a person who has the obligation to register and failed to register, the tax administration may register that person starting from the time that person was required to register. A person registered in this manner shall be responsible for all taxes in Article 65 of this law starting from the date that person was required to register.

3.- For a group of related persons of two or more persons, in which one or more persons is not a taxable person, the tax administration may treat any taxable person as being registered for the whole or part of the related economic activities. If no related person is a taxable person, the tax administration may register one or more persons in the group for the whole or part of the related economic activities.

4.- For the purpose of registration and with the approval of the tax administration, for a group of taxable persons who are related as stipulated in Article 57 of this law, the activities of various members in the group may be treated as the activity of only one nominated member. In this case, each member in the group shall be jointly and severally responsible for compliance with the provisions of this Chapter.

#### Article 78.- Value Added Tax Invoices

Value Added Tax invoices are determined as follows:

1.- A taxable person who makes a supply must issue a tax invoice with the title "Tax Invoice" and having a correct serial number to a buyer who is a taxpayer under the self-assessment regime, and must issue a regular invoice to a buyer who is not under the self-assessment regime.

2.- Persons cannot issue invoices or other documents specifying any amount as tax on the supply of goods or services unless that person is a registered taxable person in accordance with Article 77 of this law and the supplied goods or services are taxable goods or services.

3.- Besides other penalties, if any invoice is found that it is not a Value Added Tax invoice and that invoice shows that there is a tax amount to be paid, the person issuing the invoice must pay the tax amount recorded on that invoice to the tax administration within 7 (seven) days after the date of issuance of the invoice, whether the tax amount is actually payable or not.

4.- In the case of retail sales where most sales are not made to taxable persons, the invoice required by paragraph 1 of this Article shall be treated as being sufficiently fulfilled if the seller provides a detailed payment receipt recorded in the cash register or other documents.

5.- For imports, a correctly completed customs declaration with certification of tax payment shall be used as a verification document to determine the eligibility for tax credit.

#### Article 79.- Failure to Issue Value Added Tax Invoices

Failure to issue tax invoices shall be subject to the following penalties:

1.- Besides other penalties, if the tax administration finds for the second time that any business establishment of a taxable person has not issued an invoice as required, the tax administration may close that business establishment for a limited period not exceeding 7 (seven) days.

2.- If any business establishment that was previously closed as stipulated in point 1 of this Article continues to commit such an offense again, that business establishment may be closed again for a limited period not exceeding 7 (seven) days.

#### Article 80.- Journals, Records, and Information

Journals, records, and information for Value Added Tax are determined as follows:

1.- A taxable person must use a cash record system determined by the tax administration and must keep copies of all invoices issued and all invoices received.

2.- A taxable person must record and keep correctly journals of all business transactions performed, and must have accounts tracking the tax amount collected on sales and the tax amount paid on various purchases, including various requirements on value or tax amount according to the forms determined by the tax administration.

3.- Tasks as stipulated in point 2 of this Article shall be done daily and shall be summed up and balanced at the end of each month. A taxable person shall make a monthly Value Added Tax return according to the forms determined by the tax administration.

4.- Invoices, records, and other documents related to this tax must be kept in order, according to the forms and places determined by the tax administration for a period of 10 (ten) years after the completion of the latest business transaction related to these documents.

5.- Cash record system, all documents and records to be kept according to this Article, including other documents and records related to the person's business, shall be provided to the tax administration for inspection upon request.

#### Article 81.- Special Rules for Imports

Various provisions of this Chapter related to imports shall be managed by the customs administration in accordance with the Law on Customs.

#### Article 82.- Cessation of Business and Transfer of Business

Rules for cessation of business and transfer of business are determined as follows:

1.- Within 15 (fifteen) days after the cessation of business, a taxable person must file a declaration to the tax administration in the form determined by the tax administration, together with detailed information about sales and purchases after the latest tax return and detailed information about all goods in stock that have not paid tax or have already received tax credit, and pay all due taxes.

2.- The transfer of an ongoing business from one person to another person is not subject to Value Added Tax.

3.- Rules and procedures for cessation and transfer of business shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 83.- Tax Credit for Stock of Goods for Newly Registered Persons

Tax credit for stock of goods is determined as follows:

1.- For a person who has just registered and on the registration date has a stock of goods on which Value Added Tax has already been paid, that person may file a request to the tax administration to allow tax credit for the tax paid on that stock after the tax administration has verified that the invoices or copies of the customs declarations of those goods are correct.

2.- If the correctness of those documents is agreed upon, the tax administration may allow tax credit for supplies made during the period of 60 (sixty) days before the registration date. This tax credit can be used in tax returns for this tax under conditions determined by the tax administration.

#### Article 84.- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of Value Added Tax collection shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 5
# Special Tax

#### Article 85.- Object of Tax

Special tax shall be applied to certain goods or services which are luxury or not essential requirements for daily life or affect health, society, or the environment.

#### Article 86.- Scope of Tax

Special tax applies to the import, production, or distribution of goods or the supply of services subject to special tax for use in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 87.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Special Taxpayer refers to an importer or producer of goods or a supplier of services subject to special tax.

2.- Import refers to the activity of importing goods into the customs territory of the Kingdom of Cambodia by any person.

3.- Importer refers to any person who performs import by themselves or by other persons. For the purpose of this point, the recipient of goods, the owner, the person who will become the owner, or the person who has the right to dispose of the ownership of goods before the goods cease to be the object of customs inspection shall also be treated as an importer.

4.- Production refers to the activity of transforming raw materials, assembling, installing, packaging, or filling into semi-finished or finished products.

5.- Producer refers to any person who performs production of goods or any person who hires other persons to produce goods for them.

6.- Distributor refers to a person who makes supplies of goods imported or produced by themselves or purchased from importers or producers.

7.- Supply refers to sale or appropriation for personal use or provision as a gift or supply below cost of goods or services by a special taxpayer.

8.- Tax Stamp refers to stamps used for pasting on goods subject to special tax to certify that the tax has already been paid.

9.- Tax in this Chapter refers to Special Tax.

#### Article 88.- Goods or Services Subject to Special Tax

Without being limited to the following, goods or services subject to special tax include:

1.- Certain goods, such as:

a.- Petroleum products:
- Gasoline
- Diesel
- Jet fuel
- Lubricants.

b.- Beverages:
- Non-alcoholic beverages, such as carbonated beverages, beverages containing sugar or fruit flavors or other scents, and other non-alcoholic beverages, except for water and pure water, milk, and natural fruit juices.
- Wine
- Beer
- Other alcoholic beverages.

c.- Cigarettes, cigars, and all types of tobacco.

d.- Electronic products.

e.- Motorcycles.

f.- Automobiles.

g.- Vehicle spare parts.

h.- Plastic products.

i.- Cosmetic products.

j.- Glass products used as construction materials.

k.- Cement.

2.- Certain services, such as:

a.- Telecommunication services.

b.- Air passenger transport services.

c.- Various entertainment services.

3.- The Royal Government may issue a sub-decree to determine additional types or change types of goods or services subject to special tax upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 89.- Special Tax Rates

1.- Special tax rates shall be determined as a percentage rate on the value of goods or services as the base for calculating special tax, or as a fixed amount on weight, capacity, size, or quantity.

2.- Special tax rates shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 90.- Base for Calculating Special Tax

The base for calculating special tax shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 91.- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for special tax collection management shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 6

Lighting tax shall be applied to the supply of alcohol, beer, or tobacco products.

#### Article 93.- Scope of Tax

Lighting tax applies to the supply of alcohol, beer, or tobacco products in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 94.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Alcohol refers to all types of products containing alcohol.

2.- Beer refers to alcoholic beverages made by fermenting various ingredients, including, among others, beer or alcoholic beverages.

3.- Tobacco refers to products wholly or partly made from tobacco leaves as raw material and manufactured for smoking.

2.- Rules for supply shall be applied in accordance with the rules for supply for Value Added Tax as stipulated in points 1 to 4 of Article 61 of this law.

#### Article 98.- Obligation to File Returns and Pay Tax

A taxable person has the obligation to file tax returns and pay lighting tax every month at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the products were supplied.

#### Article 99.- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for lighting tax collection management shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 7
# Accommodation Tax

#### Article 100.- Object of Tax

Accommodation tax shall be applied to the supply of accommodation services in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 101.- Scope of Tax

Accommodation tax applies to the supply of accommodation services in hotels in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 102.- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1.- Hotel refers to any place providing accommodation services in the form of rooms, beds, and providing other facilities and services according to each type of accommodation service provider, such as hotels, hotel apartments, suite hotels, resort hotels, motels, lodges, bungalows, guesthouses, tourist camp sites, and other places providing accommodation services of a similar nature, regardless of what they are called. The word "Hotel" does not include rental houses or rented state properties for short or long term.

2.- Tax in this Chapter refers to Accommodation Tax.

#### Article 103.- Tax Rates

Accommodation tax shall be determined at the rate of 2 (two) percent.

#### Article 104.- Base for Calculating Tax

The base for calculating accommodation tax is the accommodation price, including other charges and various taxes, except for Value Added Tax and the accommodation tax itself.

#### Article 105.- Time of Supply

Rules for the supply of accommodation services shall be applied in accordance with the rules for the supply of services for Value Added Tax as stipulated in Article 61 of this law.

#### Article 106.- Obligation to File Returns and Pay Tax

A taxable person has the obligation to file tax returns and pay accommodation tax every month at the latest by the 20th day of the month following the month in which the accommodation services were supplied.

#### Article 107.- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for accommodation tax collection management shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 8
# Patent Tax

#### Article 108.- Object of Tax

Patent tax is an annual tax determined on each business activity of a taxpayer based on the type of taxpayer and the level of turnover.

#### Article 109.- Scope of Tax

Patent tax applies to all taxpayers carrying on business in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 110.- Tax Exemptions

Patent tax is exempted as follows:

1.- State institutions, foreign diplomatic and consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

2.- Activities of any organization organized and operated for purely religious, charitable, scientific, literary, or educational purposes, where no part of the assets or income is used for private benefit.

3.- All business activities with annual turnover below the turnover threshold for the self-assessment regime as stipulated in Article 6 of this law.

#### Article 111.- Tax Rates

Total tax rates for patent tax shall be determined by a sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 112.- Obligation to File Returns and Pay Tax

1.- Patent tax must be paid during the period from January 1 to March 31 every year.

2.- A taxpayer starting business during the first 6 (six) months of the year shall pay the full annual patent tax, but if starting business during the last 6 (six) months of the year, the taxpayer shall pay half of the patent tax.

#### Article 113. - Taxable Activities and Places of Business

Taxable activities and places of business shall be determined as follows:

1- A taxpayer with multiple business activities shall pay separate patent taxes according to each business activity.

2- Activities that are ancillary to a business activity shall be considered a single business activity.

3- A taxpayer with branches, warehouses, factories, or workshops for a single business in the same municipality or province shall pay only one patent tax.

4- A taxpayer with business activities in different municipalities or provinces shall pay patent tax according to the municipality or province where that business is located.

5- A taxpayer with branches, warehouses, factories, or workshops for a single business activity located in different municipalities or provinces shall pay the minimum patent tax for each type of taxpayer according to the municipality or province where that business is located.

6- In the event of a change of location to another municipality or province or a change to a new business activity, a new patent tax must be paid again.

#### Article 114. - Patent Tax Certificate

1- The tax administration shall issue a patent tax certificate to the taxpayer after the patent tax has been paid in full.

2- The taxpayer has the obligation to display a valid patent tax certificate at their place of business.

#### Article 115. - Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for patent tax collection management shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Chapter 9
### Publicity Billboard Tax

#### Article 116. - Objects of Taxation

Publicity billboard tax shall apply to all types of publicity billboards.

#### Article 117. - Scope of Taxation

Publicity billboard tax shall apply to all forms of advertising, both mobile and immobile, within the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 118. - Definitions

For the purposes of the provisions on publicity billboard tax, the term "publicity billboard" refers to:

1- Commercial posters made of synthetic material, ordinary paper, cloth, or other materials.

2- Business establishment signs used to identify names, locations, headquarters, offices, or places of business activities.

3- Text signs or picture signs for commercial purposes.

#### Article 119. - Tax Exemptions

Publicity billboard tax shall be exempted for advertisements for non-profit purposes as follows:

1- Signs of state institutions.

2- Signs of foreign embassies or consulates, national and international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments, non-governmental organizations, and associations.

3- Advertisements with social education objectives for the public interest, such as education and environmental protection, stopping domestic violence, protecting natural resources, traffic education, and health education.

#### Article 120. - Tax Rate and Tax Base

The tax rate and tax base for publicity billboard tax shall be determined by a Sub-decree at the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 121. - Obligation to File Tax Declarations and Pay Taxes

Publicity billboard tax shall be paid during the period from January 1 to March 31 of each year.

#### Article 122. - Failure to File Tax Declarations and Pay Taxes

If a taxpayer fails to pay the publicity billboard tax as stipulated in this Chapter, the competent authorities shall not issue permits related to signs or publicity billboards that have not yet paid the publicity billboard tax.

#### Article 123. - Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of publicity billboard tax collection shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Chapter 10
### Taxation on Petroleum and Mineral Resource Operations

#### Section 1
#### General Provisions

#### Article 124. - Objects of Taxation

Taxation on petroleum and mineral resource operations shall apply to the conduct of petroleum and mineral resource operations in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 125. - Scope of Taxation

Provisions on taxation of petroleum and mineral resource operations shall apply to petroleum and mineral resource operations within the territorial integrity and sovereignty of the Kingdom of Cambodia, including the seabed and subsoil of the exclusive economic zone and the continental shelf, as well as other areas where the Kingdom of Cambodia has sovereign rights.

#### Article 126. - Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter are defined as follows:

1- For the purposes of petroleum operations:

a- Petroleum: refers to crude oil, natural gas, and other hydrocarbons, whether in solid, liquid, or gaseous state, in their natural state, and all other substances that are components of those hydrocarbons.

b- Petroleum Agreement: refers to a petroleum product sharing contract or any form of contract granted under the Law on the Management of Petroleum and Petroleum Products and relevant provisions between the Royal Government of Cambodia and relevant taxpayers for the purpose of conducting petroleum operations.

c- Petroleum Operations: refers to all exploration, development, and production activities of petroleum carried out within a petroleum agreement area in accordance with the petroleum agreement.

d- Agreement Area: refers to the area that is the subject of a petroleum agreement as initially granted, including any part of that area that has been relinquished in accordance with the petroleum agreement.

e- Development Project: refers to a development plan, development work program, and various development work activities to develop and produce petroleum in the agreement area as approved by the institution.

f- Site Closure and Environmental Restoration Plan: refers to the site closure and environmental restoration plan of the taxpayer approved by the institution under the petroleum agreement in accordance with the Law on the Management of Petroleum and Petroleum Products.

g- Pre-contractual Costs: refers to costs incurred in exploration to determine initial petroleum geological potential without the right to conduct development and production.

h- Exploration Costs: refers to costs incurred for geological, lithological, geophysical, geochemical surveys, seismography, data analysis costs, pre-development engineering concept costs, exploration drilling costs, appraisal drilling costs, feasibility studies, environmental impact studies, general administrative costs, and other costs incurred in petroleum operations to determine the presence, location, quantity, and quality of petroleum resource reservoirs.

i- Development Costs: refers to costs incurred after the institution has approved the development project to construct production facilities for petroleum operations, such as site preparation, well drilling, construction of production wells, and construction of petroleum pipelines as production facilities, costs for purchasing tangible and intangible assets, as well as all activities related to the development of the petroleum agreement contractor in accordance with the conditions specified in the production permit issued by the institution.

j- Reserve for Site Closure and Environmental Restoration Costs: refers to the reserve allowed to be deducted for site closure and environmental restoration costs in accordance with the plan and conditions determined by the institution.

k- Total Costs for Site Closure and Environmental Restoration: refers to the total costs for site closure and environmental restoration in the agreement area approved by the institution according to the taxpayer's site closure and environmental restoration project in accordance with the petroleum agreement.

l- Commencement of Commercial Production: refers to the time when the taxpayer starts commercial production as determined in the petroleum agreement or as determined by the institution.

2- For the purposes of mineral resource operations:

a- Mineral Resources: refers to all substances, whether in solid, liquid, or gaseous form, that occur naturally through geological evolution or as a result of extraction from the subsoil, on the soil, in the sea, and under the seabed, including gemstones, decorative stones, non-metallic minerals, metallic minerals, mineral water, rocks, gravel, sand, and clay. The term "mineral resources" does not include petroleum as specified in point (a) of paragraph 1 of this Article.

b- Mineral Resource License: refers to a permit granted to a taxpayer by the institution to conduct mineral resource operations.

c- Mineral Resource Operations: refers to all activities authorized under a permit or mineral resource license. Mineral resource operations are divided into the pre-contractual stage, the exploration stage, and the mineral resource business stage.

d- License Area: refers to the area authorized for mineral resource operations and subject to the license, including any part of the area that has been abandoned in accordance with that mineral resource license.

e- Site Closure and Environmental Restoration Plan: refers to the taxpayer's environmental restoration plan approved by the institution under the mineral resource license. Site restoration includes filling mine pits, replanting trees, and various clean-up activities within the license area.

f- Pre-contractual Costs: refers to costs related to initial exploration on the ground using simple tools with minimal impact on the land surface to determine the presence of mineral resources.

g- Exploration Costs: refers to costs incurred for research to discover and test mineral indicators to determine potential, size, quality, and quantity, as well as economic feasibility through pre-contractual activities, geological, geophysical, and geochemical research, excavation, drilling, transportation, and analysis of soil, sediment, water, rock, and other mineral samples, as well as general administrative costs and other costs incurred in mineral resource operations to determine the presence, location, quantity, and quality of mineral resource reservoirs.

h- Development Costs: refers to costs incurred to develop mineral resource reservoirs to start commercial production, such as designing, construction, installing production pipes, constructing production wells, drilling production wells, and all activities related to the taxpayer's development in accordance with the conditions specified in the production permit issued by the institution.

i- Reserve for Site Restoration Costs: refers to the reserve allowed to be deducted in advance for site restoration costs that will be carried out in accordance with the site restoration plan.

j- Approved Total Costs for Site Restoration: refers to the total costs for site restoration in the licensed area approved by the institution according to the taxpayer's site restoration plan, in accordance with the license or mineral resource investment agreement, laws, and regulations on mineral resources.

k- Commencement of Commercial Production: refers to the time when the taxpayer starts commercial production as determined in the mineral resource license.

3- For the purposes of this Chapter:

a- Taxpayer: refers to a natural or legal person who has entered into a petroleum agreement with the Royal Government of Cambodia or who has received a mineral resource license.

b- Institution: refers to the Ministry of Mines and Energy or the Royal Government institution responsible for managing the petroleum sector or the mining sector.

#### Section 2
#### Income Tax Rate

#### Article 127. - Income Tax Rate

1- Taxpayers who conduct petroleum operations and taxpayers who conduct mineral resource operations as specified in Article 126 of this Law shall be subject to income tax at a rate of 30% (thirty percent) of the taxable income in the tax year. For the purposes of this Law, the term "taxable income" refers to the net income derived from the realization of gross income from petroleum or mineral resource operations, minus various expenses allowed by this Law.

2- In addition to the tax rate specified in paragraph 1 of this Article, taxpayers shall be subject to Excess Profit Tax at progressive rates by tier as specified in paragraph 3 of this Article based on the Excess Profit Ratio. The Excess Profit Ratio for any current tax year is the quotient of the accumulated income and the accumulated expenses of the petroleum or mineral resource operations until that current tax year.

3- The Excess Profit Tax rates are as shown in the table below:

| Tier | Excess Profit Ratio | Excess Profit Tax Rate |
| :--- | :--- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | | | up to | 1.3 | 0% (zero percent) |
| 2 | above | 1.3 | up to | 1.6 | 10% (ten percent) |
| 3 | above | 1.6 | up to | 2 | 20% (twenty percent) |
| 4 | above | 2 | and over | | 30% (thirty percent) |

4- Taxpayers shall be exempted from paying the minimum tax as specified in Article 24 of this Law.

#### Section 3
#### Deductions

#### Article 128. - Principles and Limitations of Expense Deductions

1- Taxpayers are allowed to calculate taxable income by deducting all expenses as specified in Article 129 to Article 133 of this Law at the end of the tax year when commercial production activities occur, including other relevant current expenses in each tax year.

2- Expenses related to petroleum or mineral resource operations in any agreement area or license area for any tax year are allowed to be deducted only from the total gross income from the petroleum or mineral resource operations in that agreement area or license area of that tax year.

3- If in any tax year, the total expenses related to petroleum or mineral resource operations in an agreement area or license area exceed the total gross income resulting in a loss, that loss shall be considered a charge to be deducted from the taxable income achieved in subsequent tax years. If that taxable income is not sufficient to be deducted in full, the remaining part of the loss shall be carried forward to subsequent tax years up to the 10th (tenth) tax year. In cases where there are losses for more than 1 (one) year, the carry-forward of losses shall be applied in the order of the years in which the losses occurred.

4- Losses incurred in any agreement area or license area cannot be carried forward to be deducted in another agreement area or license area.

#### Article 129. - Interest Expenses

Taxpayers are allowed to deduct interest expenses according to a debt-to-equity ratio of 3 (three) to 1 (one).

#### Article 130. - Reserve for Site Closure and Environmental Restoration Costs or Site Restoration Costs

The reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs or site restoration costs is determined as follows:

1- For petroleum operations:

a- If the taxpayer has a site closure and environmental restoration plan approved by the institution, the amount required for the taxpayer to pay into the site closure and environmental restoration fund shall be considered a reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs. This reserve is allowed as a provision and allowed to be deducted in the tax year to calculate taxable income. If the taxpayer has no site closure and environmental restoration plan approved by the institution, the reserve under this paragraph shall not be allowed.

b- The reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs shall be calculated based on the total costs approved by the institution for site closure and environmental restoration. The reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs in each year and the year in which the reserve is allowed to begin being deducted shall be determined according to the site closure and environmental restoration plan.

c- If the actual total costs of site closure and environmental restoration exceed the total amount of the reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs, that excess amount is allowed to be deducted from the annual taxable income.

d- If at the end of the site closure and environmental restoration, the actual total costs of site closure and environmental restoration are less than the total amount of the reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs, that difference shall be included in the taxable income for the tax year in which the site closure and environmental restoration is completed.

2- For mineral resource operations:

a- If the taxpayer has a mine site closure and environmental restoration plan approved by the institution, the amount required for the taxpayer to pay into the site restoration fund shall be considered a reserve for mine site closure and environmental restoration costs. This reserve is allowed as a provision and allowed to be deducted in the tax year to calculate taxable income. In the event that the taxpayer has no mine site closure and environmental restoration plan approved by the institution, the reserve under this paragraph shall not be allowed.

b- The reserve for site restoration costs shall be calculated based on the total costs approved by the institution for mine site closure and environmental restoration. The reserve for site restoration costs in each year and the year in which the reserve is allowed to begin being deducted shall be determined according to the mine site closure and site restoration plan.

c- If the actual total costs of mine site closure and environmental restoration exceed the total amount of the reserve for site restoration costs, that excess amount is allowed to be deducted from the annual taxable income.

d- If at the end of the mine site closure and environmental restoration, the actual total costs of mine site closure and environmental restoration are less than the total amount of the reserve for site restoration costs, that difference shall be included in the taxable income for the tax year in which the mine site closure and environmental restoration is completed.

3- Rules and procedures for the management of the reserve for site closure and environmental restoration costs or site restoration costs shall be determined by a joint Prakas between the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the institution.

#### Article 131. - Depreciation and Amortization Methods

Depreciation and amortization methods are determined as follows:

1- Taxpayers shall amortize pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs using the straight-line method in accordance with Article 132 of this Law. Taxpayers shall not amortize pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs in accordance with Article 15 of this Law.

2- For tangible and intangible assets other than pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs purchased or created after the commencement of commercial production in the agreement area or license area, the taxpayer shall depreciate tangible assets in accordance with points (a) to (d) of paragraph 1 of Article 13 of this Law and amortize intangible assets in accordance with Article 14 of this Law.

#### Article 132. - Straight-Line Depreciation and Amortization

Straight-line depreciation and amortization for petroleum and mineral resource operations are determined as follows:

1- For petroleum operations:

a- Taxpayers shall amortize pre-contractual costs and exploration costs using the straight-line method based on the estimated useful life of commercial production under the development project or 5 (five) years, whichever is shorter.

b- Taxpayers shall amortize development costs using the straight-line method based on the estimated useful life of commercial production of each production facility under the development project or 10 (ten) years for petroleum operations, whichever is shorter.

c- In the event that the estimated useful life of commercial production of any production facility under the development project is revised, the taxpayer shall adjust the amortization of the remaining pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs based on the revised estimated useful life, provided that it does not exceed the amortization periods specified in points (a) and (b) of this paragraph.

2- For mineral resource operations:

a- Taxpayers shall amortize pre-contractual costs and exploration costs using the straight-line method based on the estimated useful life of commercial production under the development project or 5 (five) years, whichever is shorter.

b- Taxpayers shall amortize development costs using the straight-line method based on the estimated useful life of commercial production under the development project or 7 (seven) years, whichever is shorter.

c- In the event that the estimated useful life of commercial production under the development project is revised, the taxpayer shall adjust the amortization of the remaining pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs based on the revised estimated useful life, provided that it does not exceed the amortization periods specified in points (a) and (b) of this paragraph.

d- In the event that the taxpayer has a development project with a duration of less than 1 (one) year, these development costs may be included in the amortization of pre-contractual costs and exploration costs.

#### Article 133. - Timing of Amortization and Depreciation

The timing of amortization and depreciation is determined as follows:

1- Taxpayers shall amortize pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs incurred before the commencement of the first commercial production at the end of the tax year in which the taxpayer starts the first commercial production in the agreement area or license area.

2- After the commencement of the first commercial production in the agreement area or license area, if the taxpayer conducts further pre-contractual, exploration, or construction of additional production facilities under the development project in the agreement area or license area, the taxpayer shall amortize pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs according to the following timeline:

a- At the end of each tax year for pre-contractual costs or exploration costs in the order of the years in which those costs were incurred.

b- At the end of the tax year in which each production facility is placed into service for development costs.

Amortization shall be performed separately for each production facility under the development project in accordance with Article 132 of this Law and shall be aggregated for deduction in the tax year.

3- For tangible and intangible assets other than pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs purchased or created after the commencement of commercial production in the agreement area or license area, the taxpayer shall depreciate or amortize at the end of the tax year in which those assets are placed into service.

#### Section 4
#### Transfer of Interests

#### Article 134. - Transfer of Interests and Development and Production

1- In the event of a partial or total transfer of interests which are rights or shares in a petroleum agreement or a mineral resource license by way of sale, exchange, or gift, the transferor taxpayer and the successor person shall implement as follows:

a- The transferor taxpayer shall record the income received from the transfer of interest in the petroleum agreement or mineral resource license as income from petroleum or mineral resource operations subject to income tax as specified in Chapter 2 of this Law.

b- The successor person shall:
- Continue to fulfill the tax obligations of the transferor taxpayer regarding the received interest or share.
- Continue to depreciate and amortize tangible assets, intangible assets, pre-contractual costs, exploration costs, and development costs according to the rules, procedures, and bases that the transferor taxpayer had previously applied.
- In the event that the transfer of interest in a petroleum agreement or mineral resource license is at a price higher than the accounting net book value, that difference shall be considered goodwill. The successor person shall amortize this goodwill using the straight-line method based on the remaining estimated useful life of commercial production or 10 (ten) years for petroleum operations and 7 (seven) years for mineral resource operations, whichever is shorter.
- In the event that the transfer of interest in a petroleum agreement or mineral resource license is at a price lower than the accounting net book value, that difference shall be considered income. The successor person shall record this income for subsequent years by allocating this income proportionally over the remaining estimated useful life of commercial production or 10 (ten) years for petroleum operations and 7 (seven) years for mineral resource operations, whichever is shorter.

2- For two or more taxpayers allowed to enter into a joint development and production agreement for joint petroleum operations in one or more reservoirs located in different agreement areas, the rules and procedures to be applied shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Section 5
#### Taxpayer Obligations

#### Article 135. - Taxpayer Obligations

1- A non-resident taxpayer who enters into a petroleum agreement or mineral resource license with the Royal Government of Cambodia as specified in Article 126 of this Law shall establish a domestic company for each agreement area in accordance with the laws in force of the Kingdom of Cambodia at least 15 (fifteen) days before starting economic activities.

2- Taxpayers shall file tax returns and pay monthly and annual taxes to the tax administration as determined by the law and regulations on taxation.

3- The operator who represents all taxpayers in the agreement area or license area shall provide information and various documents of the business operations in the agreement area or license area as required by the tax administration.

#### Article 136 .- Tax Incentives

The provision of tax incentives for any petroleum agreement or mineral resource license entered into prior to the effective date of the provisions of this Chapter shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 137 .- Application of Tax Provisions

Taxpayers who carry out petroleum operations and taxpayers who carry out mineral resource operations as stated in Article 126 of this Law shall apply the provisions in this Chapter and other provisions on taxation.

In case of inconsistency, the provisions of this Chapter shall prevail over other provisions on taxation.

#### Article 138 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of tax collection on petroleum operations or mineral resource operations shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 11
# Tax on Property Rental

#### Article 139 .- Object of Tax

Tax on property rental shall be applied to the rental of property in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 140 .- Scope of Tax

Tax on property rental applies to the owner or the successor of the owner of the property who is not a taxpayer under the self-assessment regime.

#### Article 141 .- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter shall be defined as follows:

1- Property refers to:

a- Various buildings and constructions such as houses, factories, hotels, offices, as well as their annexes.

b- Machinery and equipment attached to industrial establishments.

c- Commercial or industrial installations that are fixed in place, large buildings, or warehouses.

d- Mobile homes for residence or other business purposes.

e- Land without construction.

2- Successor refers to: A person who has rights over the property through the granting by the owner of the property.

3- Property rental fee refers to: The gross rental fee contracted or agreed upon with the lessee based on market value.

#### Article 142 .- Tax Rate

Tax on property rental is set at the rate of 10% (ten percent) of the property rental fee.

#### Article 143 .- Tax Exemptions

Tax on property rental shall be exempted as follows:

1- Rental fees from the rental of property of the Royal Government or Royal Government ministries and institutions.

2- Rental fees from the rental of property of foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

3- Rental fees from the rental of property of any organization or association that:

a- Is organized and operated for purely religious, charitable, scientific, literary, or educational purposes.

b- No part of the rental fee is used for private interest.

4- Money deposited by the lessee as security, and at the end of the contract, the owner must return the full amount to the lessee.

5- Rental fees that have already been subject to tax withholding by an enterprise which is a taxpayer under the self-assessment regime according to Article 59 and Article 26 of this Law.

6- Rental fees from the rental of property of an enterprise which is a taxpayer under the self-assessment regime and has already included them in the taxable income of the enterprise.

#### Article 144 .- Obligations of the Owner or Successor

The owner or the successor of the owner has the following obligations:

1- Must register with the tax administration in the geographical area where the property is located within 15 (fifteen) days from the date of signing the rental contract or from the date of change in the contract according to the form determined by the tax administration.

2- Must notify the tax administration in writing within 15 (fifteen) days after ending the rental, by dating and having a valid signature of the owner or an authorized representative.

3- Must issue receipts for money received to the lessee each time the rental fee is received.

4- Must submit the tax declaration on property rental to the tax administration according to the following schedule:

a- Monthly, no later than the 20th of the following month, if the contract requires monthly payments.

b- No later than the 20th of the month following the month in which the payment must be made according to the total amount, if the contract requires a total payment for several months.

#### Article 145 .- Obligations of the Lessee

The lessee has the obligation to show the payment receipts provided by the owner and must provide other information related to the rental to the tax administration.

#### Article 146 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of tax collection on property rental fees shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 12
# Tax on Property

#### Article 147 .- Object of Tax

Tax on property shall be applied to property located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 148 .- Scope of Tax

Tax on property applies to property located in the administration of the capital and provinces of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 149 .- Definitions

For the purpose of the provisions on property tax, the term "Property" refers to land, houses, buildings, and other constructions.

#### Article 150 .- Tax Rate

Tax on property shall be collected annually at the rate of 0.1% (zero point one percent).

#### Article 151 .- Tax Base

The tax base for property tax shall be based on the property value determined by the Property Evaluation Committee for property tax. The tax base for property tax and the Property Evaluation Committee for property tax shall be determined and established by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 152 .- Tax Exemptions

Tax on property shall be exempted as follows:

1- Property which is agricultural land.

2- Property which is the property of the Royal Government or Royal Government ministries and institutions.

3- Property which is the property of a community or any person that:

a- Is organized and operated for purely religious and charitable purposes.

b- No part of the property or income is used for private interest.

4- Property which is the property of foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

5- Property which is infrastructure, such as roads, bridges, clean water production systems or electricity production systems, airports, ports, railway stations, including buildings and offices that directly serve the activities of those infrastructures.

#### Article 153 .- Obligations to Submit Declaration and Pay Tax

The owner or the possessor or the ultimate beneficiary of the property has the obligation to submit the declaration and pay the annual tax.

#### Article 154 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of tax collection on property shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 13
# Registration Tax

#### Article 155 .- Object of Tax

Registration tax shall be applied to the transfer of ownership or possession, or the contribution of shares as property and movable property, the transfer of shares, contracts for the supply of goods or services using the state budget, and legal documents.

#### Article 156 .- Scope of Tax

Registration tax applies to:

1- The transfer of ownership or possession of property and movable property or the contribution of shares as property and movable property in a company.

2- The transfer of any or all shares of a company.

3- Contracts for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

4- Legal documents, including documents on company dissolution, merger, and company closure.

#### Article 157 .- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter shall be defined as follows:

1- "Roj Chon Cheat" (Related Persons/Blood relatives) refers to:

a- The circle of biological parents and biological children.

b- Husband and wife.

- Between biological grandparents and biological grandchildren

- Between biological siblings

- Between biological parents and children-in-law

- Between biological grandparents and grandchildren-in-law.

2- Gift refers to: The transfer of ownership or possession of property from living persons to relatives without charge.

3- Inheritance refers to: The transfer of ownership or possession of property from deceased persons to relatives.

4- Contract Value refers to: The value stated in a contract for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

5- Share Value refers to: The value of the transfer of shares based on market value.

6- Share refers to: Any or all shares, interests, or the sale of business of a legal entity, partnership, or sole proprietorship that is registered or not registered with the ministry or competent institution.

7- Property refers to: Land, houses, buildings, and other constructions, whether registered or not registered by the cadastral administration.

8- Property Company refers to: Any company that holds property directly or indirectly with a value exceeding 50% (fifty percent) of the total asset value of the company.

9- Movable Property refers to: All types of means of transport or vehicles, such as launches, barges, boats, motorboats, cars, truck heads, trailers, semi-trailers, motorcycles, tricycles, tractors, and similar machinery.

10- Legal Document refers to: Legal documents regarding company dissolution, merger, and closure of companies registered with the ministry or competent institution.

#### Article 158 .- Tax Rates

Registration tax is set at the following rates:

1- 4% (four percent) of the property value for the transfer of ownership or possession of property consisting of buildings and/or land, or the contribution of shares as property in a company, or the transfer of shares or similar interests of a property company.

2- 4% (four percent) of the value of movable property for the transfer of ownership or possession of movable property or the contribution of shares as movable property in a company.

3- 0.1% (zero point one percent) of the share value for the transfer of any or all shares of a company.

4- 0.1% (zero point one percent) of the contract value for contracts for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

5- 1,000,000 (one million) Riels for legal documents, including documents on company dissolution, merger, and company closure.

#### Article 159 .- Tax Base

The tax base for registration tax shall be determined as follows:

1- The value of property and movable property shall be based on the market value at the time of transfer of ownership or possession. The Ministry of Economy and Finance may determine the value of property and movable property to serve as the tax base.

2- The value of shares or similar interests shall be based on the market value at the time the transfer of shares or similar interests occurs.

3- Contract value shall be based on the value in the contract for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

4- Legal documents shall be valued at 1,000,000 (one million) Riels for each legal document.

#### Article 160 .- Taxpayer

Registration tax is applied to:

1- The receiver of ownership or possession of property and movable property.

2- The receiver of ownership or possession of shares.

3- The person who receives the contract for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

4- The receiver of rights over legal documents.

#### Article 161 .- Tax Exemptions and Incentives

Registration tax shall be exempted and incentivized as follows:

1- Receipt of ownership or possession of land in the form of a concession from the Royal Government.

2- Receipt of ownership or possession of property within the circle of relatives.

3- Receipt of ownership of all types of motorcycles, tricycles, tractors, and all types of water vessels with power up to 150 (one hundred fifty) horsepower.

4- Receipt of ownership or possession of property and movable property that must be recorded in the inventory list of various state institutions of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

5- Receipt of ownership or possession of property and movable property of foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

6- Purchase of movable property by enterprises as goods for resale.

#### Article 162 .- Obligations to Submit Declaration and Pay Tax

Taxpayers have the obligation to complete the submission of declaration and payment of tax within 3 (three) months for:

1- The transfer of ownership or possession of property and movable property or the contribution of shares as property and movable property in a company.

2- The transfer of any or all shares of a company.

3- Contracts for the supply of goods or services using the state budget.

4- Legal documents, including documents on company dissolution, merger, and company closure.

#### Article 163 .- Obstruction Due to Failure to Submit Declaration and Pay Tax

If any new owner or possessor fails to pay the registration tax:

1- The cadastral administration shall not issue or create a new certificate of title to immovable property or a certificate of possession of immovable property or similar electronic certificates.

2- Sub-national administration authorities, including capital, provinces, municipalities, districts, khans, communes, sangkats, and villages, shall not certify the transfer of possession of the property.

3- The Ministry of Public Works and Transport or competent authorities shall not issue new identification cards for the transfer of ownership of all types of means of transport and vehicles, except for all types of motorcycles, tricycles, tractors, and water vessels with power up to 150 (one hundred fifty) horsepower.

#### Article 164 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of registration tax collection shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 14
# Capital Gains Tax

#### Article 165 .- Object of Tax

Capital gains tax shall be applied to capital gains realized from the sale or transfer of capital in the Kingdom of Cambodia and abroad by individual resident taxpayers and from the sale or transfer of capital in the Kingdom of Cambodia by non-resident taxpayers.

#### Article 166 .- Scope of Tax

Capital gains tax applies to taxpayers who receive capital gains.

#### Article 167 .- Definitions

Key terms used in this Chapter shall be defined as follows:

1- Taxpayer refers to: A resident taxpayer or a non-resident taxpayer who sells or transfers capital to another person.

2- Resident Taxpayer refers to: An individual person who has a residence or a permanent place of abode in the Kingdom of Cambodia or who is present in the Kingdom of Cambodia for more than 182 (one hundred eighty-two) days in any 12 (twelve) month period ending in the current tax year.

3- Non-resident Taxpayer refers to: An individual person or legal entity who is not a resident taxpayer.

4- Capital refers to: Property, leases, investment assets, business goodwill, intellectual property, and foreign currency.

5- Capital Gain refers to: Taxable income derived from the deduction of the proceeds from the sale or transfer of capital minus the allowable expenses.

#### Article 168 .- Tax Rate

The rate of capital gains tax is set at 20% (twenty percent).

#### Article 169 .- Tax Exemptions

Capital gains tax shall be exempted for the sale or transfer of:

1- Property which is the property of state institutions.

2- Property which is the property of foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, and technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

3- Primary residence of the taxpayer.

4- Property within the circle of relatives.

5- Assets for public interest according to the Law on Expropriation.

6- Agricultural land owned or possessed by citizens who are actual farmers and have their residence in the commune/sangkat where the agricultural land is located.

#### Article 170 .- Expense Deduction Rules

Rules for expense deduction for capital gains are as follows:

1- Determination-based expense deduction method.

2- Actual expense deduction method.

#### Article 171 .- Obligations to Submit Declaration and Pay Tax

Taxpayers or withholding agents must submit the tax declaration, according to the form determined by the tax administration, and pay the capital gains tax to the tax administration within a period not exceeding 3 (three) months after realizing the capital gain.

#### Article 172 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of capital gains tax collection shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Tax on unused land applies to unused land located in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

For the purpose of the provisions on unused land tax, the term "Unused Land" refers to land with or without constructions that is not being used.

#### Article 176 .- Tax Rate

Tax on unused land shall be collected annually at the rate of 2% (two percent).

#### Article 179 .- Obligations to Submit Declaration and Pay Tax

#### Article 180 .- Rules and Procedures for Tax Collection Management

Rules and procedures for the management of unused land tax collection shall be determined by Prakas of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

# Chapter 16
# Tax on Means of Transport

#### Article 181 .- Object of Tax

Tax on means of transport shall be applied to all types of means of transport.

#### Article 182 .- Scope of Tax

Tax on means of transport applies to means of transport in the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 183 .- Definitions

For the purpose of the provisions on means of transport tax, the term "Means of Transport" refers to all types of means of transport or vehicles, such as launches, barges, boats, motorboats, cars, truck heads, trailers, semi-trailers, motorcycles, tricycles, tractors, and similar machinery.

#### Article 184 .- Tax Rate

1- Tax on means of transport shall be determined according to the types of means of transport as follows:

a- Goods vehicles.

b- Truck heads, trailers, semi-trailers.

c- Passenger vehicles.

d- Water vessels or motor vessels.

e- Tourist vehicles.

f- Modified vehicles or motor vehicles.

2- The rate of tax on means of transport shall be determined by Sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Tax on means of transport applies to the owner of the means of transport.

2- Vehicles which are property of the Royal Cambodian Armed Forces, the Royal Gendarmerie, and the National Police.

3- Vehicles which are property of foreign diplomatic or consular missions, international organizations, or technical cooperation agencies of other governments.

4- All types of motorcycles and tricycles, tractors and similar machinery, and all types of water vessels with power up to 150 (one hundred fifty) horsepower or less.

#### Article 187 ._ Obligations for Tax Declaration and Payment

The owner has the obligation to file tax declarations and pay annual taxes.

#### Article 188 ._ Tax Collection Management Rules and Procedures

Rules and procedures for the management of tax collection on means of transport shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

# CHAPTER 17
# TAX RULES AND PROCEDURES

## SECTION 1
## GENERAL PROVISIONS

#### Article 189 ._ Object

1_ The provisions of this chapter define the rights and obligations of taxpayers, the powers and obligations of the tax administration, and the procedures for reviewing taxes paid.

2_ The provisions of this chapter shall apply to all types of taxes, unless otherwise provided for any specific tax.

#### Article 190 ._ Definitions

Key terms used in Chapter 17 and Chapter 18 are defined as follows:

1_ Tax refers to all taxes and duties.

2_ Person refers to a natural person or legal person.

3_ Taxpayer refers to a person who has the obligation to pay tax.

4_ Tax Administration refers to the organization of the General Department of Taxation.

5_ Tax Official refers to tax officials of all levels serving within the framework of the General Department of Taxation.

6_ Investigation refers to carrying out all types of tax audits, including investigations of offenses against tax provisions.

7_ Tax Declaration refers to documents that tax provisions require a taxpayer or a withholding agent to complete in accordance with the conditions stated in this law.

8_ Withholding Agent refers to a person whom tax provisions require to withhold and pay tax into the state budget on behalf of a third party.

9_ Economic Activity refers to the activity of a person carried out regularly or occasionally to earn income, whether or not for profit, in supplying or intending to supply goods or services to other persons for the purpose of obtaining any benefit.

10_ Additional Tax refers to financial penalties for offenses against tax provisions, calculated at a determined rate based on the amount of tax underpaid or paid late.

11_ Interest refers to interest arising from underpayment or late payment of tax, calculated at a determined rate based on the amount of tax underpaid or paid late.

#### Article 191 ._ International Agreements

1_ Provisions of international agreements related to tax issues to which the Kingdom of Cambodia is a party shall prevail over the provisions of this law.

2_ Rules and procedures for the implementation of the provisions of international agreements related to tax issues shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 192 ._ Language used in Tax Declarations and Documents

All tax declarations, as well as various documents and letters necessary for tax assessment, tax collection, and enforcement of laws and legal regulations on taxation, or related to other procedures in tax assessment, must be made in the Khmer language.

## SECTION 2
## RIGHTS, POWERS AND OBLIGATIONS

#### Article 193 ._ Rights and Obligations of Taxpayers

The rights and obligations of taxpayers are defined as follows:

1_ A taxpayer has the following rights:

a- To receive confidentiality and have information related to their business affairs provided to the tax administration used only for the purposes specified in tax provisions, as stated in Article 197 of this law.

b- To receive regular information on the operation of the tax system and tax assessment procedures, as stated in Article 199 and Article 211 of this law.

c- To receive information about their rights, including the right to protest, as stated in Article 211 and Article 238 of this law.

d- To protest as stated in this law against any balanced decisions of the tax administration as stated in Article 211 and Article 238 of this law.

e- To pay tax no more than what is determined by tax provisions as stated in Article 213 of this law.

2_ A taxpayer has the following obligations:

a- To register with the tax administration as stated in Article 203 of this law.

b- To file tax declarations and provide information as required by tax provisions as stated in Article 201 and Article 206 of this law.

c- To pay tax by the determined date as stated in tax provisions.

d- To maintain, keep, and present accounts, supporting documents, and other documents to the tax administration as stated in tax provisions and Article 201 of this law.

e- To appear before the tax administration by the date stated in the notification letter from the tax administration as stated in Article 202 of this law.

f- To pay tax, additional tax, and various interests determined by the tax administration by the date stated in tax provisions or as notified in writing by the tax administration as stated in tax provisions and Article 213, Article 232, and Article 233 of this law.

#### Article 194 ._ Powers and Obligations of the Tax Administration

The powers and obligations of the tax administration are defined as follows:

1_ The tax administration has the following powers:

a- To assess the tax base of a taxpayer or a withholding agent as stated in Article 209 and Article 210 of this law.

b- To require a taxpayer or a withholding agent to appear as stated in Article 202 of this law.

c- To determine various necessary documents and supporting documents that a taxpayer or a withholding agent must maintain, keep, and provide to the tax administration as stated in Article 201 and Article 230 of this law.

d- To demand a taxpayer or a third party to provide information related to a taxpayer or a withholding agent as stated in Article 206 of this law.

e- To enter the residence or business establishment of a taxpayer, a withholding agent, or a third party to obtain information related to a taxpayer or a withholding agent as stated in Article 230 of this law.

f- To obtain information about persons related to a taxpayer or a withholding agent from all institutions as stated in Article 209 of this law.

g- To implement collection measures against a taxpayer or a withholding agent when that person fails to pay tax, additional tax, and other interest as required as stated in Article 217, Article 218, Article 220, Article 221, and Article 222 of this law. To re-determine business transactions among related persons as stated in tax provisions.

h- To discard and/or re-determine the true substance of any transactions in cases where it is found that a taxpayer has arranged or has any transactions that show false or non-existent gaps for the purpose of reducing or eliminating tax to be paid, which is contrary to the spirit of tax provisions.

2_ The tax administration has the following obligations:

a- To collect tax, additional tax, and various interests as stated in Article 195 of this law.

b- To maintain the confidentiality of information provided by a taxpayer or a third party, and provide this information only to persons determined by tax provisions as stated in Article 197 and Article 248 of this law.

c- To provide information to a taxpayer or a withholding agent to ensure appropriate implementation in accordance with tax provisions as stated in Article 199 of this law.

d- To refund or credit as a tax credit any overpaid tax as stated in tax provisions.

e- To provide notification letters on tax assessment to a taxpayer or a withholding agent as stated in Article 205 to Article 211 of this law.

# SECTION 3
# TAX ADMINISTRATION

#### Article 195 ._ Competent Institutions

1_ The institutions responsible for managing the implementation of tax provisions are as follows:

a- The General Department of Taxation of the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

b- Other government institutions empowered by tax provisions.

2_ The tax administration has the obligation to collect tax, implement penalties as determined by tax provisions, and sue in court in cases of law violations.

3_ The tax administration is the competent institution to decide on administrative penalties. The decision to waive partially or fully administrative penalties is the power of the Minister of Economy and Finance. The tax administration may decide to waive partially or fully these administrative penalties in cases where it receives delegation of power from the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 196 ._ Power in times of National Emergency

1_ In cases where the nation faces economic or financial crises or emergencies or urgent needs to ensure the stability of the livelihood and well-being of the people and ensure the normality of economic and social operations, the Royal Government may decide to grant concessions or exemptions or suspensions of any type of tax, partially or fully, upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

2_ The granting of concessions or exemptions or suspensions of any type of tax, as stated in the above paragraph, shall be included in the content of the financial law subsequently.

#### Article 197 ._ Confidentiality of Tax Information

1_ The tax administration and every person who is an official and agent of the tax administration shall maintain the confidentiality of information related to a taxpayer received during the performance of official duties and may provide information only to persons authorized by this article.

2_ An official and agent of the tax administration may provide information related to a taxpayer only to:

a- Other officials and agents of the tax administration during and for the purpose of performing duties in accordance with tax provisions.

b- Criminal authorities for the purpose of prosecution for tax offenses.

c- A court in a stage of litigation to determine the tax to be paid by a taxpayer or the responsibility for violations of tax provisions.

d- Competent tax authorities of another country in accordance with an international agreement.

3_ A person who has received information from another person authorized to provide information as stated in paragraph 2 of this article shall maintain the confidentiality of that information in accordance with the provisions of this article, except to the minimum extent necessary to be allowed to provide this information.

4_ Information related to a taxpayer may be provided to another person if there is written consent from the taxpayer.

#### Article 198 ._ Providing Information to Taxpayers

Providing information to a taxpayer shall be implemented as follows:

1_ Documents or notification letters provided by the tax administration to a taxpayer constitute an obligation for that taxpayer toward the tax administration only when those documents or notification letters are made in writing and delivered to the taxpayer. Documents or notification letters as stated in this paragraph include notifications via electronic systems, such as faxes and emails.

2_ In cases where tax provisions require the tax administration to provide documents or notification letters to a person, those documents or notification letters are deemed properly delivered only when delivered to that person or sent by registered mail to the legal address of that person. For notifications via electronic systems as stated in paragraph 1 of this article, they are deemed properly delivered if sent to the last address provided by the taxpayer to the tax administration.

3_ The date of documents or various notification letters is the date of delivery to the person. In the case where sending documents or notification letters is done by registered mail, the date of notification is the date of the postmark on the registered mail from the post office. In the case of notifications via electronic systems, the date of notification is the date the notification was sent out.

4_ Documents or notification letters are deemed properly delivered and received if the conditions in paragraph 2 of this article have been met, even if the person being notified refuses to accept or receive the registered mail.

5_ In the case where a person's address has changed and the person has not notified the tax administration of the change, documents or notification letters sent to the last known address are deemed properly delivered and received.

6_ In cases where for any reason documents or notification letters cannot be provided to a taxpayer as stated in paragraphs 1 to 5 of this article, the documents or notification letters are deemed properly delivered if the tax administration has:

a- Posted the documents or notification letters on the door of the residence or business establishment of the taxpayer, or

b- Published them in newspapers, radio, television, or on the website of the tax administration, requiring the taxpayer to appear before the tax administration within 15 (fifteen) days after the publication date.

#### Article 199 ._ Dissemination and Explanation of Tax Provisions

1_ The tax administration shall prepare short explanatory books on the important content of each type of tax.

2_ For any type of tax that the tax administration determines should be explained, local tax officials shall educate taxpayers to understand their obligations and rights.

#### Article 200 ._ Incentives for Tax Collection

1_ The Ministry of Economy and Finance shall establish an incentive system for the tax administration.

2_ Rules and procedures for the operation of the incentive system shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 201 ._ Maintaining, Recording, and Keeping Books, Supporting Documents, and Financial Documents

1_ A taxpayer shall maintain, record, and keep various books, supporting documents, and financial documents as determined by tax provisions and shall submit those books and documents as required to the tax administration.

2_ For taxpayers who do not have the obligation to maintain accounting books according to the Cambodian International Financial Reporting Standards as determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance, they shall maintain records of business transactions in daily and monthly order of all income and expenses related to the business in the format determined by the tax administration.

3_ In the case where a taxpayer fails to maintain or record properly accounting books or various records according to the determined conditions, the tax administration shall determine the various income and expenses.

4_ A person who must maintain, record, or keep various books, documents, or supporting documents determined by tax provisions or other provisions, shall keep those books and documents for a period of 10 (ten) years for medium and large taxpayers, and 3 (three) years for small taxpayers, starting from the end of the tax year.

5_ A taxpayer shall issue invoices for all business transactions. Invoice rules shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

6_ A taxpayer shall properly record the details of invoices in the records.

#### Article 202 ._ Right to Obtain Information

1_ For the purpose of assessing the tax to be paid by any person, or to collect tax, or to fulfill obligations in accordance with international agreements, the tax administration may issue a notification letter to a taxpayer or a third party, including banks, insurance enterprises, and various financial institutions, to:

a- Provide information related to a taxpayer or related persons as stated in the notification letter, such as information on suppliers, customers, or bank accounts.

b- Appear at the time and place specified in the notification letter to provide information and to show or provide documents or data that the person manages as clearly specified in the notification letter.

2_ In addition to the information required as stated in paragraph 1 of this article, the notification letter must contain the name and identification number of the taxpayer (if any), and the signature of the tax administration issuing the notification letter.

3_ A taxpayer or a third party, including banks, insurance enterprises, and various financial institutions, shall submit reports or letters in response to the tax administration within 30 (thirty) days after receiving the notification letter on providing information related to a taxpayer.

4_ Every person may report to the tax administration information related to taxation.

5_ Professional secrecy shall not be an obstacle to the implementation of this article and other articles of this law.

#### Article 203 ._ Obligation for Registration and Update

1_ Every person shall register with the tax administration within 15 (fifteen) days after that person starts an economic activity or other activities, including activities of institutions or various organizations, as stated in paragraph 7 of Article 4 of this law.

2_ Every person shall notify the tax administration within 15 (fifteen) days after changing address, form, name, business object, suspension, cessation of business, composition of management, or person in charge of other affairs of the enterprise.

3_ Rules and procedures for registration and update shall be determined by a Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 204.- Certificate of Registration and Taxpayer Identification Number

1- When the registration has been completed, the Tax Administration shall issue a certificate of registration describing the Taxpayer Identification Number of the person. This identification number shall be used on all documents related to taxation.

2- All units under the Ministry of Economy and Finance must use the identification number in this Article. All contracts with state institutions must bear the registration of the Taxpayer Identification Number to be considered valid.

#### Article 205.- Rights of the Tax Administration in Registering Taxpayers

The Tax Administration has the right to register any person who is required by law to register and has not come to register. In this case, the Tax Administration may determine the effective date of registration.

### Section 4
### Tax Return

#### Article 206.- Filing of Tax Return

The filing of tax return is determined as follows:

1- A taxpayer or a withholding agent shall file a tax return with the Tax Administration in accordance with the form, time, and place as determined by the Tax Administration.

2- The tax return shall be signed by the taxpayer or the legal representative of the taxpayer.

3- All investors shall file a monthly tax return for each month and an annual tax return for that tax year and pay all taxes and duties according to the provisions of each tax.

4- All investors shall attach the certificate of the Council for the Development of Cambodia confirming the compliance with the annual tax return for that tax year.

5- A taxpayer or a withholding agent or all investors may file a tax return as stated in paragraph 1 and paragraph 3 of this Article through the electronic tax return receiving system of the Tax Administration, and it has the same value as a written tax return which is the original law.

#### Article 207.- Filing of Information Return and Other Documents

Any person who has paid money to other persons shall file with the Tax Administration an information return and other documents regarding such payment according to the procedures determined by the Tax Administration.

#### Article 208.- Representation of Taxpayers

1- A taxpayer may delegate authorization in writing to another person to act as their representative in various matters related to taxation. The taxpayer may limit the scope of authority of this delegation. The taxpayer shall be directly responsible for all actions of the legal representative or the person who has received authorization from them until the time the Tax Administration has received a written confirmation from the taxpayer regarding the cancellation of such authorization.

2- A person who is a taxpayer's representative shall have the right to act on behalf of the taxpayer to:

- File tax returns

- Present reports or various documents

- Pay all taxes and duties determined by the provisions on taxation

- Make a protest

- Fulfill all obligations for which the taxpayer is responsible before the law and legal instruments on taxation.

3- The person who is the taxpayer's representative, within 15 (fifteen) days, shall provide information to the Tax Administration accompanied by written evidence of the valid delegation of authority.

4- A person who has obtained a license as a Tax Service Agent may be a representative of the taxpayer and may provide services related to taxation to the taxpayer. The rules and procedures for the management of the Tax Service Agent shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

### Section 5
### Tax Assessment

#### Article 209.- Tax Assessment

The amount of tax is determined as follows:

1- In the case that the taxpayer's tax has been paid through the withholding method and the taxpayer has no obligation to file a tax return, the tax assessment of the taxpayer is the determination of the amount of tax withheld in the calendar year.

2- In the case that the taxpayer or the withholding agent has an obligation to file a tax return, the tax assessment of the taxpayer or the withholding agent is the determination of tax that the taxpayer or the withholding agent has calculated on the tax return filed with the Tax Administration.

3- In the case that the taxpayer or the withholding agent has an obligation to file a tax return but has not filed the tax return, or has not maintained the accounting books or other documents correctly, or in the case of failing to provide the necessary information to the Tax Administration for a correct tax assessment, the tax assessment of the taxpayer or the withholding agent is a unilateral tax assessment made by the Tax Administration and delivered to that person. The unilateral tax assessment shall be based on:

a- Information contained in various tax returns or other documents filed by the taxpayer with the Tax Administration.

b- Information contained in the information returns.

c- Other information received by the Tax Administration.

4- If there is a basis confirming that the tax collection may be obstructed, the Tax Administration may make a tax assessment on the taxpayer at any time.

#### Article 210.- Re-assessment and Re-assessment Period

The re-assessment and re-assessment period shall be processed as follows:

1- For the tax assessment as stated in paragraph 1 of Article 209 of this Law, the Tax Administration may make a re-assessment within 3 (three) years after the calendar year in which the withholding occurred.

2- For the tax assessment as stated in paragraph 2 and paragraph 3 of Article 209 of this Law, the Tax Administration may make a re-assessment within any of the following periods:

a- Within 3 (three) years after the date of filing the tax return.

b- Within 10 (ten) years after the date required to file the tax return if there is evidence confirming tax evasion related to the implementation of the provisions on taxation.

c- Within any period with written consent from the taxpayer.

3- A taxpayer or a withholding agent may request to amend the tax return within 3 (three) years after the date of filing the tax return according to paragraph 2 of Article 209 of this Law based on error or misunderstanding made by the taxpayer in the previous return. If the result of such amended tax return lead to a tax refund or tax credit, the Tax Administration may conduct an audit for verification.

4- A taxpayer or a withholding agent may request the Tax Administration to amend the re-assessment within 3 (three) years after the date the Tax Administration made the re-assessment based on additional information that the taxpayer provided to the Tax Administration which was not received during the re-assessment.

5- In the case that the taxpayer or the withholding agent amends their tax assessment or requests the Tax Administration to amend the re-assessment, the statute of limitations for the re-assessment in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of this Article shall begin to run from the date of filing the amended tax return or the date the Tax Administration amends the re-assessment.

6- The rules and procedures for re-assessment and re-assessment period shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 211.- Re-assessment Procedure

The re-assessment shall be processed according to the following procedures:

1- The Tax Administration shall issue a notice of re-assessment to the taxpayer.

2- The taxpayer has 30 (thirty) days to respond to the re-assessment to the unit in charge of the re-assessment. During this period, the taxpayer may accept or object to the re-assessment. The taxpayer shall be deemed to have concurred with the re-assessment if they fail to respond.

3- If there is an objection to the re-assessment, the taxpayer shall file a protest with the Director General of the General Department of Taxation according to the procedure stated in Article 236 of this Law.

4- The unit in charge of the re-assessment of the General Department of Taxation shall send the result of the re-assessment to the tax collection unit within 30 (thirty) days after the issuance of the notice of re-assessment.

#### Article 212.- Burden of Proof

1- In the case that the taxpayer has not maintained sufficient documents or has not provided sufficient information, the Tax Administration has the right to assess the tax on the taxpayer based on the specific information that the Tax Administration possesses.

2- In the case that the taxable income or the income declared by the taxpayer has a significant discrepancy compared to the purchase of assets or materials which makes the taxpayer's expenses prominent, the Tax Administration has the right to assess the tax based on the estimated income appropriate to the size of the expenses for the purchase of such prominent assets or materials.

3- The burden of proof to show that the tax determined by the Tax Administration as stated in paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 of this Article is incorrect shall lie with the taxpayer.

### Section 6
### Tax Collection

#### Article 213.- Payment of Tax

The payment of tax is determined as follows:

1- Tax is due and payable within the period required by the provisions on taxation for filing the tax return.

2- Tax debt is due and payable within 30 (thirty) days after the delivery of the notice of tax debt.

3- Tax is due and payable within 3 (three) days after the delivery of the notice of tax debt as stated in paragraph 4 of Article 209 of this Law.

4- The Minister of Economy and Finance shall issue a Prakas on rules and procedures to:

- Determine the date for tax debt collection to avoid loss from unable to collect the tax debt

- Treat tax debt as uncollectible tax.

5- The statute of limitations for waiving rights over tax debt is 10 (ten) years from the date the tax debt was notified to the taxpayer. The rules and procedures for suspending or interrupting the statute of limitations shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 214.- Responsibility of Administrators, Liquidators, or Owners

If an administrator, liquidator, or owner of any enterprise knows and has the intention to cause the enterprise not to report or to under-report the tax in violation of the provisions on taxation or not to allow the payment of withheld tax to the Tax Administration, such administrator, liquidator, or owner shall be directly responsible for the tax to be paid.

#### Article 215.- Transfer of Business or Shares and Merger of Enterprises

1- In the case of a transfer of business or a transfer of shares, the new owner shall be responsible for all tax debts of the business or enterprise.

2- In the case of a merger of 2 (two) or more enterprises, the surviving enterprise or the enterprise resulting from the merger of 2 (two) or more enterprises shall be responsible for all tax debts.

### Section 7
### Powers of the Tax Administration in Tax Collection

#### Article 216.- Notice of Tax Debt and Notice of Intent to Collect Tax Debt

1- As stated in paragraph 4 of Article 111 of this Law, the Tax Administration shall issue a notice of tax debt specifying the amount of tax, additional tax, interest, and fines to the taxpayer. The taxpayer has 30 (thirty) days to settle the tax debt as stated in this paragraph.

2- In the case that the taxpayer fails to settle the tax debt as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article, the Tax Administration shall issue a reminder notice for tax debt collection to the taxpayer.

3- The Tax Administration may implement enforcement measures as stated in Article 217, Article 218, Article 220, Article 221, and Article 222 of this Law at least 15 (fifteen) days after the delivery of the reminder notice for tax debt collection as stated in paragraph 2 of this Article.

#### Article 217.- Rights to Place a Lien, Seize and Manage Property of Taxpayers

The rights to place a lien, seize, and manage the property of the taxpayer are determined as follows:

1- If a person obligated to pay tax as required by the provisions on taxation neglects or refuses to pay tax after the reminder notice for tax debt collection has been properly delivered, the Tax Administration has the right to place a lien, seize, and manage the property of that person at a value appropriate to the tax debt. The Tax Administration shall issue a reminder notice for tax debt collection to the taxpayer at least 15 (fifteen) days before implementing enforcement measures as stated in Article 218, Article 220, Article 221, and Article 222 of this Law.

2- The rights to place a lien, seize, and manage the property of the taxpayer arises on the date the reminder notice for tax debt collection was delivered to the taxpayer as stated in Article 198 of this Law.

3- If all conditions of this Article are properly met, the rights to place a lien, seize, and manage property as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article shall be valid and have priority over all other rights existed previously or subsequently to the rights to place a lien, seize, and manage the property of the taxpayer, except where otherwise provided in the Land Law and provisions related to security interest. The Tax Administration shall issue a public notice regarding the taxpayer's property that has been decided to be placed under lien, seized, and managed by the Tax Administration. Any security interest created over the taxpayer's property that has been decided to be placed under lien, seized, and managed by the Tax Administration after the date of public notice by the Tax Administration cannot be used to challenge the priority rights of the Tax Administration over such property. The form and procedures for public notice of the taxpayer's property to be placed under lien, seized, and managed by the Tax Administration shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

4- The expenses for managing, inspecting, and maintaining such property are the burden of the taxpayer.

5- Any person may file a protest with the Tax Administration to request the lifting of the rights to place a lien, seize, and manage their property as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article by citing errors in placing such rights.

6- If the Tax Administration finds that the placing of rights to place a lien, seize, and manage such property was in error, the Tax Administration shall issue a certificate confirming the lifting of the rights to place a lien, seize, and manage the property within 10 (ten) days after the finding, and state in the certificate that the management of the property was in error.

#### Article 218.- Rights to Use and Enjoy Benefit, Rights of Provisional Disposal and Seizure

The rights to use and enjoy benefit, rights of provisional disposal, and seizure of the taxpayer's property are determined as follows:

1- In case of necessity and to ensure the payment of tax debt, the Tax Administration has the right to use and enjoy benefit of the taxpayer's property that has been placed under lien.

2- Besides the rights to use and enjoy benefit as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article, the Tax Administration may sue in court to seize the taxpayer's property for the purpose of ensuring the payment of tax debt. During the period before a final decision on the seizure of the taxpayer's property is made, and in case of obtaining permission through a court order that receives the lawsuit, the Tax Administration may perform provisional disposal of the taxpayer's property in the form of an auction, and the proceeds from the provisional disposal shall be kept in a provisional account of the Tax Administration.

3- A person who has the duty to possess and manage the taxpayer's property that is to be seized by the Tax Administration as stated in paragraph 2 of this Article shall not return that property to the taxpayer or use that property for various payments except for payments permitted by the Tax Administration.

4- Regarding cash property, the Tax Administration may seize the taxpayer's cash or cash to be received by the taxpayer without following the procedures stated in paragraph 2 of this Article.

#### Article 219.- Disposal of Seized Property

In the case that there is a sale of property after the seizure of the taxpayer's property, the amount of the sale proceeds exceeding the taxpayer's tax debt under this law shall be returned to the taxpayer, unless otherwise disposed of by the court in accordance with the laws in force.

#### Article 220.- Blocking Bank Accounts

1- The Tax Administration may block the taxpayer's bank account through a notice from the Tax Administration, which is effective immediately upon delivery of such notice to the bank. Blocking the bank account in this Article includes blocking transactions in all forms.

2- Through this notice to block the bank account, the bank cannot open a new account for the same taxpayer and cannot perform various payments from that account, except for the withdrawal of funds determined by the Tax Administration for the payment of tax due, additional tax, interest, and other fines.

3- The blocked bank account may be reopened only with a notice from the Tax Administration.

4- A bank that fails to comply with the notice in paragraph 1 of this Article shall be responsible to the Tax Administration up to the amount of funds in the taxpayer's account at the time the notice was delivered.

#### Article 221.- Stop of Export and Import Operations

1- The Tax Administration may stop export and import operations. Stopping export and import operations refers to the blocking by the Customs Administration of the taxpayer's imported goods and goods to be exported by the taxpayer according to the notice of the Tax Administration, which is effective after such notice is delivered to the Customs Administration.

2- The Tax Administration may seize, use and enjoy benefit, and perform provisional disposal of the taxpayer's goods blocked by the Customs Administration according to the conditions stated in Article 218 of this Law.

3- The permission for export and import operations to be released from the stop shall be implemented according to the notice from the Tax Administration.

4- Goods blocked by the Customs Administration which do not belong to the taxpayer shall be released from the block with the approval of the Tax Administration.

#### Article 222.- Provisional Suspension of Permits and Licenses

The Tax Administration may issue a notice to the competent authorities requesting the provisional suspension of various permits and licenses of the taxpayer to conduct any activity.

## Chapter 18
## Penalty Provisions

### Section 1
### General Provisions

#### Article 223.- Negligence

A taxpayer or a withholding agent is deemed to be negligent if the tax paid is less than the amount of tax determined by the provisions on taxation by not more than 10 (ten) percent or if the tax was not paid by the date required by law or other legal instruments.

#### Article 224.- Serious Negligence

A taxpayer or a withholding agent is deemed to be seriously negligent if the tax paid is less than the amount of tax determined by the provisions on taxation by more than 10 (ten) percent.

#### Article 225.- Acts of Obstruction of the Implementation of Tax Provisions

The following acts committed in violation of this Law and related legal instruments shall be deemed to be an obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions:

1- Failure to register with the Tax Administration according to this Law and related legal instruments.

2- Failure to notify the Tax Administration of changes in registration information.

3- Failure to file a tax return.

4- Failure to use the recording system determined by the Tax Administration.

5- Failure to issue an invoice.

6- Failure to provide information or submit a report upon request.

7- Failure to allow the Tax Administration to examine accounting records or other documents.

8- Failure to maintain accounting records and other documents.

9- Attempting to obstruct tax assessment and collection.

10- Creating or providing false records, documents, reports, or information.

11- Intentionally concealing or destroying accounting records, records, documents, reports, or other information.

12- Issuing a false invoice.

13- Failure to allow the Tax Administration to enter the business premises to assess and collect tax.

#### Article 226.- Tax Evasion

1- Tax evasion is any act committed intentionally and with premeditation that results in the loss of tax that the tax provisions require to be paid.

2- The following cases shall also be deemed to be tax evasion:

a- Serious negligence as stated in Article 224 of this Law committed on two separate occasions within 3 (three) calendar years.

b- Serious negligence as stated in Article 224 of this Law committed on three or more separate occasions within 10 (ten) calendar years.

#### Article 227.- Ground for Settlement of Penalties

1- Settlement with the Tax Administration through the payment of tax, additional tax, and interest shall result in the termination of the criminal lawsuit for criminal offences against the tax provisions. In case the Tax Administration agrees to the settlement, the Tax Administration shall prepare a record of the settlement for submission to the prosecution office or the competent court.

2- The provision of paragraph 1 of this Article shall not apply to serious criminal offences as stated in other criminal laws.

### Section 2
### Powers of the Tax Administration

#### Article 228.- Competence in Building Criminal Case Files

1- The Tax Administration is the sole institution competent to investigate and build case files related to criminal offences against the tax provisions, by implementing in accordance with this Law and the Code of Criminal Procedure of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

2- The Tax Administration may also have competence to investigate and build case files related to other criminal offences connected to criminal offences against the tax provisions, if permitted by the prosecutor.

#### Article 229.- Judicial Police Qualification of Tax Officials

1- Tax officials shall receive the qualification of judicial police officers and judicial police agents to examine offences against the tax provisions.

2- The form and procedures for granting qualification to tax officials shall be determined by the Inter-ministerial Prakas of the Minister of Justice and the Minister of Economy and Finance.

3- All operations of tax officials related to criminal offences against the tax provisions shall be implemented in accordance with the provisions of the Code of Criminal Procedure. The Tax Administration has the right to request cooperation from competent institutions to participate in suppressing offences against the tax provisions.

#### Article 230.- Investigative Powers

Tax officials have the power to investigate as follows:

1- Tax officials have the power to enter business premises or places deemed to be business premises to examine accounting records, all types of tax returns, and other related documents for tax assessment. Entering the business premises as stated in this paragraph shall be done during business hours or at any time by following the order of the prosecution office or the court in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure in force.

2- Tax officials who have legally entered the business premises or places deemed to be business premises as stated in paragraph 1 of this Article have the right to:

a- Make extracts or copies of various documents or electronic document formats located in that place.

b- Seize various documents or other evidence that can become information or evidence for the assessment of taxes due of any person, and a record of seizure must be prepared.

c- Install various monitoring devices or apply seals, and block goods if related to any tax implementation.

d- Count the tax assets, raw materials, goods in process, finished goods, and other stocks/inventories.

ង- Seize, block, or manage the taxpayer's property to ensure tax debt.

ច- Arrest the taxpayer who committed criminal offences against the tax provisions, by implementing in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

3- In all cases when criminal offences against the tax provisions are found within the framework of investigation by tax officials, the operations of tax officials related to gathering evidence, preparing records, and implementing all other necessary criminal procedures, including the arrest of suspects, shall be performed by tax officials qualified as judicial police officers or agents, and shall be implemented in accordance with the Code of Criminal Procedure of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

4- The Tax Administration may require banking institutions in the Kingdom of Cambodia to provide information regarding the bank accounts of the taxpayer or related persons or block the accounts of the taxpayer or related persons in case there is evidence confirming the commission of tax evasion.

5- During the investigation, tax officials must maintain proper conduct, avoiding actions that affect the honour and business of the taxpayer.

### Section 3
### Administrative Penalties

#### Article 231.- Types of Administrative Penalties

1- Administrative penalties determined in this Law include additional tax, interest, monetary fines, suspension or revocation of licenses, provisional closure of business, and other administrative penalties determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

2- The decision to impose administrative penalties is the competence of the Tax Administration. The competent unit of the Tax Administration may receive the right to decide on imposing administrative penalties according to the legal instruments in force. Protests against the decision of the competent unit of the Tax Administration shall be implemented in accordance with Article 236 of this Law.

#### Article 232.- Implementation of Additional Tax

1- Additional tax shall apply to offences against the tax provisions.

2- Additional tax for underpayment or late payment shall be calculated separately from monetary fines for committing acts of obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions.

3- In case of underpayment, additional tax and interest shall be due and payable according to the procedures and level of implementation for the underpaid tax.

4- In any case, the imposition of additional tax does not affect the implementation of criminal penalties for offences against the tax provisions.

#### Article 233.- Additional Tax and Interest

Additional tax and interest are determined as follows:

1- For a person who is negligent as stated in Article 223 of this Law, the additional tax shall be equal to 10 (ten) percent of the underpaid or late-paid tax, plus interest of 1.5 (one point five) percent of the underpaid tax for each month or part of a month that the underpaid tax remains unpaid.

2- For a person who is seriously negligent as stated in Article 224 of this Law, the additional tax shall be equal to 25 (twenty-five) percent of the underpaid tax, plus interest of 1.5 (one point five) percent of the underpaid tax for each month or part of a month that the underpaid tax remains unpaid.

3- In the case of a unilateral tax assessment, the additional tax shall be equal to 40 (forty) percent of the underpaid tax, plus interest of 1.5 (one point five) percent of the underpaid tax for each month or part of a month that the underpaid tax remains unpaid.

4- In the case of a unilateral tax assessment for failure to file a tax return, the additional tax shall be equal to 40 (forty) percent of the determined tax, plus interest of 1.5 (one point five) percent of the determined tax for each month or part of a month that the tax remains unpaid.

5- If a person fails to pay tax within 15 (fifteen) days after receiving the reminder notice for tax debt collection, additional tax shall be determined at the rate of 25 (twenty-five) percent of the late-paid tax, plus interest of 1.5 (one point five) percent of the late-paid tax for each month or part of a month that the tax remains unpaid.

6- Additional tax for late payment of the tax on means of transportation and the annual stamp tax shall be equal to 100 (one hundred) percent of the tax due.

7- Interest as stated in this Article shall be calculated from the first day of the month following the month in which the tax was due. For income tax, interest shall be calculated from the first day of the month following the deadline for filing the annual result return. Interest shall not apply during the re-assessment period under Article 211 of this Law or within 30 (thirty) days after the delivery of the notice of tax debt under Article 213 of this Law.

#### Article 234.- Formal Written Decision

The Tax Administration shall issue a formal written decision to any person who commits any act of obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions as stated in paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 of Article 225 of this Law.

#### Article 235.- Monetary Fines for Obstruction of the Implementation of Tax Provisions

Monetary fines for obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions are determined as follows:

1- A fine of 500,000 (five hundred thousand) Riels shall be imposed by the Tax Administration, without considering other administrative penalties, on a person who has already received more than 2 (two) written warnings from the Tax Administration and continues to commit any act of obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions as stated in paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 of Article 225 of this Law.

2- A fine of 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels shall be imposed by the Tax Administration, without considering other administrative penalties, on a person who has committed any act of obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions as stated in paragraphs 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 of Article 225 of this Law.

3- In the case that a person who has already been decided to pay a fine as stated in paragraph 1 or paragraph 2 of this Article and such person again commits any act of obstruction of the implementation of the tax provisions as stated in paragraphs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 of Article 225 of this Law, such person shall be subject to criminal penalty as stated in Article 243 of this Law.

### Section 4
### Settlement of Taxpayer Protests

#### Article 236.- Administrative Protest Procedures

The rules for the settlement of taxpayer protests on tax issues are determined as follows:

1- A taxpayer who does not agree with the re-assessment, monetary fine, or other decision made by the competent unit of the Tax Administration may file a protest with the Director General of the General Department of Taxation. The protest shall be limited only to the basis/reasons or other information contained in the re-assessment, decision, or re-assessment procedure.

2- An administrative protest shall be made in writing according to the form stated in Article 237 of this Law and shall be submitted to the Tax Administration within a maximum period of 30 (thirty) days after the date the taxpayer received the notice of tax debt, monetary fine, or other decision made by the competent unit of the Tax Administration.

3- An administrative protest does not release the taxpayer from the obligation to pay tax, additional tax, interest, and other monetary fines specified in the notice of tax debt, monetary fine, or other decision made by the competent unit of the Tax Administration.

#### Article 237.- Contents of Administrative Protest

1- An administrative protest may be acceptable if the protest contains the following information:

a- Taxpayer identification number of the person filing the protest.

b- Reference to the tax assessment, decision, or result that is the subject of the protest.

c- The facts or errors that are the subject of the protest.

d- Reasons for the protest.

e- Date and signature of the taxpayer and signature of the taxpayer's legal representative.

2- The rules and procedures for settling protests shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 238.- Decision of the Tax Administration

1- The Tax Administration shall issue a new decision within a maximum period of 60 (sixty) days after receiving the protest to confirm the correctness or incorrectness in whole or in part of the tax assessment or other decisions protested by the taxpayer. The Tax Administration shall also specify the basis for such decision.

2- If the taxpayer does not agree with this new decision of the Tax Administration, the taxpayer may file a protest with the Committee for Tax Dispute Settlement within a maximum period of 30 (thirty) days after the date of receiving the decision.

#### Article 239.- Committee for Tax Dispute Settlement

1- The Committee for Tax Dispute Settlement is established with the duty to examine and settle taxpayer protests that are dissatisfied with the final decision of the General Department of Taxation related to tax disputes.

2- The organization and functioning of the Committee for Tax Dispute Settlement shall be determined by a Sub-decree upon the request of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 240.- Lawsuit in Court

1- A taxpayer has the right to sue in a competent court to object to the decision of the Committee for Tax Dispute Settlement within a maximum period of 30 (thirty) days after receiving the notice of such decision.

2- The taxpayer shall deposit with the National Bank an amount equivalent to at least the amount of tax related to the dispute before filing a lawsuit in court. The rules and procedures for determining and making the deposit shall be determined by the Prakas of the Minister of Economy and Finance.

#### Article 241 .- Enforcement of Decisions on Monetary Fines

1- The tax administration may exercise its powers as determined in this law to ensure the effectiveness of the enforcement of decisions on monetary fines when such decisions become final.

2- In case of necessity and to ensure the effectiveness of the enforcement of final decisions on monetary fines, the provisions of the Chapter on Penalties (General Provisions) of Title 1 (General Provisions), Chapter on Penalties (Physical Coercion) of Title 3 (Physical Coercion) of Book 8 of the Code of Criminal Procedure shall be applied in cases where the decision on monetary fines has become final.

#### Part 5
#### Criminal Sanctions

#### Article 242 .- Tax Evasion Offense

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, a director, manager, or owner of an enterprise or any person responsible for an enterprise who commits tax evasion as stated in Article 226 of this law shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 5 (five) years and a fine from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels to 200,000,000 (two hundred million) Riels.

#### Article 243 .- Offense of Obstruction of the Implementation of Tax Provisions

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, any person who commits any of the following acts of obstruction of the implementation of tax provisions shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels:

1- Attempts to obstruct the tax assessment and collection.

2- Creates or provides fraudulent records, documents, reports, or information.

3- Intentionally conceals or destroys accounting books, invoices, records, documents, reports, or other information.

4- Issues fraudulent invoices.

5- Does not allow the tax administration to enter business establishments to assess and collect taxes.

#### Article 244 .- Breach of Confidentiality

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, any person who breaches the confidentiality of tax information in violation of Article 197 of this law shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels.

#### Article 245 .- Practicing as a Tax Service Agent without a License

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, any person who practices as a tax service agent without a license shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) month to 1 (one) year and a fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels.

#### Article 246 .- Failure to Pay Collected Taxes

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, any person who fails to pay already collected taxes shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels.

#### Article 247 .- Collecting Taxes without Right to Collect

Without prejudice to other criminal offenses, any person who collects taxes without obtaining the right from the administration in accordance with the provisions of this law or other legal instruments in force shall be punished by imprisonment from 1 (one) year to 3 (three) years and a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels.

#### Article 248 .- Administrative Responsibility of Tax Officials

Any tax officials who commit acts in violation of the provisions of this law and other relevant tax provisions in the exercise of their functions and duties shall be subject to administrative sanctions in accordance with the law and other legal instruments in force. These administrative sanctions do not constitute an obstacle to criminal prosecution and punishment, as well as civil liability, in accordance with this law and other legal instruments in force.

#### Article 249 .- Responsibility of Legal Entities

1- Legal entities may be declared as criminally responsible as stated in Article 42 (Criminal Responsibility of Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code for offenses stated in Article 242, Article 243, Article 245, Article 246, Article 247, and Article 250 of this law.

2- For legal entities that commit offenses as stated in Article 242 of this law, they shall be punished by a fine from 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels to 200,000,000 (two hundred million) Riels.

3- For legal entities that commit offenses as stated in Article 243, Article 246, and Article 247 of this law, they shall be punished by a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 100,000,000 (one hundred million) Riels.

4- For legal entities that commit offenses as stated in Article 245 of this law, they shall be punished by a fine from 10,000,000 (ten million) Riels to 20,000,000 (twenty million) Riels.

5- For legal entities that commit offenses as stated in Article 250 of this law, they shall be punished by a fine from 50,000,000 (fifty million) Riels to 200,000,000 (two hundred million) Riels.

6- Legal entities may be subject to one or more additional penalties as stated in Article 168 (Additional Penalties Applicable to Legal Entities) of the Criminal Code of the Kingdom of Cambodia.

#### Article 250 .- Responsibility of Instigators and Accomplices

Any instigator or accomplice who provides assistance, lures, provides ideas, or forces another person to commit a criminal offense against the tax provisions as stated in this law and other relevant legal instruments shall be punished the same as the principal.

#### Article 251 .- Commission of Corruption Offense

1- For offenses as stated in paragraph 3 of Article 243, Article 246, and Article 247 of this law, and which are committed by public officials or citizens holding public mandates through election, they shall be defined as corruption offenses, which shall be punished in accordance with the Law on Anti-Corruption and shall be under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Anti-Corruption Unit for investigation and preparation of corruption case files for the court.

2- The investigation of corruption offenses in the tax sector and the preparation of corruption case files in this sector for the court is the exclusive jurisdiction of the Anti-Corruption Unit in accordance with the Law on Anti-Corruption.

#### Article 252 .- Application of Other Criminal Laws

The application of the provisions of Chapter 18 of this law does not prevent the application of other criminal laws in case the act defined as an offense in this law is also an offense as prescribed in other criminal laws.

### Chapter 19
### Transitional Provisions

#### Article 253 .- Transitional Provisions

All legal instruments that have been put into implementation based on the Law on Taxation promulgated by Royal Decree No. NS/RKM/0297/03 dated February 24, 1997, and the Law on Amendment of the Law on Taxation promulgated by Royal Decree No. NS/RKM/0303/010 dated March 31, 2003, and subsequent amendments, shall continue to have the effect of implementation until new legal instruments are established to replace them, in accordance with the provisions of this law.

### Chapter 20
### Final Provisions
